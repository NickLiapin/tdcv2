description: Family names in Urdu. What this file holds is NOT simply "surnames": at least five different things sit in the family-name slot here and they do not behave alike. (1) Tribe and clan names — خان, آفریدی, یوسفزئی, نیازی, بگٹی, ٹالپور, باجوہ, چیمہ, سندھو — inherited by descent but shared by tens of thousands of unrelated households, so two people carrying one are not thereby related. (2) Caste and biradari names — ارائیں, گجر, جٹ, اعوان, راجپوت, کھوکھر — which state social position openly, and are why a name here often tells a reader far more than a European surname would. (3) Claimed lineage titles — سید (descent from the Prophet), قریشی, صدیقی, فاروقی, عثمانی, ہاشمی, and the Shia نقوی, رضوی, زیدی, کاظمی — religious-genealogical claims that hardened into surnames. (4) Titles of rank turned family name — شاہ, میر, مرزا, بیگ, راجہ, رانا, نواب, چوہدری — several of which ALSO appear in person/prefix, so the same word can legitimately open or close a full name. (5) Place nisbas — دہلوی, لکھنوی, امروہوی, حیدرآبادی, جالندھری — saying where a family came from, especially common among the Urdu-speaking Muhajir families who moved from India in 1947. Two things a config cannot infer. First, a large number of people here carry NO inherited family name at all and are recorded under a second given name — Muhammad Aslam, whose son is registered as Aslam Khan — so a dataset built from this file will be tidier than the population it models. Second, Urdu family names do NOT inflect for gender: there is no Kowalski/Kowalska here, one list serves male and female rows alike, and there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack. Its absence is the design. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. No public frequency table exists for names in the Urdu-speaking world — Pakistan's census does not publish one and NADRA does not release name counts — so the list is ordered by observed commonness and carries the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.7 to 1 from head to tail. That is the right shape here, because Urdu names really are diffuse: the region has an enormous number of names in use and nothing resembling the concentration Nguyen has in Vietnam or Kim in Korea. خان is placed first because it is uncontroversially the commonest of these, but the curve does not claim to know by how much.
locale: ur
weighted: true
