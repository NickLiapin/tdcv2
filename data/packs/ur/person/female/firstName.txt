description: Female given names in Urdu. The first block is the women of early Islam — عائشہ, فاطمہ, خدیجہ, زینب, مریم — which is where a very large share of names in this population comes from, and unlike a fashion name their frequency does not fall away from one decade to the next. Three further patterns a European list has no room for: the ـہ feminine formed from a male name (عابد → عابدہ, رشید → رشیدہ, حمید → حمیدہ), which pairs most of the middle of this list to person/male/firstName; the Persian ناز and گل compounds (شہناز, گلناز, مہناز, گلنار); and بی بی and خاتون, which are honorifics that in rural Pakistan are also, on the record, a great many women's entire registered name. The last block is modern urban naming, which is much shorter and much lighter — منال, حرا, دعا, ایمان, لائبہ. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. No public frequency table exists for names in the Urdu-speaking world — Pakistan's census does not publish one and NADRA does not release name counts — so the list is ordered by observed commonness and carries the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.7 to 1 from head to tail. That is the right shape here, because Urdu names really are diffuse: the region has an enormous number of names in use and nothing resembling the concentration Nguyen has in Vietnam or Kim in Korea.
