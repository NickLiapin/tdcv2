description: Male given names in Urdu. محمد leads and the reason is structural rather than statistical: across the Urdu-speaking world it is very commonly the FIRST element of a two-part name whose SECOND element is what the man is actually called — Muhammad Iqbal is addressed as Iqbal, Muhammad Aslam as Aslam — so محمد functions as a prefix as much as a given name and stands at the head of a very large share of male records. A config joining one first name to a surname will be right; one assuming exactly two name parts will not. The compound families are the other shape a European list has no room for: عبد + a divine attribute (عبدالرحمٰن, عبدالقادر, عبدالستار) is ONE name, not two words; غلام + a holy figure (غلام رسول, غلام حسین) is another; ...الدین (شمس الدین, نظام الدین) a third; and ...اللہ (ثنا اللہ, عطا اللہ) a fourth. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. No public frequency table exists for names in the Urdu-speaking world — Pakistan's census does not publish one and NADRA does not release name counts — so the list is ordered by observed commonness and carries the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.7 to 1 from head to tail. That is the right shape here, because Urdu names really are diffuse: the region has an enormous number of names in use and nothing resembling the concentration Nguyen has in Vietnam or Kim in Korea. One consequence to know: the curve does NOT encode محمد's real prominence, which as a leading name element is far larger than 1.7 to 1 — a config that needs a realistic Muhammad share must set that weight itself rather than trust this file.
locale: ur
weighted: true
