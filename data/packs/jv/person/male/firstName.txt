description: Javanese male given name, ngoko-neutral. Weights are RANK-DERIVED on the project's default Zipf curve, not counts from a registry — Indonesia publishes no name-frequency table. Four things mark these as Javanese rather than Indonesian. (1) The SU-/SOE- prefix, from Sanskrit su- 'good': Sukarno, Suharto, Sudirman, Sutrisno, and it stacks so freely that a village census reads as page after page of Su-. (2) The FINAL -O, which is not a suffix but an ORTHOGRAPHY: Javanese pronounces a word-final /a/ as [ɔ], so a name written Suharta in Sanskrit-derived spelling is written Suharto in Indonesian-facing spelling — Widodo, Wibowo, Purwanto, Hartono. A name ending in -o in an Indonesian dataset is a strong Javanese signal. (3) The BIRTH-ORDER numerals Eko, Dwi, Tri, Catur, Panca — Sanskrit one to five, given for the order the child was born in, so Eko is very often an eldest son. (4) The PASARAN block — Paijo, Wagiman, Legiman, Ponimin, Kliwon, Ngadimin, Tukiman — names built from the day of the FIVE-DAY market week (see date/pasaran.txt) the child was born on: Pahing gives Pai-/Par-, Wagé gives Wagi-, Legi gives Legi-, Pon gives Poni-, Kliwon gives Kliwon outright. These read as rural and older-generation to Javanese ears and are getting rarer, which is itself a fact a dataset should be able to model. Javanese has SPEECH LEVELS (unggah-ungguh): ngoko for equals and intimates, madya in between, and krama for a respected addressee, with a krama inggil vocabulary layer on top for the addressee's own body, acts and possessions. THIS PACK SHIPS NGOKO, the base register — it is the citation form in every Javanese dictionary, the form Javanese Wikipedia writes its running prose in, and the only register in which a general word list is register-neutral enough to be safe. Names themselves are register-free, but the honorific put in front of one is not — see person/prefix.txt.
