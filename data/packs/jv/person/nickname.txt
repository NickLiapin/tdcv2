description: Everyday Javanese short form of a given name. The rule is the OPPOSITE of English: Javanese clips from the END of the name, not the beginning. Sutrisno is Trisno or No, Bambang is Bang or Mbang, Suharto is Harto or To, Sugiyono is Yono or No, Wahyuningsih is Ningsih or Sih, Painem is Nem. Because Su- and Sri- are carried by so many people they are almost never the part kept — the short form is taken from what follows them, which is what distinguishes one Su- name from the next. A second pattern is pure REDUPLICATION of the final syllable for children: Nono, Toto, Yoyo, Nunung, Titik, Tutik, Ninik, Nanik — these are what a Javanese is called at home for life, and many appear on business cards. The -ik and -ok endings on that block are a diminutive that only exists in the nickname system.
locale: jv
