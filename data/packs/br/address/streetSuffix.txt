description: The thoroughfare type, and in both of Brittany's languages it goes BEFORE the name — Straed ar Vugale, Hent an Aod, Rue de la Gare — so this composes leftwards onto address/streetName and a config that copies English order produces something no panel in Brittany shows. The list is mixed on purpose and the split is the whole point: the French form is what the post office reads and what the legal address is, and the Breton form is what the second line of the bilingual sign says, on the same pole, over the same street. Straed is a street, hent a road, bali an avenue, plasenn a square, kae a quay, ru the Vannetais word for a street (and the one Gwened puts on its signs), palud a salt marsh — Brittany's coastal towns really do have a Rue de la Palud, because the marsh was there before the street was — venell an alley, gwenodenn a footpath, porzh a courtyard or a harbour, kroaz a cross and kroaz-hent a crossroads. Two of the French entries are not thoroughfare types in the ordinary sense and are here because rural Brittany cannot be addressed without them: an enormous number of Breton addresses have no street at all, only Lieu-dit Kergoat or Hameau de Kervern and then the postcode, and the postal system treats that as complete. One point of grammar for anyone composing: straed and ru are FEMININE, so an adjective following them lenites — ar Straed Vras, from bras — while hent is masculine and leaves the adjective alone, an Hent Bras.
