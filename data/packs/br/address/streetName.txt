description: The name part of a street in Brittany, to be composed after a streetSuffix. Four kinds of value sit here and they take different grammar, so a config must know which it has drawn. Personal names and Breton phrases follow the type word bare — Rue Anatole Le Braz, Straed ar Vugale — and take no preposition; the French common nouns already carry their own preposition and article inside the value (de la Gare, du Port, des Ajoncs, de l'Église) and must never be given a second one. The Breton values are written with the article already mutated as the language requires and must not be regularised back to the dictionary form: ar Vugale from bugale, ar Vilin from milin, ar Wazh from gwazh, ar Groaz from kroaz, ar C'hoad from koad. Brittany's bilingual signage is real, recent and administrative rather than decorative — the Ofis Publik ar Brezhoneg's Ya d'ar brezhoneg charter, launched in 2001, commits a signatory commune to putting Breton on its road signs, and more than two hundred communes have signed it — so in Kemper, An Oriant, Karaez and Gwened a street genuinely has two names on one panel, and any dataset drawn from Brittany will meet both. That is why Anne de Bretagne stands beside Anna Vreizh, Pierre-Jakez Hélias beside Per-Jakez Helias, Jean-Pierre Calloc'h beside Yann-Ber Kalloc'h: one person twice over, which is the data and not a fault in it. The people are Brittany's before they are France's — the ninth-century rulers Nominoë, Erispoë and Alain Barbetorte who made it first a kingdom and then a duchy, Anne de Bretagne who was twice queen of France and whose marriage contract cost the duchy its independence, the Saint-Malo corsairs Surcouf and Duguay-Trouin, Jacques Cartier who sailed from the same harbour, the writers Anatole Le Braz, Tristan Corbière, Max Jacob, Victor Segalen and Anjela Duval, Roparz Hemon of the review Gwalarn, the Pont-Aven painters Gauguin, Sérusier and Émile Bernard, the cyclists Bernard Hinault, Louison Bobet and Jean Robic, the sailors Éric Tabarly and Alain Colas, and Jean-Marie Le Bris, a Douarnenez sea captain who in 1856 got a glider off the ground by towing it behind a cart on the beach. Three of the French values are worth reading closely: de Siam, the main street of Brest, is named for the Siamese embassy that came ashore there in 1686; des Terre-Neuvas and des Islandais commemorate the cod fleets that sailed from Saint-Malo and Paimpol for Newfoundland and Iceland and buried men every season; and du 18 Juin 1940 carries a weight here it carries nowhere else, because almost every man on Enez-Sun answered it and left.
