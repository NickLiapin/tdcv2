description: The opening element of a Breton place name, and the most historically loaded list in the pack. Plou- (from Latin plebs, a primitive parish), Lan- (a monastic enclosure), Tre- and Treb- (a subdivision carved out of a plou) and Loc-/Lok- (a holy place, a hermit's cell) were laid down by the British migrants who crossed the Channel between the fifth and the seventh century, so the distribution of Plou- names on a map of Brittany traces where those settlers actually came ashore; the eastern edge of Plou- country is a real line on the ground, and beyond it the toponymy turns Romance and Gallo, which means the Breton-speaking west and the Gallo east can be told apart from the map alone before anyone opens their mouth. Ker- (from kêr, a hamlet or a homestead) is the most productive element of all, names thousands of places and a long tail of families with them, and belongs to a much later and steadier wave of clearance than the parish names. The rest are landscape and structure: Pen and Penn a head or an end, Ros a hillock, Koad a wood, Lann a moor or a hermitage, Kastell a castle, Porzh a harbour or a courtyard, Enez an island, Mor the sea, Ti a house, Kroaz a cross, Feunteun a spring, Menez a mountain, Traon a valley bottom, Lez a chief's court, Bod and Bot a dwelling, Goas a stream, Poul a pool, Run a mound, Gwern an alder marsh, Prat a meadow, Sant a saint. The Koad/Coat, Gwic/Guic, Ti/Ty and Traon/Traou pairs are one element in two spellings, the second of each frozen on the French cadastral map and the first written by the modern orthography, and both are current. Two traps. First, the second element MUTATES after the first, so it is Kervihan and never Kerbihan, Kervras and never Kerbras, Penn-ar-C'hoad and never Penn-ar-Koad; a config that concatenates one of these prefixes with a raw second element produces a form that appears on no signpost. Second, the join differs by series: Ker, Plou, Plo, Ple, Pleu, Lan, Lann, Tre, Treb, Loc, Lok, Gwic and Guic write solid and swallow the following capital (Kervihan, Lokmaria, Plouzane), while Sant, Penn, Enez, Mor and Kroaz take a hyphen and keep it (Sant-Erwan, Enez-Vriad, Penn-ar-Bed). Values carry no hyphen of their own, and alone none of them names a real town.
