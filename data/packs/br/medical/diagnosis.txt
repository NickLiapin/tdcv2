description: Diagnoses that can occur in any patient, with no sex or ancestry restriction — sex-specific conditions live in diagnosisMale and diagnosisFemale, ancestry-linked ones in diagnosisByAncestry. The values are French, and that is the fact this bucket exists to record rather than a gap in the pack: the consultation at a Quimper surgery may be conducted in Breton, but the line that enters the file is French and the code under it is CIM-10, of which no Breton edition has ever existed; medical/symptom.txt holds the patient's half of the same conversation. The caseload is Brittany's and not a generic European one. Alcohol runs through it because it runs through the region's mortality tables: Brittany has carried one of France's heaviest alcohol-attributable death rates for decades, and the chain from dépendance à l'alcool through syndrome de sevrage, cirrhose alcoolique and pancréatite chronique to the cancers of the oesophagus, larynx and mouth is the most Breton sequence in this file. Intoxication médicamenteuse volontaire is here because suicide rates in the western départements, Côtes-d'Armor and Finistère above all, stand well above the national figure. Hémochromatose héréditaire and mucoviscidose are here because the founder genetics of the Celtic west put both at their French maximum in Brittany. The occupational block is the other regional signature. Troubles musculo-squelettiques — syndrome du canal carpien, épicondylite, coiffe des rotateurs, lombalgie — are the most frequently recognised occupational illness in the region, produced on the cutting and packing lines of the agri-food plants and the fish-processing halls that are Brittany's largest private employers; plaques pleurales and mésothéliome pleural are the legacy of asbestos in the naval yards at Brest and Lorient; maladie de Parkinson and lymphome non hodgkinien appear on the French agricultural occupational-disease tables and are fought over in the farming départements; intoxication au sulfure d'hydrogène is the green-algae question that recurs on the northern beaches; intoxication par phycotoxines closes shellfish beds several times a year; and accident de décompression belongs to a region whose divers and scallop dredgers work under water for a living.
