description: Familiar and affectionate forms of Breton given names. Almost the whole list is one suffix: -ig, the Breton diminutive, which is fully productive and attaches to anything — Yann gives Yannig, Fañch Fañchig, Katell Katellig, Pêr Perig, Loeiz Loeizig. It is not restricted to names, and that is the point a config writer should take from this file: paotr is a lad and paotrig a little lad, tamm a piece and tammig a little bit, mamm a mother and mammig mummy, and the plural of a diminutive is -igoù, so paotrigoù. Breton devoices a final consonant, so -ig is pronounced as if it were -ik, and when French clerks and French neighbours wrote these names down they wrote what they heard: Yannick, Annick and Soizic are Yannig, Annaig and Soazig spelled by an outsider, and they have since become full legal first names in France, borne by hundreds of thousands of people who may not know the suffix is a diminutive at all. The rest of the list is truncation rather than suffixation, and the truncations are not obvious from the full name: Gaid is Marc'harid, Naig and Nanig are Annaig, Vefa is Jenovefa, Job is Jozeb, Lom is Gwilherm, Soaz is Frañseza, and Fañch — which reads as a full name today and is one — began as the short form of Frañsez. A dataset that treats these as distinct people from the bearers of the long forms will double-count.
