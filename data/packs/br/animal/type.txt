description: Kind of animal, the farmyard first and then the wild, in Breton throughout. What to notice is how the language names an unfamiliar beast: it builds a compound out of two familiar ones, and the result is transparent in a way the Latin-derived French name never is — morhoc'h, the porpoise, is a sea-pig; dourgi, the otter, is a water-dog; hoc'h-gouez, the wild boar, is simply the wild pig. Cattle carry four separate words rather than one word with modifiers, buoc'h, tarv, ejen and leue, which is what a language does with the animal its economy rests on, and Brittany still holds a very large share of France's dairy herd. bleiz, the wolf, stays in the list although Brittany has had none for well over a century — the last were hunted out of the inland Argoad — and it survives instead as a given name and inside surnames. Every value is a bare dictionary form, and that is the trap: buoc'h is feminine and therefore ar vuoc'h after the article, never ar buoc'h, so a template that glues these strings to an article, to ma, da, daou or tri without changing them first will produce forms a speaker reads as broken.
