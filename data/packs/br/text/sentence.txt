description: Filler sentences in Breton, the lorem-ipsum prose of this pack, kept short and plain on purpose — six to fourteen words, one or two clauses, the register of a Breton primary reader or a radio bulletin: the household waking, the boat and the tide, the field and the herd, the market, school, rain and wind, the road between Brest and Roazhon, cider and crêpes, a fest-noz, an illness, a football match. The clause is built inside out by the standards of anything else spoken in France. Breton fronts exactly one element and then plants a particle in front of the verb — a when the fronted element is the subject or the direct object, e when it is anything else — so Yann a zebr bara is Yann eats bread with the subject fronted, and Bara a zebr Yann is that same sentence with the object fronted; in both, debriñ has lenited to zebr, because the particle a softens whatever follows it, and that mutation is part of the machinery rather than decoration. The particle e carries its own mixed set, which is why dont surfaces down this file as o tont and e teuan. A second frame, just as common, fronts the verbal noun itself and lets ober carry the person: Debriñ a reomp lein asambles, eating we-do breakfast together. The present progressive is periphrastic — bezañ plus the particle o (oc'h before a vowel) plus the verbal noun, emaon o labourat, I am working — and bezañ has two present tenses where French has one, a split Breton shares with Welsh and Irish and with almost nothing else nearby: emañ for where a thing is and what it is doing, eo and zo for what a thing is and what it is like, hence Emañ ar vag er porzh against Ur porzh bras eo An Oriant. The possessive particles are learned as a set, because each forces a different mutation: tad, father, gives ma zad, da dad, e dad, he zad — four different first consonants for one noun. Plurals run in -où and -ed (bagoù, merc'hed, pesketaerien), but a noun after a numeral stays singular, tri mab for three sons and never tri mibien; a plural lexical subject also leaves the verb in the third person singular, which is why these lines say ar vugale a red and not a redont. The spelling is peurunvan, the unified orthography of 1941, so zh stands at exactly the points where the KLT dialects say /z/ and Vannetais says /h/ (Breizh, brezhoneg, krampouezh, bezhin), and c'h is a single letter of the alphabet whose apostrophe belongs to the letter, distinct from ch and never to be stripped or normalised away. Tense, person and verb are varied deliberately down the file so that a random draw does not read as one drill, and nothing here is a question: every line closes on a plain full stop.
