description: Common Breton nouns, singular, in the bare dictionary form — which is exactly what makes them dangerous to concatenate. Breton mutates a noun's first consonant according to what stands in front of it, and the article alone is enough: kazh, a cat, is ar c'hazh after the definite article because kazh is masculine and the article takes the hard route on k, while merc'h, a girl, is ar verc'h because feminine singulars soften. So none of these values is usable after ar, an, al, ma, da, e, he, daou or tri without being changed first, and a generator that glues them on raw will produce forms a speaker reads as broken. Three things are worth noticing in the list itself. Kêr, with the circumflex, is the town — it is the same word as the Ker- that opens thousands of Breton place names and a long tail of Breton surnames, where it means the smaller thing, a homestead. Amzer covers both time and weather with one string, so a config drawing it for either sense is right twice. And the plural is not a suffix you can guess: -où is the commonest (tiez is irregular, bugale from bugel is suppletive, and -ed marks animates, kizhier from kazh), which is why this file holds singulars only.
