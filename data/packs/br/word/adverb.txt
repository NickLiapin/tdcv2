description: Common Breton adverbs. The interesting part of this field is how many of them are built rather than borrowed: a-wechoù, sometimes, is a plus gwech, a time, with the a forcing the soft mutation that turns gwech into wech, so the mutation is frozen inside the word and the hyphen marks the join; a-raok and a-benn ar fin are assembled the same way. Muioc'h and nebeutoc'h carry the comparative suffix -oc'h, which contains the letter c'h and must never be split. Two of these are worth a config writer's attention because they change the sentence around them: morse, never, and the negative adverbs generally require the verbal negative ne ... ket frame to be present or reduced, so Breton says ne welan morse rather than putting a bare never in front of a positive verb; and gwall, which means very when it stands in front of an adjective and badly when it stands alone, softening what follows it. Amañ, aze and du-hont are a three-way deixis — here by me, there by you, yonder away from both — where French makes do with ici and là.
