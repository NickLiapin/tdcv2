description: Common Breton verbs, given as the verbal noun, which is the form a dictionary lists and the nearest thing Breton has to an infinitive. It is genuinely a noun as well as a verb, and that single fact drives most of the grammar around it: the progressive is built by putting the particle o in front of it — emaon o labourat, I am working, oc'h evañ before a vowel — and the future and the modal constructions reach for it in the same way, so this list is the raw material for a great deal more than a dictionary entry. The endings are the giveaway: -añ and -iñ are the two productive ones (skrivañ, digeriñ), -out and -et are older and irregular (gouzout, gwelet), and a handful of the commonest verbs are bare stems with no ending at all (mont, dont, kas, koll). Bezañ and kaout, to be and to have, are both suppletive and both irregular in every tense, and bezañ splits into two present tenses that no neighbouring language distinguishes — emañ for where a thing is and what it is doing, eo for what it is, a division Breton shares with Welsh and Irish. C'hoari, to play, opens with c'h, a single letter of the alphabet, and is the one entry here that a naive alphabetiser will misfile.
