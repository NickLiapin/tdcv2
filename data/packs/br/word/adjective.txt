description: Common Breton adjectives in their dictionary form, and two rules govern every one of them. The first is position: a Breton adjective FOLLOWS its noun, ti bras a big house, ur vaouez yaouank a young woman, so a config that builds a phrase in the English order will produce something no speaker would say. The second is mutation, and it bites here rather than on the noun — an adjective after a feminine singular noun softens its own first consonant, so bihan becomes vihan in ur verc'h vihan, a little girl, while ur paotr bihan, a little boy, leaves it alone. The values in this file are therefore the unmutated citation forms and are correct only in the masculine slot. Worth knowing too: the comparative and superlative are suffixes, -oc'h and -añ, and the comparative suffix contains the letter c'h — bras, brasoc'h, brasañ — so a mechanical truncation of these strings will destroy a letter rather than trim a word. Glas is the trap every learner meets: it covers blue and the green of grass and leaves, while gwer is the green of manufactured things, a division of the spectrum Breton shares with Welsh and Irish and not with French.
