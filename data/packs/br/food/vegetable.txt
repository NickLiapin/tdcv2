description: Vegetables, Breton first. This is the one food field in which Brittany is a national heavyweight rather than a regional curiosity: the ceinture dorée, the 'golden belt' of light frost-free coastal soil running from Saint-Pol-de-Léon round to Paimpol, grows the bulk of France's cauliflower, artichoke and shallot, and the trade is organised enough to have its own politics — growers occupied the sous-préfecture of Morlaix over prices in 1961, and the producer auctions founded in that quarrel still set them. Two words for potato sit side by side on purpose: patatez is the everyday one and avaloù-douar the older 'earth-apples', and both are current, so a dataset drawn from speech will meet either. bezhin is seaweed, cut off the Léon coast and eaten as a vegetable rather than only spread on fields as manure. The mutation trap in this file is the numeral: pour 'leeks' becomes daou bour after 'two', because daou and div trigger the soft mutation, and a template that writes daou pour is wrong in a way any reader notices at once.
