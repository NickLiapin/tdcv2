description: What a Breton kitchen holds, the Breton words at the head and a French tail for the things a shopping list writes in French. The reason Brittany's butter is salted is fiscal rather than culinary: the gabelle, the French salt tax, did not apply here — the province kept its exemption as a condition of the 1532 act of union — so salt stayed cheap on this coast and butter was salted here for centuries while the rest of France ate it fresh, which is why beurre demi-sel is a Breton signature and not a preference. Note gwinizh du, literally 'black wheat', which is buckwheat and not a wheat at all: it is a knotweed, it ripens in three months on thin acid soil that will carry nothing else, and it is the grain of poor inland Brittany turned into the savoury galette. laezh-ribod is buttermilk, the sour drink poured with galettes, and French in Brittany borrowed it back as lait ribot, which is on the list too because a dataset will meet both. The trap: every Breton value here is a dictionary form, and Breton alters a word's first sound to suit the word in front of it, so bara 'bread' is unchanged in ar bara but becomes da vara 'your bread' — dropping a raw value into that slot produces a form no speaker would write.
