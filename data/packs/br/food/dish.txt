description: Dishes and drinks eaten in Brittany, the local kitchen first and the ordinary international tail after it. The list carries a fact about French rather than about Breton: kouign-amann, far, kig-ha-farz and chouchen are not translations but loans, words French took whole, so a menu written entirely in French in Quimper is already carrying Breton — and krampouezh sits behind the word crepe on every crêperie board in the region. Cotriade is the same borrowing running the other way in spelling only: it is Breton kaoteriad, 'a potful', from kaoter, a cauldron, and it is the soup a crew made from the part of the catch nobody would buy. The four drinks at the end earn a place on a dish list because a Breton table is described by them: chistr, cider, is drunk from a bolenn, a bowl rather than a glass; lambig is the spirit distilled from it; chouchen is fermented honey; and Breton beer is a real modern industry rather than a heritage act, restarted at Carhaix in 1985 by Coreff after brewing here had effectively stopped, with something like a hundred breweries following it.
