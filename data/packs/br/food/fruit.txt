description: Fruit, with the Breton names of the orchard and the hedge at the head and a French tail for everything that arrives by lorry. sivi is the one with a history: Amédée-François Frézier, a military engineer whose surname is one of the better coincidences in botany, carried five Chilean strawberry plants home to Brest in 1714, and the modern garden strawberry is the cross between that Chilean species and a Virginian one. Plougastel, across the roadstead from Brest, turned it into a trade and shipped strawberries to England by the boatload, which is why a peninsula of a few thousand people had its own steamers. lus is the bilberry of the moors, irin the sloe of the hedge, and kraoñ-kelvez the hazelnut, kelvez being the hazel tree itself. The trap for a config writer: these are unmutated dictionary forms, and Breton changes a word's first sound after certain small words — kerez 'cherries' becomes da gerez 'your cherries' under the soft mutation and ma c'herez 'my cherries' under the spirant, which is one of the places the letter c'h comes from in the first place.
