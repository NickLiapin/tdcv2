description: Concrete everyday objects a Breton household actually buys — hand tools, kitchen and table things, clothes, furniture, and the gear of a boat and a garden — given in the bare dictionary singular, which is the one form no running Breton sentence ever uses. Breton mutates a noun's first consonant according to what stands in front of it, and gender decides which nouns move: taol, a table, is feminine, so after the article it is an daol, and kador becomes ar gador and pillig becomes ar billig, while pod, a pot, is masculine and stays ar pod with nothing touched. A config that writes ar plus a raw value from this file will therefore be accidentally right on the masculine rows and visibly wrong on the feminine ones, and it will be wrong again after daou, tri, ma and da. Taol is worth a second look on its own: with feminine gender it is a table, with masculine gender the identical string means a blow or a throw, and nothing but the mutation after the article separates the two. Four values have no French replacement and are still sold as a set in any Breton hardware shop: pillig is the flat cast-iron griddle a crepe is cooked on, rozell the small wooden T-rake that spreads the batter across it, spanell the long thin blade that lifts and turns it, and bolenn the wide handleless bowl cider is drunk from rather than a glass. Two more are transparent compounds worth reading: disglavier, an umbrella, is dis- plus glav, a de-rainer, and marc'h-houarn, a bicycle, is an iron horse. Koef is the starched lace headdress, which varied by parish closely enough that a woman's home town could be read off her head at a fair, and botou-koad, written here botoù-koad, are the wooden sabots.
locale: br
