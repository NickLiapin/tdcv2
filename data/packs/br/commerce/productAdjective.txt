description: Qualities as they appear in a product name, and this is the file that fixes the order in which the four commerce word-lists combine. A Breton adjective FOLLOWS its noun. The name is noun plus adjective — kador vras, taol nevez, pod bras — never the English or Serbian order, and a generator that emits adjective-then-noun produces a string no speaker will parse as a phrase at all. The second half of the rule is the trap. An adjective standing after a feminine singular noun takes the soft mutation on its own first consonant, and a masculine noun leaves it untouched. Worked through end to end: kador, chair, is feminine, so a big chair is ur gador vras — the article softens the noun k to g, and the noun in turn softens the adjective b to v, so neither word appears in the finished phrase in the form this pack stores it in. The masculine pod, pot, gives ur pod bras with nothing moved anywhere. Soft mutation on adjectives runs k to g, t to d, p to b, g to c'h, d to z, b to v, m to v, gw to w, so brav, bras, bihan, berr, blot, bouk, buan, kaer, koant, kalet, kreñv, kempenn, ker, tev, tomm, talvoudus, dispar, divlam, dinamm, dous, garv, glan, mat, moan, marc'had-mat, pounner, padus, pizh and plaen — better than half this list — all change shape on a feminine row while the vowel-initial and l-, n-, r-, s-initial values never do. Two entries reward a second look. Marc'had-mat, cheap, is literally good market, the price you got at the fair rather than a property of the object. And ker means dear in both English senses at once, costly and beloved, separated from kêr, a town or a homestead, by the circumflex alone — the same kêr that opens Kerdraon, Kervella and several thousand Breton place names — so a pipeline that strips diacritics from this pack turns a price into a village.
