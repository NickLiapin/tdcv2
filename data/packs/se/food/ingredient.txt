description: What a kitchen keeps. The head of the list is one animal taken apart, and that is the principle worth understanding: a slaughtered reindeer yields meat, blood, liver, marrow, tongue, bone for broth, sinew for sewing thread, hide for boots and coats, and antler for knife handles and buttons, and a herding household historically wasted none of it. varra, blood, is an ingredient and not a by-product — it goes into sausage and into pancakes. dávttit, bones, are an ingredient because the broth is the point. vuostá is cheese, which in the traditional form is made from reindeer milk, a thing that requires milking a half-wild animal and was given up in most of Sápmi in the twentieth century. gáffe, coffee, deserves its place: coffee consumption in the Sami north is formidable, boiled in a pot over a fire rather than filtered, and offering it is the opening move of every visit. The Norwegian entries are the ordinary contents of a modern cupboard.
