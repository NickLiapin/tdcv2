description: Animals, and the first eleven values are the reason this pack exists. Northern Sami does not have one word for reindeer with modifiers attached; it has a separate noun for the animal at each age and standing, because the animal is the economy and the distinctions are commercial. boazu is a domesticated reindeer and goddi is a wild one, two different words for the same species. miessi is a calf of the year, čearpmat a yearling, varit a two-year-old male, vuobis a three-year-old male, goasohas a four-year-old male. áldu is an adult female that has calved, vuonjal a young female, rotnu a female that did not calve this year — which decides whether she is slaughtered. heargi is a castrated male trained to pull, the animal a family owns rather than herds. nammaláhpat describes an animal by its antlers, which are asymmetric or lopsided, and antler shape is how a herder recognises individuals in a herd of thousands at a distance. None of this is romantic. A herder saying varit is saying an age, a sex, a weight class and a price in one word, and a Norwegian or English sentence needs six words to say the same thing, which is why the herding trade in Norway conducts itself in Sami whatever language the paperwork is in. The rest of the list is ordinary: predators the herd is lost to, the farm animals of the coast, and the household ones. Note njálla, the arctic fox, against njalla in address/buildingType, the storehouse on a pole — the same shape with different first vowels, and Sami distinguishes á from a as two separate letters.
