description: Birds. The pattern to notice is which ones have Sami names and which do not, because it maps exactly onto which ones matter. rievssat, the willow ptarmigan, and giron, the rock ptarmigan, are the winter game birds and each has its own word — Giron is also the Sami name of the town Sweden calls Kiruna. gáranas the raven and guovssat the Siberian jay are the birds of the forest camp, the jay notorious for stealing from a drying rack and, in the old reckoning, for carrying news. goaskin is the golden eagle and sáhpánfálli, the mouse-hawk, is the rough-legged buzzard, and both are of direct economic interest because an eagle takes calves and a herder is compensated for what it takes. njukča the swan gives March its name, njukčamánnu, since its return is the reliable sign that the year has turned. cuonjá is the goose whose spring passage was the older signal of the same thing. For the seabirds and the small passerines the list uses the Norwegian names, and that is not laziness — it is what a Sami birdwatcher in Finnmark says, because the field guide, the school syllabus and the ringing scheme are all Norwegian, and the language never needed a word for a puffin it had no dealings with.
