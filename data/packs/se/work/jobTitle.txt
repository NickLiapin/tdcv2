description: Job titles, and the list is split down the middle by language in a way that says something exact about working life in Sápmi. The Sami-titled jobs are the ones the language has a word for because the work is done in it: herding, slaughter, craft, fishing, teaching, translation, broadcasting, administration and the health and municipal posts that a Sami-language service needs. Most of those titles are built with the agent suffix -eaddji or -i on a verb — oahpahit to teach gives oahpaheaddji, ovddidit to develop gives ovddideaddji, vuodjit to drive gives vuoddji — and that suffix is why a small language can name a profession invented last year without borrowing a word. The Norwegian-titled jobs are the rest of the labour market, and they are Norwegian because that is what the vacancy notice, the certificate and the union agreement say. A person holds both halves at once: a herder's daughter is a sykepleier at the hospital in Kárášjohka and a boazodoalli at the corral in September, and no employment register anywhere records the second one. Note also that titles here carry no gender — Sami has none — so one string covers a man and a woman, unlike the Czech or Polish equivalents of this file.
locale: se
