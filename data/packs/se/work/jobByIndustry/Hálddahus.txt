A minority language lives or dies by its offices, and this is where a large share of Sami-speaking professionals work: the three Sámediggis, the municipalities of the Sami administrative area, the county authorities and the state bodies that handle grazing, land and language. Two features are unusual. Consultation is a legal duty — Norwegian authorities must consult Sami interests on measures that affect them, which creates a whole category of case work with no equivalent elsewhere in the country. And the Finnmark Estate, established in 2005, transferred ninety-six per cent of Finnmark's land from the state to a body governed jointly by the county and the Sámediggi, which made land administration in the north a Sami responsibility rather than something done to Sami.
