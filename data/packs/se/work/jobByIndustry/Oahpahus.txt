This sector carries a weight in Sami life that it does not carry elsewhere, because the school is where the language was taken away and where it is being put back. From the 1850s until after the Second World War, Norwegian policy forbade Sami in the classroom and boarded children away from home to enforce it; the same happened in Sweden and Finland. Today a child in Guovdageaidnu or Kárášjohka can be taught in Sami from kindergarten through upper secondary, and the shortage that limits everything is teachers — a sámegieloahpaheaddji must have both the subject and the language, and there are never enough. Sámi allaskuvla, the Sami University of Applied Sciences in Guovdageaidnu, teaches in Sami and trains most of them.
