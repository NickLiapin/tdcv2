description: Male given names. Two naming systems run in parallel over the same men and this list holds both, which is the single most important thing to know before modelling Sami people. A man born in Guovdageaidnu is entered in the Norwegian population register as Nils, Per, Johan or Aslak, and is called Niillas, Piera, Juhán or Áslat by everyone he knows; the Sami form is not a nickname or a translation but the name, and the register form is the one the state could spell. On the Finnish side the same pair runs Antti and Ánte, Matti and Máhtte, Pekka and Beahkká. That doubling is a direct legacy of assimilation policy — Norwegianisation ran from the 1850s until after the Second World War, Sami was banned in schools and boarding schools took children from their homes, and a Sami-shaped name on a document was an obstacle. The compound names are the third of the list that looks unusual and is in fact the commonest shape of all: Nils-Aslak, Per-Ánte, Johan-Máhtte, where the first element is very often the father's or grandfather's name. That is because a Sami child is normally named for a specific dead or living relative, so given names cycle through a family for generations and a village accumulates many living men with the same one, and the compound plus the ancestor chain is how they are told apart. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. No state publishes name frequencies for Sami speakers as a group, because no state counts them as one; the ranks encode which names recur in Sami-language media, church books and herding-district rolls, and nothing more.
