description: Languages, and the first ten entries are the point of the list. There is no single Sami language: there are nine or ten, spread from central Norway to the Kola Peninsula, and they are not dialects of each other — a speaker of davvisámegiella, Northern Sami, and a speaker of máttasámegiella, South Sami, cannot hold a conversation, any more than a Dane and a German can. Northern Sami is the largest by a wide margin, with roughly twenty-five thousand speakers, which means it accounts for most Sami speakers alive and still would fit in a small town. The others range downward steeply: Lule and South Sami in the hundreds to low thousands, Inari and Skolt in the hundreds, Ume and Pite brought back from a handful of speakers by deliberate revival work, Kildin holding on in Russia, and Ter and Akkala effectively gone, Akkala with its last known speaker dead in 2003. Two more entries deserve attention. kveanagiella is Kven, the Finnic language of the descendants of Finnish settlers in northern Norway, and meängieli is Meänkieli, its counterpart in the Torne Valley in Sweden — both are recognised minority languages, both are neighbours of Sami rather than forms of it, and both were suppressed by the same assimilation policies. The rest of the list is built the ordinary Sami way, on the noun giella, language, with the country or people prefixed: dárogiella is Norwegian, and it splits further into girjedárogiella and ođđadárogiella, since Norway has two written standards and a Sami child in Norway meets both at school.
