description: Nationalities, formed almost without exception by adding -laš to a country name, which is the standard Sami derivation for a person from a place. Three entries break the pattern and each breaks it for a reason. sápmelaš, a Sami, comes first because the identity it names cuts across four states and matches none of their citizenships — a sápmelaš is Norwegian, Swedish, Finnish or Russian on paper and neither of those facts cancels the other. dáčča is the Sami word for a Norwegian, and it is not built on the country name at all; it is an old word for the southern, non-Sami neighbour, and it carries whatever tone the speaker gives it. láddelaš does the same job on the Finnish side and sits here beside suopmelaš, the neutral term formed from Suopma: one language, two words for a Finn, and the choice between them says something. kveana is the fourth: the Kven of northern Norway are a recognised national minority with their own language, their own long presence in Finnmark and no state anywhere, and they are neither Norwegians nor Sami.
