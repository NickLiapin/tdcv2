description: Countries as Sami-language media and the Sámediggi write them, sorted in Sami alphabetical order, which puts á after a, č after c, đ after d, ŋ after n, š after s, ŧ after t and ž last. Only the near neighbours and the old great powers have names of their own here, and those names are worth reading: Norga, Ruoŧŧa, Suopma and Ruošša are the four states Sápmi is divided among, and not one of their borders was drawn with any reference to it — the Norway-Sweden border cut the migration routes in 1751, the Finnish border closed in 1852, and families found their summer and winter pastures in different countries. The 1751 treaty carried a supplement, the Lapp Codicil, that guaranteed Sami the right to cross with their herds regardless of the border, and it is still cited today. Nuortariika, Austria, is literally the eastern realm; Duiska is Germany; Vuolleeatnamat is the Netherlands, the low lands, rendered word for word rather than borrowed; Ruonáeatnan is Greenland, the green land, likewise; Juovlasuolu is Christmas Island, named for the day it was sighted; Frankriika, Kiinná, Japána and Durka are old borrowings worn into Sami shape. Everything else is the internationally current name written with Sami spelling — that is not a shortcut but what actual Sami practice does, since a language with twenty-five thousand speakers has had no occasion to coin a native word for Vanuatu.
