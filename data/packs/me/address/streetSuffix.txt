description: Montenegrin thoroughfare type. It goes BEFORE the specific name — Ulica Njegoševa, Bulevar Svetog Petra Cetinjskog, Trg republike — and is written lowercase when it trails the name instead, which is a formatting decision for the config rather than a second list. Three entries are geography rather than vocabulary. Riva is the paved waterfront a Boka town is built along, and the word is Venetian, left behind by the four centuries when Kotor, Perast and Budva were Venetian and the mountain behind them was not; a Montenegrin coastal address can sit on a riva while an address twenty kilometres inland has no use for the word at all. Mahala is the Ottoman quarter, the counterpart term, and it survives where the other empire reached — Podgorica's Stara varoš, Pljevlja, Rožaje, Plav, Bijelo Polje. Vijenac, a crescent, shows the ijekavian reflex that runs through the whole pack: Serbian writes venac, Montenegrin writes vijenac, from the same long jat that gives Njegoš's Gorski vijenac its title. Brijeg is the same rule again, against Serbian breg. Sokak, from Turkish, is the narrow lane of an old town and is what you actually walk in Kotor, where the streets are too narrow for the word ulica to feel right.
