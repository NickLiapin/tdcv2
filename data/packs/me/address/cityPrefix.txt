description: Leading element of a Montenegrin place name (Bijelo Polje, Herceg Novi, Donja Gorica, Veliki Zalaz). The qualifier comes FIRST and has to agree in gender with the settlement noun behind it, which is why the list runs in masculine/feminine/neuter triples: Novi Bar but Nova Varoš but Novo Selo. The colour pair is the one to look at. Serbian writes the ekavian beo/bela, Montenegrin writes the ijekavian bijeli/bijela/bijelo, and that single vowel is the difference between Belo Polje and Montenegro's fourth-largest town, Bijelo Polje. The other colour, crn, names the country itself: Crna Gora, the black mountain, the calque Venetian sailors turned into Montenegro when they looked at the dark pine cover of the Lovćen massif from the water. Sveti has no neuter form in the list because saints are men and women, and it produces the coastal names — Sveti Stefan, Sveti Nikola, Sveta Nedjelja. Combine with a real toponym; on its own the prefix names nothing.
