description: Fruit. Smokva, nar and maslina lead because the Montenegrin coastal strip is properly Mediterranean — figs and pomegranates ripen unaided around Bar and Ulcinj, and the olive groves of the Bar municipality run to hundreds of thousands of trees and include the Stara maslina at Mirovica, over two thousand years old and the oldest living thing in the country. Grožđe follows because vranac and krstač are grapes native to the Skadar basin and the Ćemovsko polje vineyard outside Podgorica, planted on stony river gravel, is one of the largest single vineyards in Europe. The list then climbs inland: šljiva, jabuka, kruška and dunja belong to the continental north around Bijelo Polje and Berane, while malina, borovnica and dren come off the Durmitor and Bjelasica uplands, where berries are still gathered wild rather than farmed. Note lješnik and not lešnik — Montenegrin is ijekavian without exception, and the reflex of the old jat vowel is what marks a text as belonging here faster than any single word does.
