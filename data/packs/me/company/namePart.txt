description: Evocative noun for a Montenegrin company name, to be composed with a legalSuffix. The list leans hard on rivers, mountains, plemena and towns because that is genuinely how firms here are named: the brewery at Nikšić is called Trebjesa after the wooded hill it stands under, the insurer is Lovćen after the mountain above Cetinje, the freight forwarder at the Port of Bar is Zetatrans after the river and the plain, and the vineyard on Ćemovsko polje — one of the largest single vineyards anywhere in Europe — trades as Plantaže. Two of the grape names are in the list for the same reason: vranac, the black-red native red, and krstač, the native white grown almost nowhere else, are trademarks as much as cultivars. Note Zvijezda and Svjetlost rather than the Serbian Zvezda and Svetlost, and Uspjeh and Pobjeda rather than Uspeh and Pobeda — the ijekavian reflex is not decoration on a company name, it is the spelling the register will hold. Aluminijum earns its place from the Podgorica smelter that carried the country's exports for a generation, and Divokoza, the chamois, from Durmitor, where the animal is on the national park's own emblem. Katun-country names such as Sinjajevina and Bjelasica read to a Montenegrin as pasture and summer rather than as scenery.
locale: me
