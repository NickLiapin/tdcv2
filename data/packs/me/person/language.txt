description: Language name in Montenegrin. The first four values are a political fact rather than a linguistic list: crnogorski, srpski, bosanski and hrvatski are mutually intelligible standards of one Štokavian system, and Montenegro's constitution names Montenegrin the official language while making the other three in official use. At the 2011 census 36.97 per cent of the population named Montenegrin as their mother tongue and 42.88 per cent named Serbian, which is why a Montenegrin data set cannot assume its own language is the majority answer to that question. Albanian and Romani follow because they are constitutionally in official use in the municipalities where those communities live — Ulcinj, Tuzi, Plav and Gusinje for Albanian. Note njemački rather than the ekavian nemački, and slovenački for Slovene against slovački for Slovak, a one-letter difference that swaps two countries.
