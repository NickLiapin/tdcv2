description: Short form of a name in Montenegro. The dominant male pattern is unmistakable and almost mechanical: take the first syllable and add -o. Mišo from Mihailo, Boro from Borislav, Savo from Savo or Savatije, Vuko from Vukašin, Đoko from Đorđije, Peko from Petar, Rako from Radoslav, Šćepo from Šćepan. The -o ending is a vocative-derived masculine hypocoristic and it is so productive here that many of these have hardened into legal given names in their own right and appear on identity cards; several are also the first half of surnames, which is how Bošković and Perović were built in the first place. The female forms use -a instead and cut differently, keeping a stressed interior syllable rather than the opening one: Goca from Gordana, Ceca from Svetlana, Jeca from Jelena, Zaga from Zagorka, Snježa from Snježana. Baćo, meaning brother, and Seka, meaning sister, are kinship words used as names for a person whose place in the household outranks what they were christened.
