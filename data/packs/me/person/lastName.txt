description: Montenegrin surname, ONE list for both sexes — Montenegrin surnames do not inflect for gender, so Milica and her brother Marko are both Popović, which is why this pack ships no person/female/lastName. Almost every value ends in -ić, and that is not a stylistic preference but the shape of the society that produced them: a Montenegrin surname is a frozen patronymic naming the founder of a bratstvo, the brotherhood of families descended from one man, and several bratstva together make a pleme, the tribe. Vasojevići, Kuči, Piperi, Bjelopavlići, Njeguši, Rovca, Drobnjaci and Cuce are plemena, and a Montenegrin asked who he is will often name his before his surname, because the surname alone does not identify him: Popović occurs in unrelated bratstva all over the country, and Popović from Bjelopavlići and Popović from Rovca are not relatives. Some names here are their own history — Šćepanović and Vukčević carry the ć and šć of the local phonology, Petrović-Njegoš was the ruling house out of the Njeguši pleme on Lovćen, Karadžić and Hadžić and Mustafić come from the Muslim northeast, and Orlandić and Lipovina from the Venetian-ruled coast. The weights are the pack's rank-decay curve on a steeper exponent than the given-name lists, because Montenegro publishes no surname incidence table and yet the concentration is real: the country has about 620 000 people and a few hundred surnames cover most of them.
