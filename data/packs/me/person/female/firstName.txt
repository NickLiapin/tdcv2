description: Montenegrin female given name. The ijekavian reflex of jat is visible in the list itself and not only in the grammar: Snježana rather than the Serbian Snežana, Cvijeta rather than Cveta, Vjera rather than Vera. Those are the same three words, and the ije/je is the whole difference. The list also carries the country's Muslim minority — Amina, Emina, Selma, Amra, Šejla and Lejla are ordinary names in Rožaje and Plav, where Bosniaks are the majority — because a Montenegrin name list that stopped at the Orthodox stock would be describing about four fifths of the country. Older Slavic names ending in -ka and -va (Vukosava, Milosava, Radojka, Savka, Krstinja, Novka) belong to the generations now in their seventies and eighties and are what a registry of births from the 1940s and 1950s looks like; the top of the list is what a maternity ward records now. Weights are the pack's rank-decay curve rather than measured shares, because no Montenegrin given-name frequency table is published.
