description: Montenegrin male given name. Two stocks sit in one list, which is a fact about the country rather than a compromise: the Slavic Orthodox names that dominate the west and the old tribal heartland, and the Muslim names of the northeast — Rožaje, Plav, Gusinje, Petnjica and much of Bijelo Polje — where Bosniaks are the majority, plus a few Albanian names from Ulcinj and Tuzi. Bosniaks are about nine per cent of Montenegro and Albanians about five, so a name list that stopped at Marko and Nikola would describe a country that does not exist. Several of the Slavic names are Montenegro's own rather than shared with Serbia: Balša from the medieval Balšić lords of Zeta, Šćepan and Šćepo carrying the ć/šć shift that gives Šćepan Polje and the surname Šćepanović, Batrić and Vukašin and Vukota out of the epic, Labud, Peko, Perko and Radule with the hypocoristic -o and -e endings the coast and the Katunska nahija favour, and Tripko from Saint Tryphon, whose cathedral has stood in Kotor since 1166. Weights are the pack's rank-decay curve, not a measured register: Montenegro publishes no given-name frequency table, and the real distribution is flat enough that no single male name reaches a few per cent of the population.
