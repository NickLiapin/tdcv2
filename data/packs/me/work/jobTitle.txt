description: Job title in Montenegro, given in the masculine because that is the form a job advertisement prints. Montenegrin has a feminine for nearly all of them — profesorica, ljekarka, vozačica, sudinica, inženjerka — so a config describing a woman cannot simply reuse these strings. The vocabulary itself is ijekavian, and the difference is not decorative: a doctor is a ljekar, not a lekar, because the word is built on lijek; a shift supervisor is a nadzornik smjene, not smene; a court expert is a vještak; a dairyman is a mljekar. Four industries are over-represented here for good reason. Tourism is the largest sector of the economy and fills the hotel and restaurant block. The sea fills another: the Pomorski fakultet in Kotor has trained deck and engineer officers since 1959, in a bay whose navigation school at Perast was teaching Russian noblemen sent by Peter the Great in 1698, and Montenegrin officers sail on foreign flags all over the world. Mining and heavy industry fill a third, around Pljevlja coal, Nikšić bauxite and steel, and the Podgorica aluminium plant. The fourth is the state: in a country of 620,000 people the public administration, the schools, the health service and the police are between them the largest employer there is.
