description: Filler words in Montenegrin, the lorem-ipsum vocabulary of this pack. Written by hand: data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs assembles these three files from public-domain books and there is no Montenegrin title to draw on, so the list was compiled rather than extracted. It is deliberately ordinary — kin, the body, the house, food, the land and the sea, animals, plants, weather, time, the town, work, abstractions, adjectives, verbs, numerals and function words — so that a random draw reads as Montenegrin rather than as a glossary of one subject. Two features run through it, and together they are why the list cannot be swapped for a Serbian one. The first is jat, the old Slavic vowel: long it becomes ije, giving dijete, mlijeko, vrijeme, snijeg, rijeka, cvijet, brijeg, riječ, zvijezda, sijeno, tijesto and korijen; short it becomes je, giving mjesec, vjetar, djeca, djed, dječak, medvjed and pjevati. Serbia's eastern standard has a plain e in every one of them. The second is the pair of letters the 2009 Pravopis crnogorskoga jezika added to the alphabet, ś and ź, which no other Štokavian standard writes: eight words here carry them — śekira, śever, śenka, śećanje, ośećaj, śutra, źenica and iźutra. Four nouns have no clean equivalent anywhere else. katun is the summer settlement a family moves its sheep up to above the treeline and abandons in September; krš is the bare grey limestone that covers most of the country; čeljad counts a household as souls rather than as persons; and pleme is the tribe, the unit that organised Montenegrin society below the state and above the family, and which a Montenegrin can still name for himself.
