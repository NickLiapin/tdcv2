description: Product material in Montenegrin, given as a noun; compose it with od and the genitive — sto od hrastovine, stolica od nerđajućeg čelika, kuća od kamena. Note the wood pattern, which has no English equivalent: hrast is the oak tree and hrastovina is the timber cut from it, and the -ovina suffix does the same for beech and pine, so a material name and a species name are different words rather than the same word used twice. Two materials mean more here than a shopping list suggests. Aluminijum is the metal Montenegro made itself: the Podgorica smelter ran on the country's own hydro power and for decades dominated its exports, so aluminium sections are a domestic product rather than an import. Kamen is the national building material — the Njeguši villages on Lovćen and the whole of Boka Kotorska are built in dressed limestone and roofed in stone slabs, dry-laid without mortar in the older work, and a stone facade is still what a Montenegrin house aspires to. Pjena and memori pjena carry the ijekavian jat against the Serbian pena. Prevrnuta koža is what a listing calls suede; brušena koža is the same thing in a technical specification.
