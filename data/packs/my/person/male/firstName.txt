description: Burmese man's name, weighted. EVERY STRING HERE IS A WHOLE NAME, NOT A GIVEN NAME. Burmese has no family names, so there is nothing for this to be the first half of — see person/lastName.txt, which explains at length what that file actually contains and why joining the two is a Western form filling itself in rather than a Burmese person naming themselves. A Burmese name is one to four syllables long and is chosen, not inherited; siblings share nothing, and a wife's name does not change on marriage. Names have grown longer over time, so a single syllable (လှ, နု, သန့်) reads as an older generation and four syllables (အောင်ကောင်းမြတ်) as a younger one. REDUPLICATION IS REAL AND VERY COMMON — ကျော်ကျော်, ထွန်းထွန်း, မြမြ, နီနီ are ordinary names, not diminutives. HONORIFICS ARE NOT PART OF THE NAME and are not in this list: ဦး, ဒေါ်, ကို, မ, မောင် live in person/prefix.txt because they change with the speaker's age and status relative to yours, so the same person is ကိုအောင် to a friend and ဦးအောင် at sixty. THE FIRST LETTER IS NOT FREE: a Burmese child is traditionally named from the DAY OF THE WEEK IT WAS BORN, and the day fixes the class of consonant the name must start with. Sunday takes the vowels and အ; Monday က ခ ဂ ဃ င; Tuesday စ ဆ ဇ ဈ ည; Wednesday လ ဝ; Thursday ပ ဖ ဗ ဘ မ; Friday သ ဟ; Saturday တ ထ ဒ ဓ န. Wednesday afternoon is a separate eighth day, ရာဟု, and takes ယ ရ. This list is GROUPED IN THAT ORDER, Sunday first, so a config that wants day-consistent names can slice it rather than filter it — and a config that draws at random is producing people whose names and birthdays disagree, which any Burmese reader will notice immediately. The practice is astrological and it is still ordinary: the eight posts around a pagoda are the eight days, and a Burmese person knows their birth day the way a Westerner knows their star sign. The list is built from the ordinary Burmese name morpheme stock — အောင်, ကျော်, မင်း, ဇော်, ထွန်း, သိန်း, မိုး and the rest — combined the way Burmese names are actually combined, and every combination here is a name real men in Myanmar carry. NO NAME WAS INVENTED BY ANALOGY: where a combination looked plausible but is not one Burmese uses, it was left out rather than added to reach a target length. The weights are a gentle tiered curve, NOT measured frequencies: Myanmar publishes no name-frequency statistics of any kind, so a steep curve here would claim knowledge that does not exist. Note that many of these elements are unisex — မိုး, ဇင်, ခိုင်, ထက်, ဝင်း, အေး, ကြည် — so this list and person/female/firstName.txt OVERLAP ON PURPOSE and the overlap is not a duplication bug.
