description: Common adjective (Welsh), in the masculine radical. Two things this list cannot carry. FIRST, the adjective goes AFTER its noun, unlike English — tŷ mawr, "big house" — with a short closed set of exceptions that go in front (hen, prif, unig, ambell, holl). SECOND, a handful still have a separate FEMININE form made by changing the vowel inside the word, not by adding anything: gwyn/gwen, melyn/melen, trwm/trom, byr/ber, crwn/cron, llym/llem, dwfn/dofn, tlws/tlos. So "a white house" is tŷ gwyn but "a white cat" is cath WEN — feminised AND soft-mutated, two changes at once, from a string that offers no clue that either is due.
