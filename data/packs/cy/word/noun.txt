description: Common noun (Welsh), given in the RADICAL — the unmutated dictionary form. That decision matters more here than in almost any other language, and this file is where the pack records it. A Welsh noun changes its FIRST LETTER according to what stands in front of it, and there are THREE separate mutation systems. SOFT (treiglad meddal) turns p/t/c into b/d/g, b/d into f/dd, g into nothing at all, m into f, ll into l and rh into r: cath becomes ei GATH ("his cat"), gardd becomes yr ARDD. NASAL (treiglad trwynol) turns c/p/t into ngh/mh/nh and g/b/d into ng/m/n, and it is what makes the Church in Wales YR EGLWYS YNG NGHYMRU rather than yn Cymru, and Bangor YM MANGOR. ASPIRATE (treiglad llaes) turns p/t/c into ph/th/ch: ei CHATH, "her cat", against ei gath, "his cat" — the ONLY thing distinguishing his from hers is which mutation the noun takes. So a value drawn from this list cannot be dropped into a sentence unchanged, and no amount of inspecting the string tells a generator which of the four forms is wanted. Two further facts a config cannot infer. Welsh nouns are MASCULINE or FEMININE and the list does not mark it, yet gender decides mutation: a feminine singular noun mutates after the article (y GADAIR from cadair) and mutates the adjective after it (cadair FACH). And the plural is not a suffix system: Welsh has around two dozen plural patterns, several of which change the vowel inside the word rather than adding anything (bachgen/bechgyn, dafad/defaid, oen/ŵyn, tŷ/tai, brawd/brodyr) — while a large group runs the OTHER WAY, the collective being basic and the singular derived with -en or -yn (coed/coeden, plant/plentyn, sêr/seren, adar/aderyn, pysgod/pysgodyn).
locale: cy
