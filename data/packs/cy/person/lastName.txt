description: Welsh surnames, weighted by their real share of the population of Wales — the count is per MILLION, so Jones,57500 means 5.75 per cent of everyone in the country. The concentration is the point and it is genuine, not an artefact of a short list: JONES alone is about one person in seventeen, the top five (Jones, Davies, Williams, Evans, Thomas) are together about nineteen per cent of the population, and the top twenty about a third. No other European country is anywhere near that. The reason is historical: Wales had a patronymic system (Dafydd AP Gwilym, "David son of William") and no fixed family names until English administration imposed them from the sixteenth century onward, at which point clerks froze whatever father's name was to hand and added an English genitive -s. So Jones is "John's", Davies "David's", Williams "William's", Evans "Ifan's", Roberts, Edwards, Griffiths the same — the whole stock descends from a few dozen common baptismal names, which is why so few surnames cover so many people. The other visible pattern is the fused particle: PRICE is ap Rhys, POWELL ap Hywel, PRITCHARD ap Richard, PROSSER ap Rhosier, PUGH ap Huw, PARRY ap Harri, BEVAN ab Ifan, BEYNON ab Einion, BOWEN ab Owain — the p- or b- at the front IS the word for "son". A few are epithets rather than patronymics: LLOYD is llwyd (grey), VAUGHAN is fychan (the younger), GOUGH is coch (red), GETHIN cethin (swarthy), VOYLE moel (bald). Welsh surnames DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER — the same string serves a man and a woman — so there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack and its absence is the design. That is worth stating because the historic patronymic DID mark sex (ap/ab "son of" against ferch/verch "daughter of"); the surname froze on the male form and stopped varying. The handful of ap- and ferch- entries at the foot of the list are the modern conscious revival, real but rare. This list is much shorter than a Polish or Spanish one and it is not padded: it is short because Welsh surnames genuinely are few.
