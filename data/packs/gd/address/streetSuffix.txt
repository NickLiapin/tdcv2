description: Gaelic thoroughfare generic. IT GOES BEFORE THE NAME, not after it — Sràid a' Chaisteil, Rathad an Locha, Ceàrnag nan Gàidheal — which is the mirror of the English order, and a config that copies English produces something no bilingual street sign in Scotland shows. Grammatical gender matters here and is not visible in the word: SRÀID, SLIGHE, CEÀRNAG, DROCHAID, FAICHE, CÙIRT and CLADACH are feminine and lenite a following adjective or personal name (Sràid Sheumais from Seumas), while RATHAD, CIDHE, BEALACH, GÀRRADH, BRUTHACH and CNOCAN are masculine and leave it alone. address/streetName.txt sidesteps that by shipping definite genitives, which do not vary.
