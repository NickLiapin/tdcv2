description: The leading generic of a Gaelic place name. Gaelic settlement names are overwhelmingly TRANSPARENT COMPOUNDS — a landscape word plus a qualifier — which is why they can be composed at all: BAILE is a township, ACHADH a field, CILL a church-cell, DÙN a fort, INBHIR a river-mouth, CAMAS a bay, RUBHA a point, ÀIRIGH a shieling. Combine with address/citySuffix.txt. This does NOT by itself name a real place, and the real settlements of Scotland belong in a country pack. Note that several of these are feminine (Cill, Beinn, Creag, Coille, Àirigh, Innis, Abhainn) and so LENITE the qualifier that follows: Cill Mhoire, not *Cill Moire.
