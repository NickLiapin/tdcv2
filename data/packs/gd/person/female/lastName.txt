description: Scottish Gaelic surnames — the form a WOMAN carries, parallel to person/lastName.txt line for line and DERIVED FROM IT BY RULE, never edited by hand. The rule has two clauses and no exceptions. (1) MAC becomes NIC: MacDhòmhnaill/NicDhòmhnaill, Mac a' Ghobhainn/Nic a' Ghobhainn, Mac an t-Saoir/Nic an t-Saoir. Nic is not a different word from Mac but a contraction of NIGHEAN MHIC, "daughter of the son of"; the element after it is left exactly as it stands, because the lenition frozen there was caused by the -c that Nic ends in just as Mac does. (2) Every other surname LENITES its initial consonant, written as an inserted H: Caimbeul/Chaimbeul, Camshron/Chamshron, Grannd/Ghrannd, Moireach/Mhoireach, Gòrdan/Ghòrdan, Dòmhnallach/Dhòmhnallach, Bochanan/Bhochanan, Siosal/Shiosal. Three environments block clause (2) and the surname comes through unchanged, which is the rule working and not a gap: L, N and R are never marked for lenition (Leòdach, Rothach, Robasdan), a vowel takes none, and the clusters SG SM SP ST refuse it (Stiùbhart stays Stiùbhart). A Gaelic woman does NOT take a husband's surname in the masculine form — Màiri NicDhòmhnaill married to a Caimbeul is Màiri Chaimbeul, never Màiri Caimbeul. Counts are copied from the masculine list so the two stay aligned.
