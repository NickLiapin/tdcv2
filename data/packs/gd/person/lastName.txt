description: Scottish Gaelic surnames — the form a MAN carries. Draw once: a Gaelic full name has one surname. THE PREFIX IS GENDERED, which is the single fact a config must know about this list: MAC is "son", and a woman takes NIC instead — Nic is a contraction of NIGHEAN MHIC, "daughter of the son of", so MacDhòmhnaill is a man and NicDhòmhnaill his sister. Non-Mac surnames mark the same distinction by LENITING for a woman: Caimbeul becomes Chaimbeul, Grannd becomes Ghrannd. See person/female/lastName.txt, which is derived from this list line for line by that rule. Inside the surname itself the lenition is already frozen in place and shows as an inserted H: Dòmhnall gives MacDhòmhnaill, Tòmas MacThòmais, Pàrlan MacPhàrlain, Fionnghan MacFhionghain, Beathan MacBheathain. Where you see no H the rule was blocked, not forgotten — C and G do not lenite after the -c of Mac (MacCoinnich, MacGriogair, never MacChoinnich), and L, N and R have no written lenition at all (MacLeòid, MacNèill, MacRath). Two other shapes appear here. MAC AN / MAC A' + a noun is a trade or an office rather than a father: Mac a' Ghobhainn is the smith's son (Smith), Mac an t-Saoir the joiner's (MacIntyre), Mac an t-Sagairt the priest's (MacTaggart), Mac an Aba the abbot's (MacNab), Mac a' Phearsain the parson's (MacPherson), Mac na Ceàrdaich the craftsman's (Sinclair in Caithness). And the -ACH forms (Dòmhnallach, Leòdach, Rothach, Frisealach, Sutharlanach, Cuimeanach, Rosach, Mèinnearach) are clan adjectives used with the article — AN DÒMHNALLACH, "the MacDonald" — which coexist with the Mac- form rather than replacing it, so both are here on purpose. Counts are a Zipf estimate of prevalence in the Gaelic-speaking areas, taken from the published frequency of the anglicised equivalent (MacDhòmhnaill is MacDonald, Moireasdan is Morrison, Mac a' Ghobhainn is Smith); they are a fitted shape and not a census.
