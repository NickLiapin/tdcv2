Ranked by how common the name is among Gaelic speakers — the order follows the anglicised equivalents in the Highland and Western Isles birth registers (Iain/John, Dòmhnall/Donald, Calum/Malcolm, Aonghas/Angus, Murchadh/Murdo lead by a wide margin) — and the counts are a Zipf taper fitted to that shape, NOT a measured frequency: no register has ever tallied the Gaelic spellings. The concentration is steep on purpose and is real. Every name is a genuine Scottish Gaelic form, not an Irish one: Gaelic writes only the GRAVE accent (Dòmhnall, Pàdraig, Mìcheal), where Irish writes only the acute (Dónall, Pádraig, Mícheál), so a name here is distinguishable from its Irish cousin by the direction of the stroke alone. BHALTAIR (Walter) is written with its lenition fossilised into the citation form; that is the received spelling and not an error. MAOL CHALUIM is the older full form of Calum. In direct address every one of these takes the VOCATIVE — a Iain, a Dhòmhnaill, a Chaluim — which lenites the initial and slenderises the ending, so a name drawn from this list cannot be spoken to a person unchanged.
