description: Gaelic verb in its ROOT form, which is also the second-person singular imperative. GAELIC HAS NO INFINITIVE: a dictionary heads a verb with the bare command — ITH, "eat" — and every other form is built from it, so there is nothing here corresponding to English "to eat" and a config that expects one will not find it. Two further facts a config cannot infer. Gaelic word order is VERB FIRST — Chunnaic mi an cù, "saw I the dog" — so a verb drawn from this list opens the sentence rather than sitting in the middle of it. And GAELIC HAS NO VERB "TO HAVE": possession is said with the preposition AIG, "at" — tha taigh agam, "a house is at me" — so the commonest verb in English has no entry in this list at all, and its absence is the design.
