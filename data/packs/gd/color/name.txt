description: Colour word. Gaelic does NOT cut the spectrum where English does, and this is the clearest case in the pack of a list that cannot be translated word for word. GORM is blue — and also the green of growing grass, so a Gaelic field is gorm. GLAS is grey — and also the grey-green of the sea and of unripe things. UAINE is green, but only the vivid or artificial kind, the green of paint and not of a hillside. LIATH is a pale blue-grey, the colour of hair going white. RUADH is a red-brown that English splits between russet, auburn and ginger. BÀN is fair or blond where GEAL is bright white; DONN is brown but is the ordinary word for brown hair. CROTAL and GUIRMEAN are named from dyestuffs — crotal is the lichen that gives Harris Tweed its brown, guirmean the woad that gives blue. Colour adjectives follow their noun and lenite after a feminine one: bròg dhearg, not *bròg dearg.
