description: Diagnosis. Read the first block before using this list: MANY GAELIC DISEASE NAMES ARE LEXICALISED WITH THEIR ARTICLE and are not words without it — A' CHUING is asthma, A' CHAITHEAMH consumption, A' BHUIDHEACH jaundice, AN CNATAN the common cold, AN T-TRIUTHACH whooping cough, A' GHRIÙTHRACH measles, AN LÒINIDH rheumatism, AN TINNEAS-SIÙCAIR diabetes. A config that strips the article to make the list uniform destroys the word. That old stratum is followed by transparent compounds built on AILLSE (cancer), GALAR (disease), FÀILLIGEADH (failure) and CLACH (stone), which is how Gaelic names a condition it has no single word for, and finally by the eponyms and international terms, kept exactly as written because they are names rather than descriptions.
