description: Tibetan given-name elements borne by men, rank-ordered. THREE THINGS A CONFIG CANNOT INFER. (1) A full Tibetan personal name is normally TWO of these elements drawn one after the other and joined by a tsheg, e.g. bsTan-'dzin + rGya-mtsho = Tenzin Gyatso; the inventory of elements is small and closed, and the variety lives in the pairing, not in the list length. (2) Many of these are UNISEX and appear identically in person/female/firstName.txt on purpose - Tsering, Tenzin, Tashi, Pema, Kelsang, Yeshe, Rinchen, Karma, Sonam, Jamyang and the seven weekday names Nyima, Dawa, Migmar, Lhakpa, Phurbu, Pasang, Penpa are all given to men and to women. (3) The spelling is historical and does not predict the pronunciation: bkra-shis is read Tashi, rdo-rje is read Dorje, brtan is read ten. Written Tibetan keeps prefixes and superscripts that fell silent a thousand years ago, so any transliteration or sort key must be derived from the spelling, never from the sound. The weights are the project's default decay curve over a qualitative frequency ranking: there is no name census for Tibetan anywhere, so these are NOT measured shares - only the head of the list (Tenzin, Tsering, Dorje, Sonam, Tashi) is securely commoner than the tail.
