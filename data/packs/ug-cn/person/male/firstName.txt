NO NAME FREQUENCY TABLE IS PUBLISHED for Uyghurs, so the weights are rank-derived Zipf values and not registry counts: read them as an ordering, not as shares. Three layers are visible. The Arabic-Islamic core is by far the largest — Abdulla, Muhemmet, Exmet, Ismail, Yaqup, Sulayman — and the Abdu- prefix (Abdurehim, Abdukerim, Abdurshit, Abduweli) is the single most productive element in the whole list. A DAY-AND-FESTIVAL LAYER IS SPECIFICALLY UYGHUR and has no counterpart in Turkish or Uzbek: Jume is a boy born on Friday, Rozi one born in Ramadan, Heyt one born at the Eid, Qurban one born at Qurban Heyt, Barat one born at Berat. The Turkic layer is smaller but distinctive — Tömür, Arslan, Bughra, Yalqun, Qutluq. THE SUFFIX -jan (soul, from Persian) attaches to almost any of these to make Tursunjan, Exmetjan, Ablajan, and is not itself a separate name. Every vowel here is written with a letter of its own, and a word-initial vowel carries the hemze ئ — see world/writingSystem.txt.
