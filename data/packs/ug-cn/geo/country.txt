THE FIRST ROW IS THE FACT TO KNOW: Uyghur calls China Junggo, straight from the Chinese Zhongguo, and not by any Turkic or Persian word — where Uzbek says Xitoy and Turkish Cin, ug-cn borrowed from the state language it lives beside. Most other names arrive with a Persian-Turkic shape: the -iye ending does the work of -ia (Germaniye, Italiye, Turkiye) and the Persian -istan means land-of (Pakistan, Qazaqistan, Afghanistan, Özbékistan). England stands for the whole United Kingdom, as it does in most of Central Asia. Country names take case suffixes like any other Uyghur noun, and the suffix HARMONISES: Junggoda in China but Türkiyede in Turkey, Iranda but Németde — a config that appends a fixed -da will be wrong half the time.
