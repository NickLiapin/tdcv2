description: Uyghur dish. THE THREE THAT DEFINE THE KITCHEN are lengmen, hand-pulled noodles served under a stir-fried topping; polu, the rice pilaf cooked with mutton and carrot that is the food of a wedding; and kawap, mutton grilled over charcoal with cumin and chilli, which is why zire (cumin) is the first entry in food/spice.txt. NAN IS NOT A SIDE DISH BUT THE STAPLE — baked on the wall of a clay tonur, in a dozen named shapes, and girde nan is the small thick ring. Several names are borrowed straight from Chinese and kept: dapenji is 大盘鸡, big-plate chicken, and lengpung is 凉粉, cold starch jelly. Meat means mutton unless another animal is named; pork appears nowhere in this pack, and that is not an oversight.
