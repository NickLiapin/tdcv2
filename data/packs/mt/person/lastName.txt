description: Maltese surnames, commonest first. Four layers are visible in this list and they are the island's history: the Arabic-era names that survived the Christianisation (Borg, Zammit, Farrugia, Sammut, Xuereb, Fenech, Mifsud, Bugeja, Zarb, Cutajar), the Sicilian and Italian names of the late-medieval settlement (Camilleri, Vella, Micallef, Azzopardi, Caruana, Cassar, Pisani, Rizzo, Testa, Bonello), a thin Norman-French and Catalan-Aragonese layer, and the DOUBLE-BARRELLED surnames at the tail of the list, which are ordinary in Malta because the pool of family names is small and a distinguishing second name is often carried. NOT ONE SURNAME IN THIS LIST CARRIES A MALTESE DIACRITIC, and that is not an encoding failure — it is the second fact worth knowing about Maltese names. Family names were fixed in writing by Sicilian and Italian notaries centuries before the Maltese alphabet was standardised in 1924, so they keep the old spellings and use letters the Maltese alphabet does not even contain: Zammit not Żammit, Fenech and Grech and Cauchi with a CH that Maltese orthography has no use for, Cutajar and Camilleri with a bare C, Buhagiar with a plain H. A validator that requires Maltese text to contain ċ ġ ħ ż will reject this file, and the file is right. The given-name lists are the same: the register spells them in English and Italian. MALTESE SURNAMES DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER: a woman named Borg is Borg, and there is no feminine form — which is why this pack ships NO person/female/lastName.txt, unlike the Slavic locales. Its absence is the design. The counts are the pack's DEFAULT DECAY CURVE, not measured frequencies; the curve is defensible here because no Maltese surname dominates — the commonest, Borg, is a low single-digit percentage of the population, nothing like Vietnamese Nguyen.
