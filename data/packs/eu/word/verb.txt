description: Common verb (Basque, perfect-participle citation form — the shape a dictionary lists and the shape most auxiliary constructions build on). Basque is ERGATIVE-ABSOLUTIVE. Whether a verb is transitive decides how its subject is marked: a transitive verb makes its subject take -k, an intransitive one leaves the subject bare, so "the man came" is gizona etorri da while "the man ate the bread" is gizonak ogia jan du. The finite verb then agrees with up to THREE arguments at once — who did it, what was done, and who it was done to — which is why eman ("to give") has hundreds of finite forms and why no config can assemble one by string concatenation.
