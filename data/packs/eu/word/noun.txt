description: Common noun (Basque, bare citation form — the definite article is the SUFFIX -a, so etxe becomes etxea, and a config that wants a natural-looking noun must append it). The case ending of an ERGATIVE-ABSOLUTIVE language lands on these nouns: the subject of a transitive verb takes -k (gizonAK ogia jan du, "the man ate the bread") while the subject of an intransitive verb takes nothing at all (gizonA etorri da, "the man came") and is identical in form to the object of the transitive one. Spanish and French mark the object instead and leave every subject alike; Basque does the exact opposite, and this is the deepest structural difference between it and the languages surrounding it. One more thing no config can infer: the kinship terms below split by the SPEAKER's sex — a man's brother is anaia and his sister arreba, a woman's brother is neba and her sister ahizpa.
