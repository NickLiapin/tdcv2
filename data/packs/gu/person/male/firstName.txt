description: Gujarati male given names across four living generations, which is why the list looks stylistically mixed — that mixture is the correct answer, not noise. THE HONORIFIC IS THE FIRST THING A CONFIG MUST KNOW: in ordinary Gujarati a man is almost never addressed or referred to by his bare given name. The suffix -ભાઈ (literally "brother") attaches to it — રમેશભાઈ, નરેન્દ્રભાઈ, દિનેશભાઈ — and this is not decoration but the default register, used by neighbours, shopkeepers, colleagues and the local newspaper alike; the bare form reads as curt or as a formal record. -ભાઈ is often written into official documents and business names too, so a realistic Gujarati dataset should append it for spoken or informal contexts and leave it off for a passport line. The female equivalent is -બહેન — see person/female/firstName.txt. THIS LIST DOUBLES AS THE MIDDLE NAME. The Gujarati name of record is given name + FATHER'S GIVEN NAME + surname (નરેન્દ્ર દામોદરદાસ મોદી, મોહનદાસ કરમચંદ ગાંધી), so a config building a three-part Gujarati name draws this file twice — see person/lastName.txt. THE GENERATIONS, oldest first. Names ending in -લાલ, -દાસ, -ચંદ, -જી and -ભાઈ (છગનલાલ, જમનાદાસ, પ્રેમચંદ, વેલજી, ડાહ્યાભાઈ) belong to men born before roughly 1950 and are now mostly grandfathers and middle names; કરમચંદ, Gandhi's father's name, is exactly this type. The -એશ wave (રમેશ, મહેશ, સુરેશ, દિનેશ, જયેશ, હિતેશ, જિગ્નેશ) and the -એન્દ્ર wave (જિતેન્દ્ર, નરેન્દ્ર, મહેન્દ્ર) dominate men born 1950-1985 and are the single most recognisable Gujarati name shape. Short modern names (હાર્દિક, ધ્રુવ, પાર્થ, યશ, મીત, હેત, આરવ) belong to men born since about 1990. RELIGIOUS STOCK, which a Gujarati reads as a community marker. Vaishnav names are the largest group and several are specific to Gujarat: રણછોડ is Krishna as worshipped at Dakor, ઘનશ્યામ, નીલકંઠ and સહજાનંદ are the three names of Swaminarayan, and વલ્લભ is Vallabhacharya of the Pushtimarg. The Jain set (મહાવીર, વર્ધમાન, અરિહંત, જિનેન્દ્ર, પાર્શ્વ, શ્રેયાંસ) matters more in Gujarati than in most Indian languages because Gujarat holds the largest Jain population in India; કુમારપાળ, વસ્તુપાળ and તેજપાળ are the medieval Jain rulers and ministers of Gujarat and are still given to boys. ફિરોઝ, રુસ્તમ, જમશેદ, હોમી, કેરસી and મહેરવાન are Parsi — the Parsis of Gujarat and Bombay are Gujarati-speaking, and their given names are Persian while their surnames are Gujarati. The Muslim set at the end is used by Gujarat's Vohra, Memon, Ghanchi and Mansuri communities. WEIGHTS are a plausible ordering rather than a count; India publishes no given-name register. The decay is deliberately gentle because Gujarati genuinely has no dominant male name — the -એશ names are common as a CLASS, not individually.
