description: The name element of a Gujarati road, before a streetSuffix. COMPOSE SUFFIX LAST: Gujarati puts the thoroughfare type after the name (ગાંધી રોડ, આશ્રમ રોડ, નહેરુ માર્ગ), the reverse of Polish ulica Długa. These are the name-stock found on signage across Gujarat rather than one city's street register — a real named street in Ahmedabad or Surat is country data and belongs in the India country pack. The political names split two ways and the split is worth reading: ગાંધી, સરદાર પટેલ, વિવેકાનંદ and મોરારજી are Gujarat's own contribution to national life — Gandhi was born in Porbandar, Vallabhbhai Patel in Nadiad, and both Morarji Desai and Vikram Sarabhai were Gujaratis — so on Gujarati signage they outnumber the imported national names. The literary set is likewise local and reaches back six hundred years: નરસિંહ મહેતા is the fifteenth-century saint-poet who wrote વૈષ્ણવ જન તો, અખો and પ્રેમાનંદ are medieval, નર્મદ and દલપતરામ made modern Gujarati prose in nineteenth-century Surat and Ahmedabad, and ઝવેરચંદ મેઘાણી collected the Saurashtra folk tradition. શ્યામજી કૃષ્ણ વર્મા and દયાનંદ સરસ્વતી were both born in Gujarat. The remainder are the landmark and descriptive names that generate a road name in every town.
