description: Country and territory names in Sindhi, kept in the same order as the Urdu list so the two packs can be read against each other. The spelling is Sindhi throughout, and the differences from Urdu are systematic rather than cosmetic: a borrowed English t is written ٽ and a borrowed d is ڊ, and every word ending in a long i — ترڪي, چلي, هنگري — ends in plain ي, because Sindhi uses one letter here where Urdu keeps two different final forms. Where Sindhi has its own word it is used instead of an Arabic one: the compass points are اتر north and ڏکڻ south, so the divided countries come out as اتر ڪوريا and ڏکڻ ڪوريا, ڏکڻ آفريڪا, ڏکڻ سوڊان; and an island group is ٻيٽ, giving مارشل ٻيٽ and سليمان ٻيٽ. India is ڀارت, the name Pakistan uses for the modern state rather than for the subcontinent. See geo/country.txt for the weighted subset used with capitals and currencies.
