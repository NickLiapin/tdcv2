description: Leading element of a Sindhi place name. ٽنڊو is the one to learn first: it means the settlement founded by the man whose name follows it, which is why Sindh has Tando Adam, Tando Allahyar and Tando Muhammad Khan — three separate towns, none of them a place called Tando. ڳوٺ is the village as people live in it and ديهه the revenue village as the state records it; a rural address gives both, and they need not share a name. ڳڙهي is a small fortified settlement, جهوڪ a riverine hamlet. The last five are colours, and two of them show why Sindhi needed its own alphabet: ڪارو is black and کارو is salty, identical but for plain ڪ against aspirated ک. کارو names the delta villages of Thatta and Sujawal where the sea has pushed inland and the ground has turned brackish — Kharo Chan is a whole taluka named for it. Combine with a personal name or a toponym; alone, none of these names a real place.
