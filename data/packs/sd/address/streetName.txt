description: Street and road names as Sindhi forms them. The Sindhi street draws on wells no European list has, and the deepest is Shah Abdul Latif's Shah Jo Risalo: سسئي, مارئي, مومل, راڻو, سهڻي and ميهار are the heroines and heroes of its surs, not names picked at random, and streets in Hyderabad and Karachi genuinely carry them. The poets follow — لطيف and ڀٽائي for Bhittai himself, سچل for Sachal Sarmast, سامي, اياز for Shaikh Ayaz, قاضي قادن who is the earliest Sindhi verse we have, and مرزا قليچ بيگ, who wrote the first Sindhi novel and translated Hamlet into it. لاڙ, سيرو and وچولو are the three traditional regions of Sindh, lower, upper and middle, and ٿر, ڪيرٿر, منڇر and ڪينجهر are its desert, its hill range and its two great lakes. کجي is the date palm, which is not decorative here: Khairpur district is one of the largest date-growing areas in the world. Compose with address/streetSuffix — a Sindhi street name is the qualifier, and the suffix carries the noun. Names of specific national figures and cantonment roads are deliberately absent, because those belong to a country pack rather than to the language.
