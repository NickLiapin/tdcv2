description: Symptoms in Sindhi, as a patient reports one. Sindhi and Urdu share most of the technical layer, so the differences a reader meets first are orthographic: Sindhi writes ي where Urdu writes ی or ے, ه where Urdu writes ہ, and it keeps ڪ and ک apart as two letters — plain k in ڪمزوري against aspirated kh in کنگهه and کجلي — where Urdu has only one. The everyday layer is where the languages genuinely part. A Sindhi patient says سور for pain where an Urdu one says درد, تپ for fever, مٿو for head and اڃ for thirst; ۾ ("in") and ۽ ("and") are characters Unicode names after Sindhi because no other language writes them. گهٻراهٽ is worth reading as language rather than medicine: it carries palpitation, dread and nausea in one word and is among the commonest presenting complaints in a Sindhi clinic without mapping onto any single English symptom.
