description: Honorific placed BEFORE a name in Sindhi. سائين is the one to know: it is the general Sindhi term of respect and it has no single equivalent, standing in for sir, master, reverend and your honour depending on who is speaking - it precedes a name, follows one, or is used entirely on its own to address someone whose name the speaker does not know. مخدوم and پير are the heads of the Sufi shrines, of which Sindh has more per district than anywhere else in Pakistan, and their prefixed names are read out at gatherings and printed on letterheads today, not archaically. ڄام is an old Sindhi ruling title, opening with the implosive ڄ that only this alphabet has. وڏيرو is the landholder, a title of real local power in rural Sindh. سيد and مير mark claimed descent from the Prophet and حاجي marks someone who has performed the hajj - both are used in place of a title for life. Several of these words are also family names (see person/lastName), so the same word may legitimately open or close a full name. There is no Mr/Mrs pair doing all the work here: which honorific is used says what kind of authority the person holds.
