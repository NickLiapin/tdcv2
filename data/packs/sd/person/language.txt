description: Language names in Sindhi, led by سنڌي itself. Sindhi is one of Pakistan's two provincial languages with a full official life - it is the language of instruction, of the provincial assembly's record and of the courts in Sindh, which is why a Sindhi-language pack has government and legal vocabulary at all where a پنجابي one would not. The alphabet is the reason this list looks unlike an Urdu one on the page. Sindhi's Perso-Arabic script has 52 letters, the most of any Arabic-derived alphabet, because it had to write sounds Arabic and Persian never had: the four implosives ٻ ڄ ڏ ڳ, the retroflexes ٽ ٺ ڊ ڍ ڻ ڙ, the nasal ڱ and ڃ, and single letters for aspirates that Urdu spells with a following ھ. The consequence that trips up a reader coming from Urdu is that Sindhi splits k in two: ڪ is plain k and ک is aspirated kh, two letters for two sounds, so ڪتاب is a book and کٽ is a bed and ڪير who is not کير milk. Urdu writes its single k as ک, which makes that letter look like a giveaway when it is in fact the one both languages own - the letters that really separate them are ه against Urdu's ہ, ي against ی and ے, and ٽ ڊ ڙ against ٹ ڈ ڑ. The list then runs through the other languages of Sindh - ڪڇي, ميمڻي, ڍاٽڪي, ڀيلي and ڪولهي are spoken by communities inside the province and several are close enough to Sindhi to be argued about as dialects - then the rest of Pakistan, the large South Asian languages, and the world languages a Sindhi form would offer.
