description: Family names in Sindhi, and the ending is the lesson. A very large share of Sindhi surnames close in اڻي - نظاماڻي, قنبراڻي, سنجراڻي, ميراڻي, ملڪاڻي, عمراڻي, هنڱوراڻي - and the suffix is not decoration: it means 'descendants of', so نظاماڻي is the house of Nizam and قنبراڻي the house of Qambar. It attaches to a named ancestor and turns a lineage into a name, which is why the pattern can be extended to a new founder at any time and why so many of these names have a story attached that the family can still tell. The same suffix crossed the border in 1947 and is the commonest Sindhi Hindu surname ending too - آڏواڻي, گدواڻي, ڪرپالاڻي, واڌواڻي, ڀمڀاڻي, ڪيولراماڻي - which is why a Sindhi name can point to India as easily as to Pakistan and why the last block of this file is here at all. The rest divides into: large tribes and clans (سومرو, ٽالپر, ڀٽو, چانڊيو, مگسي, لغاري, ڪلهوڙو, جتوئي) several of which once ruled Sindh and gave their names to its dynasties, so a سومرو or a ڪلهوڙو is carrying a period of the province's history; the trading and religious communities (ميمڻ, شيخ, سيد, مخدوم, پيرزادو); titles of rank and office that hardened into names (شاهه, مير, ڄام the old Samma ruling title, سردار, نواب), several of which also appear in person/prefix so that the same word can open or close a full name; and occupational names still transparent to any speaker - ڪنڀر potter, لوهار blacksmith, واڍو carpenter, ماڇي and ملاح fisherman, موچي cobbler, مڱڻهار the hereditary musicians who sing at weddings. Two things a config cannot infer. Sindhi family names do NOT inflect for gender - one list serves male and female rows alike, and there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack. And a great many rural Sindhis carry no inherited surname at all, appearing on record under a second given name or a father's name, so a dataset built from this file will be tidier than the population it models. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. Pakistan's census publishes no name frequencies and NADRA releases no counts, so the list is ordered by observed commonness and carries the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.7 to 1 from head to tail. That shape is right here: Sindhi surnames are tribal and therefore numerous and regional, with nothing resembling the concentration Nguyen has in Vietnam or Kim in Korea.
