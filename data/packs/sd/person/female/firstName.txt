description: Female given names in Sindhi. The first block is the women of early Islam - عائشه, فاطمه, خديجه, زينب, مريم - which is where a very large share of names in this population comes from, and unlike a fashion name their frequency does not fall away from decade to decade. The second block is what makes this list Sindhi rather than Pakistani, and it has no counterpart anywhere else: مومل, سسئي, مارئي, نوري, سهڻي, ليلا and سورٺ are the seven heroines of the Sindhi folk romances that Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai set into verse in the Shah Jo Risalo, and every Sindhi knows their stories. سسئي walked into the desert after the caravan that took Punhun and died of thirst looking for him. مارئي, carried off by a king and held in his fort, refused his wealth and would not take off her homespun clothes because she meant to go back to Malir. نوري was a fisherwoman whom the Jam of the Samma court married above all his queens, and her tomb still stands in Keenjhar lake. To be named for one of them is to be named for constancy, and these are ordinary living names, not literary curiosities. بينظير, for Benazir Bhutto, sits with them for a related reason. Three further patterns: the ـه feminine formed from a male name (عابد becomes عابده, رشيد becomes رشيده), which pairs most of the middle of this list to person/male/firstName; the Persian ناز and گل compounds (شهناز, گلناز, گلنار); and بي بي and خاتون, honorifics that in rural Sindh are also, on the record, a great many women's entire registered name. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. Pakistan's census publishes no name frequencies and NADRA releases no counts, so the list is ordered by observed commonness and carries the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.7 to 1 from head to tail - the right shape for a naming pool this diffuse.
