description: Familiar and household names in Sindhi. Three different things are on this list and all three are how a person is actually addressed. First, the clipped given name plus ي - عثمان becomes عثي, فاطمه becomes فاطي, سعد becomes سعدي. Second, the گهر جو نالو, the house name, a completely separate name given in childhood that often bears no relation at all to the registered one: منو, مني, گڏي, گڊو, پپو, ٽيپو, مٺو, ڪارو. A great many Sindhis are known by such a name for life inside the family and by their legal name nowhere else, so a nickname column here is NOT a diminutive of the first-name column and must not be derived from it. Third, kinship words used to address people who are not relatives, and here Sindhi differs sharply from Urdu: ادا is what a Sindhi calls an elder brother or any man of about his own age, ادي the corresponding sister, and both are so ordinary that they function as a general polite address between strangers. سائين is the great Sindhi honorific, used to a landowner, a saint's descendant, a teacher or simply to a man being shown respect, and وڏيرو names the village landholder, a social position as much as a form of address.
locale: sd
