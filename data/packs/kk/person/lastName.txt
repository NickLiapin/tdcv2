description: Kazakh surname, roughly frequency-ordered, with rank-derived Zipf weights rather than registry counts — Kazakhstan publishes no surname frequency table, and no single surname dominates the way Nguyen or Kim does, so a gentle Zipf decay is the honest curve here rather than a sharply concentrated one. THREE SYSTEMS SIT SIDE BY SIDE AND ALL THREE ARE CURRENT. The first and by far the largest is the Soviet pattern: a father's or grandfather's given name plus the Russian genitive -ов / -ев, or -ин after a soft stem — Ахмет became Ахметов, Әли became Әлиев, Сейфулла became Сейфуллин. This was imposed on the population during registration in the 1920s-30s and is what most Kazakhstanis still carry. The second is NATIVE and is now officially encouraged: the father's given name plus ұлы, "his son" — Момышұлы, Байтұрсынұлы, Құнанбайұлы. It was the pre-Soviet norm, was pushed out, and has been returning since 1991; the state has repeatedly said it will make -ұлы / -қызы the standard, without yet compelling it, so families choose. The third and smallest is the bare ancestral name with no suffix at all — Бөкейхан, Шоқай, Мұртаза — often a deliberate de-Russification of an -ов form (Бөкейханов to Бөкейхан). THE FEMININE FORM IS SHIPPED, in person/female/lastName.txt, DERIVED FROM THIS FILE BY SCRIPT line for line, because the two must not drift: -ов takes -а, -ев takes -а, -ин takes -а, ұлы is REPLACED by қызы ("his daughter"), and the bare names do not change. Note that ұлы and қызы do NOT obey vowel harmony — they are frozen full words, ұл + possessive and қыз + possessive, so they are the same after a front stem and a back one, which is unusual in Kazakh and worth knowing before a config tries to harmonise them.
