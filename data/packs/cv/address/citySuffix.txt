description: Trailing element of a Chuvash place name. Two conventions share this list, and the hyphen tells them apart: a value written as a separate word (касси, вӑрман, ҫырма) is joined with a space, while a value opening with a hyphen (-лӑх, -ово, -ская) attaches straight onto the base with no space, which is how the Russian-language forms in the official register are built. The most distinctively Chuvash element here is касси, the village quarter — a Chuvash village is traditionally a cluster of named касси rather than one block, and hundreds of settlement names in the republic end in it, with кассӗ as the variant spelling that shows up on older maps and in the Chuvash press. The landscape nouns behind it are the ordinary furniture of a Volga toponym: вӑрман forest, ҫырма the ravine with a stream in it, ҫаран and ана for meadow and ploughed strip, вар a dry gully, ҫӑл a spring, тӑвайкки a hillside, пуҫ literally a head and used for the top or source of something. The two derivational endings -лӑх and -лӗх are collective place formants, the ones that turn юман into an oak grove and хӑмла into a hop field. Everything from -ово onward is Russian, and the honest reason to include it is that most settlements in Chuvashia carry two names at once, a Chuvash one that people use and an official Russian one that the address register uses, and a config that only ever produced the Chuvash shape would be generating half a republic.
