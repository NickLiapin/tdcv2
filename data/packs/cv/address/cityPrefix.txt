description: Leading element of a Chuvash place name (Ҫӗнӗ Шупашкар for Novocheboksarsk, Хӗрлӗ Чутай for Krasnye Chetai, Аслӑ Тӑван for the larger of a pair of villages). Nothing here is inflected, and that is the structural difference from the Serbian equivalent: Chuvash has no grammatical gender at all, so there are no masculine, feminine and neuter triples to choose between — one form of Ҫӗнӗ or Пысӑк serves every settlement noun that can follow it. Place names are built as plain noun-plus-noun or qualifier-plus-noun compounds and the qualifier simply sits in front unchanged. Four of these values are ethnonyms rather than adjectives, and they matter more here than they would anywhere else: Chuvashia is dotted with paired settlements distinguished only by who founded them, so Чӑваш, Вырӑс, Тутар and Ҫармӑс (Chuvash, Russian, Tatar, Mari) open the names of villages that otherwise share a base word with a neighbour a few kilometres away. The last eight values are Russian, and deliberately so: roughly half the republic's officially registered settlement names are Russian-language forms carrying Новые, Старые, Большие, Малые, Верхние, Нижние, Средние or Красные, and a list that pretended otherwise would not match the register. Combine with a real toponym; on its own no value here names a place.
