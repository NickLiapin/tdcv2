The thing to watch is the end of the clause: Chuvash is verb-final, and the verb closes the sentence every time — ача кӗнеке вулать is child-book-reads, in that order. There are no prepositions at all, and the work they would do is carried by case suffixes and by postpositions that stand behind their noun: килте at home, киле homewards, килтен from home, ҫул ҫинче on the road, юман айӗнче under the oak, урок хыҫҫӑн after the lesson. Possession is a suffix rather than a separate word — аттен ҫурчӗ father's house, манӑн кӗнекем my book. There is no gender and no article, so a noun stands bare, and the plural -сем is added only where the number is not already obvious: ача a child, ачасем children, but виҫӗ ача three children. The forms are varied deliberately down the file so that a random draw does not read as one drill — present against past, singular subjects against plural, one postposition against the next, and the negative, which is not a separate word here but a syllable inside the verb: пӗлет he knows against пӗлмест he does not know, килчӗ he came against килмерӗ he did not come. Nothing in the list is a question, so every line closes on a plain full stop.
