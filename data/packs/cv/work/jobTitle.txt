description: Job titles across every sector of working life in the Chuvash Republic, and Russian from end to end, which is the honest thing to record rather than a shortcut: employment here is administered in Russian. The contract, the entry in the employment record book, the establishment table and the vacancy advertisement are federal Russian documents, and the unified qualification catalogues that grade a turner or a fitter are federal too, so a personnel office in Cheboksary writes токарь and nothing else. That is an administrative fact and not a linguistic one. Chuvash is the only surviving Oghur Turkic language, the sole living relative of the extinct Bulgar of the Volga Bulgars, and it split from the rest of Turkic so early that it is mutually unintelligible with every other Turkic language — where Common Turkic has z and sh, Chuvash has r and l: Turkish kiz against Chuvash хӗр for "girl", Turkish tas against Chuvash чул for "stone". It has its own words for work and for the people who do it — ӗҫ work, ӗҫлекен a worker, хресчен a peasant farmer, тимӗрҫӗ a blacksmith, вӗрентекен a teacher, тухтӑр a doctor, кӗтӳҫӗ a herdsman — and they are alive in speech, in literature and in the village, but not on a staffing schedule, so scattering a few of them through a list that a real personnel department writes in Russian would be a fiction. The list leans where the republic leans: heavy on the machine, electrical and instrument trades that Cheboksary lives by, carrying хмелевод because Chuvashia grows the greater part of Russia's hops, and carrying учитель чувашского языка и литературы, which is a funded, timetabled post because the language has state status here and the subject is examined. On form: the titles are masculine, which is what a vacancy prints. Russian has feminine counterparts for a great many of them — учительница, продавщица, повариха — but they sit lower in register than the masculine, the official document keeps the masculine even for a woman, and whether to use the feminine at all is an argument still running in Russian. A config writing about a woman can therefore neither reuse these strings unthinkingly nor derive the alternative by rule.
