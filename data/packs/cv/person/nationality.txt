description: Peoples, in Chuvash, lowercase — Chuvash follows Russian orthography and does not capitalise an ethnonym. The list is ordered outward from home, and the first eleven are the ones that matter: Chuvashia sits in the middle of the most ethnically layered corner of Europe, with Tatars to the east, Mari to the north across the Volga, Mordvins to the west and Russians everywhere, and Chuvash keeps its own ancient names for exactly those neighbours rather than borrowing Russian ones — ҫармӑс for a Mari, пушкӑрт for a Bashkir, мӑкшӑ and ирҫе for the two Mordvin peoples who are counted as one nation and do not speak one language. Two more of these old names are worth knowing: нимӗҫ, a German, and акӑлчан, an Englishman, both far older in Chuvash than any modern contact. Beyond the neighbours the Russian form is simply taken over, which is what Chuvash speech and Chuvash print actually do. The Chuvash themselves are not one undivided group either: the вирьял of the north, the анат енчи of the middle and the анатри of the south differ in dress, dialect and custom, and only анатри speech underlies the literary standard — but those are subdivisions of чӑваш, not separate nationalities, so they are not listed here.
