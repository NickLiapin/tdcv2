description: Female given name in the Chuvash Republic, in the same three layers as the male list and with one name at the top of the second that has no counterpart anywhere else. Нарспи is the heroine of Konstantin Ivanov's 1908 poem Нарспи, the Chuvash national epic — a girl married off to the rich Тӑхтаман against her love for Сетнер — and she is a living, ordinary girl's name today BECAUSE of that poem, given by parents who read it at school. Around her sits the rest of the pre-Christian and literary stock, most of it built on -пи or -пике, the old feminine element: Илемпи from илем "beauty", Кӗмӗлпи from кӗмӗл "silver", Хӗвелпи from хӗвел "sun", plus the flat word-names Чечек (flower), Чӗкеҫ (swallow) and Савни (beloved). The first layer is the Chuvash form of a baptismal name, which is what a woman is actually called at home: Марье for Maria, Праски for Praskovya, Улюк for Ulyana, Кӗтерне for Yekaterina, Ҫинук for Zinaida, Санюк for Aleksandra, Марук for Marya, Оксинье for Aksinya, Варук for Varvara. The third layer is the Russian passport form — Мария, Анна, Елена, Ольга, Татьяна, Ирина — and it is the largest, because that is what the internal passport says; the older Orthodox forms further down the list, Аграфена, Акулина, Матрёна, Пелагея, Феодосия, are the generation now in the villages rather than in Cheboksary. Note what this file does NOT have beside it: there is no person/female/lastName.txt, because a Chuvash woman's surname is derived from the masculine list by appending а, Иванов to Иванова. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES; like the male list the curve is deliberately flat, because no single given name here carries a large share of the republic.
