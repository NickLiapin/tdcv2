description: Everyday short name in the Chuvash Republic, in two blocks that belong to the same people. The first thirty are Chuvash hypocoristics, built by cutting the name back to a syllable or two and adding -ук / -юк / -кка / -ҫ: Ваҫук and Ваҫҫа from Vasily, Мишук from Mikhail, Петюк and Петрук from Pyotr, Наҫтук from Nastasya, Верук from Vera, Матрюк from Matryona. The suffix is doing the same work as Russian -ша or -ня, but it is a native Turkic diminutive and it attaches to a name that has already been reshaped by Chuvash sound rules, which is why Хӗветюк is barely recognisable as Fyodor. The remaining forty-two are the ordinary Russian short forms, and they are not an intrusion: the same man is Ваҫук to his mother, Вася to his foreman and Василий on his passport, and all three strings are in daily use for him. Note that the boundary between a Chuvash pet name and a Chuvash given name is genuinely blurred — several of these also appear in person/male/firstName and person/female/firstName, because in a village they ARE the name, the one that goes on a grave marker.
