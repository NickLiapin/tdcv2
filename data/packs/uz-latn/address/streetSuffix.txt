description: Uzbek thoroughfare type — like Turkish, Uzbek puts it AFTER the name (Alisher Navoiy koʻchasi, Amir Temur xiyoboni, Mustaqillik maydoni). WHERE THIS DIFFERS FROM TURKISH AND AZERBAIJANI, AND IT MATTERS FOR A CONFIG: the form below is the noun plus the third-person possessive, and in Uzbek that suffix DOES NOT HARMONISE. Standard Uzbek has lost vowel harmony, so the ending is -si after a vowel and -i after a consonant and nothing else — koʻcha + si, yoʻl + i, maydon + i, xiyobon + i — where Azerbaijani has to choose between -ı, -i, -u and -ü by the last vowel of the noun. That makes Uzbek street suffixes mechanically predictable. The prospekt / massiv / kvartal block came in through Russian.
locale: uz-latn
