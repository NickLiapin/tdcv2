description: Uzbek surname, roughly frequency-ordered, with rank-derived Zipf weights rather than registry counts — Uzbekistan publishes no surname frequency table and inventing one would be worse than saying so. TWO SYSTEMS SIT SIDE BY SIDE. The great bulk of the list ends in -ov / -yev, a Russian genitive ending fastened onto an Uzbek or Arabic given name during the imperial and Soviet periods: Karim became Karimov, Yoʻldosh became Yoʻldoshev. That is what most Uzbeks carry today and it did not change in 1991. The tail of the list is native: the Perso-Arabic nisba in -iy (Qodiriy, Behbudiy, Avloniy, Niyoziy), the Persian -zoda meaning born of (Hakimzoda), and bare-noun names carried mostly by writers (Gʻulom, Malik, Olimjon, Doʻstmuhammad). THE FEMININE FORM IS SHIPPED, in person/female/lastName.txt, and is DERIVED FROM THIS FILE BY SCRIPT line for line: -ov takes -ova and -ev takes -eva, while every native form is left untouched, because Uzbek has no grammatical gender of its own and the inflection is borrowed Russian morphology. SEPARATELY, the patronymic, which is NOT a surname and occupies the middle slot of an official record: father's name + oʻgʻli for a man, + qizi for a woman — Sardor Anvar oʻgʻli, Nilufar Anvar qizi. That slot IS sex-marked even though the family name usually is not, and a few families now carry -oʻgʻli as the surname itself. Note also the Mat- / Mamat- element in Mamatov, Matyoqubov and Toshmuhamedov: a worn-down Muhammad.
