description: Uzbek male given name. NO UZBEK NAME FREQUENCY TABLE IS PUBLISHED, so the ordering is honest about what it is: the first sixty names are ranked by plausible real frequency and the rest of the list is ALPHABETICAL. The weights are rank-derived Zipf values, which means they carry information for the head of the list and are flat filler below it — do not read them as registry counts. Four layers are visible. The Arabic-Islamic core (Muhammad, Ibrohim, Ismoil, Abdulla, Umar) is the largest. The Persian layer is nearly as large and is what separates Uzbek from the Turkic languages further north: Jahongir, Bahrom, Parviz, Sherzod, Firdavs, Shohrux. A Turkic-Timurid revival gathered pace after 1991 — Temur, Ulugʻbek, Bobur, Sanjar, Otabek. And a Russian-period residue survives in everyday paperwork. TWO SUFFIXES ARE PRODUCTIVE and account for a large share of names registered since the 1990s: -bek (Otabek, Nodirbek, Asilbek, Sardorbek) and -jon, an affectionate Persian element meaning soul (Rahimjon, Akmaljon, Sobirjon). A config may compose them, but the forms below are the ones actually registered. The letters oʻ and gʻ appear here — Yoʻldosh, Toʻlqin, Ulugʻbek, Gʻayrat, Oʻktam — and together with q and x they are the fastest proof that a list is Uzbek rather than Turkish or Azerbaijani.
