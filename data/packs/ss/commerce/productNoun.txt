description: Product noun in Eswatini, and reading down it is a tour of the noun classes. Class 5 li- and its class 6 plural ema- take most of the newer household goods — lifoni, litafula, lipuleti, lihembe — because li- is the slot siSwati has used for borrowings since the missionaries arrived, and its counterpart in Zulu is a bare i-. Class 7 si- and class 9 in- hold the older items: sipunu a spoon, situlo a chair, imbita a clay cooking pot, insipho soap. Three values are not for sale in any European catalogue. Lihiya is the printed cloth worn wrapped for ceremony and everyday alike, sidvwaba the ox-hide skirt of a married woman, and iphalafini the paraffin that a large share of rural homesteads still light and cook with, sold by the bottle at the same counter as the bread.
locale: ss
