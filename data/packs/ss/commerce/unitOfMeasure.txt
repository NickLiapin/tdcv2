description: Unit of measure in Eswatini. The metric units at the top are the legal ones and are what a scale in a shop reads, but the imperial block below them is not dead weight: Eswatini shares a border and a building trade with South Africa, where timber is still bought by the foot, land is still spoken of in acres by older farmers, and a person's height is given in feet and inches in ordinary conversation. Both systems are in daily use and a data set that offers only one describes half the country. Note that every metric unit here is a class 9 loan taking i-, while the household measures at the end — sipunu a spoon, inkomishi a cup, sikhwama a bag, sicheme a bundle — are native words in their own classes, and those are how produce is actually sold at a market in Manzini.
locale: ss
