description: Street and road name material for Eswatini. Nine kinds of name are mixed here in the proportions the country actually uses. Kings and queens come first and dominate the centre of both cities: Sobhuza II reigned for 82 years and is the longest-verified reign in recorded history; Somhlolo is Sobhuza I; Gwamile is the praise name of Queen Regent Labotsibeni, who held the country together through the concessions crisis and paid for its first school; Dzeliwe, Lomawa, Ntfombi and Nukwase are queen mothers, and Ludzidzini, Lobamba, Lozitha and Engabezweni are royal residences. Then the towns and tinkhundla; the rivers Lusutfu, Komati, Mbuluzi and Ngwavuma; the hills and reserves — Sibebe, the granite dome above Mbabane, Emlembe, the highest peak, the Lubombo escarpment on the Mozambique border and the Mdzimba range where kings are buried. Then trees and crops with umganu, the marula, at their head, and animals led by ligwalagwala, the purple-crested turaco, whose crimson wing feathers only the royal family and the regiments may wear. Then abstractions, which is where a Swazi street name most often comes from — inkhululeko freedom, kuthula peace, bunye unity — and finally the ceremonies: Umhlanga, the reed dance, and Incwala, the kingship ritual, both public holidays and both used as road names. These are the SPECIFIC halves only; the generic (Street, Road, Umgwaco) comes from streetSuffix, because Eswatini signposts the specific in siSwati and the generic in English.
