description: Nationality in siSwati, singular, and the list splits into two grammatical halves for a reason worth reading. Peoples that Nguni speakers have long known have a real ethnonym in class 1: umSwati a Swazi, umZulu a Zulu, umXhosa, umSuthu, umTsonga, umBhunu an Afrikaner, umNgisi an Englishman — and each takes the class 2 plural in ema-, so emaSwati is the Swazi nation. Peoples known only as modern states take a possessive locative instead, waseNamibia, "one of Namibia", which is a phrase rather than a noun and behaves differently in a sentence. Neither half inflects for sex; umSwati is a Swazi man or woman alike. The country's own demonym is the awkward case for English: the state is Eswatini, the people are emaSwati, the language is siSwati and the adjective is Swazi, and none of those four is interchangeable with the others.
