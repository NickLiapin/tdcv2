description: Family name in Eswatini, ONE list for both sexes. The first value is not an artefact of the curve: Dlamini really is the commonest surname in the country by an enormous margin, because it is the name of the royal house — every king since Ngwane III has been a Dlamini, and the clan absorbed and gave its name to a large share of the population as the kingdom formed. A Swazi surname is a sibongo, a clan name, and it carries a praise-name and a set of taboos with it; asking someone their sibongo is asking which clan they belong to, not merely how they are filed. The list shows one thing a config cannot infer: the SAME clan appears in this file under two spellings, Thwala and Tfwala, Mthethwa and Mtsetfwa, because siSwati writes tf and ts where Zulu writes th, and Eswatini's civil registers carry both the Zulu-orthography form inherited from mission schooling and the siSwati one. Surnames do not inflect for gender, which is why there is no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack; a married woman is customarily ADDRESSED as La- plus her father's clan name, LaDlamini or LaNkosi, but that is a form of address and never what is written in a surname column. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. Eswatini publishes no surname frequency table, so the curve is the project's own: a very large head for Dlamini and then floor(72000 * (rank-1) ** -0.62) for the rest, which spreads about 27 to 1 from the second name to the last. The shape is what matters — one dominant royal clan, a dozen large ones behind it, and a long flat tail of regional names.
weighted: true
locale: ss
