description: Danish everyday short name. Danish forms these by shortening the stem and adding -e, -i or -en — Lars becomes Lasse, Birgitte becomes Gitte or Bitten, Cecilie becomes Sisse, Sebastian becomes Sebbe — and the result is used as an ordinary first name, not only in the family. The Danish twist, and the reason this file matters more here than in a neighbouring locale, is that the process ran to completion centuries ago for the men's names: Jens and Hans are frozen short forms of Johannes, Niels of Nikolaus, Mads of Mathias, Lars and Lasse of Laurentius, Per and Peder of Petrus, Jeppe and Ib of Jakob, Mogens of Magnus, Bent of Benedikt, Esben of Asbjørn, Keld of Ketil and Troels of Torgils. Those are now full legal names and appear in person/male/firstName.txt rather than here; what is left in this list is the living, still-informal layer, and it is noticeably richer on the female side.
