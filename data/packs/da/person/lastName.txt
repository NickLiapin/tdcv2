description: Danish surnames, frequency-ordered, with real bearer counts rather than a rank curve — the concentration is the whole point, and Denmark is the most concentrated of the three Nordic packs. Roughly two thirds of Danes carry a frozen patronymic in -sen, and the top ten names alone are carried by about 1.5 million of 5.95 million people, close to a quarter of the country; Sweden's top ten cover about a sixth and Finland's under four per cent. Nielsen and Jensen are within a thousand of each other at the top and have swapped places between census years. A default rank curve would flatten all of that and make Denmark look like Finland, which is why the leading figures here are bearer counts on the scale Danmarks Statistik publishes, rounded, with a smooth decay anchored to the same scale for the tail. Danish surnames DO NOT inflect for gender — Jensen is Jensen for a woman, and there is no -datter form in use — so there is no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack and its absence is deliberate. The -sen ending froze into inherited family names when the Navneloven of 1828 and 1856 stopped the practice of forming a new patronymic each generation, which is exactly why so few different surnames cover so many people; the -gaard, -kjær and -toft names in the tail are farm names taken instead, and they are Jutlandic in origin.
