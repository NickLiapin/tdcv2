description: Danish male given names, frequency-ordered. Counts are bearer figures on the scale Danmarks Statistik publishes for the leading names; the tail is a smooth decay anchored to the same scale, not the default rank curve. Given names in Denmark are NOT concentrated the way surnames are: Peter, the commonest, is carried by roughly 48 000 of 5.95 million people, under one per cent, and the top ten together cover about six per cent — so this list is deliberately flat at the top while person/lastName.txt is deliberately steep, and the difference between the two shapes is real. One thing Danish does NOT share with Swedish: there is no block of hyphenated double names here, because Danish does not form them. Where a Swede of that generation is Lars-Erik or Sven-Olof written with a hyphen and counted as one name, a Dane with two given names simply has two, written apart — Hans Peter, Jens Christian — and the register treats them as separate names. Denmark also regulates given names: the navneloven requires a name to be on an approved list or individually cleared, which is part of why the distribution here has a shorter tail than an English-speaking country's would.
