description: Writing system (Danish). Two Danish notes. The Danish alphabet has 29 letters: the Latin 26 followed by æ, ø and å in that order at the END, so a Danish sort puts Aarhus and Ærø after Z, not among the As. Å replaced the doubled aa in 1948, which is why Aalborg and Aabenraa still write themselves the old way and why a Danish dataset has to handle both. Runer are not decoration here: the Jelling stones carry the first written use of the name Denmark and are called the country's birth certificate.
