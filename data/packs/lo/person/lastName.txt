description: Lao family name. TWO THINGS A CONFIG CANNOT INFER. (1) LAO SURNAMES ARE NEW: they were made compulsory only by royal decree in 1943, under King Sisavang Vong, so a Lao family name is at most four generations old and was CHOSEN by the family rather than inherited from an ancient lineage. That is why the distribution is genuinely FLAT — there is no Lao equivalent of Vietnamese Nguyen at 38% or Korean Kim at 21%, because no surname had time to spread. (2) THE SURNAME IS NOT THE SORT KEY AND NOT THE ADDRESS FORM: a Lao person is listed, addressed, alphabetised and introduced by GIVEN name. The prime minister Sonexay Siphandone is ທ່ານ ສອນໄຊ — Mr Sonexay, never Mr Siphandone — and a Lao telephone directory, a class register and a conference badge all sort on the given name. A config that builds a 'Dear Mr {lastName}' salutation or an index keyed on this column is producing something no Lao document does. Most of these are long Pali-Sanskrit compounds of three to six syllables, which is why they look nothing like the one-syllable Lao given names. Lao surnames do NOT inflect for gender, so there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack — its absence is the design. THE COUNTS ARE A NOMINAL RANK CURVE, NOT MEASURED DATA: Laos publishes no surname frequency table, and given how recent surnames are the true curve is flatter than this one, not steeper. SCOPE: these are ethnic-Lao surnames. Roughly a third of the country is Hmong, Khmu or another minority whose family-name stock is entirely separate; this list does not cover it and nothing here should be read as representing it.
