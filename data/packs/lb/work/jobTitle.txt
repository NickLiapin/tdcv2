description: Job titles in Luxembourgish, and the file where BOTH of this language's defining traits are visible at once. FIRST, THE FRENCH LAYER. Look at the trades: a lawyer is an AFFEKOT, a hairdresser a COIFFER, a firefighter a POMPJEE, a postman a FACTEUR, an accountant a COMPTABEL, a civil servant a FONCTIONNAIRE, a plumber an INSTALLATEUR, a mechanic a MECANICIEN, a nurse an INFIRMIER, a waiter a SERVEUR or a GARÇON, a researcher a CHERCHEUR, a jeweller a BIJOUTIER, a notary a NOTTÄR, a driver a CHAUFFER, a shop assistant a VENDEUR. German has a native word for every single one of those and Luxembourgish took the French one instead — that is the vocabulary layer German does not have, and it sits in the most ordinary part of the lexicon rather than in the fancy part. SECOND, AND UNIQUELY, THREE FEMININE SUFFIXES FROM THREE LANGUAGES COEXIST IN ONE WORD-FORMATION SYSTEM. Luxembourgish's own is -ESCH: Bäcker becomes BÄCKESCH, Metzler METZLESCH, Dokter DOKTESCH, Auermécher AUERMÉCHESCH, Chauffer CHAUFFESCH. The German one is -IN: Ingenieur/Ingenieurin, Journalist/Journalistin. And a job borrowed from French keeps the FRENCH feminine: Coiffer/COIFFEUSE, Installateur/INSTALLATRICE, Bijoutier/BIJOUTIÈRE, Vendeur/VENDEUSE, Infirmier/INFIRMIÈRE. Which suffix a title takes is decided by where the word came from, not by a rule a config could apply — so a generator that appends -in to every male title will produce forms no Luxembourger writes. Both genders are listed here where the LOD carries both. All values are LOD lemmas.
