description: Surnames of Luxembourg, weighted by the number of people who bear them — real bearer counts out of Forebears' Luxembourg surname index, not a decay curve. Four facts sit in this list that no other country's list has. FIRST, the top name is SCHMIT with 6,827 bearers, and Schmit is the LUXEMBOURGISH spelling of smith: no -d-, no -tz. Its neighbours' spellings are in the same list at other ranks — Schmitz at 7 (Rhineland), Schmitt at 81 (Germany) — and plain German Schmidt does not make the top 150 at all. Which of the four you carry records which side of a border your family was registered on, not how the name is pronounced. SECOND, MULLER at rank 2 has no umlaut: Luxembourg's civil registers were kept in French, on French typewriters, and Müller became Muller and stayed Muller. THIRD, the country is 47% foreign nationals, and the surname index shows it long before any statistic does: da Silva is 12th, dos Santos 16th, then Pereira, Fernandes, Ferreira, Gonçalves, Rodrigues, Gomes, Martins, Lopes, Marques, Mendes, Monteiro, Oliveira, Teixeira, Soares, Almeida, Duarte, Pinto, da Costa, de Sousa — 21 of the 150 names in this file, one in seven, are Portuguese, in a country whose national language is West Germanic. The lowercase particle (da, dos, de) is kept exactly as the register writes it. FOURTH, Adrovic 59, Sabotic 71, Rastoder 95 and Muhovic 144 are the Montenegrin and Bosniak names of the 1990s migration, and they are written WITHOUT their diacritics — Adrović was entered as Adrovic and the register form is now the legal one. What is genuinely and only Luxembourgish is the rest: Thill, Majerus, Flammang, Weydert, Konsbruck, Trierweiler, Erpelding, Huberty, Schintgen, Bertemes, Grethen, Thinnes, Glod, Mousel, Goergen, Wagener — village names and trade names from a territory the size of a county. Luxembourgish surnames DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER, so there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack and its absence is the design. Even the top name is thin cover: 6,827 bearers in a country of about 680,000 residents is about one person in a hundred, so this is a country with a very flat surname distribution and no Nguyen and no Kim. Source: Forebears, Luxembourg surname index.
