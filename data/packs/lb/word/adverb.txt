description: Adverbs and the small words that hold a Luxembourgish sentence together. Several of them are exactly where the Eifel Rule bites hardest, because they are short and end in -n: AN becomes A before most consonants, WANN becomes WA, and the negation NET begins with one of the five letters that PROTECT a preceding -n rather than removing it. GÄR is the word to know: "ech hunn dat gär" is how Luxembourgish says it likes something, and it has no single-word English equivalent. Every single-word value is an LOD form.
