description: Ordinary Luxembourgish adjectives — and the file that records THE ALPHABET. Luxembourgish is written in the Latin alphabet with THREE diacritics of its own: Ë, É and Ä. Ë is the language's signature: a schwa-like vowel that German has no letter for at all, and it is everywhere in the ordinary words — grouss/kleng, dënn, dréchen, lëschteg, wäertvoll. É is a close long e — schéin, méi, gréng, fréi, séier. Ä is a fronted long a — wäiss, wäit, kläppeg, spéit. Beside them Luxembourgish also writes Ü and Ö, but ONLY in words taken from German (Schütze, mürb), and the full French accents in words taken from French (Crème, Congé, Français), so one Luxembourgish sentence can require characters from three national keyboards. THE TEST: a file claiming to be Luxembourgish that contains no ë is almost certainly German with the labels changed — that is the single cheapest check on this whole pack, and it is why this pack's own verification includes a census of ë, é and ä. All values are LOD lemmas.
