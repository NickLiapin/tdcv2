description: Ordinary Luxembourgish nouns — and the file that records WHAT THIS LANGUAGE IS AND HOW YOUNG ITS WRITTEN FORM IS. Luxembourgish is a WEST GERMANIC language, a Moselle Franconian variety, and its closest relative is German; the two share most of their grammar and a large part of their vocabulary, and a pack that pretended otherwise would be lying. Measured against the state dictionary itself: of the 27,780 LOD entries glossed into both German and French, 21.1% have a Luxembourgish headword spelled EXACTLY like its German translation, against 8.5% spelled exactly like its French one. So the German overlap is real and about two and a half times the French one — and the French 8.5% is the part German does not have at all. THE WRITTEN LANGUAGE IS NEW. Luxembourgish became the NATIONAL LANGUAGE only on 24 February 1984, by a law that in the same breath made French the language of legislation; a unified spelling was decreed on 10 October 1975, revised by grand-ducal regulation on 30 July 1999, and only completed by the Zenter fir d'Lëtzebuerger Sprooch — the state language centre, created by the loi du 20 juillet 2018 — in a rule set that came into force on 15 NOVEMBER 2019. A "Zertifikat Lëtzebuerger Orthografie" has existed only since November 2024. THIS PACK FOLLOWS THE ZLS STANDARD as the LOD applies it. What that means practically: variant spellings are everywhere in real Luxembourgish text and are not errors, because most people who speak the language were never taught to write it — schooling runs in German and then French — so they spell it as it sounds. The national address register proves the point better than any argument: it holds Duerfstrooss, Duerefstrooss, Duarrefstrooss and Duärrefstrooss as four different streets. A dataset that deduplicates Luxembourgish strings on exact match will merge things that are not the same and split things that are.
