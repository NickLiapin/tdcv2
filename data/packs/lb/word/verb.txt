description: Ordinary Luxembourgish verbs, and THE FILE THAT RECORDS THE EIFEL RULE, because every one of them is subject to it. Luxembourgish infinitives end in -EN — schaffen, kachen, goen, kommen, liesen — and that final -n DISAPPEARS, IN WRITING, according to what comes next. The rule (Eifeler Regel) is: keep the -n before a vowel and before d, t, h, n, z; drop it before anything else. Luxembourgish school children learn the five letters by the sentence "DENG TIRETT HÄLT NET ZOU!" — your zip is not shut — whose stressed words begin d, t, h, n, z. So: "si hunn dech gefaart" but "si hu béis gekuckt"; "an England" but "a Frankräich"; "an 10 Deeg" but "a 5 Deeg", because zéng begins with z and fënnef with f. THIS IS NOT A PRONUNCIATION FOOTNOTE. IT CHANGES THE SPELLING OF THE WORD, so the same verb, the same article and the same conjunction have two written forms and a config that treats a Luxembourgish word as a fixed string will emit text no native reader accepts. German has nothing remotely like it — German writes haben and an unchanged whatever follows. The scale is measurable: the state dictionary (LOD) stores an explicit n-rule variant for 12,202 inflected forms, i.e. it keeps TWO spellings of most plurals as data. moment.js's lb locale carries a function called eifelerRegelAppliesToNumber for the same reason. Verbs here are given in the citation form with the -n; the rule applies to every one of them in running text.
locale: lb
