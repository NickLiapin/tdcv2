description: Word-forming front halves of Luxembourgish place names. This is not a decorative list: Luxembourgish settlement names really are built out of a small kit, and the same first elements recur across the country — Nidder- and Uewer- for the lower and upper village of a pair, Al- for the old one, Nei- for the new one, Grouss- and Kleng- for the big and small one, Rous-, Bier-, Eesch-, Wal-, Bech-, Bous-, Schëtt-. The point of shipping them is that a config building a plausible Luxembourgish toponym should build it from these and not from German Ober-/Unter-, which is the same idea spelled differently. Real Luxembourg places belong to luxembourg.geo.city and luxembourg.geo.communeLb.
