description: Word-forming tails of Luxembourgish place names, and this is the half that dates them. -ANGE and -INGEN are the same Germanic suffix twice: the FRENCH form -ange is what the state writes (Bettembourg, Differdange, Pétange) and the LUXEMBOURGISH form -eng is what people say and what the commune paints on its own signs (Beetebuerg, Déifferdeng, Péiteng). -DORF/-DUERF is a village, -BURG/-BUERG a fortified place, -HOF/-HAFF a farm, -SCHEUER/-SCHEIER a barn, -MÜHLE/-MILLEN a mill. Where two forms of one suffix are listed, they are the same suffix in the two languages the country writes its own place names in, and that duplication is the entry rather than a mistake.
