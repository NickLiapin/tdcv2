description: Israeli surnames — the real mix, not one community's. Ashkenazi names brought from Central and Eastern Europe (פרידמן, גולדשטיין, רבינוביץ), Sephardi and North African ones (אזולאי, ביטון, אוחיון, בוזגלו, תורג׳מן), Mizrahi names from Iraq, Yemen and Iran (ששון, שרעבי, מדמוני, עדני), the priestly and biblical names (כהן, לוי, בן דוד), and the HEBRAISED surnames adopted after 1948, when a new Hebrew surname was close to a civic duty for public figures and compulsory for diplomats and army officers — דוד בן־גוריון was born Grün, משה שרת was Shertok, לוי אשכול was Shkolnik, גולדה מאיר was Meyerson, יגאל אלון was Peikowitz, and short nature-and-place words like שגב, נבו, ארבל, לביא, אלון, שחר, ניר and תמיר entered the surname pool that way. Israeli surnames do NOT inflect for gender, so there is no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack and its absence is the design. Weights: כהן and לוי genuinely lead by a wide margin — together roughly 4% of the Jewish population, because they are the priestly lineage names rather than family names in origin — so they carry a boosted weight; below the top few the list uses the pack's default gentle decay, because Israel's Central Bureau of Statistics publishes no per-name frequency table. Two gaps a config should know about: Russian-speaking immigrants, about a sixth of the country, generally kept their Russian surnames unchanged, which live in the `ru` pack, and Arab citizens' surnames live in `ar`.
