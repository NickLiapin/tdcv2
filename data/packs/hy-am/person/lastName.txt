description: Armenian surnames, ordered by real-world frequency and weighted on the project's default gentle curve. THE ORTHOGRAPHY IS THE FIRST THING TO KNOW. This pack is EASTERN Armenian in the REFORMED (Soviet, 1922/1940) spelling that the Republic of Armenia's own registers, passports and statutes use, so the patronymic ending is -ՅԱՆ: Սարգսյան, Աբովյան, Թումանյան. Western Armenian and the diaspora — Beirut, Aleppo, Los Angeles, and Iranian-Armenians — kept the classical Mesropian orthography and write the same ending -ԵԱՆ: Սարգսեան, Աբովեան. The two spellings are the SAME NAME and will not string-match, and in Latin the same split shows as -yan against -ian, which is why Sargsyan and Sarkissian are one surname. A deduplication run across a Yerevan register and a diaspora register that compares strings will find no matches at all. SURNAMES DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER — Սարգսյան is Սարգսյան for a man and for a woman — so there is deliberately NO person/female/lastName.txt in this pack; its absence is the design. The suffix -յան means 'of, descendant of'. Three older patterns survive: Տեր- marks descent from a married priest (Տեր-Պետրոսյան), Մելիք- from a hereditary Armenian lord, and the classical -ունի names the naxarar princely houses (Բագրատունի, Ամատունի, Արծրունի) and takes no -յան at all. Many stems are occupational loans from Turkish or Persian: Նալբանդյան is the farrier, Քեշիշյան the priest, Թերզյան the tailor, Դարբինյան the smith, Ոսկերչյան the goldsmith. ON THE WEIGHTS: the ORDER is the real ranking — Հարությունյան really is the commonest surname in Armenia — but the magnitudes are the project's default decay, not measured shares. Armenian surnames are not sharply concentrated: the commonest is on the order of two percent of the population, nothing like Vietnamese Nguyen or Korean Kim.
