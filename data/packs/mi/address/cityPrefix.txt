description: Leading element of a Maori place name. New Zealand toponymy is overwhelmingly Maori and overwhelmingly COMPOUND: a place name is a small descriptive sentence, and this file holds the words that start one. WAI- is water and is the single most productive element in the country (Waikato, Waitangi, Waiheke, Wairoa). WHANGA- is a harbour or bay, ROTO- a lake, AWA- a river, MANGA- a stream, PUKE- a hill, MAUNGA- a mountain, MOTU- an island, PAPA- flat ground, WHARE- a house, RUA- a pit or the number two, KAI- food or the act of eating. TE and NGA are the definite articles, singular and plural, fused onto the front (Te Awamutu, Ngaruawahia). O- and its long form O with a macron is the possessive "of": Otaki is "the place of Taki", Opotiki "the place of Potiki" — the name embeds a person, and the macron matters because it is the possessive marker. A config that concatenates a prefix and a suffix from these files will produce a well-formed Maori name shape; whether it produces a name that exists is another matter, and REAL New Zealand place names live in new_zealand.geo.city.
locale: mi
