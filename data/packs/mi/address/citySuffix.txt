description: Trailing element of a Maori place name. This is the half that carries the description: NUI is great, ITI small, ROA long, RUA two or a pit, TAPU sacred, TEA white or clear, KINO bad, PAI good, ORA alive or well, MATE dead, TANGI weeping, MARINO calm. Others name what was found there — TUNA is eel (and NOT tuna the fish, which is a false friend that catches every English reader), KOURA crayfish, KOWHAI and TOTARA are trees, KOHATU stone, UKU clay, PARA mud, ONE sand. So Waitangi is "weeping water", Waimate "dead water", Rotorua "second lake", Kaikoura "eat crayfish", Waiuku "clay water". Compounding is unlimited and productive, which is why New Zealand place names run long: Taumatawhakatangihangakoauauotamateaturipukakapikimaungahoronukupokaiwhenuakitanatahu is one word built exactly this way.
