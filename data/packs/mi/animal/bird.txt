description: Bird (Maori). MANU are the closest thing Aotearoa had to land mammals — there were only bats — so the forest was a bird world and te reo names it in extraordinary detail. Almost none of these has an English name that is not simply the Maori one borrowed: kiwi, tui, kea, kaka, kakapo, takahe, weka, pukeko and kokako are the English words too. Several names are the bird's own call written down — RURU is the morepork saying its name, PIPIWHARAUROA the shining cuckoo, KIHIKIHI is a cicada by the same logic. HUIA and MOA are extinct: the huia was hunted out by about 1907 for its tail feathers, which only rangatira wore, and the moa was gone within about two centuries of Polynesian arrival. They are on the list because the words are current — a Maori speaker uses them constantly, in whakatauki and in place names — and a list of Maori birds without them would be a list of what survives rather than of what the language names.
