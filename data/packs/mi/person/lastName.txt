description: Maori family name, weighted. FOUR things a config cannot infer. FIRST, THERE WAS NO INHERITED SURNAME before European contact. A person was identified by a single name plus whakapapa — the recited genealogy that places them in a descent line — and by hapu and iwi. Fixed family names arrived with mission baptism, land-court registration and the 1913 Births and Deaths Registration Act, so every name in this file is at most about six generations old as a HERITABLE name, even when the word itself is ancient. Many were formed by freezing an ancestor's personal name (Te Heuheu, Te Wherowhero, Tawhiao, Ngata, Maniapoto) into a family name. SECOND, THIS LIST IS NOT THE WHOLE PICTURE and must not be used alone. A very large share of Maori today carry English, Scottish or Irish surnames inherited from settler ancestors — Smith, Walker, Jones, Grace, Solomon, Campbell are all borne by prominent Maori families. Those are English words and live in en.person.lastName; a realistic New Zealand dataset draws from BOTH addresses. Keeping them out of this file is what makes it a te reo Maori list rather than a New Zealand one. THIRD, a distinct class here is the transliterated settler surname, reshaped by Maori phonotactics rather than respelled: Wilson became WIRIHANA, Busby PUHIPI, Shortland HOTERENE, Graham KEREAMA, Watson WATENE, Brown PARAONE, Richardson REIHANA, Maunsell MANIHERA, Kemp KEPA, Davis REWITI. Every one of them ends in a vowel and contains no consonant cluster, because te reo permits neither. FOURTH, MAORI SURNAMES DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER — the same string serves a man and a woman — so there is deliberately NO person/female/lastName.txt in this pack and its absence is the design. The weights are the project default decay curve ordered by prominence; Statistics New Zealand publishes no surname frequency table, so no measured distribution exists to encode and none is claimed here.
locale: mi
weighted: true
