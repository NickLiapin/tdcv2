description: Affectionate name or term of address (Maori). MAORI HAS NO DIMINUTIVE SUFFIX — nothing corresponds to English -y or -ie, Spanish -ito or Russian -ochka — so affection is carried three other ways and this list holds all three. FIRST, the vocative particle E before a name or kin term: e hoa, e tā, e kō, e kare. SECOND, KIN TERMS USED AS ADDRESS, which is by far the commonest: moko and mokopuna to a grandchild, tama to a boy, kotiro to a girl, whaea and matua to an adult, koro and kuia to an elder, and these are used to people well outside one's own family. THIRD, genuine endearments — kare, tau, ipo, aroha. A handful of clipped given names (Wiri from Wiremu, Rawi from Rawiri, Hem from Hemi, Tama from Tamati) are at the foot of the list; they are real but the practice is much less systematic than in English, so the list is short on purpose rather than padded.
