description: Job title (Maori). ONE MORPHEME BUILDS MOST OF THIS LIST AND A CONFIG SHOULD KNOW IT: KAI- prefixed to a verb makes the person who does it. Ako (to teach) gives KAIAKO, a teacher; mahi (to work) KAIMAHI; tiaki (to care for) KAITIAKI; whakahaere (to run something) KAIWHAKAHAERE, a manager; tuhi (to write) KAITUHI; whakawa (to judge) KAIWHAKAWA. The pattern is fully productive, which is why te reo could name every modern occupation without borrowing: a job title is a verb plus kai-. Two other patterns run beside it. MATANGA means an expert in something and TOHUNGA a master of it — but tohunga is not merely "specialist": a tohunga whakairo is a master carver whose work is tapu, and before the Tohunga Suppression Act 1907 a tohunga was also a priest and healer. PUKENGA is a skilled person and, in a wananga, a lecturer. The loans at the foot are the trades English brought with it — carpenter KAMURA, blacksmith PARAKIMETE, nurse NEHI, doctor TAKUTA, lawyer ROIA, police officer PIRIHIMANA — each rebuilt so every syllable ends in a vowel.
