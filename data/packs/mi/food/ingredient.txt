description: Cooking ingredient (Maori). This list is the clearest single place in the pack to see how te reo handles a borrowed word. English butter, milk, sugar, salt, cheese, coffee, tea, rice, honey, cream and flour arrive as PATA, MIRAKA, HUKA, TOTE, TIHI, KAWHE, TI, RAIHI, HONI, KIRIMI and PARAOA — every one ending in a vowel, every consonant cluster broken open by an inserted vowel, and every sound te reo lacks mapped onto its nearest neighbour (s to h or t, b to p, l to r, f to wh or p, g to k). These are REBUILDINGS, not transliterations: nothing about "milk" predicts "miraka" letter by letter, but everything about Maori syllable structure does. PARAOA is worth a second look — it began as "flour", then came to mean bread as well, and PARAOA is also the sperm whale, an unrelated word te reo simply lives beside.
