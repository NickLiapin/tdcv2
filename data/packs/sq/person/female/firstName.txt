description: Albanian female given names. The same three strata as the male list — Muslim-origin (Fatime, Emine, Hatixhe), Christian (Drane, Mrika, Prena, Tereza) and revival coinages made from ordinary words (Drita 'the light', Pranvera 'spring', Shpresa 'hope', Luljeta 'flower of life', Fitore 'victory', Qendresa 'resistance', Liri 'freedom'). Teuta and Donika are an Illyrian queen and Skanderbeg's wife.
  MALE AND FEMALE FORMS ARE OFTEN THE SAME WORD IN DIFFERENT GENDER: Fatmir / Fatmira, Bardhyl / Bardha, Agim / Agime, Besnik / Besnike, Shkelzen / Shkelzene. The feminine usually takes -e or -a. That is a derivation, not a rule a config may apply blindly — Fatime is not the feminine of anything, and Yllka is not built from Yll by adding -a.
  WEIGHTS. There is no published frequency table of given names for Albania, so the counts here are the project's default gentle decay curve and not measurements — and unusually, that curve is close to right for this language. The commonest name in Kosovo, Fatime, is borne by 6,339 people out of about 1.6 million (census ReKos 2024), roughly four tenths of one per cent; Albanian given names really are that evenly spread, so a sharp curve would be the lie here rather than the flat one. The ORDER, which is what the curve turns into a weight, is evidence-based: the names the Kosovo census reports as commonest first, then the stock that is verifiable across public life, then names attested in Newmark's Albanian-English Dictionary (OUP 1998) and in the Fjalor me emra njerezish (Tirane 1982).
  A generational break runs straight through this list and a config drawing a whole population from it will mix two eras. The names at the top belong to people born before about 1990; children registered in Albania in the last decade are overwhelmingly given short international names — Noel, Aron, Andrea, Sara, Amelia — which the Civil Status office reports as the most-registered names of the last five years. Both kinds are in the list. If you need one era, filter.
