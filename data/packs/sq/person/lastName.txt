description: Albanian surnames with the REAL number of bearers recorded by the Albanian census of 2011 — these are measured counts, not a decay curve. Read the shape rather than the names: Hoxha, the commonest surname in the country, is borne by 37,160 people out of some 2.8 million, about 1.3 per cent, so Albania has one of the FLATTEST surname distributions in Europe. Nowhere near a Vietnamese Nguyen or a Korean Kim. The reason is historical and recent: most Albanians had no family name at all before 1912, and surnames were only fixed during the twentieth century, so the stock was invented late and locally rather than inherited from a few medieval lineages.
  SURNAMES DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER, and that is why this pack has NO person/female/lastName.txt — its absence is the design, not an omission. Rita Ora, Vjosa Osmani, Dua Lipa and Majlinda Kelmendi carry exactly the form a man of the same family carries. Do not add -a, -e or anything else for a woman; Albanian is not Slavic and does not work that way, whatever the neighbours do.
  The endings tell you where a name came from. -I and -U are the definite article welded on (Hoxha the cleric, Hoxhaj its Gheg plural). -AJ marks a clan, 'the people of', and is northern and Kosovan: Gjonaj, Hasanaj, Ahmetaj, Beqiraj. Names that are bare nouns are occupational or descriptive: Prifti the priest, Bardhi the white one, Plaku the old man, Çuni the boy, Bregu the shore. Hoxha and Prifti sitting near the top of one small country's list — the Muslim cleric and the Christian priest — is the plainest evidence of Albania's religious mixture that any dataset carries.
  One transcription note: the census table as republished lists Gjoni twice, at ranks 14 and 21, with two different counts. Only the higher entry is kept here; the duplicate has been dropped rather than guessed at.
