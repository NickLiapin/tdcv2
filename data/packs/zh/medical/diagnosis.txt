description: Diagnosis names as they are written on Taiwanese charts and National Health Insurance claims. NHI covers virtually the whole population and every encounter is coded, so clinic wording tracks the official ICD-10-CM Traditional Chinese term list rather than colloquial speech — 第2型糖尿病, 慢性腎臟病, 缺血性中風. Some entries carry unusual local weight: chronic hepatitis B once affected roughly 15–20 percent of adults, which is why Taiwan launched the world's first nationwide infant hepatitis B immunisation programme in 1984, and gout is diagnosed here at among the highest rates anywhere. The vocabulary also parts from mainland usage at many points — stroke is 中風 where the mainland writes 腦卒中, hepatitis is lettered (B型肝炎, C型肝炎) rather than numbered, asthma is 氣喘, the prostate is 攝護腺, and schizophrenia was formally renamed 思覺失調症 in 2014 to shed the stigma of the older name.
