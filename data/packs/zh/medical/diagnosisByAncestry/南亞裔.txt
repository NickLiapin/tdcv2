description: Conditions markedly more frequent in people of South Asian ancestry. Cardiometabolic disease appears at a lower body-mass index and close to a decade earlier than in European populations, which is why Asian-specific thresholds of 23 for overweight and 27.5 for obesity are used in place of the standard cut-offs — visceral fat distribution, early insulin resistance and high lipoprotein(a) drive both the type 2 diabetes and the premature coronary disease. Beta thalassaemia and G6PD deficiency reflect malaria selection across the subcontinent, and sickle cell disease is concentrated in the tribal populations of central India rather than being absent from Asia as is often assumed. Adult lactase non-persistence is the ancestral state outside the northwestern dairying belt, and vitamin D deficiency is widespread despite the latitude. A prevalence signal, not an exclusive one.
