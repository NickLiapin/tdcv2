description: Generic drug name as Taiwan's Food and Drug Administration renders it. The transliterations are not the mainland's and cannot be reached by swapping characters: aspirin is 阿斯匹靈 rather than 阿司匹林, paracetamol is 乙醯胺酚 rather than 對乙酰氨基酚, amoxicillin is 安莫西林 rather than 阿莫西林, and furosemide keeps the older chemical name 呋喃苯胺酸. Three characters mark the divide across the whole list — Taiwan writes 醯 for the acetyl group where the mainland writes 酰, 胺 for an amine where the mainland writes 氨, which on the mainland is also the word for ammonia, and 黴 for the mould that gives the -mycin antibiotics their name. The last five entries are 科學中藥: classical herbal formulas decocted, concentrated and spray-dried into granules by GMP-licensed manufacturers, so that a prescription is dispensed by the gram from a jar instead of boiled at home overnight. They are not folk remedies sitting outside the health system. A licensed 中醫師 prescribes them under the National Health Insurance, which reimburses the consultation and the formula on the same card that pays for the western drugs above them, and roughly a quarter of the insured population files at least one such claim in a year.
locale: zh
