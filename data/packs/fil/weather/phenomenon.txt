ABOUT TWENTY TROPICAL CYCLONES ENTER PHILIPPINE WATERS EACH YEAR, more than reach any other country, and the state weather bureau gives every one of them its OWN Philippine name from a rotating list independent of the international one — so the same storm is Yolanda here and Haiyan abroad, and a name retired after a catastrophe is never used again. Amihan and habagat are the two monsoons and are also directions, north-easterly and south-westerly. Lindol and bulkan are ordinary vocabulary in a country on the Pacific Ring of Fire with two dozen active volcanoes, and lahar — the volcanic mudflow that buried towns around Pinatubo for years after the 1991 eruption — is a word Philippine news readers use without explaining.
