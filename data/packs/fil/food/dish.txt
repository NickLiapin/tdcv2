description: Dish, in Filipino. Adobo comes first because it is the closest thing to a national dish and because it explains the cuisine: meat simmered in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic and bay, where the vinegar is a preservative — the technique predates Spanish contact and the Spanish name was applied to it afterwards. THE PHILIPPINE FLAVOUR AXIS IS SOUR AND SALTY, NOT HOT: sinigang is soured with tamarind, kinilaw is raw fish cured in vinegar, and only Bicol cooks with chilli as a matter of course. The silog block — tapsilog, tocilog, longsilog — is a Filipino portmanteau system: any meat plus SInangag (garlic fried rice) plus itLOG (egg), so the name of the breakfast tells you its three parts. Balut, a duck egg incubated to the point where the embryo is formed and then boiled, is a street food eaten in the evening and is the one Filipinos are always asked about.
