description: What a product is made of. The Philippine entries are the ones a European list has no room for and they are ordinary trade goods here, not craft curiosities: abaka is Manila hemp, the banana-family fibre that rigged the world's ships before nylon and still makes rope and specialty paper; pinya is pineapple-leaf fibre, woven into the sheer cloth a barong Tagalog is made of; capiz is the flat translucent oyster shell used for window panes in Spanish-era houses and now for lamps; buri, nito, tikog, sabutan and pandan are the palms, vines and sedges Philippine baskets and mats are woven from; narra, molave and kamagong are the hardwoods. Kawayan, bamboo, is a construction material here and not a garden plant.
