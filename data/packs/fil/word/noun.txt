description: Common noun. FILIPINO NOUNS DO NOT INFLECT FOR NUMBER: bahay is house and houses alike, and plurality is carried by the separate particle mga placed before the noun — mga bahay. Nor do they take case endings; the particles ang, ng and sa in front of the noun do that work instead, marking which noun the verb is focused on. Reduplication changes meaning rather than number: bahay-bahay is not 'houses' but 'from house to house', and araw-araw not 'days' but 'daily'. Several nouns here are the roots the ma- adjectives in word/adjective are built from — ganda, lakas, bilis, bigat — which is worth seeing side by side. The last two entries begin with NG, a SINGLE LETTER of the Filipino alphabet sorted between N and O — ngipin (tooth) and ngiti (smile) are ordinary words, and a sort that splits NG into N plus G files them wrongly.
locale: fil
