description: Common adverb. FIVE OF THESE ARE REDUPLICATED AND THAT IS GRAMMAR, NOT EMPHASIS. Araw-araw is 'daily', from araw (day); gabi-gabi is 'nightly'; dahan-dahan is 'slowly'; unti-unti is 'little by little'; sabay-sabay is 'all together'. Doubling a root in Filipino makes a new word with a regular meaning — distributive, repetitive or diminished — and it does the work that separate adverbs and prefixes do in European languages. The same device makes bahay-bahay 'from house to house' and isa-isa 'one by one'. Note also that most manner adverbs are simply the ma- adjective placed after the verb with the linker: tumakbo nang mabilis, 'ran quickly', is literally 'ran in a fast manner', so Filipino has no -ly suffix and no separate adverb form at all.
locale: fil
