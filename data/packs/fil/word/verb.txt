description: Common verb, in citation form. THIS IS THE FILE WHERE FILIPINO DIVERGES MOST SHARPLY FROM EVERY EUROPEAN LANGUAGE IN THIS PACK, and no config can infer it. Filipino has NO INFINITIVE and NO SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT: a verb never changes for person or number. What it changes for is FOCUS — which participant in the event the sentence is about — and the affix on the verb announces the choice. Take the root SULAT, 'write'. SUMULAT puts the writer in focus: Sumulat ang bata, the child wrote. ISULAT puts the thing written in focus: Isulat ang liham, write the letter. SULATAN puts the person written TO in focus: Sulatan mo si Ana, write to Ana. IPANULAT puts the instrument in focus. One root, four verbs, four different sentence shapes, and the noun in focus is marked with ang while the others take ng or sa. English has to reach for the passive, for prepositions, or for a different verb entirely, and it can only foreground the subject and the object; Filipino can foreground the recipient, the location, the instrument and the beneficiary as easily. The list below therefore mixes affixes deliberately: the mag- and -um- forms are actor focus, the -in and i- forms object focus, the -an forms direction or location focus. Pairs on the same root — magbigay/ibigay/bigyan, magluto/lutuin, magbasa/basahin — are included so the pattern is visible in the data itself. The last pair, ngumiti and ngitian, also begins with NG, the single Filipino letter that a naive sort splits in two.
