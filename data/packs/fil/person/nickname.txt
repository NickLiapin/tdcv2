description: Familiar name. TWO PHILIPPINE SYSTEMS MEET HERE AND NEITHER IS EUROPEAN. The first is REDUPLICATION: a syllable of the given name is doubled to make the household name, so Ferdinand becomes Bongbong, Benigno becomes Noynoy, Jose becomes Jojo or Pepe, and Filipinos carry these into adult public life — presidents, senators and cabinet secretaries are known nationally by them. The second is the KINSHIP ADDRESS SYSTEM borrowed from Hokkien and used constantly: Kuya is an older brother, Ate an older sister, and below them Diko and Ditse are the second brother and sister, Sangko and Sanse the third, Siko and Sitse the fourth, with Bunso for the youngest child of all. These are not names but they are what a Filipino is CALLED, by siblings, by cousins, by the waiter and by strangers of the right apparent age, and a record of how Filipinos address each other that omits them is missing the commonest words in it. Mang and Aling for older men and women, and Tito and Tita for any adult of the parents' generation, work the same way.
