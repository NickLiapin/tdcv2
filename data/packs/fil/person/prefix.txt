description: Honorific placed before a name. THE REAL POLITENESS OF FILIPINO IS NOT IN THIS FILE AND CANNOT BE. Respect is carried by the enclitic particles PO and HO, which are dropped inside the sentence rather than attached to the name — 'Opo', 'Salamat po', 'Ano po ang pangalan ninyo?' — and a child who answers an elder without po is corrected on the spot. Po is the stronger and ho the softer; both are invariant, both are placed after the first word or phrase, and neither has any slot in a name-prefix list, so a config that models Philippine courtesy by choosing a title has modelled the wrong thing entirely. What IS here: the Spanish-derived G./Gng./Bb. for Ginoo, Ginang and Binibini; the Philippine habit of titling by PROFESSION, so a lawyer is always Atty., an engineer Engr., an architect Ar. and a woman doctor Dra. rather than Dr.; and the everyday address terms Mang and Aling for older men and women, Ka before a first name for a respected equal, and Kuya and Ate for anyone slightly older than the speaker. Hadji marks a Muslim Filipino who has made the pilgrimage to Mecca.
