description: Male given name, roughly by frequency. FILIPINO GIVEN NAMES ARE NOT FILIPINO WORDS, and that is the fact of the matter rather than an omission: three and a half centuries of Spanish rule and half a century of American rule left the country naming its children Jose, Juan and Antonio, then Mark, Michael and Justin, and the Austronesian names at the tail of this list (Bayani, Dakila, Makisig, Diwa) are a deliberate nationalist revival begun in the twentieth century and still a small minority. Several of the revival names are UNISEX and appear in the female list too — Kalayaan, Malaya, Dalisay, Hiraya — as does Angel, which is an ordinary boy's name and an ordinary girl's name in the Philippines; the two files are meant to overlap and a config drawing from both will see the same strings. The middle block shows a genuinely Philippine invention: parents blend their own names into a new one for the child, so Jose and Maria give Jomar, and the practice is productive enough that whole cohorts of Jomars, Jerwins and Marlous exist with no forebear of that name. The weights are a gentle decay, lifted at the top for Jose and Juan which are genuinely far commoner than rank alone would give; no published Philippine given-name frequency table exists at this granularity, so the curve is a shape and not a measurement.
