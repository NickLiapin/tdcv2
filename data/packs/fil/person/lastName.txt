description: Surname — THE AUSTRONESIAN STRATUM ONLY, AND ITS SMALLNESS IS THE POINT. The Philippine surname stock as a whole is NOT in this pack: it belongs to the country, not the language, because it was created by one administrative act — the 1849 Claveria decree that handed out a printed catalogue of 60,662 mostly Spanish surnames town by town, alphabetically — and it lives at philippines.person.lastName, which is where a config generating Philippine people should draw from. What is left for a LANGUAGE pack is the part the decree did not touch: the surnames that are Tagalog and Philippine WORDS, kept by families allowed to keep them, and readable as sentences by any Filipino. Dimaculangan is 'cannot be diminished'; Dimagiba 'cannot be destroyed'; Dimaano 'cannot be harmed'; Dimalanta 'cannot be withered'; Macapagal 'one who tires'; Magsaysay 'one who tells'; Panganiban 'to be wary of'. The Gat- names (Gatmaitan, Gatbonton, Gatdula) carry the pre-Hispanic noble title Gat. Tañada is spelled with Ñ, which is a letter of the modern 28-letter Filipino alphabet and not an accent that may be dropped. The Mindanao names at the end are Maranao, Maguindanao and Tausug, from a region Spain never governed. DRAWING FROM THIS FILE GIVES A DELIBERATELY UNREPRESENTATIVE SAMPLE of Philippine surnames — it over-represents the indigenous stratum enormously — and it is unweighted for the same reason: no frequency data exists for this subset alone and inventing a curve would claim knowledge that does not exist. NO FEMININE FORM IS SHIPPED and its absence is the design: Philippine surnames do not inflect for gender, and a woman named Panganiban is Panganiban.
