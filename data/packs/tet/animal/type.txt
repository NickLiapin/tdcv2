description: Animals in general, and lafaek is first because in Timor the crocodile is not merely one animal among others. The founding legend has a boy find a sick crocodile stranded and drag it back to the water; the crocodile repays him by carrying him across the sea for years, and when it dies its body becomes the island, ridged back and all. Timorese therefore call the crocodile avô lafaek, grandfather, address it as kin, and read the outline of their own country as its spine — which is why a fatal attack in the Dili estuaries is treated as a matter of broken relationship and customary settlement rather than as simple misfortune. The rest of the native block is the livestock a household actually owns and the wild things it lives beside: karau buffalo, bibi goat, bibi-rusa deer, fahi pig, kuda horse, asu dog, busa cat, manu, which is chicken and bird alike with no separate word for either, laho rat and lekirauk monkey. After that comes the zoo and the schoolbook, entirely Portuguese respelled, and three of those show the rules at their clearest: hipopótamo drops its initial h to give ipopótamu, esquilo writes qu as k to give eskilu, and girafa writes g before i as j to give jirafa. Bibi-malae, sheep, is literally foreign goat, which is the standard Tetum way of naming something that came off a ship.
