description: Filler words in Tetun Dili, the lorem-ipsum vocabulary of this pack. The list was compiled rather than extracted: data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs cuts text/word, text/sentence and text/paragraph out of public-domain books, and there is no Tetum title for it to cut, so this one was assembled by hand and spread deliberately across kin, the body, the house, food, the coffee garden, the land, animals, weather and time, the town, the market, school and church, the state, abstractions, adjectives, verbs, numbers and function words, so that a random draw reads as Tetum rather than as a glossary of one subject. The seam it is really showing is the one between the Austronesian base and four centuries of Portuguese: where Tetum has a word of its own it stands here in place of the loan — uma for a house and not kaza, osan for money and not dinyeiru, be'e for water and not agua, loron for a day and not dia, matan for an eye and not ollu, hahán for food and not komida — while the abstract and administrative vocabulary has no native equivalent and arrives from Portuguese, respelled on the way in: educação to edukasaun and informação to informasaun by the -ão to -aun rule, religião to relijiaun with j for the soft g, escola to eskola with k for c before a back vowel, hospital to ospitál with the initial h dropped and the final stress written with an acute. Two later layers sit on top of that and both are represented: kantór, buku, meja, guru, jam, susu, gula and kaka are Indonesian or Malay, from the 1975-1999 occupation and from older trade contact, and workshop, training, budget, donór, meeting and staff came in with the UN administration after 1999 and stayed in the aid and office register. Nothing in the file is inflected. Nouns take no plural ending — plural is the postposed sira, as in labarik sira, and it is dropped whenever a numeral or the context already carries the count — and there is no grammatical gender, so one adjective form serves every noun and nia is he, she and it alike.
locale: tet
