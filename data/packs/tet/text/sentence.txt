description: Filler sentences in Tetun Dili, the lorem-ipsum prose of this pack, compiled by hand because the corpus builder in data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs carries no Tetum title and Project Gutenberg holds no public-domain book in the language to cut lines from. The one thing to read here is the verb: it never changes shape. Ba is go for every person and every tense, and the work Portuguese does with endings is done in Tetum by small words standing beside the verb — ona for something finished (nia ba ona, he has gone), sei for what has not happened yet (nia sei ba), hela for something still under way (nia servisu hela), seidauk for not yet, la for plain negation, atu for purpose, katak to open a reported clause. Portuguese vou, fui, irei and ia all land on the same two letters here. The vocabulary is Portuguese in large part but the spelling is not: escola hardens the c before o into k and comes out eskola, while hospital drops its word-initial h and takes an acute on the final stressed syllable to give ospitál; the same two habits produce kuziña, kamiaun, kartaun, konstrusaun, kolleita and baunilla. Three loan layers sit on one Austronesian frame — Portuguese from the colony (igreja, semana, governu, sidade, merkadu), Indonesian from the 1975-1999 occupation (kantór, buku, guru, susu, meza, toko, kios), English from the UN administration and the aid sector after it (projetu, workshop, relatóriu, internet, donór, staff) — while the bones of every sentence stay native: uma, be'e, hahán, labarik, foho, tasi, rai, loron, tinan, karau, ikan. The apostrophe is a letter and not punctuation; it writes a glottal stop, so be'e water, ki'ik small, di'ak good, lorosa'e east and ha'u I are simply misspelled without it. Every person and place named in these lines is Timorese.
