description: Filler paragraphs in Tetun Dili, three to five sentences on one physical line each, written by hand for the same reason as text/word and text/sentence — the corpus builder has no public-domain Tetum book to cut them from. What a paragraph shows that a single sentence cannot is the particle work that holds several clauses together, because the Tetum verb itself never moves: no person, no number, no tense. A state already reached is marked by ona placed after the clause (udan tau ona, the rain has come; sira fila ona, they have gone back); something still under way takes hela after the verb (nia servisu hela, he is working); what has not happened yet takes sei in front of it, la negates, and seidauk is not yet. Reported speech and reported thought are opened by katak, which is why ema dehan katak and nia hatene katak recur down the file — Tetum uses the one particle where English needs both that and a tense shift. Tanba gives a reason, maibé sets a clause against the one before it, bainhira opens a when-clause, and those five words plus ona do nearly all the joining here. The scenes are Timor-Leste's and not generic: a coffee harvest in Ermera, a boat to Atauro, a tais laid over a coffin, a tara bandu sign hung at the edge of a reef, market day in Baucau, the road between Same and Ainaro after the rains.
