description: Occupations in Timor-Leste, and the list is built around a word-forming device with no European counterpart. Na'in means owner of, master of, the one who holds a thing, and it attaches to almost any noun to produce an agent noun: to'os is the dry garden plot cut into a hillside and to'os-na'in is the farmer who works it, natar is the irrigated paddy and natar-na'in the man who floods it, tais is the woven cloth and tais na'in the weaver, kafé gives kafé-na'in, tua the palm spirit gives tua-na'in, masin the salt gives masin-na'in. It is the most productive suffix in the language, and it is the reason a Tetum occupation list cannot be produced by translating a Portuguese one. Lia-na'in is the same construction on lia, word or speech — the master of the word, the elder who holds a lineage's oral history and speaks for it when a barlake is negotiated or a reconciliation is opened — and it names a genuine office rather than a personal quality. Beside him stand Liurai, the traditional ruler, and Matan dook, literally the far-seeing one, the diviner consulted before a house is raised or a quarrel settled. Xefe Suku and Xefe Aldeia belong on an employment list because they are salaried elected offices with a term and a stipend, not honorifics. Katekista is the largest religious occupation in the country: in a nation this Catholic, with far more chapels than resident priests, the catechist leads the Sunday service, prepares the sacraments and keeps the register in parishes a priest reaches a few times a year. Over the Austronesian base sit three imported layers. Portuguese supplies the professions and the trades wholesale, from Enjeñeiru and Farmaséutiku down to Pedreiru and Telladór. Indonesian supplies the transport words and Xofer, kept for the driver of a mikrolet, an angguna or an ojek, while Motorista is reserved for the salaried professional driver — one job, two registers, and a Timorese reader hears the difference at once. English supplies the aid economy, which is a real employer and not a curiosity: Jestór projetu, Ofisiál programa, Koordenadór klaster, Ofisiál M&A for monitoring and evaluation, Ofisiál WASH for water and sanitation, Mobilizadór komunitáriu, Tradutór, Interprete and Motorista ONG are ordinary lines on a Dili curriculum vitae. The state closes the formal economy with Funsionáriu públiku, Administradór postu, Ofisiál PNTL and Soldadu F-FDTL — and the informal one, Nain kios, Fa'an-na'in merkadu, Vendedór ambulante, Peskadór and Hakiak animál, carries more people than everything else on the list put together.
