description: Sports. The order is Timor-Leste's rather than Europe's: football first, then futsal, which may in practice be bigger because a concrete court fits inside a schoolyard where a full pitch does not, and volleyball, which needs only a net strung between two posts and is therefore the sport of every village square and every mission compound in the interior. Two entries carry a history a plain list would hide. Arte marsiál and Pencak silat are not hobbies here: the martial arts groups — PSHT, KORK, Kmanek Oan Rai Klaran and the rest — came in with the Indonesian period, recruited heavily among young men after 1999, and were banned outright by parliament in 2013 after their rivalries turned into street violence, so a Timorese record naming one is naming something politically loaded. Mergullu and Snorkeling are here because the reefs off Atauro were measured to hold the highest average reef-fish diversity of any site surveyed anywhere, and diving is the country's one export-grade tourism product. Sa'e foho, literally climb mountain, is the overnight walk up Ramelau. Hoki jelu stays on the list as a world sport and not a local one, for the obvious reason.
