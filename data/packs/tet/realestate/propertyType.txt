description: Property types. Uma kakuluk is the traditional house under a thatch of palm or grass, kakuluk being the roof or the crown of a thing, and it is still a large share of the rural housing stock rather than a heritage category. Uma iha foho is a house up in the hills, using iha, the locative particle that covers at, in and there is, and foho, mountain — the phrase is how a Dili family describes the place they return to for Christmas and for funerals, and a great many households genuinely maintain two homes on that pattern. Rai mamuk is a vacant plot, from rai land and mamuk empty; rai is also the word for earth, soil, and country, so rai-na'in means both landowner and, in customary usage, the spiritual guardian of a place, and the ambiguity is not a coincidence.
