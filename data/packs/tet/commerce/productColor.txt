description: Colour as written on a Timorese product listing, and the top of the list is the whole native inventory. Tetum's inherited colour terms number about five: mutin white, metan black, mean red, kinur yellow and matak. Matak is the interesting one, because it is not simply green — it is the word for raw, unripe and uncooked as well, so modo matak is a green vegetable and also a raw one, and ikan matak is uncooked fish. There is no inherited word for blue at all: azúl is Portuguese, and the shades built on it here are Tetum compounds, with azúl lalehan taking lalehan, the native word for sky. Rai-henek is sand and is native too, literally earth-of-grains. Everything below the first block came in with Portuguese and shows the rules at work — cinza to sinza with c before i to s, chocolate to xokolate with ch to x, castanho to kastañu with c to k and nh to ñ, vinho to vinyu, marfim to marfin, salmão to salmaun. Because Tetum adjectives do not agree, none of these needs a feminine form, and one entry serves a kadeira and a tais alike. Kór is the word for colour itself and is borrowed from cor.
locale: tet
