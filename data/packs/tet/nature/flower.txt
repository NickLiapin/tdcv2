description: Flowers, and this list is almost entirely Portuguese for a reason worth stating: Tetum names plants that are eaten, burned, built with or slept under, and a decorative flower has usually arrived with the person who planted it in a pot. Ibisku and buganvília are the exceptions that matter — hibiscus and bougainvillea grow along every wall in Dili and are what a Timorese street actually looks like — and ai-kamboja is the frangipani, which came through Malay and stands in cemeteries and outside churches all over the country. Note ibisku: Portuguese hibisco loses its initial h, exactly as hospital gives ospitál and hora gives oras, because Tetum does not write a letter it does not pronounce. Jirasól, from girassol, is the busiest respelling in this pack: g before i is written j, the double s is reduced to one, and the stressed final syllable takes an acute — three rules in six letters. Narciso gives narsizu, açucena gives asusena, crisântemo gives krizántemu, and gerânio gives jerániu on the same g rule as the sunflower.
