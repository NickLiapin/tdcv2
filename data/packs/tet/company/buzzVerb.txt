description: Business buzz verb in Tetum. A Portuguese or English list of these has to pick a citation form — the infinitive — and Tetum has none to pick, because the verb does not inflect for person, number or tense at all: what is written here is the whole verb, and the same shape serves I optimise, she optimised and they will optimise. Time is carried by separate particles that stand outside the verb, ona for a completed action, sei for the future and hela for something still going on, so a config that appends a tense ending to any of these has invented morphology the language does not have. The native verbs are built with a causative prefix that is genuinely productive and visible here: sa'e is to rise and hasa'e is to raise or increase, metin is firm and hametin is to strengthen, di'ak is good and hadi'a is to improve or repair, bi'it is strength and hakbi'it is to empower, which is why hakbi'it became the standard Tetum rendering of empowerment in development documents. Harii is to build or found, hamutuk is to be together and gave its name to one of the country's best-known research organisations, and fahe, buka, hetan, fó, simu, troka, halo, tulun and lao are the plain verbs of ordinary speech doing corporate work. Hanorin, to teach, and hanoin, to think, differ by a single letter and are worth keeping apart. The Portuguese verbs are borrowed as a bare stem with the -ar, -er or -ir ending simply cut off, which is why the list reads organiza, implementa, dezenvolve and produz rather than organizar or desenvolver — the loan enters as a Tetum verb, not as a Portuguese one.
locale: tet
