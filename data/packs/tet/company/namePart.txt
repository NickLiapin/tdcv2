description: Evocative noun for a Timorese company name; compose with company/legalSuffix or a buzz adjective. A real Timorese trade register reads as two vocabularies interleaved, and the file is built that way on purpose. The first is Tetum, and it reaches for the country itself and for the things that identify it: the mountains Ramelau, Tatamailau, Matebian and Kablaki, the islands Atauro and Jaco, the two seas Tasi Mane and Tasi Feto, and Lorosa'e and Loromonu, east and west. Lafaek, the crocodile, is the most loaded word on the list — the founding story has a boy save a dying crocodile that carries him across the sea and becomes the island, whose long shape is read as its back, and Timorese address the animal as grandfather; a firm called Lafaek is claiming the country, not an animal. Belak, the chest disc, Kaibauk, the crescent headpiece, Surik, the sword, Morteen, the coral beads, and Tais, the woven cloth, are the ceremonial regalia and function here the way a crown or a lion functions in a European register. Nakroman and Naroman are light and brightness, Kmanek is fine or beautiful, Hakbi'it is to empower and is straight out of the development sector's Tetum, Moris Foun is new life and Rai Foun new land, and Ukun is to rule or govern, the root of ukun rasik an, self-determination. Sunrise is English and is the Greater Sunrise gas field, whose name has been attached to Timorese ventures for two decades. The second vocabulary is the Portuguese abstraction the colonial register bequeathed — Orizonte, Meridianu, Vértise, Kume, Zenite, Aurora and the mineral and constellation words behind them — arriving through the usual rules, so cume gives Kume, raiz gives Raís, carvalho gives Karvallu with lh to ll, golfinho gives Golfiñu with nh to ñ, falcão gives Falkaun and força gives Forsa. Aço, steel, is deliberately absent: it would respell to asu, which is already the Tetum word for dog.
locale: tet
