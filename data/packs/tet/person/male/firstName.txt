description: Male given names in Timor-Leste. The Portuguese sanctoral supplies almost the whole list, and the reason is as much administrative as devotional: for four centuries the parish baptismal book was the civil register outside Dili, so the name a Timorese man carries is the name a priest wrote down, drawn from the stock of names that priest had - the Portuguese calendar of saints. That is why this file is full of names an ear in Lisbon hears as antique and Timor hears as ordinary: Longuinhos, Anacleto, Aniceto, Egídio, Constâncio, Estanislau, Hermenegildo, Cesaltino, Venâncio, Bendito, Aleixo, Anselmo. João, José, Manuel, Domingos and Francisco lead as they do across the Lusophone world, but Domingos runs hotter here than in Portugal because a boy born on a Sunday - loron domingu - is very often simply named for the day. The occupation of 1975-1999 laid an Indonesian spelling over the same saints, and it is a spelling and not a different set of men: Yohanes is João, Yosef is José, Dominggus is Domingos, Antonius is António, Yulius is Júlio, Petrus is Pedro, Stefanus is Estêvão, Fransiskus is Francisco. Someone born in 1985 can hold the Indonesian form on a school certificate and the Portuguese form on a baptismal record, and both are legitimately his, which is why both forms sit in this one file rather than in two. The closing block is Austronesian. Mau is the male title of Tetun Terik, answered by Bui for a woman; it stands alone as a name and it opens the compound Maubere, a word the resistance lifted from the commonest Timorese man's name and turned into the name of the Timorese people. Bere, Berek, Loro, Lelo, Nai, Seran, Leki, Hoar, Taek and Klau are names of the same order. Xanana earns its place because it stopped being a nom de guerre and became a given name: boys born through the 1980s and 1990s were named for Kay Rala Xanana Gusmão while he was still in the mountains and then in Cipinang prison. Ukun (to rule, hence self-rule), Funu (struggle, war) and Rai (land) are the male half of the same political naming that produced Liberdade and Vitória for girls.
locale: tet
