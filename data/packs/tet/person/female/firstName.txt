description: Female given names as Timor-Leste actually registers them, and the first thing to know is that this is a Catholic list. Around ninety-seven per cent of Timorese are baptised Catholic, the highest share in Asia and among the highest anywhere on earth, and a first name here is normally a saint's name received at baptism rather than a name chosen for its sound - which is why Maria, Ana, Joana, Isabel, Teresa and Lúcia stand at the head, and why so much of the tail is deep Portuguese sanctoral (Ermelinda, Guilhermina, Emerenciana, Escolástica, Feliciana, Marcolina) that has thinned out in Portugal itself but is still given every year in Ainaro and Baucau. A second pattern is unmistakably Timorese: the name taken from a feast or from an abstraction. Natalina belongs to a girl born at Natal and Páscoa to one born at Easter, while Assunção, Conceição, Anunciação, Encarnação, Purificação, Visitação, Natividade and Epifânia each name a day in the calendar rather than a person, and Fátima, Lurdes, Rosário, Imaculada, Aparecida and Dores are Marian titles pressed into service as given names. The abstractions in this file are political as much as devotional - Esperança, Liberdade, Vitória, Paz and Independência spike sharply in the 1999-2002 birth cohort, the children of the referendum and of the restoration of independence, so a dataset that joins these names to a birth year should not spread them evenly. The occupation of 1975-1999 left a spelling layer rather than a new stock of names: Yustina, Yohana, Yuliana, Yosefina, Agustina, Petronela, Veronika, Lusia, Sesilia, Fransiska, Marselina and the compound Maria Fatima are the same saints wearing Bahasa Indonesia orthography off a school register. The Austronesian base survives thinly here because baptism displaced most of it, but it is real: Bui is the female counterpart of the male title Mau and is a name in its own right, and Ita, Dasi, Buti, Bete, Mali, Domin (love) and Kmanek (good, beautiful) are Tetum words used as names. One orthographic point a config author will trip on - a Portuguese name keeps its Portuguese spelling and its accents in the register even where Tetum would respell the same string as a common noun, so the name Conceição is written Conceição while the ordinary noun is konsesaun, and Assunção stays Assunção against asunsaun.
locale: tet
