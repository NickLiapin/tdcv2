description: Nationality adjectives in Tetum, and the first thing to notice is that they do not vary. Tetum has no gender agreement and no adjectival number, so italianu is italianu for a man, a woman and a crowd, and the pt list's masculine-singular caveat has nothing to caveat here — one form fills every row. Timor-oan leads, and it is not a Portuguese demonym at all: oan is the native word for child or offspring, so a Timorese is literally a child of Timor, and the same suffix builds Dili-oan, foho-oan and labarik-oan. The ordering after it is Timorese rather than European. Indonesia and Australia come next because they are the neighbours, the destinations for work and study, and the two states most present in the country's recent history. Kubanu is on the list for a reason no other Southeast Asian pack would have: Cuba has run a medical brigade and a scholarship programme in Timor-Leste for two decades, sending hundreds of doctors to district clinics and training a Timorese cohort in Havana, so Cuban nationality turns up on real Timorese hospital records. Portugal, Brazil and the African Portuguese-speaking states follow as the CPLP bloc that supplies teachers, cooperation staff and much of the Portuguese-language administration.
locale: tet
