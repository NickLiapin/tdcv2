description: The commonest verbs, in the only form they have. Portuguese needs two verbs for to be and conjugates each of them six ways in the present alone; Tetum has neither ser nor estar and normally has no copula at all — Nia mestre means he is a teacher with nothing standing between the two words, and iha covers both there is and to have, while sai covers to become. Nothing on this list changes for person, number or tense. Several entries are compounds that show how Tetum coins rather than borrows: hanoin-hetan is to remember, literally to find a thought, from hanoin a thought and hetan to get; hahú is to begin; hatudu is to show, built on the same ha- causative prefix that makes hatene, to know, out of tene. Note serbisu, which is a noun in word/noun.txt and a verb here, unchanged — because a Tetum verb has no distinguishing shape, the same form does both jobs and the syntax decides which.
