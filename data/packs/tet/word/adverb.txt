description: Adverbs, and Tetum makes them in a way Portuguese does not. There is no suffix answering to -mente: an adjective simply stands in adverbial position unchanged, so lalais is both fast and quickly and neineik is both slow and slowly, and the twenty-two-letter rapidamente of the Portuguese list has no Tetum counterpart at all. Where a manner needs spelling out, Tetum builds it with ho, meaning with, plus a noun — ho kuidadu is with care, ho aten-brani is with courage from aten the liver and brani brave, and ho ksolok is with joy. A few high-frequency items are native single words that repay learning because they turn up in every second sentence: deit, only or just, placed after what it limits; beibeik, often; dala ruma, sometimes; liuliu, especially; karik, perhaps; and sei, which marks both still and the future depending on where it sits.
