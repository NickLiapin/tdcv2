description: Conditions markedly more frequent in people of Melanesian and Papuan ancestry, named in Tetum, and a file with direct bearing inside Timor-Leste itself, where the Fataluku of Lospalos, the Makasae of Baucau and the Bunak of the Bobonaro highlands speak Papuan rather than Austronesian languages and the deep ancestry tends to follow the language. Ovalositoze Sudeste Aziátiku is a deletion in the band 3 red cell membrane protein that leaves the cells oval and stiff; it is found at high frequency in Melanesia and almost nowhere else, it is lethal when inherited from both parents, and in one copy it gives strong protection against cerebral malaria — the same bargain the sickle and thalassaemia variants strike in the files either side of this one. Fenótipu Gerbich-negativu, a red cell phenotype vanishingly rare across the rest of the world, reaches double-digit frequency in parts of Papua New Guinea for what appears to be the same malarial reason, and it shows up first at the crossmatch bench as an unexpectedly difficult match rather than as a diagnosis.
