description: General diagnoses as a Timorese hospital records them, and the shape of the list is this country's epidemiology rather than a European textbook's table of contents. Two burdens sit on the same outpatient bench here. The older one has not gone: dengue arrives with every wet season and kills children in it, typhoid and cholera follow the water, soil-transmitted worms and scabies are ordinary rather than exotic, leprosy has never quite been finished off, and rabies exposure is a live risk on the land border. Malária stays on the list even though locally transmitted malaria was driven to zero here in 2021, after a decade that took the country from hundreds of thousands of cases a year to none — the diagnosis is still made, but it is now imported or historical, and a record set that quietly deletes it loses the most successful thing Timorese public health has done. The newer burden came with the towns and now fills the same clinic: diabetes, hypertension, stroke, chronic kidney disease. Doensa is Portuguese doença with the ç written s and the nasal ending resolved to -a; the word a patient actually uses is moras, which covers being ill and being in pain at once, and that is the word the symptom list next door is built out of.
