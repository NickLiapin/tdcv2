description: Medical diagnosis that can occur in any patient — no sex or ancestry restriction. Sex-specific conditions live in diagnosisMale / diagnosisFemale, ancestry-linked ones in diagnosisByAncestry. IBÀ leads the list because it leads the real one: malaria is the commonest diagnosis in Nigeria by a wide margin, and "ibà" is the word for both the parasite disease and a fever generally, so a Yoruba speaker saying they have ibà may mean either. A note on method that applies to every medical file in this pack: NIGERIAN CLINICAL RECORDS ARE WRITTEN IN ENGLISH, because English is the language of Nigerian medical training and of the hospital card. Yoruba does have established names for the conditions people talk about — ibà (malaria), àtọ̀gbẹ (diabetes), ẹ̀jẹ̀ ríru (hypertension), jẹjẹrẹ (cancer), wárápá (epilepsy), jẹ̀dọ̀jẹ̀dọ̀ (hepatitis), àtọ̀sí (gonorrhoea), ẹ̀tẹ̀ (leprosy), ikọ́ fée (tuberculosis), ọgbẹ́ inú (peptic ulcer), àìtó ẹ̀jẹ̀ (anaemia) — and those are used here. Where the language has no settled term, the entry is either a transparent Yoruba description built from ordinary words (àrùn kíndìnrín, "kidney disease") or the clinical English, which is what a Yoruba speaker actually reads on their own file. Nothing has been coined to make the list look more Yoruba than the language is.
