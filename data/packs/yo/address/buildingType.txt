description: Building type, in Yoruba. Yoruba names a building by what happens in it, with ilé ("house") plus the activity: ilé ìwé is "house of books" = school, ilé ìwòsàn "house of healing" = hospital, ilé ìfowópamọ́ "house where money is kept" = bank, ilé ìkàwé "house of reading" = library. The one with no English counterpart is AGBO ILÉ, the walled compound that houses a whole ìdílé — several families around a shared courtyard — which is the traditional Yoruba residential unit and still the commonest form of housing in the old quarters of Ìbàdàn, Abẹ́òkúta and Ilé-Ifẹ̀. Ilé gogoro is a storey building (gogoro = tall), Búkà a roadside eating house, Ààfin the palace of an ọba.
locale: yo
