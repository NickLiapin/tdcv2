description: Yoruba male given names. A Yoruba given name is a SENTENCE, not a label: Adébáyọ̀ is "the crown meets joy", Olúwaṣeun "thank you God", Ọlátúnjí "honour wakes again", Bàbátúndé "father has come back". The list is grouped by its head morpheme because the head is the meaning — Adé- crown (royal descent), Akin- valour, Olú-/Olúwa- God, Ọlá- honour/wealth, Ayọ̀- joy, Ògún- and Fá-/Ifá- the lineage òrìṣà. The last block is àmútọ̀runwá, names a child ARRIVES with rather than is given: Táíwò and Kẹ́hìndé are the first and second twin, Ìdòwú the child after twins, Àlàbá the one after Ìdòwú, Òjó born with the cord round his neck, Dàda born with matted hair, Ìgé born feet first, Àjàyí born face down, Sàlàkọ̀ born in the caul. Rótìmí, Málọmọ́, Kòsọ́kọ́, Dúrójayé, Bánjókó, Ìgbẹ́kọ̀yí and Kúkọ̀yí are àbíkú names, pleaded at a child whose siblings died young — "stay with me", "do not go again", "there is no hoe left to dig a grave with". Not in this list, and deliberately: the Yorubaised Arabic names Yoruba Muslims carry, and the English and Portuguese surnames-turned-given-names of the 19th-century Saro and Amaro returnee families. Weights are rank-derived on the brief's gentle curve, not counts from a registry — Nigeria publishes no given-name frequency table.
