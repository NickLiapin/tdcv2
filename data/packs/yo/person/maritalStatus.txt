description: Marital status in Yoruba. The list is longer than a European one because Yoruba marriage is not binary. Àpọ́n is an unmarried man and Wúndíá an unmarried woman; a married person "has done ìgbéyàwó". Ìyàwó is a new or junior wife and ÌYÁLÉ the senior wife of the same husband — a status with no English word, and one that matters, because polygyny (ìgbéyàwó opó lóbìnrin) is legal under Yoruba customary law and still practised, so "married" alone under-describes a household. Opó is a widow or widower. Ìdálẹ́kọ̀ọ́ / ìkọ̀sílẹ̀ is divorce.
locale: yo
