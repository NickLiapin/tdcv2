description: Yoruba female given names, sentences like the male ones: Adúnní is "sweet to have", Fúnmiláyọ̀ "give me joy", Mọ́rẹ́nikẹ́ "I have found someone to pamper", Bọ́lánlé "meet wealth at home". Read this list beside person/male/firstName.txt, because the two OVERLAP ON PURPOSE and a config must not treat them as disjoint: Táíwò, Kẹ́hìndé, Ìdòwú, Dàda, Àbíọ́lá, Àbíọ́dún, Àbímbọ́lá, Ọlámidé, Ọmọ́tayọ̀, Ẹ̀nítàn, Ìbùkún, Ìfẹ́olúwa, Ọ̀pẹ́yẹmí, Olúwaṣeun and Olúwaṣèyí are carried by both sexes. Yoruba grammar has no gender at all, so nothing in the FORM of a name marks it; what marks the female names above is convention — the -kẹ́ names (pamper), the Ìyá-/Yé- names (Yétúndé, "mother has come again", for a girl born after a grandmother's death, against Bàbátúndé for a boy), and Àìná and Tàlàbí, the female counterparts of Òjó and Sàlàkọ̀. Weights are rank-derived on the brief's gentle curve, not registry counts.
