description: Everyday short form of a Yoruba given name. Yoruba clipping is systematic and it is worth knowing which end goes: the name is a sentence, and the part that survives is almost always the TAIL, the part that says something particular, while the head — the theophoric or lineage element Olúwa-, Adé-, Ọlá-, Fọlá-, Bàbá-, Akin- — is what gets dropped. Olúwaṣeun becomes Ṣeun, Adékúnlé Kúnlé, Ọlánrewájú Lánre, Fọláṣadé Ṣadé, Bàbátúndé Túndé, Olúwatóyìn Tóyìn. The head can be kept instead (Adéwálé to Adé, Ọláyínká to Ọlá) but it identifies far less, because half the town shares it. A short form drawn from this list is a plausible pet form for MANY of the full names in person/male/firstName.txt and person/female/firstName.txt at once, and like the full names it is mostly not sex-specific.
locale: yo
