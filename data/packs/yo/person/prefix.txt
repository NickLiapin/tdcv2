description: Yoruba honorific and form of address. Ọ̀gbẹ́ni, Ìyáàfin and Arábìnrin are the everyday Mr / Mrs / Miss. The rest have no European equivalent and outrank them: Ẹ̀gbọ́n and Àbúrò are pure AGE terms — elder and younger — and Yoruba uses them constantly as address, because relative age fixes the whole register of a conversation. Bàbá and Ìyá are used for any older man or woman, not only a parent. Olóyè is a holder of a chieftaincy title, Ọba a crowned king, Ààrẹ a national or association president, Ọ̀túnba a specific chieftaincy grade, Ìyálọ́de the senior woman chief of a town. Àlàhájì and Àlàhájà (returned from the hajj), Ìmámù and Àlùfáà are Muslim, Pásítọ̀ and Bíṣọ́ọ̀bù Christian, and both sets are in ordinary daily use.
locale: yo
