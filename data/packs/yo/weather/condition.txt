The list is shaped by a tropical year with two seasons: ÒJÒ and its degrees dominate half of it, and Ọ̀YẸ́, the harmattan, dominates the other half — a dry dust-carrying wind off the Sahara that drops the temperature, greys the sky and cracks skin, and which no European weather vocabulary has a word for.
