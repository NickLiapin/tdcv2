description: Kind of case. ẸJỌ́ OYÈ — a chieftaincy suit — is the entry a European list has no room for, and it is not rare: who is entitled to a vacant stool, which ruling house's turn it is, whether an installation was valid. These cases run for years and reach the Supreme Court. ẸJỌ́ ILẸ̀, land, is the other Yoruba staple, because land is held by families under customary tenure and the boundaries are remembered, not registered.
