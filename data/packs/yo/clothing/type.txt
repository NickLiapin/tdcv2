description: Garment. This is the fullest Yoruba category in the pack, because Yoruba dress is a complete and living system. A man wears BÙBÁ (the loose top) over ṢÒKÒTÒ (trousers), often the wide KẸ̀MBẸ̀ cut, with a FÌLÀ cap — abetí ajá ("dog's ear", with flaps that can be turned up) or gọ̀bí — and over it all the AGBÁDÁ, the great flowing robe, or the shorter dàńṣíkí. A woman wears bùbá with ÌRÓ (the wrapped skirt), ÌBORÙN or ÌPÈLÉ over the shoulder, and GÈLÈ, the head-tie, whose tying is a craft in itself. The cloths are named separately and are the expensive part: AṢỌ ÒKÈ is hand-woven on a narrow loom in strips, in the prestige weaves sányán (beige wild silk), àlàárì (crimson) and ẹtù (deep indigo); ÀDÌRẸ is indigo resist-dyed cotton from Abẹ́òkúta. AṢỌ ẸBÍ is not a garment but a rule: the family or hosts of a ceremony choose one cloth and everyone attending buys and sews it, so a photograph of a Yoruba wedding shows the guests colour-coded by which side they belong to.
