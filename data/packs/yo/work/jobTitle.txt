description: Job title in Yoruba. Yoruba builds an occupation name from the WORK, with one of four agent prefixes: a- / o- (one who does it: àgbẹ̀ farms, apẹja fishes, akọ̀wé writes), olù- (one who leads it: olùkọ́ teaches, olùdarí directs), oní- (one who owns or deals in it: onílù the drum, oníṣòwò the trade, onígbàjámọ̀ the razor), and ẹlẹ́- (the same with a different vowel: ẹlẹ́mu, palm wine). So the list is transparent in a way an English job-title list is not — you can read the work out of the word. The first block is the traditional crafts, most of which are still live occupations: gbẹ́nàgbẹ́nà the carpenter, alágbẹ̀dẹ the smith, ahunṣọ the aso-oke weaver, onídìrí the hair-plaiter, alápatà the butcher, ẹlẹ́mu the palm-wine tapper, and bàbáláwo the Ifá diviner, who is a consulted professional and not a curiosity. The second block is the modern professions, which Yoruba coins the same way where it can (amòfin "one who knows the law", oníròyìn "one who deals in news", onímọ̀ ẹ̀rọ "one who knows machines") and borrows where it cannot.
