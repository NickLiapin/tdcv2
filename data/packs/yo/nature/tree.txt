description: Tree. IRÓKÒ is the one to know: a huge hardwood held to be inhabited, felled only after propitiation, and the standard Yoruba metaphor for a great person ("igi ganganran"). ÀRÀBÀ the silk-cotton and ÒSÈ the baobab are the other two giants. ÀKÒKÓ leaves are pressed on the head of a new chief at installation, and PEREGÚN is planted to mark a boundary or a shrine, so both are legal and ritual objects as much as plants. Ògànwó (mahogany) and àrẹ̀rẹ̀ (obeche) are the timber trees; kòkó, obì, orógbó, ọ̀pẹ and àgbọn are the cash crops that paid for the region.
