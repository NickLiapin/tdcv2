ÀGỌ́ AYẸYẸ — the rented canopy — is where most Yoruba ceremonies actually happen: canopies and plastic chairs are put up in the family compound or across a closed street, and a hired hall is the more expensive alternative rather than the default. ÀGBÀLÁ ILÉ is that compound courtyard, and ÀÀFIN the ọba's palace, which hosts festivals and installations.
