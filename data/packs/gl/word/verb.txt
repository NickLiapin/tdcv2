description: Galician verb, infinitive. THE THING TO KNOW ABOUT THE GALICIAN VERB: there is NO PERFECT TENSE. Galician has no compound past at all - no 'haber + participle' the way Castilian has 'he comido' and Portuguese has 'tenho comido'. Where those languages use a compound, Galician uses the simple past: 'comin' covers both 'I ate' and 'I have eaten', 'fun' covers 'I went' and 'I have gone', 'aínda non chegou' is 'he has not arrived yet'. The auxiliary haber survives only in the obligation phrase 'hai que' and in 'haber de'. This is the single largest structural difference from both neighbours and it means a template that builds a Galician perfect by gluing 'ter' or 'haber' onto a participle produces something no Galician speaker would write. Second point, and it also breaks naive joining: the infinitive assimilates to a following definite article. 'comer o pan' is written and said come-lo pan, 'facer as cousas' is face-las cousas - the verb loses its final r and the article turns into -lo/-la. The same happens after a final s: 'todos os' becomes todolos. So concatenating a verb from this list with an article from elsewhere gives a form that is grammatical nowhere. Third: Galician has a PERSONAL INFINITIVE that inflects for its own subject (para comermos nos, para comerdes vos), which Castilian lacks entirely and Portuguese shares.
