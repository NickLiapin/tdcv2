description: Galician surnames in the STANDARD Galician forms of the Real Academia Galega's Guia de apelidos galegos; every entry here is attested in that guide and nothing is invented. ONE list for both sexes: Galician surnames do NOT inflect for gender - Uxia Carballo and her brother Brais Carballo carry the identical form - so there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack, unlike pl, cs or ru. That absence is the design. A Galician carries TWO surnames, paternal then maternal (Manuel Rodriguez Castro), so a full name draws from this list TWICE; since 1999 Spanish law lets parents reverse the order, but paternal-first remains the default and the overwhelming norm. The head of the list is the -ez patronymic layer, frozen genitives meaning 'son of' (Rodriguez < Rodrigo, Fernandez < Fernando, Vazquez < Vasco), and Rodriguez heads it because Rodriguez - not Garcia, as in Spain overall - is the commonest surname in Galicia; a handful of these patronymics cover a large share of the population. Everything after them is overwhelmingly TOPONYMIC: Souto is a grove, Carballo an oak, Veiga a river meadow, Outeiro a knoll, Chao a flat, Fraga rough woodland, Regueiro a brook, Pena a crag, Vila a settlement, Pazos manor houses, Lamas a mire - a Galician surname past the patronymic head usually names the piece of land the family held. CAUTION, and this is the sharpest thing to know about this file: the Spanish civil registry holds most of these surnames in CASTILIANISED spellings, so the standard Galician form here will often NOT string-match the same person's identity card. Outeiro is registered Otero, Igrexas is Iglesias, Rei is Rey, Branco is Blanco, Ribas is Rivas, Lourenzo is Lorenzo, Monteiro is Montero, Vermudez is Bermudez, Mosqueira is Mosquera, Nunez carries no tilde in the Galician form, and Martins/Martis are the Galician forms of what the registry writes Martinez. The RAG guide exists to support restoring these forms, and this pack ships the language's form because it is a LANGUAGE pack; registry-shaped Spanish data belongs to the Spain country pack. The list is deliberately NOT weighted, and the reason is specific rather than lazy: the only published bearer counts are the registry's, and they count the Castilianised strings, so attaching Otero's count to Outeiro would be a measurement of a different word. Order past roughly the first sixty carries NO frequency claim.
locale: gl
