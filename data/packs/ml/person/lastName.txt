description: The trailing name element across Kerala, weighted to the 2011 census shares (Hindu 54.73%, Muslim 26.56%, Christian 18.38%). The masses are measured; the shape within a stock is a Zipf estimate with exponent 1.0, chosen because Kerala's family-name slot really is concentrated — നായർ alone lands near 13% of the state, which is the right order of magnitude for the Nair share of Kerala's population, whereas this project's default gentle curve would have given it under 2% and claimed നായർ and പിഷാരടി were equally common. THERE IS NO person/female/lastName.txt AND ITS ABSENCE IS THE DESIGN: no Malayalam family name inflects for gender — ശ്രീമതി രാധ മേനോൻ and ശ്രീ കൃഷ്ണൻ മേനോൻ carry the identical word, unlike Polish -ski/-ska or Russian -ov/-ova. Two further things a config cannot infer. Kerala's official name order is INITIALS FIRST: the expanded house name and the father's name are reduced to initials that precede the personal name (കെ. ആർ. നാരായണൻ, എം. ടി. വാസുദേവൻ നായർ, ഇ. എം. എസ്. നമ്പൂതിരിപ്പാട്), so first + last is the short form and person/initial.txt supplies the rest. And a large minority of Malayalis — most Muslims, and many Hindus — have no inherited family name whatsoever; the slot holds a house name, a father's name or a title instead. Kerala is not one naming pool. The Hindu, Muslim (Mappila) and Syrian/Latin Christian stocks are almost disjoint, and a row that pairs a given name from one with a family name from another describes a person who does not exist. Drawing this list independently of person/male/firstName.txt does exactly that. Use the community-pure addresses — ml.person.hindu.*, ml.person.muslim.*, ml.person.christian.* — or the fullName generators, which draw one community per row. Orthography: reformed (post-1971) Malayalam throughout the pack, with the chillu letters as the atomic codepoints Unicode 5.1 gave them — ൻ U+0D7B, ർ U+0D7C, ൽ U+0D7D, ൾ U+0D7E, ൺ U+0D7A. The pre-Unicode-5.1 spelling of a chillu as consonant + virama + ZWJ, and the old-orthography use of ZWNJ to break a conjunct, appear NOWHERE in this pack: there is not one U+200C or U+200D in any file. This matters most here, because a Kerala family name almost always ends in a chillu (നായർ, മേനോൻ, തങ്ങൾ, പിള്ള), so a pack that mixed the two encodings would produce two spellings of the same surname that no string comparison could match.
