description: Allergens, and the file splits where the Malian consultation splits. The first eleven are Bambara because they are food and dust — what a patient names — and they are Malian food: `tiga` groundnut, which is in the sauce at almost every meal, `sumbala` the fermented néré condiment, `si tulu` shea butter, `woro` kola, `nɔnɔ` milk, `kili` egg, `jɛgɛ` fish, `buguri` dust. `Buguri` earns its place: the harmattan carries so much Saharan dust between December and February that respiratory presentations rise every year with it. The second half stays French because it is pharmacy: penicillin, sulfonamides, chloroquine and quinine are named on the packet and in the register in French, and a drug allergy is recorded by a health worker rather than described by a patient. Chloroquine and quinine are on the list at all because antimalarials are the most-dispensed drugs in the country.
