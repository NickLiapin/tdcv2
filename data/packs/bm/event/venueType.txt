description: Where a Malian event actually happens, and the first three answers are not buildings. `Kɛnɛ` is the open ground of a quarter — swept sand, no structure — and it is where weddings, wrestling, funerals, political meetings and the balani show all take place; a Bamako family renting chairs and a canopy for a Saturday is renting them onto the kɛnɛ. `Bulon` is the roofed entrance vestibule of a compound, where a delegation is received and a marriage is negotiated. `Cour de concession` is the courtyard inside, which becomes the venue for anything smaller. The formal French venues below are real but far outnumbered: a Malian civil servant may spend a career attending seminars in hotel conference rooms and still be married on the sand.
