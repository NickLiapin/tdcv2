description: What a Malian kitchen actually holds. `Sumbala` is the one that defines the taste: néré seeds boiled for a day, fermented for several more and pressed into black balls with a smell that clears a room and a flavour that is the base note of almost every sauce in the country — it is Mali's equivalent of fish sauce or miso, and it is made and sold by women. `Si tulu` is shea butter, pressed from the nuts of the karité tree by women's cooperatives, used for cooking and for skin alike. `Malo`, `Ɲɔ`, `Kaba` and `Fini` are rice, millet, maize and fonio, and fonio deserves the note: it is the oldest cultivated cereal in West Africa, ripens in six weeks on poor soil, and is the grain that carries a household through the hungry season before the millet is ready. `Nɔnɔ` is milk. Note that `Sumbala` and `Soumbala` both appear — the Bambara spelling and the French one, and Malian packaging uses both.
