description: Malian dishes across the whole day, from the fritters sold at dawn to the evening tô. The rhythm of eating here is worth knowing before reading the list: there is one large communal meal, usually at midday, eaten from a shared bowl on a mat with the right hand, men and women often in separate groups, and the rest of the day is small purchases from street sellers — `Ŋɔmi`, `Beignets`, `Toubani`, a `Sandwich` of baguette and beans. `Pain-beurre` is the standard Malian breakfast, bread and butter and sweet coffee or tea. The sauces are listed separately from the starches because that is how the food is built: the starch is nearly constant and the sauce is where the variety, the season and the household's money show.
