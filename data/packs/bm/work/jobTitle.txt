description: What Malians do for a living, and the file is split down the middle on purpose because a Malian CV is. The first hundred values are Bambara, and they cover the work most of the country actually does: `sɛnɛkɛla` a farmer, `begɛnmarabaga` a herder, `mɔnnikɛla` a fisherman, `jagokɛla` a trader, `numu` a blacksmith, `garanke` a leatherworker, `jeli` a griot, `karamɔgɔ` a teacher or Qur'anic master, `donsokɛ` a hunter, `baarakɛla` a worker. Seven or eight Malians in ten live from farming, herding and fishing, so `sɛnɛkɛla` alone outweighs everything in the second half. Note how these are built: `-kɛla` is one who does, `-baga` is one who acts, `-la` marks the agent, `-tigi` the owner — so `feere` to sell gives `feerekɛla`, `dilan` to make or mend gives `dilanbaga`, `butiki` a shop gives `butikitigi`. The second half stays French, and that is not laziness: a `développeur logiciel`, a `sage-femme`, a `magistrat`, a `transitaire` and a `contrôleur de gestion` are jobs that arrived in Mali under those names, are certified by a French-language diploma, and appear in French on the payroll of anyone who holds them. There is no Bambara word for them because there is no Bambara conversation that has needed one.
locale: bm
