description: Making things. The Bambara half is the artisan economy and two of its names are castes rather than occupations: a `numu` is a blacksmith by birth, one of the nyamakala lineages that marry within themselves and hold a kind of dangerous authority — the smith forges, carves the masks and circumcises the boys — and the trade is not something one takes up. `Fininyɛla` weaves, `Kalalikɛla` sews, `Bɔgɔlanfini dilanbaga` makes mud cloth, `Sumbala dilanbaga` and `Si tulu dilanbaga` ferment néré and press shea, both women's trades that carry real income. The French titles belong to the factory floor — the cotton gins of Koutiala, the oil mills, the rice hullers — where the machinery and its vocabulary arrived together.
