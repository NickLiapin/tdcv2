description: Filler sentences in Bambara, the lorem-ipsum prose of this pack, written by hand because the corpus builder at data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs cuts its sentences out of public-domain books and there is no Bambara book for it to cut. They are short and plain on purpose — the register of a Malian primary reader or a radio bulletin, six to fourteen words, a clause or two about the household waking, the market, the field, the river, school, rain, the road between Bamako and the regions, tea, a wedding, an illness, a football match. The thing to watch is the slot between the subject and everything else: a Bambara clause carries its predicate marker there, and the object then sits in front of the verb, so n bɛ ji min is I-PROG-water-drink and a ye so ye is he-PFV-house-saw — the verb closes the clause every time. Where English and French put a preposition in front of a noun, Bambara puts a postposition behind it: so kɔnɔ house-in, tabali kan table-on, n fɛ me-with, Bamakɔ la Bamako-at, jiri kɔrɔ tree-under. The plural is the suffix -w and it is left off when the number is obvious: den a child, denw children, but misi fila two cows. The markers are varied deliberately down the file so that a random draw does not read as one drill — bɛ for the imperfective against tɛ for its negative, ye with a following object and the suffixes -ra, -la and -na without one for the perfective, ma for the negative perfective, bɛna for the future, ka for the infinitive and for chaining a second verb onto a first, and the pair ka and man that carries the quality verbs where English reaches for an adjective: a ka ɲi it is good, a man ɲi it is not good. Nothing here is a question, so every line closes on a plain full stop.
