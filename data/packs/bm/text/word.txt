description: Filler words in Bambara, the lorem-ipsum vocabulary of this pack, compiled by hand: data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs cuts these lists out of public-domain books and there is no Bambara title for it to cut, so the words were gathered rather than extracted. The spread is deliberately ordinary — kin, the body, the compound, food and cooking, farming, animals, the village and the town, water and weather, trade, the state and the school, abstractions, adjectives, verbs, numbers and function words — so that a random draw reads as Bambara and not as the glossary of one subject. Where Bambara has a word of its own it stands here in place of the French one heard beside it: mɔgɔ a person, du the walled family compound, dugu a village, ji water, sɛnɛ farming, jiri a tree, tile the sun and the day, nɛgɛ iron. Verbs are in their bare citation form, kɛ, taa, dun, fɔ, because Bambara has no infinitive ending — the infinitive is the separate particle ka standing in front of the stem — and the postpositions la, kɔnɔ, kan, fɛ, ma, bolo are listed as words in their own right, since Bambara puts them after the noun and has no prepositions at all. A word that carries two senses appears once and once only: san is the year and the sky, kɔ is the back and the postposition after, bolo is the hand and the postposition in the keeping of, segin is both the number eight and the verb to return. The settled French loans belong here too and are not a lapse — lakɔli, dɔgɔtɔrɔ, lopitali, tabali, banki, butiki, gomina, minisiri, telefɔni, ɔridinatɛri — because school, medicine, administration and commerce are conducted in French in Mali and the loan is what a speaker actually says. Tone is phonemic in Bambara but is left unwritten here, as it is in everyday Malian practice.
