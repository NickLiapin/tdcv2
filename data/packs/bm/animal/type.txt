description: Animals in general, and the second half of the list is a lesson in why a Bambara word list cannot be mapped one-to-one onto a European one. Mali is the Bambara for hippopotamus, which is why the country's name is so often explained that way; sigi is the buffalo, and the buffalo of Do is the shapeshifting woman whose killing opens the Sundiata epic. There are two ordinary words for lion, waraba and jara, and jata sits inside Sundiata's name. Suruku the hyena and sonsan the hare are not merely animals but fixed characters — the hyena is the greedy fool of every evening tale and the hare the trickster who ruins him. Note also that Bambara has no grammatical gender, so misi is cattle of either sex and you need the separate word ntura for a bull and sagajigi for a ram, the animal slaughtered at Tabaski. The five French values at the end are the large game a Malian is more likely to meet in a schoolbook than in a field.
