description: Nationality. The first six show the Bambara pattern: the suffix `-ka` attaches straight to a place name and makes the inhabitant noun — `Malika` a Malian, `Senegalika` a Senegalese, `Faransika` a French person, `Bamakɔka` someone from Bamako, `Segukaw` the people of Ségou. It is completely regular and works on any toponym, which is why a Bambara speaker never has to learn that Burkina Faso gives Burkinabè the way a French speaker must. The rest are given in French deliberately rather than by default: a Malian passport, residence permit, border form and school atlas are all in French, and coining sixty Bambara country-stems to feed the suffix would be inventing vocabulary rather than recording it — the productive rule is the fact worth shipping, not a manufactured list. The West African block is not padding: ECOWAS free movement is real, the Malian diaspora in Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal is enormous, and these are the nationalities a Malian record actually contains.
