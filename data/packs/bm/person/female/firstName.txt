description: Malian female given names — Arabic-Islamic on top, Bamanan underneath. The Arabic layer thickens as it passes through French: Fatima yields Fatoumata, Fatimata, Fadima, Fadimata, Fatouma, Fatou and the clipped Fatim, and Khadija yields Kadiatou, Kadidia, Kadidiatou, Kadiata and Kadia. Every one of those is a separate name to a Malian and a separate line on a civil register, and the same woman can appear under two of them on two documents, because the registrar wrote down what he heard. The endings -tou and -ta that produce so many of them are the French rendering of the Arabic feminine, which is why Amina, Aminata and Aminatou are three names rather than three spellings of one. Awa and Aoua are the same name written twice over, and both are in daily use. The Bamanan layer has no Arabic in it at all — Sadio, Naba, Nènè, Ténin, Wassa, Djita, Tènè, Sira, Sitan, Sounkoura, Worokia — and Kandia and Nagnouma belong to the jelimuso line, the women praise-singers who are among the most famous voices in the country. Two facts a config will otherwise get wrong. Adama is a man's name and a woman's name alike and stands in both files, so given names cannot sort rows by sex. And a woman keeps her father's jamu when she marries — she never takes her husband's — so the surname field carries no information about her marital status, and she is in any case usually addressed not by any name in this list but as Ba plus her eldest child's name. Weights are an ordering, not measured shares: Mali publishes no name frequencies, so the ranks record observed commonness only.
locale: bm
