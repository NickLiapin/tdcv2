description: Forms of address written before a Malian name, and the list is three registers stacked. The French administrative titles come first because that is what a form, a summons and a payslip use. Then the Islamic ones, which carry more weight socially than any of the French ones: `El Hadj` and its feminine `Hadja` mark a person who has made the pilgrimage to Mecca and are used as a permanent part of the name afterwards, `Cheick` and `Chérif` mark religious lineage, and `Karamɔgɔ` is the master of a Qur'anic school — a title of real authority in a country where the medersa network teaches a large share of children. Last, the Manding titles of rank that history left behind and Malians still use half-seriously: `Mansa` was the emperor's title, the one Mansa Moussa carried; `Fama` a ruler; `Jatigi` a host or patron, the man a griot is attached to. `Modibo` began as the title of a learned man and became a first name — which is how Mali's first president came to be called Modibo Keïta.
