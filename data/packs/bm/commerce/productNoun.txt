description: Goods sold in Mali. The Bambara block at the head is the market register — `malo` rice, `ɲɔ` millet, `kaba` maize, `tiga` groundnuts, `kɔgɔ` salt, `nɔnɔ` milk, `sumbala` the fermented néré condiment, `si tulu` shea butter, `fini` cloth, `nɛgɛso` the bicycle — and several of them appear again below in French, because that is exactly what a Malian record looks like: the same sack of rice is malo to the woman selling it and riz on the invoice. Read as a whole the rest describes an economy. The energy block is the tell: `Panneau solaire`, `Lampe solaire`, `Groupe électrogène`, `Onduleur` and `Batterie` are mass-market consumer goods here, because the grid reaches a minority and load-sheds where it does, so a household's most consequential electrical purchase is the thing that works when the power does not. `Moustiquaire` is a public-health intervention sold in every market, in a country where malaria is the leading cause of death in children. `Charbon de bois` is what most urban Malians cook on, and its consumption is the main driver of deforestation around the cities.
