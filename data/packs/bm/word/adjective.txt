description: Bambara qualifiers, and the file quietly teaches how Bambara builds them. Most of these are not adjectives in the European sense but QUALITATIVE VERBS: `a ka jan` is not he-is tall but roughly he tall-s, with the qualitative marker `ka` doing the work a copula does elsewhere. To use one attributively beside a noun you add the suffix -man, which is why `jan` and `janman`, `surun` and `surunman`, `misɛn` and `misɛnman` both appear — they are the same root in its two syntactic lives, not two words. The colour terms `jɛman`, `finman` and `bilenman` carry that same -man and are the only three basic colour adjectives the language has. Note the negative: the opposite of `a ka ɲi`, it is good, is not a different adjective but `a man ɲi`, with `man` replacing `ka`.
locale: bm
