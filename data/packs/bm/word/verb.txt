description: Bambara verbs in their bare citation form, which is also the imperative and the form that follows the infinitive marker `ka`. Bambara verbs do not conjugate for person or number at all — `n taara`, `u taara`, I went and they went, differ only in the pronoun — and tense and aspect are carried by a separate predicate marker sitting between subject and verb: `bɛ` for the imperfective, `tɛ` for its negative, `bɛna` for the future, `ye` for the transitive perfective, and a suffix -ra/-la/-na for the intransitive one. That is why the verb here has no ending to strip. The other thing to notice is transitivity: in a transitive clause the object sits BEFORE the verb, so `n bɛ ji min` is I imperfective water drink, and the verb closes the clause.
locale: bm
