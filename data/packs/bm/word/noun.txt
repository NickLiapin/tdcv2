description: Ordinary Bambara nouns, the vocabulary a first-year literacy class covers. Three features show through if you read down the list. There is no gender and no article: `so` is a house, the house, or houses depending only on context, and the plural is the suffix -w when it is marked at all, so `mɔgɔ` person becomes `mɔgɔw` people. Compounding is heavy and transparent: `kalanden` is a pupil from `kalan` study plus `den` child, `karamɔgɔ` is a teacher, `dɔgɔtɔrɔ` and `lakɔli` are the French docteur and l'école reshaped. And several words carry senses that a bilingual list has to choose between — `ba` is mother, river and big all at once, `kɔnɔ` is a bird and also the postposition inside, `san` is a year and the sky, `bolo` is a hand and also the postposition marking possession.
locale: bm
