description: Bambara adverbs and adverbial phrases, and half of this list is really the postpositional system in disguise. Bambara has no prepositions at all: where French puts sur, dans, derrière and devant in front of a noun, Bambara puts `kan`, `kɔnɔ`, `kɔ fɛ` and `ɲɛ fɛ` AFTER it — `tabali kan` is table-on, `so kɔnɔ` is house-inside. Several entries here are those postpositions standing alone as adverbs. The time words are worth a second look too: `bi` today, `sini` tomorrow and `kunun` yesterday are single morphemes, while anything further out is built by compounding, and `sini` also supplies the ordinary word for the future. `Dɔɔni dɔɔni` — little by little — is reduplicated, which is the standard Bambara way of making a manner adverb out of a quantity.
locale: bm
