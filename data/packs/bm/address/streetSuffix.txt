description: The thoroughfare word in a Malian address, and unlike the Serbian one it always comes first — Rue Modibo Keïta, Avenue de l'OUA, never the reverse. The list is overwhelmingly French because the address system itself is French, laid down by the colonial administration and kept after 1960 for land titles, the postal service and street signage, so a Bambara calque here would be a word no letter carrier in Bamako has ever read. Cité, lotissement and secteur are the units of a planned Malian subdivision and appear in an address exactly where a street type would. The last four are the Bambara that survives in speech and on hand-painted signs — sira is a road or a path, siraba the big road out of town, kɛnɛ an open square, and dankun the crossroads, which in Bambara is not merely a junction but the place where an offering is set down.
