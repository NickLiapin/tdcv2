description: Surnames of Bosnia and Herzegovina, all three communities, roughly by frequency. THIS FILE IS DRAWN FOR EVERY GENDER AND THERE IS DELIBERATELY NO person/female/lastName.txt. A Bosnian surname in -ić, -ović or -ević is a NOUN, not an adjective, so it does not change form for a woman: Amina Hodžić and Amar Hodžić are both Hodžić — as in Croatian and Serbian, and unlike Polish (Kowalski/Kowalska), Czech (-ová) or Russian (-ova). Do NOT apply the Slavic feminine-suffix rule some tools apply blindly; Hodžićeva and Hodžićova are not Bosnian. What does differ is declension, not form: a man's surname declines through the cases (Hodžića, Hodžiću) while a woman's stays frozen in the nominative in all seven — a data pack cannot express that and a config must not assume it. THE BOSNIAN SIGNATURE IN THIS LIST is the Ottoman rank-and-trade layer: Hodžić (hoca), Softić (softa, a madrasa student), Čaušević (çavuş), Dizdarević (dizdar, a fortress commander), Serdarević, Spahić (sipahi), Kapetanović, Muftić, Hafizović, Imamović, Ćehajić (kethüda), Zaimović, Ajanović (ayan), Delibašić, Buljubašić, Mutevelić — plus the craft names Kujundžić (goldsmith), Terzić (tailor), Kazazović (silk-weaver), Kalajdžić (tinsmith), Saračević (saddler), Ćurčić (furrier), Tufekčić (gunsmith), Berberović (barber) and Bostandžić (gardener). Those trades entered the surname stock here and largely did not in Croatia. Hadžić carries hadž, the pilgrimage. Bošnjak is a CROAT surname of Bosnia, not a Bosniak one — the ethnonym and the surname are different things.
