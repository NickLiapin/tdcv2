description: Music genre. THE FIRST FOUR ARE BOSNIAN AND ONLY BOSNIAN. SEVDALINKA is the urban love song of the Ottoman towns, slow, in a minor mode, sung to saz and later accordion, and it is a UNESCO-listed tradition; the word is from sevdah, itself from Arabic sawda, black bile, by way of Turkish — the melancholy is in the name. ILAHIJA and KASIDA are the sung religious forms of Bosnian Islam. GANGA is the harsh two-part shouted singing of the Herzegovinian and Dalmatian hinterland and sounds like nothing else in Europe. Ravna pjesma and izvorna are the rural forms; NOVOKOMPONOVANA is the manufactured folk of the 1970s and turbofolk its post-war successor, and both are named with the contempt they were given.
