description: Leading element of a Frisian place name. The first thing to know is that the article belongs to the name and is printed on the road sign: It Hearrenfean, It Amelân, It Heidenskip, De Jouwer, De Gordyk, De Westereen, De Knipe - a Frisian town is quite often called 'the' something, and stripping the article leaves a name that does not exist. The second is that most of these come in pairs and the pairs are historical rather than decorative. Where there is a Nij- there is almost always an Ald- a few kilometres off, because the second village was settled out of the first: Nijegea against Aldegea, Nijhoarne against Aldhoarne, Nijemardum against Aldemardum. Boppe and Ûnder work the same way for upstream and downstream of the same peat cut, and Grut and Lyts for a mother village and its offshoot. The compass forms are Noard, Súd, East and West, never the Dutch Noord/Zuid/Oost, and East is spelled exactly as the English word against Dutch oost - one of the many places where Frisian sides with English. Sint- opens the saint's-parish names of the northwest clay, and Ter- is a worn-down 'at the', the same element as in Terherne and Terwispel. None of these names a place on its own; combine it with a toponym.
