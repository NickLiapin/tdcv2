description: Street names as they are signed in Fryslân. Like Dutch, a Frisian street name is ONE fused word that already carries its own thoroughfare element (Tsjerkestrjitte, Molewei, Ljouwerterdyk), so these are complete names and address/streetSuffix.txt lists the element welded onto them rather than something to add with a space; the number follows the name, 'Tsjerkestrjitte 12', never '12 Tsjerkestrjitte'. Three things here are Frisian and not Dutch. Buorren, literally 'the neighbours', is the main street of a Frisian village, and in hundreds of villages that word alone is the whole street name with no suffix at all - there is no Dorpsstraat to translate. The -er and -ster forms are the adjective a Frisian town makes of itself: Ljouwert gives Ljouwerter, Drachten gives Drachtster, Feanwâlden gives Feanwâldster, and a Ljouwerterdyk is not a street named after Ljouwert but the road that goes there, which is why the same name occurs in a dozen villages at once and always points the same way. And the people are the province's own rather than the country's: Gysbert Japicx, who in the 1600s made Frisian a written literary language again when it had been reduced to a spoken one, Waling Dykstra and the brothers Halbertsma who carried it through the 1800s, Obe Postma, Douwe Kalma, Simke Kloosterman, Fedde Schurer, Nynke van Hichtum; Grutte Pier the sixteenth-century Frisian rebel; and Escher and Alma Tadema, both born in Ljouwert. Street names tied to one town belong in a country pack, not here.
locale: fy
