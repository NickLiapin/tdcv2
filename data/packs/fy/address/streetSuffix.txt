description: The thoroughfare element of a Frisian street name, and like the Dutch one it is FUSED onto the name as a single word - Tsjerkestrjitte, Molewei, Ljouwerterdyk - never written apart the way English Street or Spanish Calle is, so this file holds the tail already welded onto address/streetName.txt rather than something to append with a space. The reason a Frisian list is worth keeping beside the Dutch one is that in Fryslân a street can hold two official names at once: the province is bilingual, most of its municipalities have adopted the Frisian place and street names as legally valid alongside the Dutch, and since the 1990s village after village has taken down Kerkstraat and put up Tsjerkestrjitte without either name being wrong. A data set that assumes one street has one name will mis-handle Frisian post. The water words are not ornament in a province that was drained rather than built: a feart is a dug canal, a djip the deep channel of one, a slús a sluice, a dyk a dyke, a wâl and a kade a quay, and a fear the ferry crossing - each of them names real streets in every Frisian village. Three of the land words sit closer to English than to Dutch: ekker is the English acre, fjild the English field, and paad and wei are path and way, while tún is the English word town, which in Frisian never travelled beyond its first sense of an enclosed piece of ground and so still means a garden.
