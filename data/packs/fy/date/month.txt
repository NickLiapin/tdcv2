description: Month name (West Frisian). LOWERCASE, as in Dutch and unlike English and German - capitalising them is the commonest mistake in written Frisian. The forms are the standard calendar ones, and they differ from the Dutch spelling in exactly the places Frisian phonology differs: jannewaris and febrewaris carry the -ewaris ending, maaie has the Frisian -ie diphthong, juny and july are written with y, and September, November and December take i rather than e in the stressed syllable (septimber, novimber, desimber), which is the same vowel shift that gives timmerman and winter. Fryslân also keeps a complete second set of month names, the old agricultural ones, and they are still printed in almanacs and used in place names and titles: foarmoanne, sellemoanne, gersmoanne, blommemoanne, simmermoanne, hoaimoanne (hay month), rispmoanne (harvest month), hjerstmoanne, wynmoanne, slachtmoanne (the month the pig was slaughtered) and wintermoanne. They name the farming year rather than the Roman one, so hoaimoanne is July because that is when the hay comes in, and a dataset that needs them needs a different list from this one.
