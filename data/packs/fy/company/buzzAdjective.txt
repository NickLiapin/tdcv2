description: Business buzz adjective (West Frisian, for catch-phrases), uninflected base form. The productive suffix here is -ber where Dutch writes -baar - skaalber, betrouber, útwreidber, konfigurearber, kontrolearber, trasearber - and it is the single most visible difference between a line of Frisian marketing copy and a Dutch one. Past participles used as adjectives drop the Dutch ge- prefix as well: yntegrearre and automatisearre, not geïntegreerd and geautomatiseerd. Tûk is the ordinary Frisian for clever and does the work Dutch gives to slim; dreaun in datadreaun comes from driuwe, where Dutch has drijven and the v turns to a w or an f right through the language. Meartalich is not padding on this list: Fryslân is the only officially bilingual province in the Netherlands, so multilingual is a claim a local firm genuinely makes about itself. English is borrowed whole wherever the trade borrows it whole, which is why omnichannel, realtime, next-generation and cloud-native stand here unaltered.
locale: fy
