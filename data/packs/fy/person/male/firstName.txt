description: West Frisian male given names, roughly frequency-ordered. Given names are the exact mirror image of surnames in Fryslân: family names were frozen into the civil registry in Dutch spelling in 1811 and have never been respelled, but a given name is chosen fresh by every set of parents, so the forename is the one part of a Frisian record that is genuinely written in Frisian. Hence Sjoerd, Jorrit, Wytse, Djoerd, Tsjeard, Wopke and Ybele, and hence also the Frisian forms of names the two languages share but spell apart - Jehannes for Johannes, Jabik for Jacob, Piter for Pieter, Durk for Dirk, Geart for Gerard, Freark for Frederik, Joast for Joost, Kornelis for Cornelis. The trap for anyone building test data off a Dutch list is at position 33: Anne is a man's name here, and so are Sytse, Hylke, Wybren, Rients, Jelle, Bauke, Auke, Douwe, Wiebe, Gjalt and Fokke, all of which read as feminine to a Dutch or an English eye because Frisian marks the female counterpart with a suffix instead (see person/female/firstName.txt). This list and the female one share no value. The tail from Sem onward is not padding but a fact about naming now: young Frisian parents register Dutch and international names as readily as anyone else in the Netherlands - Sem, Lucas, Noah, Daan, Levi, Finn, Milan - so a cohort born this decade looks markedly less Frisian than one born in 1940, and a dataset that models a living population needs both layers in it. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. The Dutch civil registry publishes given-name counts for the country and not for the province, so the list is ordered by observed commonness in Fryslân and carries the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.8 to 1 from head to tail - a shape that suits a diffuse naming pool but deliberately encodes no claim about Jan's real share.
