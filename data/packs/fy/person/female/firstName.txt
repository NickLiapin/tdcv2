description: West Frisian female given names, roughly frequency-ordered. The shape of this list is a live derivation rather than a coincidence: Frisian forms a woman's name from a man's with the diminutive suffixes -ke, -tsje and -je, so Bauke gives Baukje, Jelle gives Jeltsje, Sjouke gives Sjoukje, Fokke gives Fokje, Sipke gives Sipkje, Lolke gives Lolkje, Eelke gives Eelkje, Klaas gives Klaaske, Sjoerd gives Sjoerdtsje and Ruurd gives Ruerdtsje. The rule is still productive - a speaker can build the pair for a male name that has no attested female counterpart yet - which is why so much of this file looks like person/male/firstName.txt with a suffix bolted on, and why the two lists nevertheless share not one value. The suffix -tsje is the Frisian reflex of what Dutch writes -tje, and it carries the same tsj- cluster that gives tsjerke (church) and tsiis (cheese); -ke attaches to stems that already end in a consonant Frisian will not double up. Which choice a given name takes is lexical and cannot be computed: Doede yields Doetsje, not Doedeke. Note too that the derivation runs one way only, so a name in this file is never also a man's name - Anne, Sytse, Hylke, Rients and Bauke are men in Fryslân and appear only in the male list. Reinou, Rikst, Berber, Elbrich, Gerbrich, Sybrich, Wybrich and Amerins are the older layer that predates the diminutive pattern entirely; the -ich names are the feminine of Germanic compounds in -brand and -bern. The tail from Emma onward is real and not filler: Frisian parents today register Dutch and international names alongside the traditional ones - Emma, Sanne, Julia, Fleur, Noa - so a list drawn only from the head would model a generation now mostly retired. THE WEIGHTS ARE AN ORDERING, NOT MEASURED SHARES. The Dutch civil registry publishes given-name counts nationally and not by province, so the order is observed commonness in Fryslân under the project's default gentle decay, floor(1000000 * rank ** -0.1), which spreads only about 1.7 to 1 from head to tail.
