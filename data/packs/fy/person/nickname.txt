description: Familiar and household forms of Frisian given names. The mechanism a foreign reader will get wrong is the direction of the clipping. Dutch shortens from the back (Johannes to Jan, Elisabeth to Bep), but Frisian very often eats the front of the word instead, so Jehannes gives Hânnes and Jehanna gives Hanne, a habit Frisian shares with English Bess and Beth rather than with Dutch. The productive suffix is -ke, and its palatalised twin -tsje after a front vowel or a dental, which is why the women's forms here cluster so heavily: Aaltsje, Antsje, Hiltsje, Jeltsje, Grietsje, Tetsje, Pytsje, beside Baukje, Foekje, Ymkje, Klaaske, Rinske and Nynke. The suffix is a true diminutive on a noun (hûs gives hûske, faam gives famke) but on a name it is no longer diminutive at all - Jeltsje is not a small Jelte, it is a woman's name in its own right, and a Frisian adult called Baukje is not being addressed as a child. The second thing a config cannot infer is that this file overlaps the first-name file on purpose. Frisian short forms have been accepted as registered given names for generations, so Jelle, Sipke, Douwe, Wytse and Nynke are simultaneously nicknames and legal names, and the pet form is very often the only name a person has - unlike the Dutch pattern in which the registry holds a Latinate Johannes Cornelis and the village says Joop. Note also that Frisian keeps the male short forms in -e and -ke that Dutch lost: Fokke, Doeke, Romke, Sipke, Popke and Lolke are all men.
locale: fy
