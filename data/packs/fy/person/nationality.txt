description: Nationality and demonym in Frisian, adjective form, and every one of them ends in -sk. That suffix is the same one English turned into -ish and German and Dutch into -isch and -s, but Frisian never palatalised it, which is why Frysk sits next to English fresh and Frisian, fisk next to fish, and skiep next to sheep - the unsoftened sk is the single most reliable signal that a word is Frisian and not Dutch. The list opens with Frysk rather than with Nederlânsk, which is a deliberate choice and not a mistake about statehood: Fryslân is a province of the Netherlands and its passports say Nederlânsk, but Frysk is the answer a great many people in the province give first, and it is the same word for the language, the people and the adjective all at once. That single word hides a genuine ambiguity for anyone building datasets. Frisian is not one language but three, and they are not mutually intelligible. West Frisian, the language of this pack, has around 450,000 speakers in Fryslân and is the second official language of the Netherlands. North Frisian is spoken by perhaps ten thousand people on the Schleswig-Holstein coast and the North Sea islands of Germany and is itself split into nine dialects whose speakers struggle with each other. Saterland Frisian, in three villages in Lower Saxony, has around two thousand speakers and is the last surviving remnant of East Frisian - the East Frisians of Ostfriesland, the region that still carries the name, gave the language up centuries ago and speak Low German. So a record marked Frysk is unambiguous only because this is a West Frisian pack.
