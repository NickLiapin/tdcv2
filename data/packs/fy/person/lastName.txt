description: Surnames as they are registered in Fryslân, roughly frequency-ordered, and the endings are the whole story. Something like a third of Frisian family names close in -stra or -ma, a concentration found in no other province of the Netherlands. The -stra ending is toponymic and means roughly 'of the', naming the place or feature the family lived beside - Dijkstra of the dyke, Terpstra of the terp (the man-made dwelling mound that lifted a village clear of the flood in a country with no natural hills), Hoekstra of the corner, Veenstra of the peat, Zandstra of the sand, Woudstra of the wood, Westra of the west, Zijlstra of the sluice; the variant -gra in Algra and Bosgra is the same suffix after a vowel. The -ma ending is older and possessive rather than locative, from an Old Frisian genitive that marked a household, so Bootsma is Boote's people, Ypma is Yp's, Sytsma is Syts's, Jansma is Jan's and Boersma the farmer's. Both cluster here for an administrative reason: Fryslân kept patronymic naming deep into the modern period, and when Napoleon made surnames compulsory in 1811 a Frisian household usually handed the registrar the ancestor-plus-suffix form it was already using, which is also why -inga and -enga (Osinga, Hettinga, Elzinga, Wieringa), -ia (Brunia, Runia, Hania) and -werda (Ferwerda, Strikwerda) are here in numbers. THE SPELLING IS DUTCH, NOT FRISIAN, and that is the trap in this file. Family names were fixed in a Dutch-language civil registry and have never been respelled, so a Frisian family writes Dijkstra and not Dykstra, Sijtsma and not Sytsma, and Bruinsma, Zijlstra, Wijnstra, Kijlstra and Nieuwenhuis keep the Dutch digraph ij that Frisian itself writes as y everywhere else. Given names in this pack are genuinely Frisian, because parents choose them fresh each generation and the registry accepts them; surnames are not, because nobody has chosen them since 1811, and mixing the two conventions is the quickest way to make a Frisian dataset look wrong to a Frisian reader. Names that open with a tussenvoegsel (de, van, van der) follow the Netherlands rule unchanged - alphabetisation ignores the particle, so de Vries files under V and van der Meer under M, and the particle is lowercase after a given name or an initial (Jan de Vries, J. de Vries) but capitalised when the surname stands alone (De Vries wie der al). The values here are stored in the lowercase form, which is correct for '<firstName> <lastName>'. The weights carry the project's default gentle decay and are an ordering, not measured provincial shares.
