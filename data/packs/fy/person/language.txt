description: Language name in Frisian, and the first five entries are all spoken inside one small province. Frysk is the second official language of the Netherlands and the only one of the five with that status. Stedsfrysk, town Frisian, is what is spoken in Ljouwert, Snits, Dokkum, Boalsert, Harns and Frjentsjer, and it is neither Frisian nor Dutch but a hybrid that arose when the towns switched their public life to Dutch in the sixteenth century while keeping Frisian sentence structure underneath: Dutch words on a Frisian frame, which makes it hard to classify and easy to mistake for bad Dutch. Biltsk is older and stranger - Hollandic dikers reclaimed Het Bilt in 1505, married local women, and the language their households produced has been passed down as a distinct variety ever since. Stellingwerfsk, in the southeastern corner of the province, is not Frisian at all but Low Saxon, so the provincial border and the language border do not coincide. The island varieties are not on the list for want of room but are worth knowing about: Skiermûntseagersk and the Aastersk and Westersk of Skylge are separate Frisian dialects, and Hylpersk, the speech of the little town of Hylpen, is different enough that Frisians elsewhere need a moment. Note the shape of every entry: Frisian language names are the -sk nationality adjectives from person/nationality.txt, unchanged, so the language and the people share a word.
