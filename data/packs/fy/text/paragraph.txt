description: Filler paragraphs in West Frisian, three to five sentences each, written out by hand rather than cut from a book, for the reason set out at greater length in text/word: the only Frisian titles the corpus builder could have drawn on are all older than the 1945 spelling reform, and their sentences would have taught this locale a stavering the Fryske Akademy retired eighty years ago. What a single sentence cannot show is here instead. The perfect tense chooses its auxiliary the way English once did and German still does — hawwe for most verbs but wêze for verbs of motion and change, so wy hawwe holpen but wy binne nei hûs gongen — and a Frisian speaker will use the perfect where an English speaker reaches for a simple past. The progressive is oan it plus a verbal noun in -en: hy is oan it meanen, de bern binne oan it boartsjen, mem is oan it bakken. And dat, like om't and doe't, sends the verb to the end of its own clause while the main clause keeps it in second position, which is why so many lines here open on something other than the subject and put the verb straight after it.
