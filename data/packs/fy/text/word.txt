description: Filler words in West Frisian, the lorem-ipsum vocabulary of this pack, compiled by hand. data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs cuts these lists out of public-domain books from Project Gutenberg, and Gutenberg is not short of Frisian ones — the Halbertsma brothers' Rimen en Teltsjes, Simke Kloosterman, Douwe Kalma — but every one of them was set in type before the 1945 spelling reform and so writes fen for fan, hjar for har and ljeaf for leaf, almost entirely without the circumflexes that today tell one word from another; the same script already turns away a pre-1918-orthography Russian book on precisely that ground, that it is not the language the locale promises, and the Frisian case is the same case, so this list was written out in the modern Fryske Akademy standard instead. It is deliberately ordinary — kin, the body, the house, food, land and water, the year, plants, animals, the village, work, abstractions, adjectives, verbs, numerals and function words — so that a random draw reads as Frisian rather than as a glossary of one subject, and wherever Frisian and Dutch part company the Frisian word stands here: sipel not ui, baarch not varken, aai not ei, hynder not paard, ark not gereedschap. Two of them no Dutch speaker would guess at all: heit and mem, father and mother, which have no Dutch cognate to reason from; pake and beppe, grandfather and grandmother, are the pair every Frisian child learns first and the pair Dutch speakers here borrow back. Every verb in the file is an infinitive, and a Frisian infinitive ends in -e or -je — komme, meitsje, wurkje, boartsje, skriuwe — never in the Dutch -en.
