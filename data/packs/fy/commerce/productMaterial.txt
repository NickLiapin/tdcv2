description: Material a product is made of (West Frisian), given as a noun - compose it with fan, as in in tafel fan ikehout, in stoel fan roestfrij stiel. Frisian names a timber by the tree plus hout and joins the two with an e where Dutch uses an n, so it is ikehout and beukehout against eikenhout and beukenhout, and the bare tree name would read as the living tree rather than the sawn wood. Three entries on this list are the province rather than the trade. Reid is reed, and in Fryslân it is a roofing material and not a craft curiosity: the beds cut in De Alde Feanen and along the Snitser Mar have thatched farmhouses here for centuries and still supply the thatchers who keep them. Klaai is sea clay, and it is also a place - the province divides into De Klaai, the reclaimed clay flats of the northwest, and De Wâlden, the sandy wooded east, and a Frisian will tell you unprompted which of the two he is from. Flaaks is flax, grown here for linen long before it was grown for oil. The two entries an English reader will recognise fastest are glês and lear, which sit closer to glass and leather than the Dutch glas and leer do.
locale: fy
