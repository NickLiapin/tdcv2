description: Product noun (West Frisian), as a shop lists it. Frisian nouns are de-words or it-words - it, not the Dutch het - and the article is no more predictable from the shape of the word than it is in Dutch, so a config that needs one cannot derive it from this list. What the list does show, better than a grammar table would, is where the language sits. The kitchen shelf alone gives Tsiis against Dutch kaas and English cheese, Brea against brood and bread, Tsjettel against ketel and kettle, and Ferstelbere kaai, in which the spanner is a kaai - the same word as English key, which Dutch dropped in favour of sleutel. Tsiis and brea are two thirds of the shibboleth every Frisian child learns: bûter, brea en griene tsiis, wa't dat net sizze kin is gjin oprjochte Fries. Three of the food entries are the province's own baking rather than the country's - sûkerbôle, the sugar loaf eaten in slices with butter; dúmkes, the hard thumb-shaped aniseed biscuits; and oranjekoek, the flat almond cake with pink icing that is cut at Frisian birthdays and funerals alike. Bicycle products (Fyts, Bakfyts, Fytstas, Fytsslot, Fytspomp) are an ordinary retail department here and not a niche.
locale: fy
