description: Sport (West Frisian). Fryslân is the only province in the Netherlands with a sporting calendar of its own, and the head of this list is it rather than football. Keatsen is Frisian handball, played three to a partoer on a grass field with a small hard ball and a bare or gloved hand, and the PC in Frjentsjer, held every year since the 1850s, is among the oldest annual sporting fixtures in Europe. Fierljeppen is vaulting a stretch of canal on an aluminium pole up to thirteen metres long, and the vaulter climbs the pole while it is falling, so distance is a function of how fast you can go up it - the Dutch name for the same sport, polsstokverspringen, describes it far less well than the Frisian one, which just means far-leaping. Skûtsjesilen races the flat-bottomed sailing barges that once hauled freight through canals too shallow for a keel; the boats are the originals, one per town, and the fortnight of racing on the Frisian lakes in August is a provincial event rather than a regatta. Reedriden is the everyday word for skating - a reed is a skate - and it is a means of travel here as much as a sport, which is what makes the Alvestêdetocht possible when the ice holds. Frysk damjen is Frisian draughts, played on the same hundred-square board as the international game but with its own capture rules, and it has its own federation. Two Dutch entries are deliberately absent: American football and softbal, which have essentially no presence in the province, and their slots went to skûtsjesilen and Frysk damjen. TRAP inherited from Dutch: bare 'hockey' means FIELD hockey, and ice hockey is always iishockey.
