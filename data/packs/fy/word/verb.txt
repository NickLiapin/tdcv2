description: Common verb (West Frisian), infinitive, lowercase. The Frisian infinitive ends in -e or -je and never in the Dutch -en: sjonge, wurkje, boartsje, skriuwe, keapje, brûke. That one ending is the fastest way to tell a real Frisian word list from Dutch that has merely been respelled, and the -je class is not cosmetic - it is a second conjugation with its own past tense and its own participle. The exceptions are the old monosyllables, which end in a bare -n: dwaan, gean, stean, sjen and jaan, the same small class Dutch keeps as doen, gaan, staan and zien. Two are false friends worth knowing. Rinne is to walk, not to run - Dutch lopen - and a Frisian who actually runs draaft. Harkje is to listen and is the same word as English hark, which Dutch lost entirely. Boartsje, to play, has no Dutch relative either, and farre, to sail, is an ordinary everyday verb in a province of lakes in the way fytse is everyday in a country of bicycles.
locale: fy
