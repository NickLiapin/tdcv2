description: Common noun (West Frisian), lowercase - Frisian follows Dutch here and does not capitalise its nouns, unlike German across the border. The plurals are the least guessable part of the language: many nouns break the vowel between singular and plural, so beam becomes beammen, stien becomes stiennen and foet becomes fuotten, and a config that simply appends -en to the words in this list will be wrong on a good number of them. Bern is child and is the same word as Scots bairn, which neither Dutch nor standard English kept; each, dei and wei are eye, day and way, all three sitting closer to English than to Dutch oog, dag and weg. Plak is place and does not descend from Dutch plaats. Jild loses the Dutch g of geld to a j, the way jaan does to geven. Terp and dyk are on this list as high-frequency ordinary nouns rather than as local colour, because in Fryslân they are simply the words for where a village stands and what keeps the sea off it.
locale: fy
