description: Insect (West Frisian), and the list reads as a short vocabulary test for the Anglo-Frisian relationship. Waps is wasp, where Dutch has wesp with the consonants the other way about; flie is flea; wjirm is worm, so a glow-worm is a glimwjirm and an earwig an earwjirm; and flinter is a butterfly, which is neither the Dutch vlinder nor anything English kept. Two of them separate what Dutch runs together: mich is the ordinary fly and stekmich, sting-fly, is the mosquito, so the Dutch pair vlieg and mug maps onto one Frisian root with a modifier. Sprinkhoanne and bidsprinkhoanne end in hoanne, a cock or rooster, which is what a grasshopper and a mantis were named after.
