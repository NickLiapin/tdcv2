description: Generic drug names spelled the way West Frisian orthography spells them rather than the way the box is printed. Frisian has effectively no c and no x of its own: c becomes k before a, o and u and s before e and i, and x is written ks, which is why paracetamol comes out Parasetamol, diclofenac Diklofenak, amoxicilline Amoksisilline and naproxen Naproksen. A word-initial v turns to f, giving Fenlafaksine and Atorfastatine, and in- becomes yn- in Ynsuline. The final -e of the stem is kept because these are the Dutch-register forms a pharmacy in Ljouwert dispenses against — metformine, not metformin. Asenokoumarol is on the list because acenocoumarol is the vitamin-K antagonist of the Netherlands, where German-speaking practice reaches for phenprocoumon instead.
locale: fy
