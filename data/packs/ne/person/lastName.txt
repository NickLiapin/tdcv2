description: Nepali family names, all seven surname stocks merged into one national draw. THE WEIGHTS ARE CONSTRUCTED, NOT MEASURED, and it is worth saying exactly how, because no surname frequency table exists for Nepal: each name's count is its group's share of the population (from the 2021 census, aggregated as in person/surnameGroup.txt) divided within the group by a rank**-0.5 curve. So the GROUP proportions are real to about a percentage point and the ordering inside a group is judgement; the individual numbers are not a claim that श्रेष्ठ is exactly this much commoner than महर्जन. The point of building it this way rather than on the project's default curve is that a plain draw from this file reproduces Nepal's actual ethnic composition — roughly a third indigenous, a fifth Chhetri, a seventh Bahun, a seventh Dalit, a seventh Madhesi, one in twenty Newar, one in seventeen Muslim — instead of an even spread across pools that no Nepali population has ever had. If you need the surname to agree with an ethnicity column, do not use this file: draw person/surnameGroup.txt and then person/lastNameByGroup/<group>. NEPALI SURNAMES DO NOT INFLECT FOR GENDER — a woman is थापा, श्रेष्ठ and अधिकारी exactly as a man is — so this one list serves both and there is deliberately NO person/female/lastName.txt; its absence is the design. On marriage a woman conventionally takes her husband's surname, which moves her between pools but never changes the form of the name. Two shapes here have no European counterpart: के.सी., जि.सी. and बि.क. are ordinary surnames written as Devanagari initials with full stops and must survive punctuation-stripping, and थापा क्षेत्री is a two-word surname whose second word disambiguates it from थापा मगर.
