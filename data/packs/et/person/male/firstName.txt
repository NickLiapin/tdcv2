description: Estonian male given names, frequency-ordered; the leading figures are bearer counts on the scale the population register publishes and the tail is a smooth decay anchored to the same scale. Two generations are visible in the order. Jaan, Toomas, Andres, Tiit, Mart, Rein and Vello belong to men born roughly 1940-1975; Rasmus, Sander, Oliver, Markus, Robin and Kaspar are the children of Estonia today, and Uku, Kaur, Sten and Tambet are part of a deliberate return to old native names that began in the 1990s. Tõnis, Tõnu and Tõnn carry the letter õ, which does not exist in Finnish, and are the quickest proof in this file that the names are Estonian rather than Finnish. Estonians normally carry ONE given name, unlike Finns, who carry two or three and answer to the first. The block from Aleksander down is the older German- and Scandinavian-derived layer that entered through the Lutheran church registers. Russian given names are NOT here even though about a quarter of the country carries them — see person/lastName for why, and draw from the ru pack for that share.
