description: Common verb (Malay), in the bare root form. MALAY HAS NO CONJUGATION AT ALL — no tense, no person, no number, no gender. 'Saya makan', 'dia makan' and 'mereka makan' use the identical word, and so does yesterday's eating and tomorrow's. TIME IS AN ADVERB, carried by a particle beside the verb and never inside it: sudah or telah for completed, sedang for in progress, akan for future, belum for not yet, pernah for ever-having. So 'dia sudah makan' is he has eaten and 'dia akan makan' is he will eat, with 'makan' unchanged in both. What the affixes DO change is transitivity and voice, not time: from 'ajar' come mengajar (to teach someone), belajar (to study), mengajarkan (to teach a subject), diajar (to be taught) and pelajari (to study a thing). The di- prefix is the passive and is very common in written Malay. A config that needs a past tense adds a word; it never changes this one.
