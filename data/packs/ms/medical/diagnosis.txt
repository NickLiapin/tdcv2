description: Medical diagnosis in Malay that can occur in any patient — no sex or ancestry restriction. Sex-specific conditions live in diagnosisMale and diagnosisFemale, ancestry-linked ones in diagnosisByAncestry. Malay clinical vocabulary differs from Indonesian on the organs themselves, not just the disease names: the kidney is BUAH PINGGANG in Malaysia and ginjal in Indonesia, an infection is a JANGKITAN and not an infeksi, cancer is KANSER (through English) and not kanker (through Dutch), and abstract nouns take -ITI where Indonesian takes -itas, so obesity is obesiti not obesitas. Everyday Malaysians say darah tinggi for hypertension, kencing manis for diabetes and tibi for tuberculosis, and those forms appear on public health posters beside the clinical ones. DEMAM DENGGI is first among the infections for a reason: dengue is endemic in Malaysia, notifiable, and the largest single infectious-disease burden in the country. Thalassaemia is also unusually common here and is screened for.
