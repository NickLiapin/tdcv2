description: Marathi family names, ordered by real prominence in Maharashtra. The one pattern a config cannot infer is the -कर suffix: it is PLACE-DERIVED and still productive, so तेंडुलकर, आंबेडकर, कोल्हापूरकर and कणकवलीकर all literally mean 'the family from Tendulkar / Ambadave / Kolhapur / Kankavli'. Essentially every Maharashtrian town has a real surname of this shape borne by families who left it, which is why the tail of this list is thick with them and why a Marathi-looking name ending in -कर is far more likely to be genuine than one ending in -कर would be in any other Indian language. Marathi surnames do NOT inflect for gender — a woman is जोशी, कुलकर्णी or पाटील exactly as a man is — so this single list serves male and female rows alike and there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt; its absence is the design, not an oversight. This is where Marathi parts company with Polish, whose Kowalski becomes Kowalska. Note also that these are Marathi, not Hindi, even though both are written in Devanagari and no script test can tell them apart: the proof is vocabulary and the letter ळ, the retroflex L that Marathi kept and Hindi does not have at all — कांबळे, साळुंखे, शेळके, सपकाळ, जोगळेकर, गाडगीळ, माडगूळकर, दाभोळकर, ढसाळ, हळदणकर, केळकर, इंगळे, माळी, यवतमाळकर, दळवी, वळवी and वाळके could not be written in Hindi at all. The counts are the project's default gentle curve, and that is the honest choice here: Maharashtra has a genuine long tail of thousands of surnames with nothing approaching the concentration of Vietnamese Nguyen or Korean Kim. The ORDER reflects real prominence; the numbers do not claim to be measured shares.
