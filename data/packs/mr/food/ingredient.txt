description: Kitchen staple, in Marathi. Two entries are Maharashtrian spice blends that a config should not translate away: गोडा मसाला is the dark, coconut-and-stone-flower blend that gives Pune and Deshastha cooking its particular taste, and कांदा-लसूण मसाला is the onion-and-garlic blend of the Kolhapur and Deccan kitchens. The two are not interchangeable and a household usually keeps both. ज्वारी, बाजरी and नाचणी — sorghum, pearl millet and finger millet — head the grain list rather than wheat, because the everyday bread of most of Maharashtra is a millet भाकरी and not a wheat chapati. तांदूळ, डाळ, गूळ, तीळ and लसूण all carry ळ and have no Hindi spelling.
