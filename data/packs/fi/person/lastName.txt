description: Finnish surnames, frequency-ordered, with real bearer counts rather than a rank curve. Two facts about the shape of this list. First, the -nen block: Finnish surnames fall into two historical families, the nature-derived names in -nen (Virtanen from virta, a current; Nieminen from niemi, a headland; Mäkinen from mäki, a hill; Järvinen from järvi, a lake; Koskinen from koski, a rapid) and the older house-and-farm names taken from the holding a family worked (Mäkelä, Heikkilä, Mattila, Ojala, Uusitalo). The -nen block is heavily concentrated at the top — around four fifths of the thirty commonest Finnish surnames end in -nen — and a flat rank curve would hide that. Second, and the opposite of Sweden: Finland has no equivalent of the Swedish -sson dominance. The commonest Finnish surname, Korhonen, is carried by roughly 23 000 people out of 5.6 million, about 0.4 %, and the top ten together cover under 4 % of the country. That flatness at the very top is itself the real distribution and is deliberate here, not a smoothed curve. Counts for the leading forty are bearer figures on the scale the Digital and Population Data Services Agency (DVV) publishes, rounded; the tail is a smooth decay anchored to the same scale. Finnish surnames DO NOT inflect for gender — Virtanen is Virtanen for a woman — so there is no person/female/lastName.txt in this pack and its absence is deliberate. Finnish has no grammatical gender at all, so nothing in a name agrees with the bearer's sex. A Finnish full name carries a single surname. What this list deliberately does NOT contain: Finland-Swedish surnames. About 5.2 % of Finns speak Swedish as their mother tongue, mostly along the south and west coasts and on Åland, and they carry Swedish surnames — Lindström, Nyman, Grönroos, Westerlund, Holmberg, Sundström, Ekholm, Söderholm. Those are real Finnish citizens' names, but they are Swedish-language names, and blending them into a Finnish-language frequency list would mean guessing their proportions. A dataset that needs a realistic bilingual Finland should draw from this pack and the sv pack at roughly a 95/5 split rather than expect this file to cover both.
