description: Finnish male given names, frequency-ordered. Counts for the leading thirty are bearer figures on the scale the Digital and Population Data Services Agency (DVV) publishes for names in FIRST position, rounded; the tail is a smooth decay anchored to the same scale, not the default rank curve. The first-position qualifier matters more here than in most locales. Finns are normally given two or three names and are called by the first (kutsumanimi), while the second is drawn from a small pool of traditional names — Juhani, Johannes, Olavi, Antero, Tapani, Kalevi, Tapio, Ilmari, Petteri, Sakari. Counted in any position, Juhani is the commonest male name in the country with over 300 000 bearers, but almost nobody is called Juhani, so ranking by that figure would produce a country of men answering to a name no one uses. This list ranks by first position, and those traditional names appear further down where they belong as everyday first names. Note also the generational split running through the list: Juha, Timo, Kari and Markku belong to men born roughly 1950-1975, while Onni, Väinö, Eino, Elias and Leevi are the current children's names — old Finnish names that came back into fashion after a century out of it. Aslak at the end is Sámi.
