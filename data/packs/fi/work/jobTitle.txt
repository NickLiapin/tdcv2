description: Finnish job titles across sectors (tech, healthcare, trades, transport, business, service, public). Finnish job titles are written in lower case, and — this is the fact worth recording — NONE OF THEM ARE GENDERED. Finnish has no grammatical gender, so there is no equivalent of chairman versus chairwoman, of Lehrer versus Lehrerin, of nauczyciel versus nauczycielka: opettaja is a teacher, puheenjohtaja a chair, and the word carries no information about who holds the post. The one apparent exception is the block of old compounds ending in -mies (lakimies, palomies, esimies, lautamies): -mies means man, but the compounds are grammatically genderless and have always been used of women too. Finland has nonetheless been replacing them deliberately over the last decade — esimies has largely become esihenkilö in Finnish workplaces and collective agreements, palomies is giving way to pelastaja — and both forms are in this list because both are current. The public-sector titles have no clean English equivalents: a hyvinvointialueen johtaja runs one of the 21 wellbeing services counties created in 2023, a kunnanjohtaja is the appointed chief executive of a municipality rather than an elected mayor, and a kansanedustaja is a member of Parliament.
locale: fi
