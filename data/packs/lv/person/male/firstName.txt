description: Latvian male given name. Latvian writes the given name FIRST and the surname second, and a person carries one of each. Male given names are grammatically male: essentially every one ends in -s, -š or -is, which is a hard rule rather than a tendency — Jānis, Andris, Kārlis, Mārtiņš, Toms. The only entries here without it are the indeclinable foreign loans that Latvian tolerates whole (Ivo, Bruno, Hugo, Oto, Nikita). Because the endings are grammatical, there is NO overlap at all with the female list: a unisex Latvian given name does not exist, which is unlike Croatian Saša or English Jordan. The head of the list is the real order in Latvia's population register — Jānis is by a wide margin the commonest, followed by Andris, Juris and Māris — and the Slavic block (Sergejs, Vladimirs, Dmitrijs, Aleksejs) is here because about a quarter of Latvia's residents are Russian-speaking and their names are entered in the same Latvianised form, with a Latvian -s, as everyone else's. Weights are the pack default rank-decay curve rather than measured shares.
