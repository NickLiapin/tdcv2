description: Bosnian domestic transaction account (transakcijski račun / трансакциони рачун) — the 16 digits a person reads off a card or a payment slip, and the same 16 digits that sit inside bosnia_and_herzegovina.finance.iban after the BA39. The Central Bank's Instrukcija o strukturi računa za obavljanje platnih transakcija fixes the shape as four groups, XXX YYY ZZZZZZZZ KK: three digits for the bank from the catalogue the Centralna banka Bosne i Hercegovine keeps, three for the bank's own organisational unit, eight for the customer account the bank assigns freely, and two control digits by ISO 7064 MOD 97-10 over the fourteen in front of them. Printed, the groups are separated by hyphens — 154-160-20044490-67 is a real Sarajevo account written the way a Bosnian writes it — and this file emits the bare digits, because the hyphens are a display convention and joining on them fails. ONE PLAN FOR THE WHOLE COUNTRY: unlike the courts, the tax administrations, the holidays and the school curricula, domestic payments were never split between the entities. The CBBH runs the giro clearing and the RTGS for the Federation, Republika Srpska and Brčko District alike, so a payment from Banja Luka to Mostar is a domestic transfer with no entity in the routing, and the bank code is the only thing in the number that identifies anybody. Verified against six published Bosnian accounts, each of which satisfies the MOD 97-10 control: Sparkasse Bank d.d. BiH 1990000000000023, Nova banka a.d. Banja Luka 5550000000000018, Općina Tuzla 1321000185060197, NLB Razvojna banka 5620998094682310, Sberbank BH 1401010022091880 and 3387202206245506 at UniCredit Bank d.d. Mostar. The eight customer digits are drawn freely, so an account is correctly formed and correctly checked but is not a real account at a real branch.
