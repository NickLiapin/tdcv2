description: Bosnian fixed-line number in E.164 form — +387 then a two-digit geographic area code and six subscriber digits, written nationally with a trunk zero as 033 123 456. The area codes are pre-war and were never re-cut: 030 Central Bosnia (Travnik, Bugojno, Jajce), 031 Posavina (Orašje), 032 Zenica and Doboj, 033 Sarajevo, 034 Canton 10 (Livno, Tomislavgrad), 035 Tuzla, 036 Mostar and the Neretva, 037 Bihać and the Una, 038 Goražde, 039 West Herzegovina (Široki Brijeg, Ljubuški), 049 Brčko District, 050 Mrkonjić Grad, 051 Banja Luka, 052 Prijedor, 053 Doboj, 054 Šamac, 055 Bijeljina, 056 Zvornik, 057 Pale and Istočno Sarajevo, 058 Foča, 059 Trebinje. Read the 032/053 pair carefully: BOTH cover Doboj. The town sits on the old Zenica-Doboj telephone area and inside Republika Srpska, so the two entities' operators each serve part of the same conurbation from a different code, and 032 and 053 are neighbours on the map rather than a mistake in the list. The same thing happens at 033 and 057, which split Sarajevo between the Federation and Istočno Sarajevo. The codes do line up with the postcodes and with the cantons where a canton exists — 033 Sarajevo against 71xxx, 035 Tuzla against 75xxx, 036 Mostar against 88xxx, 051 Banja Luka against 78xxx — because both grids were drawn in Yugoslavia against one map, which is exactly why neither of them respects the Inter-Entity Boundary Line. Landline numbering is licensed at state level by the Regulatorna agencija za komunikacije; three incumbent operators serve it, the same three that carry the mobile prefixes at bosnia_and_herzegovina.phone. Subscriber length is a uniform six digits, so a Bosnian fixed-line number is +387 plus eight digits with no variation, unlike the mobile plan.
