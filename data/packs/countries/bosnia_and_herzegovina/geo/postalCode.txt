description: Bosnian postal code (poštanski broj) — five digits, and the most entity-neutral thing in the country, for a reason that is worth stating precisely. The numbers are NOT a post-Dayton design: they are the Yugoslav federal plan of 1971 left exactly where it stood. Under that plan the republic held the whole 7xxxx block plus 80xxx, 88xxx and 89xxx; 81xxx to 87xxx went to Montenegro and were never issued here, which is why the two-digit prefixes in use are 70, 71, 72, 73, 74, 75, 76, 77, 78, 79, 80, 88 and 89 and nothing between 81 and 87. The deliberate part is what did NOT happen after 1995: the plan was never re-cut along the Inter-Entity Boundary Line, so a prefix straddles the entities freely. 71xxx is the Sarajevo delivery area and covers Sarajevo, Ilidža and Visoko in the Federation together with Pale, Sokolac and Han Pijesak in Republika Srpska; 76xxx covers Brčko District, Bijeljina in Republika Srpska and Gradačac and Orašje in the Federation; 88xxx covers Mostar and Nevesinje. THREE OPERATORS SHARE ONE NUMBERING SPACE — BH Pošta, Pošte Srpske and Hrvatska pošta Mostar — and their territories do not match the entities either: HP Mostar runs offices in Brčko District (76204, 76210, 76277) and in Jajce, Vitez, Žepče, Livno and Drvar, and BH Pošta runs offices in Mostar (88103, 88110) and in Brčko (76120). Brčko itself has three codes from two operators: 76100 and 76101 Pošte Srpske, 76120 BH Pošta. The first two digits are the delivery area, roughly the old postal centre: 70 Jajce and Bugojno, 71 Sarajevo, 72 Zenica, 73 Goražde and Foča, 74 Doboj, 75 Tuzla, 76 Brčko and Bijeljina, 77 Bihać, 78 Banja Luka, 79 Prijedor, 80 Livno, 88 Mostar, 89 Trebinje. A main office ends 000 or 101 and smaller places do not; the last three digits are drawn freely here, so a code lands in the right block and the right shape but is not looked up against a post office.
