description: Tanzanian VRN — the VAT Registration Number, and unlike Kenya's, IT IS A REAL SECOND IDENTIFIER. A Tanzanian VAT-registered trader carries two numbers side by side on every tax invoice: the nine-digit TIN (tanzania.tax.tin) and this VRN, which is issued separately when the trader crosses the registration threshold (TZS 200 million of taxable turnover in twelve months, or TZS 100 million in six). Anyone porting the Kenyan model across the border will look for the PIN to double as the VAT number and will be wrong; anyone porting the EU model will look for a country-prefixed number and will also be wrong. The form is eight digits and a trailing letter, printed on the certificate and on invoices in three groups — 10-005094-G — and printed on EFD fiscal receipts with the dashes stripped, 40314492Y. This file emits the dashed certificate form. THE TRAILING LETTER IS A CHECK CHARACTER AND THIS FILE DOES NOT COMPUTE IT; it is drawn uniformly from A-Z, so a VRN generated here will not pass TRA's own validation. That is a stated refusal, not an oversight: only five real VRNs could be traced to published sources — 10-005094-G, 10-000075-T, 10-018950-J, 40-014680-H, 40-314492-Y — and five samples cannot pin down a rule with eight digit positions in it. Five points would fit any nine-parameter model, so a "verified" letter derived from them would be an artefact, and TRA publishes no algorithm. Contrast tanzania.tax.tin in this same directory, where 718 published numbers WERE enough and the check digit is computed for real; the difference between the two files is the difference between having evidence and wanting it. The two-digit lead is drawn from the two blocks actually observed, 10 and 40, rather than from 00-99, because nothing published says the other 98 are in use. Tanzania's standard VAT rate is 18%. Zanzibar administers its own VAT separately under the Zanzibar Revenue Authority for supplies consumed in Zanzibar, but the VRN format is the same union-wide.
