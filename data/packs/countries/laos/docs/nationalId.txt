Laos publishes no checksum specification for this number: not in the law on identity cards, not by the ministry that issues it, and there is NO ENTRY FOR LAOS IN python-stdnum, the usual home for national identifier algorithms — which carries modules for Thailand and Vietnam, the countries on either side of it, and none for LA. Validation in Laos is a lookup against the civil register, not arithmetic. A reverse-engineered mod-11 rule would make this file LOOK more rigorous while quietly rejecting real cards and accepting fake ones, which is worse than no checksum because it looks verified. THE LENGTH IS TWELVE DIGITS and no published source is unanimous about it, so if your data has to match a specific Lao system, check its column width first. WHAT THE NUMBER IS NOT. It is not derived from a date of birth, a sex or a province the way a PESEL, a Thai national ID or a codice fiscale is, so it cannot be joined to an age or a gender column and it will not locate the holder — nothing in this pack derives one field from another through it. It is also not the only identity number a Lao person has: civil registration assigns a unique identification number at BIRTH REGISTRATION and the identity card is drawn against that number later, so a Lao record system may hold the birth-registration number, the card number, or both, and a schema that stores one and calls it both has merged two fields. The household is registered separately again, in the ປຶ້ມສຳມະໂນຄົວ at laos.docs.familyBook. DIGITS ONLY, LEADING ZERO POSSIBLE, so store as text. Laos began issuing chip cards in 2015 and started a nationwide digital-ID rollout in late 2025, which is when the card, the household record and the birth registration finally converge on one identifier; a data set from before that is not guaranteed to be internally consistent about which number it holds. This is protected personal data and no real citizen's number appears anywhere in this pack.
