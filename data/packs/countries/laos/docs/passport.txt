address: laos.docs.passport
description: A Lao passport number — one Latin letter followed by seven digits, e.g. P1234567. Latin, not Lao, and that is the point worth knowing: the passport is an ICAO 9303 travel document with a machine-readable zone, so the one document a Lao citizen carries whose number is written in the Latin alphabet is this one, while the identity card, the family book and the vehicle registration are all in Lao script. THE PATTERN IS NOT PUBLISHED. Neither the Ministry of Foreign Affairs nor the Department of Immigration publishes the composition of the number, and no document-verification reference gives it, so the shape here is the ICAO ordinary-passport convention of a class letter plus seven digits in a nine-column MRZ field, not a Lao specification, and this description says so rather than dressing a guess up as a fact. NO CHECK DIGIT EXISTS INSIDE THE NUMBER. The check digits people associate with a passport are the ICAO 9303 7-3-1 weighted digits computed over the machine-readable ZONE — over the document-number field, the date fields and the composite — and they are a property of the PRINTED MRZ, not of the number. Those are computed for real at laos.docs.passportMrz, which is the only arithmetic in this pack that can be verified against a published specification. The passport number is not derived from and does not contain the identity-card number at laos.docs.nationalId. Laos has issued biometric e-passports since 2016; the earlier books are expired and only the current form is generated. The nationality code on a Lao travel document is LAO — three letters, and it is not LA (that is the two-letter ISO 3166-1 code) and not LAP or LAS.
