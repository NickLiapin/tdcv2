description: The currency of Laos: the ກີບ (kip, ISO 4217 LAK, sign ₭ at U+20AD). ONE CIRCULATING CURRENCY, WHICH IS WHERE LAOS DIFFERS FROM ITS NEIGHBOUR. Cambodia runs on riel and US dollars together and a Cambodian money column needs a currency flag in every row; Laos does not work that way — the kip is the money of account, prices are quoted in kip, and while Thai baht and US dollars circulate in the border towns and in tourism, they are foreign currency rather than a second legal tender, and the government has repeatedly moved to stop domestic pricing in them. So a Lao amount without a currency flag is a kip amount, and that is a safe default in Laos where it is a bug in Cambodia. THE ອັດ IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN THIS LIST. ISO 4217 gives LAK two minor units and the ອັດ (att) is nominally a hundredth of a kip, but the att has not circulated for decades, the smallest note in issue is 1,000₭ and the largest 100,000₭, so a kip amount with a fractional part is a conversion artefact. CLDR agrees and gives LAK ZERO fraction digits. An address that emitted the att one draw in two would make a dead unit look like half of Lao money. WHAT THE MAGNITUDE MEANS: the kip has lost a great deal of value — it ran near 8,000 to the US dollar for years, then depreciated sharply from 2022 and now trades in five figures — so a Lao price is a big number, an ordinary monthly salary is seven digits, and a kip column stored in a 32-bit integer will overflow on a house. Store kip amounts in a wide integer type and never in a float. The kip dates from 1953 in name and from 1979 in its present form; the Pathet Lao issued a separate currency in the zones it held before 1975 and the two were merged at a punitive rate, so Lao personal financial records do not run continuously across that year. See laos.finance.amountKip for how a kip figure is printed, which is not how an English-speaking system would print it.
