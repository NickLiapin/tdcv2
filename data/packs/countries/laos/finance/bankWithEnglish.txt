description: The paired table behind laos.finance.bank and laos.finance.bankEnglish — the bank's Lao name, a vertical bar, then its Latin trading name. Kept as one file so a bank can never drift away from its own English name. The list is the register of licensed commercial banks as the ທະນາຄານແຫ່ງ ສປປ ລາວ (Bank of the Lao P.D.R.) itself publishes it, in the regulator's own groups: the one state-owned commercial bank (ທະນາຄານ ການຄ້າຕ່າງປະເທດລາວ ມະຫາຊົນ, BCEL, which is where most Lao salaries land), the specialised banks, the joint state-owned banks that pair the Lao state with a Vietnamese, French or Chinese partner, the private banks, the subsidiaries of foreign banks, and the branches of foreign banks. THE SHAPE OF LAO BANKING IS IN THAT ORDERING and it is unlike a European register: of 36 licensed banks, more than half are foreign — six Thai, six Vietnamese, three Chinese or Taiwanese, one Malaysian, one Cambodian, one Korean, one Japanese — because Laos is landlocked between five much larger economies and its banking follows its trade. Only about eight of these are banks a Lao household would hold an account with. LAOS HAS NO IBAN, so if you are looking for one, stop: laos.finance.bank says why at length. The Lao names keep the legal-form suffix ຈຳກັດ (limited), ມະຫາຊົນ (public) or ຈຳກັດຜູ້ດຽວ (sole limited) because the regulator prints it and because it distinguishes two otherwise similar entries; those words are ordinary Lao commercial vocabulary and would belong in the language pack if this file were about language. NOTE THE SPACES. Lao does not put spaces between words, but almost every one of these names has them, because a space in Lao marks a phrase boundary and a transliterated foreign name is a phrase — ທະນາຄານ ມາຣູຮານ ເຈແປນລາວ ຈຳກັດ is "Maruhan Japan Lao" spelled out phonetically with a space around each borrowed word. That makes bank names the one Lao list in this pack with internal spaces, the opposite of the problem the geography files have.
