description: North Macedonian vehicle plate in the current format — a two-letter registration-area code, the national emblem, four digits and two letters, written SK 1234 AB. The emblem has no character, so this generator writes a space, as the Serbian, Croatian and Slovenian packs do for theirs. THE BLUE BAND SAYS NMK, NOT MK, AND THAT IS THE MOST VISIBLE CONSEQUENCE OF THE PRESPA AGREEMENT ON ANY EVERYDAY OBJECT IN THIS COUNTRY. The Ministry of the Interior began issuing plates with the international code NMK on Monday 17 June 2019, four months after the constitutional name change took effect on 12 February 2019; before that the band read MK. Older MK plates stay valid until the vehicle is re-registered, so both are on the road, and NMK is the only one being issued — which means a dataset keyed on the two-letter MK for Macedonian vehicles is reading a code the country stopped using and that is now, on a plate, the area code of Македонска Каменица instead. NMK is not an ISO code: ISO 3166 still assigns MK and MKD, and the vehicle code is a separate UNECE registration, so the country genuinely has both and neither replaced the other. The plate carries BOTH SCRIPTS at once: the area code is stamped in Latin beside the emblem and repeated in Cyrillic in a small rectangular field, together with the two-letter series, so SK 1234 AB is also СК 1234 АБ on the same piece of metal. This generator writes the Latin, which is what is read off the mark. The four digits are drawn 1000-9999 rather than 0000-9999 because issued marks do not begin with a zero. No excluded-letter list for the two-letter series could be sourced — Serbia dropped seven letters from its plate alphabet in 2017 and no equivalent Macedonian rule was findable — so the full Latin A-Z is used and that is stated here rather than a plausible-looking exclusion being invented. Diplomatic, military, temporary and personalised series are out of scope.
