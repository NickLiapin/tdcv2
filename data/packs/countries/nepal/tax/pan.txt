description: PAN — the Permanent Account Number issued by Nepal's Inland Revenue Department (आन्तरिक राजस्व विभाग), NINE DIGITS, purely numeric, and the only tax number this country has. Do not read it as India's PAN: the Indian one is ten characters shaped AAAAA9999A and encodes a holder type and a surname initial, the Nepali one is nine bare digits and encodes nothing that has been published. ONE NUMBER COVERS EVERYTHING, which is the thing to know about Nepali tax data. The same 9-digit PAN is the personal income-tax number, the company's tax number, the withholding (TDS) number and — see nepal.tax.vat — the VAT registration number. Nepal issues no separate VAT identifier and no letter-prefixed EU-style number, so a Nepali record needs one tax column and not two, and a validator written for the shape "two country letters plus digits" will reject every genuine Nepali registration. NO PUBLISHED CHECK DIGIT, and this pack refuses to invent one. One commercial ID-validation vendor asserts that the ninth digit is a check digit; it publishes no algorithm, the Inland Revenue Department publishes neither an algorithm nor a claim that a check digit exists, and no worked example could be found to test either way. So the nine digits here are drawn uniformly with a non-zero leader, and the honest statement is that a Nepali PAN cannot be validated offline: IRD's own verification is a database lookup at its public PAN-search service, which returns the registered name for a number, and that is the only check there is. Fabricating a mod-11 would make every real PAN in a test set fail while looking rigorous. Also not modelled: which nine-digit blocks IRD has actually allotted, and the distinction between a PAN issued to a person and one issued to a company — they come from the same series and cannot be told apart by shape, which is itself a fact about Nepali tax data worth knowing.
