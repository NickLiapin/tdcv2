address: nepal.phone
description: A Nepali mobile number in E.164 form — +977 then TEN digits, the first of which is always 9. Nepal Telecommunications Authority's numbering plan reads the second and third digits together as the operator: 984, 985 and 986 are Nepal Telecom's GSM blocks (the state operator, and the only one whose signal reaches most of the mountains); 980, 981 and 982 are Ncell's; 974 and 975 are Nepal Telecom's CDMA, which is why a Nepali number beginning 97 is a different technology rather than a different city. The second digit is the technology marker in the original plan — 8 for GSM, 7 for CDMA, 6 for the newer entrants — and it is one of the few numbering plans in the world that encodes that. TWO OPERATORS, NOT FIVE, which is what an older dataset will show: Smart Telecom (961, 962, 988), Hello Mobile (963) and UTL (972) all lost or surrendered their licences, so those prefixes are no longer issued and a number carrying one is a dead or ported number rather than a current subscriber. They are excluded here. Written nationally the number is 98XXXXXXXX with no leading zero — Nepal has no trunk prefix on mobile numbers, so a Nepali mobile is ten digits flat and a dataset that stores it as "098..." has added a digit that does not exist. Landlines are a different shape entirely and are generated separately as nepal.phoneLandline. The mobile number matters beyond telephony in Nepal for the same reason it does across South Asia: it is the handle for eSewa and Khalti, the wallets through which a large share of retail payment moves, so in a Nepali payment record the phone column and the wallet column are frequently the same value.
