address: nepal.vehicle.plate
description: A Nepali embossed number plate, e.g. बागमती प्रदेश B CH 1234. IT IS WRITTEN IN TWO SCRIPTS AT ONCE and that is the thing datasets get wrong about this country: the province is printed in NEPALI (Devanagari) and everything after it — the class letter, the two series letters, the four digits — in LATIN, in the German FE-Schrift font, black on white with a left-hand strip carrying the Nepali flag and the blue letters NEP. So a plate string is genuinely mixed-script, a length check in bytes will be roughly three times the length in characters for the Nepali half and one for the rest, and code that assumes a plate is ASCII will truncate the province off the front. The Department of Transport Management's own statement of the composition is तीनवटा अक्षर तथा चार वटा अङ्क — three letters and four digits — which is the class letter from nepal.vehicle.vehicleClass plus a two-letter series plus a 0001-9999 running number; the province is the fourth element and is a plate legend rather than part of that serial. Series letters advance when a number range fills, so the letter pairs in the real fleet are not the even spread drawn here. THE PROVINCE FIELD IS THE ONE PART OF THIS FILE THAT RESTS ON A SECONDARY SOURCE: DoTM has not published the plate specification in a form that could be verified, and the claim that the province appears as its Nepali NAME comes from Nepali press reporting on the rollout ("the province name in Nepali, the rest in English"), which is consistent with the photographs and with the Supreme Court case that held the rollout up — the 2017-2019 injunction was won on the argument that dropping Devanagari from the plate was unconstitutional, and the plate that finally issued in July 2020 kept Devanagari for the province. Several secondary sources instead give a single-letter province code (का, ख, ग …) and are not corroborated by any government source; they are not followed here. The full official Nepali name of a province includes the word प्रदेश (कोशी प्रदेश, बागमती प्रदेश) and it is printed that way here; nepal.geo.province holds the bare name, so a config wanting it without the word can use that address directly. WHAT THIS PLATE REPLACED, and it matters because both are on the road: until 2020 the plate carried a ZONE, and the zones were abolished in 2015 — see nepal.vehicle.zonalPlate for the old form and nepal.vehicle.zoneCode for the fourteen codes. Nothing on a Nepali plate is a check digit and nothing verifies anything else: the plate is an allocation, and validating one means asking DoTM, which is what the RFID chip embedded in the new plate is for.
