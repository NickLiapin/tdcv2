address: nepal.docs.citizenshipNumber
description: नागरिकता नम्बर — the number on the Nepali citizenship certificate (नागरिकता प्रमाणपत्र), which until the National ID arrived was THE identity document of Nepal and which still is for most practical purposes: a bank account, a passport, a SIM, a land transfer, a driving licence, a government job and the vote all require it, and it is what the NID is being issued against. IT HAS NO NATIONAL FORMAT, AND THAT IS THE FACT WORTH SHIPPING. The certificate is issued by the District Administration Office of the holder's home district — 77 of them — and each office runs its own register and its own numbering; there is no central database of citizenship certificates and no national serial. So the number cannot be validated by shape, there is no check digit to compute, and confirming a certificate means writing to the issuing DAO. That is also why a Nepali record NEVER carries the citizenship number alone: it always carries the number AND the issuing district AND usually the issue date, because the three together are what identifies the certificate and the number by itself does not. This address emits the shape most commonly printed — a district-office segment, a ward or sub-office segment, a two-digit Bikram Sambat issue year and a running serial, hyphen-separated, e.g. 27-01-72-04831 — and says plainly that the segment WIDTHS and the ranges vary between districts and between decades, so this is a plausible Nepali citizenship number and not a claim about any particular district's register. A config that needs a coherent record should draw the district from nepal.geo.district and print it beside this number rather than expect the digits to encode it. Note the BS year: the issue year on a citizenship certificate is a Bikram Sambat year, so a certificate issued in 2015 CE reads 72, and a two-digit year in a Nepali document is 56 or 57 behind the Western one rather than ahead of it. Deliberately absent from this pack: the PASSPORT number. Nepal moved to ICAO e-passports in 2021 and the Department of Passports has published no specification of the number's composition that could be verified, so no passport address is offered rather than a guessed one.
