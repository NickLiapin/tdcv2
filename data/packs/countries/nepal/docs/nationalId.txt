description: NIN — the राष्ट्रिय परिचय नम्बर, the National Identity Number carried on Nepal's राष्ट्रिय परिचयपत्र smart card, issued by the Department of National ID and Civil Registration (DoNIDCR) under the National Identity Card and Civil Registration Act 2076. TEN DIGITS. That length is the department's own — its published FAQ says १० अंकको, ten digits, non-repeating and unique — and it is worth stating because secondary sources circulate 11-digit and 16-digit claims; the 16-digit number is the ENROLMENT APPLICATION number, which is a different thing that a person also gets and which is not this. THE NUMBER HAS NO PUBLISHED STRUCTURE AND NO CHECK DIGIT, and this pack will not invent either. DoNIDCR publishes that the NIN is unique and that it does NOT change when a cardholder corrects a name, an address or a date on the card; it publishes nothing about what any digit means and nothing resembling a checksum. So the ten digits here are drawn uniformly, with the first constrained away from zero so that a numeric column cannot silently eat a leading digit. Making up a mod-11 for it would be worse than useless: every real NIN a test set was checked against would fail the invented rule, and the rule would look verified because it was arithmetic. Verifying a Nepali NIN offline is not possible — it means asking DoNIDCR. What the number is FOR is the interesting part, and it is very new: the NID is intended to become Nepal's single identity key, replacing the citizenship certificate as the thing a bank, a SIM registration, a passport application and a social-security payment key on, and since 2024-25 a passport application can be made with a NIN alone. Enrolment is not complete, so a Nepali dataset drawn from life will contain people with a NIN and people without, and a config that gives every generated person one is describing a country a few years ahead of the real one. Children have NINs too, issued alongside the birth registration. Not modelled: the card's ICAO machine-readable zone, which does carry real check digits — that is a generic ID-1 document feature and lives in the common pack under common.docs.mrz, not here.
