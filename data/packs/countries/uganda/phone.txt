address: uganda.phone
description: Ugandan mobile number in E.164 form — +256 followed by nine digits, of which the first three are the operator prefix. Written nationally the same number starts 0 instead of +256, so +256772123456 is dialled 0772123456; the leading 0 is a trunk prefix and you write one or the other, never both. The prefixes here are the real Uganda Communications Commission allocation and not a range: MTN Uganda holds 076x, 077x, 078x and 079x (079 granted in March 2025 because 077 and 078 were exhausted for new customers); Airtel Uganda holds 070x, 074x and 075x; Lycamobile 072x; the former Uganda Telecom 071x; Africell 073x. Drawing uniformly across these hundred prefixes puts 40% of numbers on MTN and 30% on Airtel, which is roughly their real subscriber split, and that ratio matters more here than in most countries because a Ugandan phone number is also a payment address: the number IS the MoMo or Airtel Money wallet, and the network prefix decides which one. Two consequences for generated data. A record pairing a 077 number with an Airtel Money transaction is internally impossible. And the whole population is SIM-registered against a National Identification Number under the 2018 mandatory registration rules, so a Ugandan phone number and a Ugandan NIN are linked in the real registry in a way that makes a phone number a weaker pseudonym than it looks. Landline numbering is in uganda.phoneLandline.
