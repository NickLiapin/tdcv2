address: uganda.vehicle.plate
description: Ugandan civilian number plate — U, two more letters, a space, three digits and a suffix letter, written UAA 123A. THE LETTERS CARRY NO REGIONAL MEANING, which is the opposite of what a reader coming from Poland, Germany or Spain will assume, and it is also not what several travel sites claim: registration is national, run by URA, and the letters advance in ISSUE ORDER. A plate tells you roughly WHEN a vehicle was registered and nothing at all about WHERE, or about the owner. The sequence is easy to state and easy to get wrong. Within a three-letter block the numbers run 001 to 999 with suffix A, then start again at 001 with suffix B, and so on through the suffix alphabet; when the suffixes are exhausted the third letter advances — UAA, then UAB, on to UAZ, then the second letter rolls and UBA opens. I and O are skipped throughout, in the letter block and in the suffix, because they read as 1 and 0, which is why this file draws from a 24-letter alphabet rather than 26. Fixing the first letter at U is not a simplification: every civilian plate carries it. The second letter is restricted to A and B on purpose — the UA blocks ran from the mid-1990s and are fully issued, the UB blocks have been in issue since, and those are the plates on Ugandan roads. HOW FAR INTO UB THE SEQUENCE HAS RUN COULD NOT BE SOURCED, so unlike kenya.vehicle.plate — which restricts its second letter to the four blocks Kenya has actually reached — this file draws the third letter across the whole alphabet and will therefore emit some UB combinations that have not been issued yet. That is a stated limitation rather than a silent one. Special series are outside this format and are not generated: UG followed by a two-digit ministry code for central government, UP for police, UU for prisons, dark-green plates for the UPDF, red for MDAs and international organisations, and numbered CD plates for diplomatic missions. There is NO CHECK CHARACTER anywhere in a Ugandan plate. Since November 2023 Uganda has been replacing plates with digital ones carrying an embedded tracking chip under the Intelligent Transport Monitoring System — government vehicles first, then motorcycles in November 2024, then imports and private cars from January 2025, with full compliance required by January 2027 — but the digital plate keeps this same alphanumeric format, so the string modelled here is unchanged by it.
