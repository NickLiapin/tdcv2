description: A Ugandan bank account number — commonly 13 digits, with some banks running 10 to 16. STRUCTURAL ONLY, for two separate reasons that are both worth stating. First, UGANDA HAS NO IBAN. It is not in the IBAN registry, has never adopted the scheme, and there is no UG.. account string to compute a MOD-97 over; anyone reaching for uganda.finance.iban is looking for something that does not exist, and this pack deliberately does not ship one. An inbound international transfer to Uganda is addressed by SWIFT/BIC plus account number — Stanbic is SBICUGKX, Centenary CERBUGKA, dfcu DFCUUGKA, Absa BARCUGKX, Equity EQBLUGKA — and the remittance forms that show an "IBAN" field for Uganda are showing a field that cannot be filled. This is the same answer Kenya gives (see kenya.finance.account), and it is the normal answer across East Africa: no EAC member is in the IBAN registry. Second, there is no national account-number standard. Each bank sets its own length and its own internal check rule and publishes neither, so nothing generated here can be validated against anything, and a generated number is well-formed rather than plausible-to-a-bank. Balances are in Ugandan shillings (UGX). The shilling has no subdivision in practical use — cents were abandoned long ago and the smallest circulating coin is 50 shillings — so a Ugandan money column with two decimal places is modelling a currency Uganda does not have. Note also that for most Ugandans the primary money account is not this one: it is a mobile-money wallet addressed by phone number (see uganda.finance.paymentMethod).
