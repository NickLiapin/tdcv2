address: uganda.docs.nin
description: Ugandan NIN — the National Identification Number issued by NIRA, 14 characters, and NOT 14 digits. This is the thing most models of Uganda get wrong: half the published descriptions call it "a unique 14-digit code", and it is nothing of the sort. It is a fixed alphanumeric shape — two letters, two digits, six digits, four alphanumerics — and the first four characters are not opaque, they are a statement about the person. C is citizen; M or F is sex; the two digits after that are the LAST TWO DIGITS OF THE YEAR OF BIRTH. CM97xxxxxxxxxx is a male citizen born in 1997, CF91xxxxxxxxxx a female citizen born in 1991. So a Ugandan NIN discloses sex and birth year on its face to anyone who reads it, with no lookup and no database — a property it shares with the Polish PESEL and the South African ID and does NOT share with its immediate neighbour, since the Kenyan national ID is a plain 8-digit serial that reveals nothing at all (see kenya.docs.nationalId). Two consequences follow that matter when generating test data: the sex letter and the year digits must AGREE with any date of birth and sex carried in the same record or the record is internally impossible, and a NIN cannot be treated as a pseudonym, because it is a partial disclosure. This generator therefore draws a real birth date and slices its year, rather than drawing two digits at random. THERE IS NO CHECK CHARACTER. The last four characters are described by every source that describes them at all as carrying no meaning and existing only to widen the permutation space; NIRA has published no encoding scheme and no validation algorithm, and the identity-verification vendors that integrate with the NIRA API state plainly that none is publicly documented. So one changed character produces another well-formed NIN, and a NIN can only be validated by querying NIRA — there is nothing in the string that verifies the rest of it. This file computes nothing on purpose. The two-digit year is genuinely ambiguous about century (a 1997 and a 2097 birth would both encode 97); nothing in the number resolves it, and the range drawn here, 1940-2009, is people who are adults today, since registration is from age 16. Non-citizens are out of scope here: lawfully resident foreigners get an Alien Identification Number in the same 14-character shape but beginning A rather than C, and refugees are registered separately again; only the citizen form is generated. The card also carries a separate 9-digit card number, which is not the NIN and is not modelled. Historical note for anyone comparing datasets: mass enrolment ran from 2014, cards issued then began expiring in 2024, and the renewal exercise from late 2024 re-issued cards while KEEPING the NIN — the NIN is for life, the card is not.
