address: uganda.geo.district
description: The 135 districts of Uganda, grouped by region and alphabetical within each, index-aligned with uganda.geo.districtRegion. THE DISTRICT IS UGANDA'S FIRST-LEVEL DIVISION AND THE ONLY ONE THAT IS A TIER OF GOVERNMENT — each has an elected chairperson and a district council, and the four regions above them do not (see uganda.geo.region). AS OF THE 9 MAY 2024 CENSUS; put a date on any Ugandan district list, because the count moves. Uganda had 39 districts in 1990, 56 in 2002, 80 in 2005 and 112 in 2010; Parliament authorised 23 more in September 2015, phased in one financial year at a time through July 2020, and further districts have been approved for 2025/26 (Mukuju, Mulanda and Kisoko out of Tororo, Bughendera out of Bundibugyo). A list five years old is short by a dozen, and a list ten years old is short by twenty. The count that gets quoted, 146, is NOT 146 districts: at the 2024 census Uganda was divided into 135 districts AND 11 cities, and the cities are separate local governments at the same tier, not sub-units of the districts they sit in. They are held apart in uganda.geo.charteredCity. Note that a city and its district can both exist and both be populated — Mbale District and Mbale City are two governments, and Jinja District and Jinja City likewise — so a record joining a district to a city must not assume the city's population is inside the district's. Kampala is the exception on the other side: it has no district, only Kampala Capital City Authority. Spellings follow the census: Luwero not Luweero for the district (the TOWN is Luweero), Ssembabule with two s, Kassanda with two s, Madi-Okollo hyphenated.
