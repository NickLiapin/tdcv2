description: A Ugandan post office box, which is what a Ugandan postal address actually is. THIS FILE EXISTS BECAUSE uganda.geo.postalCode DOES NOT, AND THAT IS NOT AN OVERSIGHT. UGANDA HAS NO POSTAL CODE IN GENERAL USE. There is no five-digit code, no alphanumeric code, no code at all that a Ugandan writes on a letter or that a Ugandan form asks for; the address-validation vendors that publish a "Uganda postcode format" are describing an aspiration, not a country. The aspiration is documented: the Ministry of ICT's National Postcode and Addressing System Policy, still a draft in February 2019, exists precisely because Uganda had no such system, and the only implementation it could point to was a PILOT confined to Entebbe Municipality, run as a learning exercise before a national rollout that has not happened. So a generator that emits a Ugandan postcode is emitting a number that no post office reads and no resident could recognise, which is worse than emitting nothing — and this pack emits nothing. What mail is addressed to instead is a box: "P.O. Box 7062, Kampala", the box rented at a named post office, with the town doing the work the postcode does elsewhere. Posta Uganda runs over 300 offices; box numbers are per-office and low, so 7062 in Kampala and 7062 in Mbarara are different boxes at different post offices and the town is not decoration. Physical delivery to a street address is rare outside central Kampala, and where there is no box a Ugandan address falls back on a plot number, a road and a landmark — "Plot 12, Nakasero Road, opposite the Golf Course" is a normal, deliverable Ugandan address. The value is emitted with its "P.O. Box" prefix rather than as a bare number on purpose: a bare 7062 in a postcode-shaped column is exactly the mistake this file is here to prevent. The range drawn is the real one — Ugandan box numbers run from 1 into the low tens of thousands, not into six figures.
generator: tdc
locale: en
