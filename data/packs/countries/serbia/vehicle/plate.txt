address: serbia.vehicle.plate
description: Serbian vehicle plate, the format in force since 1 January 2011 — a two-letter registration-area code, the Serbian cross shield, three or four digits, a dash and two letters (BG 123-AB). The shield has no character, so this generator writes a space, as the Croatian and Slovenian packs do for their emblems. LATIN, in a pack that is otherwise Cyrillic, and this is the one place where Serbia's digraphia is visible on a single physical object: the area code is stamped in Latin beside the shield and REPEATED in Cyrillic in small type beneath it, while the two letters of the registration mark itself are Latin only. So a plate carries both scripts at once and the generator can only carry one of them; it carries the Latin, because that is the string a camera, a database or a person reads off the mark. The permitted letters in the mark are the 23 that survived 1 August 2017: A B C D E F G H I J K L M N O P R S T U V X Z. Č, Ć, Đ, Š and Ž were dropped that day, as were Y and W, which had been admitted earlier; Q was never in the Serbian Latin alphabet at all. Diacritics remain in the AREA codes, which were fixed before the change and were not revised — ČA Čačak, ĆU Ćuprija, KŠ Kruševac, KŽ Knjaževac, PŽ Požega, ŠA Šabac, ŠI Šid, VŠ Vršac, BČ Bečej, BĆ Bogatić. That is the same split Croatia does NOT make: Croatia allows diacritics in the area code and its plates are Latin throughout, with no Cyrillic anywhere. All 74 registration areas outside Kosovo and Metohija are here. The seven Kosovo codes — ĐA Đakovica, GL Gnjilane, KM Kosovska Mitrovica, PE Peć, PR Priština, PZ Prizren, UR Uroševac — are not drawn: Serbia's ministry does issue plates bearing them and Kosovo's authorities issue RKS plates over the same territory, the two have been the subject of a formal dispute, and this pack describes rather than adjudicates. Personalised plates and the separate series for diplomatic, military and temporary registration are out of scope.
