description: Serbian postal code (поштански број) — FIVE digits, and the reason Serbia has five where Slovenia has four is the same reason Slovenia has four. Both came out of the Yugoslav federal plan of 1971, in which the leading digit was the republic: 1 Serbia proper, 2 Vojvodina, 3 the rest of central Serbia and Kosovo, 4 Croatia, 5 Croatia's coast, 6 Slovenia, 7 Bosnia, 8 Montenegro, 9 Macedonia. Slovenia sat entirely inside the 6xxxx block, so at independence it simply dropped the leading 6 and shortened to four digits. Serbia sat across 11000 to 38xxx and could drop nothing, so it kept the federal numbers unchanged — 11000 Београд, 21000 Нови Сад, 18000 Ниш, 34000 Крагујевац, 24000 Суботица are the same digits they were in 1971. The first two digits are the delivery area, roughly a district: 11 Београд, 12 Пожаревац, 14 Ваљево, 15 Шабац, 16 Лесковац, 17 Врање, 18 Ниш with Пирот and Прокупље, 19 Зајечар with Бор, 21 Нови Сад, 22 Сремска Митровица, 23 Зрењанин with Кикинда, 24 Суботица, 25 Сомбор, 26 Панчево, 31 Ужице, 32 Чачак, 34 Крагујевац, 35 Јагодина, 36 Краљево, 37 Крушевац. Eight two-digit prefixes in the 10-39 span are not issued at all — 13, 20, 27, 28, 29, 30, 33 and 39 — so the generator draws from the twenty real prefixes rather than from 10000-39999. The twenty-first, 38, is Kosovo and Metohija: it is in Serbia's plan and Pošta Srbije does not deliver against it, so it is described here and not generated, matching serbia.geo.city. A district seat ends 000 and smaller places do not; the last three digits are drawn freely here, so a code is in the right block and the right shape but is not looked up against a post office.
