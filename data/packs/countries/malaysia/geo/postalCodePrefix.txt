description: The 84 two-digit prefixes that really open a Malaysian poskod. A Malaysian postal code is five digits and the first two identify the state or federal territory, so a code beginning 40 is in Selangor and one beginning 93 is in Sarawak, with no exceptions and no overlap. The blocks are Perlis 01-02, Kedah 05-09, Pulau Pinang 10-14, Kelantan 15-18, Terengganu 20-24, Pahang 25-28, Perak 30-36, Selangor 40-48 and again 63-68, Kuala Lumpur 50-59, Putrajaya 62, Negeri Sembilan 70-73, Melaka 75-78, Johor 79-86, Sabah 88-91, Sarawak 93-98. Five real prefixes are DELIBERATELY LEFT OUT, and this is the departure worth knowing about: 39, 49 and 69 are Pahang's three highland exclaves (Cameron Highlands 39000-39200, Bukit Fraser 49000, Genting Highlands 69000), 87 is the whole of Labuan (87000-87033), and 60 is the single value at the top of the Kuala Lumpur range. Each covers between one and about two hundred live codes, so appending three free digits to them would produce an invalid code better than 90% of the time; leaving them out costs the pack all of Labuan and Pahang's hill stations, which is the cheaper of the two errors. Selangor's two blocks are not a mistake — 63-68 was added when 40-48 filled up, and 68100 Batu Caves is inside Selangor even though some tables file it under Kuala Lumpur. The draw is uniform over the 84 prefixes, which means the small states are over-represented against their share of the population: Perlis has 0.9% of Malaysians and 2.4% of the prefixes.
