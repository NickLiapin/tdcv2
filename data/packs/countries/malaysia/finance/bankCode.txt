address: malaysia.finance.bankCode
description: The eight-character SWIFT BIC of each bank, index-aligned with malaysia.finance.bank. This is the whole of a Malaysian bank identifier — there is no separate national sort code, no clearing number and no branch code in the account itself. The first FOUR letters are the institution code (MBBE Maybank, CIBB CIMB, PBBE Public Bank, RHBB RHB, HLBB Hong Leong, ARBK AmBank) and that four-letter code is what most Malaysian payment forms mean when they ask for a "bank code"; the next two are the ISO country code MY, and the last two the location, usually KL for Kuala Lumpur but not always — Standard Chartered is SCBLMYKX, MUFG BOTKMYKX, Mizuho MHCBMYKA, Bank of America BOFAMY2X, Boost Bank BOBEMYK2 — so the suffix cannot be derived and each code here was read off the SWIFT directory rather than constructed. Several codes do not resemble their bank's current name, which is a feature of BICs rather than an error: Affin Bank is PHBM from Perwira Habib Bank, Alliance is MFBB from Multi-Purpose Bank, MBSB Bank is AFBQ from the Asian Finance Bank whose licence it bought, and MUFG is BOTK from the Bank of Tokyo. MALAYSIA HAS NO IBAN. It is not in the IBAN registry, so this pack ships no malaysia.finance.iban and any twenty-odd-character MY-prefixed string presented as a Malaysian IBAN is fabricated. A domestic transfer is addressed by bank plus account number over IBG, RENTAS or DuitNow — and increasingly by a DuitNow ID, which is a mobile number, a MyKad number or a business registration number standing in for the account entirely. Account numbers are set by each bank, run 10 to 16 digits, follow no national format and carry no check digit, which is why this pack ships no account-number generator either.
