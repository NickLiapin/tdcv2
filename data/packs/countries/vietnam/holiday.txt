description: Public holidays and widely observed days in Vietnam. THIS LIST IS NAMES ONLY — it carries no dates and nothing here converts a holiday to a date, which matters more in Vietnam than almost anywhere, because the biggest holidays are LUNAR AND MOVE. Tết Nguyên đán (Lunar New Year) falls somewhere between 21 January and 20 February, a swing of a full month against the Gregorian calendar; Giỗ Tổ Hùng Vương is the 10th day of the 3rd lunar month; Tết Đoan Ngọ, Lễ Vu Lan, Tết Trung thu and Tết Nguyên tiêu are lunar too. Vietnam's lunar calendar is computed on UTC+7, which is why its Tết occasionally lands a day off the Chinese New Year. The first six entries are the statutory paid holidays under Điều 112 of the 2019 Labour Code — 11 days off in total, because Tết Nguyên đán is five days and Quốc khánh is two; the rest are observed nationally but are ordinary working days. Statutory days that fall on a weekend are made up with the following working day, so even the fixed-date ones do not give you a usable date without a calendar. Values are Vietnamese with stacked diacritics on single vowels.
