description: Cambodian postcode — SIX digits, no letters, no space, written bare (120101, 171203). Cambodia Post replaced the old five-digit codes with this scheme in 2017-18, and the two are not convertible: a five-digit Cambodian postcode in a data set is pre-2018 and cannot be widened by adding a digit. The structure is strictly hierarchical, which is unusual and makes the code far more informative than a European one — the FIRST TWO DIGITS ARE THE PROVINCE, drawn here from the real set in cambodia.geo.provinceCode so the province half of every value exists and matches the ISO 3166-2:KH number; the middle two are the ស្រុក, ក្រុង or ខណ្ឌ (district, municipality or urban district) inside it; the last two are the ឃុំ or សង្កាត់ (commune or quarter). So the whole Cambodian address hierarchy below the province is encoded in four digits, and truncating a code to two or four digits is a valid roll-up to province or district — a thing you cannot do with a UK or a Dutch postcode. WHAT IS MODELLED AND WHAT IS NOT: the length, the digits-only shape and a real province prefix are real; the district and commune halves are drawn from plausible ranges (01-14 and 01-20) rather than from Cambodia Post's commune table, which it does not publish as an open file. So a value here is a well-formed code in a province that exists, addressed to a district and commune that usually are not there — a province has a handful of districts, not fourteen everywhere, and commune counts vary from three to forty. If you need the province half to agree with a printed province name, draw cambodia.geo.provinceWithCode once and split it yourself; drawing this address and cambodia.geo.province separately gives you two independent provinces. Store as text: 01xxxx and 02xxxx are real Banteay Meanchey and Battambang codes and lose their leading zero the moment anything reads them as a number.
