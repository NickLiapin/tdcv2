description: The SWIFT/BIC code of a Cambodian bank, eight characters, sliced out of cambodia.finance.bankWithBic so every value belongs to a bank that exists. THIS FILE IS WHERE A CAMBODIAN IBAN WOULD HAVE GONE, AND IT IS HERE BECAUSE THERE IS NO SUCH THING. Cambodia is not in the ISO 13616 IBAN registry — the registry lists 90 country codes and KH is not among them, no Cambodian bank issues an IBAN, and no Cambodian regulation defines one. A string beginning "KH" followed by check digits and a BBAN is an invention with nothing to validate it against, and the mod-97 arithmetic that makes an IBAN self-checking is therefore unavailable for Cambodia at any price. What a payment into Cambodia actually needs is this BIC plus the beneficiary's account number at that bank, and for a domestic payment neither: retail transfers overwhelmingly go through Bakong, the National Bank of Cambodia's own settlement system, and its KHQR standard, where the payee is addressed by a QR code or a Bakong handle rather than by an account number at all. HOW TO READ THE CODE, which is a published ISO 9362 structure and the one piece of self-describing arithmetic-free validation available here: characters 1-4 are the institution, 5-6 are the ISO 3166-1 country code and are KH on every Cambodian bank without exception, 7-8 are the location — PP for Phnom Penh on nearly all of them, because Cambodian banking is run from one city. An 11-character form exists and identifies a branch; the head-office form here is what a cross-border payment uses, and appending XXX to it (ACLBKHPPXXX) is the same code written the way some payment systems demand. BIC IS NOT A CHECKSUMMED IDENTIFIER: it has a fixed shape and a registry, and that is all — a well-formed BIC that is not in the registry is undetectable by arithmetic, which is exactly why the codes here were taken from the SWIFT directory rather than constructed.
