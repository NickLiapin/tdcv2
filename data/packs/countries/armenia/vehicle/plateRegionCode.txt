description: The two-digit region code that opens an Armenian registration plate. It is a real geographic code and unlike Moldova's, it survived: an Armenian plate still tells you where the vehicle was registered. The blocks are NOT contiguous, because each province was given a low code in the 1996 series and then extra blocks as it ran out — Yerevan holds 01-11, 13, 19 and 61-68; Armavir 12 and 27-29; Shirak 14 and 45-49; Aragatsotn 15 and 21-24; Gegharkunik 16 and 31-35; Kotayk 17, 42 and 43; Vayots Dzor 18 and 56; Ararat 25 and 26; Lori 36-39 and 41; Syunik 51-54; Tavush 57-59. So consecutive codes routinely belong to different provinces (43 is Kotayk, 45 is Shirak, 44 is neither), and a range check on the leading pair is the wrong shape of test. This file draws only the 61 province-linked codes. THE 38 CODES IT DOES NOT DRAW, and why: 20, 30, 40, 44, 50, 55, 60 and the whole block 69-99 are issued as individual plates that carry no province at all — the vanity series, bought rather than assigned. They are perfectly valid on the road and I have no published figure for how often they are issued, so drawing them at the uniform 38-in-99 rate the code space suggests would put a third of Armenia's cars on vanity plates, which is nonsense. Two codes have a history worth knowing before you reuse an old dataset: 22 and 90 were Nagorno-Karabakh's, plates in those ranges were discontinued in 2024 when the territory was dissolved, and 22 has since been reallocated to Aragatsotn and 90 to the individual series — so the same code means one thing on a 2019 record and another on a 2026 one.
