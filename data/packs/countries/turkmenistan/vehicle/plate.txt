description: An ordinary Turkmen civilian number plate — two series letters, four digits, and the two-letter region code, black on white, 520 by 110 millimetres, with the national flag and the code TM in a blue band on the left: AA 1234 AG. The digits are written as two pairs on the plate itself (AA 12 34 AG) and the region code is LAST, which is the detail that catches a parser written for Russia or Kazakhstan, where the region number goes on the right of a very different layout but the letters that look like a region code sit at the front. The series letters are drawn from the Latin A-Z here; Turkmen plates use plain Latin letters and none of the Turkmen diacritics, because a plate has to be readable at a border. Not generated, because each has its own shape and its own colour: diplomatic plates are white on blue and read "NN CD NNN", where the first pair identifies the mission; government plates are silver on green; foreign nationals get black on yellow; military plates end GM or MG; the border service uses a four-digit block with an S; trailers and motorcycles have their own two-row formats. What a Turkmen plate does NOT encode is anything about the vehicle — no fuel type, no vehicle class, no year — and the region code is where the vehicle is registered, which for a country where internal movement is controlled is a more meaningful field than it would be elsewhere. Source: the plate register as tabulated on ru.wikipedia and en.wikipedia. Turkmenistan does not publish its plate specification in a form this pack could reach, and the digit-block layout below is the one both tables agree on.
