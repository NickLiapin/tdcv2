description: The Icelandic VAT registration number, the VSK-númer (virðisaukaskattsnúmer), issued by Skatturinn to a business when it registers for virðisaukaskattur. It is FIVE OR SIX DIGITS AND NOTHING ELSE — no letters, no country prefix in domestic use, and NO CHECK DIGIT, which is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly: python-stdnum's is.vsk validates length and formatting only, because there is no published checksum to validate. Numbers were issued sequentially from a low starting point, so five digits are the older registrations and six the newer, and both are current. IT IS NOT THE KENNITALA AND THIS IS THE MISTAKE EVERY SECONDARY SOURCE MAKES. Several tax-guide sites state flatly that an Icelandic company's kennitala is its VAT number; it is not. The kennitala identifies the legal person and the VSK number identifies the VAT REGISTRATION, they are different lengths, different registers and different numbers, and an Icelandic invoice carries both — kennitala for the entity, VSK-númer for the tax. A business that is not registered for VAT has a kennitala and no VSK number at all, which is exactly the distinction the two numbers exist to make. What IS true is that Iceland issues no separate TIN: the kennitala is the tax identification number for income tax, for both persons and companies (see iceland.docs.kennitala). Iceland is in the EEA but outside the EU and outside the EU VAT area, so an Icelandic VAT number is NOT in VIES and cannot be checked there; the check is Skatturinn's own public VSK register. The standard rate is 24% with a reduced rate of 11%.
