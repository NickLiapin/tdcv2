description: An Icelandic mobile number in E.164 form. Country code +354; the national number is SEVEN digits and starts with 6, 7 or 8. THE SEVEN DIGITS ARE THE WHOLE NUMBER — Iceland runs a closed numbering plan with NO AREA CODES and no trunk prefix, so what you dial at home is exactly what follows +354 abroad, with nothing added and nothing stripped. That has been true since 3 June 1995, when the country abolished its regional codes and moved every subscriber to seven digits at once. A validator written for a European neighbour will therefore reject half of Iceland twice over: once for the length, because seven digits is shorter than almost anywhere, and once for the missing leading zero, because there is no national prefix to strip. The mobile blocks are 6, 7 and 8 (fixed lines are 4 and 5 — see iceland.phoneLandline), which are the ranges the regulator Fjarskiptastofa allocates to mobile service. DO NOT READ THE OPERATOR OFF THE PREFIX: Iceland has had full mobile number portability for years and the blocks were historically issued to Síminn, Nova and Sýn/Vodafone, but a number now sits wherever its owner last moved it. Numbers are written in speech and print as three digits, a space and four (696 4321), never in pairs. The emergency number is 112 and has been since 1 October 1995. The ranges outside 4-8 are not subscriber numbers at all: 1xxx is service and directory, 800 is freephone, 9xx is premium rate, and 3xx is machine-to-machine with a NINE-digit number, which is the one exception to the seven-digit rule and the reason an Icelandic telecoms dataset can contain longer numbers that are still valid.
