---
address: iceland.geo.streetName
description: Real street names of the Icelandic capital region, from Iceland Post's own street register (Götuskrá, postur.is/gogn/Gotuskra/Gotuskra.txt), restricted to the twenty-one built-up postal codes 101-113, 170, 200-225 and 270 — the area where 64% of Icelanders live. These are names, not a pattern, because no Icelandic street name can be generated: an Icelandic street is a COMPOUND NOUN whose second element is the generic and whose first element inflects into it — Laugavegur (pool-road), Skólavörðustígur (school-cairn-path), Hverfisgata (district-street), Kringlumýrarbraut (round-marsh-road). THE GENERIC IS A SUFFIX AND NOT A SEPARATE WORD, which is the difference from every neighbouring country and the reason a config must never append a street type to these values: there is no Icelandic "Laugavegur Street", the -vegur IS the street, and "Laugavegur gata" is nonsense. The common generics are -gata and -vegur (street, road), -stígur (path), -braut (avenue), -hlíð (slope), -holt (hill), -tún (hayfield), -nes (headland), -ás (ridge), -mýri (marsh), -lundur (grove) and -bakki (bank) — a list that reads as a description of the ground Reykjavík was built on. The house number follows the name with a space and is a plain integer, occasionally with a letter (Laugavegur 22b); odd and even numbers take opposite sides. RURAL ADDRESSES ARE NOT HERE AND DO NOT WORK LIKE THIS: outside the towns an Icelandic address has no street and no number at all, only the name of the farm, and those names are in iceland.geo.farmName. The register also names each street in the dative (Laugavegi, Hverfisgötu), which is the form used after a preposition; the nominative given here is the form an address line carries.
locale: is
