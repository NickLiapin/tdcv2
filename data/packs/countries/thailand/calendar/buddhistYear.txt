address: thailand.calendar.buddhistYear
description: A year in the Buddhist Era (พุทธศักราช, abbreviated พ.ศ.), which is THE CIVIL CALENDAR OF THAILAND — not a religious alternative to the Gregorian one but the year that government documents, court judgments, tax filings, receipts, ID card and driving licence expiry dates, newspaper mastheads and school terms are all dated in. The offset is 543 years: พ.ศ. 2568 is 2025 CE, พ.ศ. 2500 is 1957. Months and days are the Gregorian ones (the Thai new year moved to 1 January in 1941, so BE and CE now turn over together and a single constant is enough); only the year number differs. This address emits BE 2500 to 2575, that is 1957 to 2032 CE. WHAT IS NOT MODELLED, and this is the important part: NOTHING IN THIS PACK CONVERTS BETWEEN THE TWO ERAS. The engine's date generator produces Common Era dates and no file here shifts them; thailand.tax.corporateTin embeds a BE year because those three digits are literally what the registration number contains, and that is the only place the era appears inside another value. If you need a BE-dated record you must add 543 yourself, and you must do it to the year alone. Two traps if you do: the offset is a constant only for dates after 1941 — between 1889 and 1940 the Thai year began on 1 April, so a January-to-March date in that window is 542 behind, not 543, and this is why old Thai records show what look like off-by-one years. And a four-digit year in Thai data is ambiguous on sight: 2540 is a BE year, 1997 is its CE twin, and both are four digits, so a column of Thai years cannot be told from a column of Western ones by shape — only by label.
