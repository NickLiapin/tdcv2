address: maldives.tax.tin
description: The Maldivian taxpayer identification number, thirteen characters — a seven-digit business partner number, a three-letter revenue code and a three-digit sector sequence, 1000001GST501. Two things about it are unusual enough to break most tax-number handling. FIRST, A TAXPAYER HAS MORE THAN ONE. The seven-digit business partner number is the taxpayer; the TIN is the taxpayer plus a REGISTRATION, so a company registered for GST, green tax and employee withholding holds 1000001GST501, 1000001GRT001 and 1000001EWT001 at once, all equally valid, all naming the same legal person. A schema with one tax_id column per company cannot hold a Maldivian taxpayer. The stable key is the first seven digits, and that is the field to join on. SECOND, THERE IS NO CHECK DIGIT AND THIS GENERATOR DOES NOT INVENT ONE. The Maldives' own submission to the OECD describes the structure character by character in a section headed "TIN Structure" that explicitly invites jurisdictions to explain "the use of check digits", and describes none: the business partner number is simply the next number MIRA issues. So the seven digits here are drawn, and the only arithmetic that could be done on a Maldivian TIN is none. Individuals mostly do not have one at all — the Maldives does not issue TINs to residents automatically, only on registration, and an individual only has to register once business income averages over MVR 40,000 a month or gross income over MVR 60,000 a month, so in a country of about 383,000 citizens the great majority of people are outside the system entirely and their identity number (maldives.docs.nationalId) is the only number they carry. Verified against the published example: MIRA's own worked case is business partner 1000001, which becomes 1000001GST501 on registering for general-sector GST and 1000001GRT001 on registering for green tax. Source: OECD CRS jurisdiction sheet for the Maldives, sections I and II.
