address: maldives.tax.vat
description: THE MALDIVES HAS NO VAT NUMBER. It has no value-added tax at all. What it has is a goods and services tax, introduced on the tourism sector in January 2011 and on everything else in October 2011, and the number a Maldivian business quotes on an invoice is not a VAT registration but a GST TIN — the taxpayer's seven-digit business partner number followed by GST and a three-digit sector code, 1000001GST501. That is what this file generates, because it is the field a VAT column in a foreign schema would be filled with, and calling it anything else would leave the column empty for an entire country. Three differences from an EU-style VAT number are worth stating before anyone reuses a European routine here. There is no country prefix: a Maldivian GST number begins with a digit, not with MV, and prefixing it produces something that exists nowhere. There is no VIES and no cross-border validation service; MIRA publishes a GST lookup of its own at mira.gov.mv and that is the only check there is. And the rate depends on the number itself rather than on the goods: the sector code says which of the two GST rates the registration carries, 501 for the general sector at 8 percent and 001 for the tourism sector at 17 percent, so a resort and a corner shop charge different tax on the same bottle of water and you can tell which from the last three digits. Registration is compulsory above MVR 1 million of annual taxable turnover and compulsory from the first rufiyaa for anyone in the tourism sector. 901 is social housing. The three sector codes are drawn evenly here because MIRA publishes no breakdown of the register by sector; in reality the general sector dominates by count and social housing is a handful of registrations, so a config that needs a realistic mix should pass its own percentages rather than take this one. See maldives.tax.tin for the general form and maldives.tax.revenueCode for the other taxes.
