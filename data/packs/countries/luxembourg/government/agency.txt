description: State administrations, services and public establishments of Luxembourg, as the government lists them. Luxembourg is ADMINISTRATION-shaped rather than agency-shaped: the ministries in luxembourg.government.ministry set policy and the work is done by a body whose name almost always begins with "Administration de", "Service de", "Institut", "Direction" or "Office", each created by its own law and each holding the register or the register-like thing it is named for. Four are worth knowing before reading any Luxembourgish dataset. The Centre commun de la sécurité sociale issues and holds the matricule, the thirteen-digit number in luxembourg.docs.matricule that doubles as the tax reference. The Administration des contributions directes and the Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA split the tax system in two — direct tax on one side, VAT and registration duty on the other — so "the Luxembourg tax office" names two different bodies depending on the tax. The Administration du cadastre et de la topographie keeps the national register of localities and streets, which is where every place name and postcode in this pack comes from. And the Centre des technologies de l'information de l'État runs the population register the residency figures come from. Names move: the police were renamed Police Lëtzebuerg — a Luxembourgish name for a state body, which is itself notable in a country that legislates in French — and the Centre pour la langue luxembourgeoise (ZLS) is the body charged with the national language's spelling and dictionary. Two financial supervisors are absent from the government's own list because they are independent public establishments rather than administrations, and both are added here since any Luxembourgish financial dataset names one of them: the CSSF, which supervises the fund industry, and the Banque centrale du Luxembourg. Source: gouvernement.lu, Administrations et services de l'État.
