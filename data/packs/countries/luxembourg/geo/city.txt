address: luxembourg.geo.city
description: Every inhabited locality of Luxembourg — 549 of them — weighted by the number of residents the national register held on 30 June 2026. The locality, not the commune, is what goes on an envelope: a Luxembourg address reads "L-4010 Esch-sur-Alzette" or "L-4460 Belvaux", and Belvaux is a locality inside the commune of Sanem, which never appears in the address at all. That is the single most common way a foreign address schema gets Luxembourg wrong — it stores the commune and prints it after the postcode, producing an address the post office cannot match. The scale is the other surprise: this is a country of 693,916 residents in which the CAPITAL holds 138,215 and the second town 38,323, and 337 of the 549 localities have fewer than 500 residents each, 426 fewer than a thousand, so a uniform draw over the list gives you a hamlet nine times out of ten and a weighted draw gives you something a person actually lives in. Names are the official French forms of the national address register; there is no Luxembourgish column in that register except for the 80 aliases in luxembourg.geo.localityLb. Sources: Administration du cadastre et de la topographie, Registre national des localités et des rues, for the names; CTIE, Extrait du RNPP — Résidents par localité au 30 juin 2026, for the counts.
