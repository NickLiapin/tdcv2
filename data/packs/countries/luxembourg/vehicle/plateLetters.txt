address: luxembourg.vehicle.plateLetters
description: The 566 two-letter combinations that appear in the standard-series issue sequence for Luxembourg number plates, published with the règlement grand-ducal du 17 juin 2003 that governs vehicle identification. Luxembourg drops two letters from the alphabet — I and O, because they read as 1 and 0 — leaving twenty-four and 576 possible pairs, and 566 of those appear in the prescribed sequence. Three of the ten missing ones are documented reservations: AA is held for official vehicles including the police, CD for diplomatic plates (which take the form CD nn-nn and are not in this series at all), and ZZ is reserved for special purposes. The remaining seven that do not appear — HJ, KK, KZ, PD, SA, SS, WC — are absent from the published sequence for reasons the text does not give, and this file simply does not emit them rather than guessing. Nothing in the pair encodes geography or age: plates are issued nationally from one pool, the order of the sequence deliberately ascends and descends through the alphabet so that consecutive registrations do not look consecutive, and the letters therefore tell you nothing about where a car is kept or when it was registered. The sequence is an ISSUE ORDER, not a set with meaning, so this file holds it sorted alphabetically; the order in which the letters were and will be used is in the règlement.
