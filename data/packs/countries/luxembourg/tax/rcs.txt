description: The Luxembourg trade and companies register number — the numéro RCS, issued by the Registre de commerce et des sociétés and quoted on every invoice, letterhead and contract a Luxembourgish company writes. One letter naming the section of the register, then up to six digits: B246607. The letter is the whole of its structure — B for commercial companies, which is the overwhelming majority and the only section this generator emits, A for civil-law entities and associations, F for a natural person trading in their own name, G for economic interest groupings — and everything after it is a plain sequence number in order of registration, so a low B number means an old company and B2 is older than B200000. WHAT IT DOES NOT HAVE IS A CHECK DIGIT. There is no checksum in an RCS number of any kind: any letter followed by any digits is well-formed, a typo in one digit gives another company's number, and the only way to validate one is to look it up. That is worth saying out loud, because the two other identifiers a Luxembourg company carries both DO carry arithmetic — the VAT number in luxembourg.tax.vat has its modulus-89 check, and the matricule for legal persons has its own — and it is easy to assume the RCS number must too. The three are separate numbers on separate schemes issued by separate bodies and none is derivable from another, so a record holding only one of them cannot compute the others. Written with no space and no punctuation, "RCS Luxembourg B246607" in full on a letterhead. Source: Luxembourg Business Registers; guichet.public.lu, Immatriculation de l'entreprise et publications au RCS.
