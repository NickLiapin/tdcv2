description: A Bhutanese ordinary passport number. THE COMPOSITION IS NOT PUBLISHED and this description says so rather than dressing a guess up as a fact: neither the Department of Immigration nor the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and External Trade publishes the make-up of the number, and no document-verification reference gives it, so the shape used here is the ICAO ordinary-passport convention — a class letter followed by seven digits inside a nine-column machine-readable field — and not a Bhutanese specification. What IS certain about a Bhutanese passport: the nationality and issuing-state code is BTN, three letters, not BT (that is the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code and the country-code top-level domain) and not BHU (that is the IOC and FIFA code, which is why Bhutan is BHU at the Olympics and BTN in a passport). Bhutan has issued ICAO-compliant machine-readable passports since 2006 and biometric books since. THERE IS NO CHECK DIGIT INSIDE THE NUMBER. The check digits people associate with a passport belong to the printed machine-readable ZONE, not to the number, and they are computed for real at bhutan.docs.passportMrz — the only arithmetic in this pack that can be checked against a published specification, because Bhutan publishes no checksum for the Citizenship Identity Card, none for the taxpayer number and none for the vehicle registration, and there is no Bhutanese IBAN to compute a modulus-97 over. The passport number is not derived from and does not contain the CID.
