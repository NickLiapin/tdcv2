description: A Bhutanese fixed-line number in E.164 form — +975, a ONE-DIGIT area code from 2 to 8, and a six-digit subscriber number, seven digits of national significant number in all against the mobile's eight. That asymmetry is the thing to know: a Bhutanese phone number is seven digits or eight depending on whether it is fixed or mobile, so a fixed-width phone column is wrong for one of the two, and the plan's stated minimum and maximum national number lengths are 7 and 8. The area codes ARE geographic, unlike the mobile prefixes: 2 is Thimphu and the west-central valleys, 3 Trongsa and Bumthang, 4 the east, 5 Phuentsholing and the south-west, 6 Gelephu and the south-centre, 7 Samdrup Jongkhar and the south-east, 8 Paro and Haa. Written nationally with a leading zero on the area code — 02 322319 is the Department of Revenue and Customs in Thimphu — which is the one place a leading zero appears in Bhutanese numbering, and dropping it when converting to E.164 is the usual bug. The subscriber ranges inside each area are allocated block by block to individual towns rather than left open (Thimphu holds 32XYYY, 33YYYY and 34YYYY; Gasa holds only 688YYY), so most of the seven-digit space is unassigned and a randomly drawn number is unlikely to be a real one — that is deliberate. Bhutan's first telephone exchange opened in the 1960s and the network reached every dzongkhag only in the 1990s. Emergency numbers are three digits and sit outside this range: 110 fire, 112 ambulance and the health help centre, 113 police. Source: ITU-T Operational Bulletin, Bhutan (+975), BICMA communication of 28 September 2021.
