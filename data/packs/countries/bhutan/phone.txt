description: A Bhutanese mobile number in E.164 form — +975 and then eight digits, of which the first two are the operator's network destination code. The full MSISDN including the country code is ELEVEN digits, which BICMA states outright, and it is the number to design a column width around. The allocated mobile NDCs are 17 and 16 for Bhutan Telecom Limited, the state operator, and 77 and 87 for Tashi InfoComm (TashiCell), the private one; 17 and 77 date from 2018, 87 was issued in May 2025 and 16 in August 2025, so a validator written against a 2024 reference rejects two live ranges and a regex of ^\\+975(17|77) is now wrong for a growing share of the country. Nothing in the prefix is geographic — mobile numbers are explicitly non-geographic in the plan — and the prefix identifies the operator that ISSUED the number, which in a market of exactly two operators is a real signal rather than a residual one. DO NOT CONFUSE 19 AND 79 WITH MOBILE NUMBERS: those are the SIM-based IoT ranges, introduced 21 February 2023, and they are TWELVE digits rather than eight, laid out as a two-digit machine-to-machine application code plus an eight-digit device number, so a smart meter in Thimphu is 975 19 01 YYYYYYYY. Bhutan had no mobile network at all until 2003 and no television or public internet until 2 June 1999, when both arrived on the same day; the country went from no broadcast media to near-universal mobile coverage inside twenty years, which is why almost every Bhutanese phone number in existence is a mobile and why bhutan.phoneLandline is the smaller and older story. Source: ITU-T Operational Bulletin, Bhutan (country code +975), BICMA communications of 26 August 2025 and 28 September 2021.
