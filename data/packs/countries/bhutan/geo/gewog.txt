description: All 205 gewogs of Bhutan — the block, the group of villages, the unit a rural Bhutanese actually lives in and the level at which the Gewog Tshogde and its elected Gup govern. Listed dzongkhag by dzongkhag in the order of bhutan.geo.dzongkhag, and ALPHABETICALLY WITHIN EACH DZONGKHAG, which is the order that assigns the gewog code in digits 4 and 5 of a Citizenship Identity Card. The gewog matters more in a Bhutanese address than a foreign schema expects, because outside the thromdes there is no street name and no house number to fall back on: the Universal Postal Union's own specimen of a rural Bhutanese address is a house number, a village, a gewog, then the dzongkhag and postcode — "House No. DA-2-04 / Khopi, Biru / Samtse 22001" — so the gewog is doing the work a street does elsewhere. THE ROSTER MOVES AND THE OLD CODES DO NOT. There were 199 gewogs in 2002 and 205 by 2005, and the changes since are real re-drawings rather than renamings: Chhukha lost Bhulajhora and gained Sampheling, Samtse lost Ghumauney, Mayona and Nainital and gained Ugentse and Yoeseltse, Thimphu lost Bapbi, Tsirang lost four gewogs and gained four others, and on 26 April 2007 the whole Lhamoizingkha dungkhag — three gewogs and its town — was transferred from Sarpang to Dagana without anybody moving. A Citizenship Identity Card issued before those changes still carries the gewog number it was issued under, which is why real Bhutanese cards carry Sarpang gewog codes as high as 16 against a current roster of 12, and why the gewog digits of a CID are a record of where a family was registered rather than a lookup into this file. Two gewogs called Norbugang exist, one in Pemagatshel and one in Samtse, and two called Gakiling, in Haa and Sarpang — the gewog name alone is not a key. Source: the Election Commission of Bhutan's 2011 final delimitation notifications, one per dzongkhag; the per-dzongkhag counts were cross-checked against the same tables and sum to 205.
