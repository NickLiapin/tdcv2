description: BHUTAN ISSUES NO SEPARATE VAT OR GST REGISTRATION NUMBER, and that is the whole content of this file. It is not an omission and not a gap in the research: Bhutan introduced a Goods and Services Tax at a flat 5 per cent on 1 JANUARY 2026, replacing the Bhutan Sales Tax after a six-year delay — the GST Act passed Parliament in February 2020 and implementation slipped repeatedly through the pandemic and a system rewrite, and the original 7 per cent rate was cut to 5 before it took effect. But the registration identifier is the EXISTING Taxpayer Number. The Goods and Services Tax Rules 2026, made by the Ministry of Finance, provide that a person whose GST registration was cancelled may re-register "using the same taxpayer number", and nowhere in 114 pages do they create a GST number, a GSTIN or any prefix or suffix on the TPN. So the correct value for a Bhutanese VAT-number column is the TPN itself, drawn here from bhutan.tax.tpn — there is no NO-style country prefix, no MVA-style suffix, no Indian-style state code and no check digit, because there is no separate number to hang them on. Two further facts for anyone reusing a European VAT routine: Bhutan is not in the EU and not in VIES, so a Bhutanese number cannot be validated by lookup; and registration is compulsory above an annual turnover of Nu. 5 million with voluntary registration from Nu. 2.5 million, so a great many small Bhutanese businesses hold a TPN and are not GST-registered at all — holding the number says nothing about being registered, which is the exact opposite of the Norwegian arrangement where the MVA suffix IS the registration flag. Source: Goods and Services Tax Rules 2026, Ministry of Finance, Royal Government of Bhutan.
