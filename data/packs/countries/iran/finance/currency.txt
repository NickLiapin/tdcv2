description: THE TWO IRANIAN MONEY UNITS, and the reason every Iranian money column needs a stated unit. The legal currency is the ریال (rial, ISO 4217 IRR) — it is what the law, the banks, the IBAN balance and the tax return use. Almost nobody quotes prices in it. Shops, salaries, rents, car adverts and everyday speech use the تومان (toman), which is not a separate currency but a name for ten rials: 1 تومان = 10 ریال, so the SAME price is 500,000 in one unit and 5,000,000 in the other and neither figure carries a marker saying which. This is the single commonest corruption in Iranian financial data, and it is a factor of exactly ten in either direction — large enough to matter, small enough to look plausible, so it survives review. A price scraped from an Iranian shop is in tomans; the balance on the same customer's bank statement is in rials; joining the two without converting understates spending tenfold. Two further traps: rial amounts are large (a modest monthly salary runs to hundreds of millions of rials), so an Iranian money column overflows a 32-bit integer at ordinary values, and Iran has legislated a redenomination that drops four zeros and renames the unit to the تومان outright — passed but not in force at the time of writing, so historic and current data are on the old scale. This file emits the unit NAMES only; no generator in this pack emits an amount, precisely because an amount without its unit is the bug. The rial has no circulating subdivision: the دینار, one hundredth of a rial, is legally defined and has been worthless for decades.
