---
address: iran.finance.iban
description: Iranian IBAN, known at home as شماره شبا — 26 characters: IR + 2 ISO 7064 MOD-97-10 check digits + a 3-digit bank code + a 19-digit account number (BBAN pattern 3!n19!n). The MOD-97 here is the real one and is computed the standard way, by moving "IR00" to the end, mapping I to 18 and R to 27, and folding the resulting 26-digit number mod 97; the published example IR710570029971601460641001 decomposes as bank 057 (Bank Pasargad) and account 0029971601460641001, and its check digits 71 are what this arithmetic produces. Two things catch people out. First the length: at 26 characters Iran is four longer than the 22-character IBANs of much of Europe, and the account part alone is 19 digits, which overflows a 64-bit integer — it is a string. Second, the 19-digit account in the IBAN is NOT the number on the customer's card or passbook; Iranian banks each use their own shorter account numbers and the شبا is derived from them by a bank-specific padding rule, so a SHEBA cannot be reconstructed from an account number without knowing which bank issued it. The bank code is drawn from iran.finance.bankCode, so the institution in the IBAN is one that exists. Iran is listed by SWIFT as a partial/experimental IBAN country: the format is fixed and the checksum is real, but Iranian IBANs are not routable through the international correspondent network the way an EU one is.
generator: tdc

<sequence name="bank"><gen type="template" value="iran.finance.bankCode"/></sequence>
<sequence name="account"><gen type="regex" value="[0-9]{19}"/></sequence>
<sequence name="iban"><compute>
  <let name="bban"><concat><field name="bank"/><field name="account"/></concat></let>
  <!-- ISO 13616: move the country code and a "00" placeholder to the end. -->
  <let name="rearranged"><concat><use name="bban"/><str v="IR"/><str v="00"/></concat></let>
  <let name="digits"><join><each>
    <over><use name="rearranged"/></over>
    <do><encode as="base36"><current/></encode></do>
  </each></join></let>
  <!-- Horner fold, so the 26-digit value never has to exist as one number. -->
  <let name="rem"><reduce>
    <over><use name="digits"/></over>
    <init><int v="0"/></init>
    <do><mod><add><multiply><acc/><int v="10"/></multiply><current/></add><int v="97"/></mod></do>
  </reduce></let>
  <let name="check"><pad width="2" fill="0"><subtract><int v="98"/><use name="rem"/></subtract></pad></let>
  <result><concat><str v="IR"/><use name="check"/><use name="bban"/></concat></result>
</compute></sequence>
<data>${{iban}}</data>
