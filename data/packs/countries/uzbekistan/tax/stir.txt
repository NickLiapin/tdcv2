description: STIR — soliq toʻlovchining identifikatsiya raqami, the Uzbek taxpayer identification number, called INN in Russian-language paperwork and on every invoice. NINE digits: eight of number and a ninth check digit. The number space is fixed by the Cabinet of Ministers decree on taxpayer identification, point 10, as amended on 27 December 2021: issuance uses 20000000X through 29999999X, so a real STIR STARTS WITH 2, and the blocks 00000000X-19999999X, 30000000X-39999999X and 80000000X-99999999X are reserved for future expansion and are not in use. That single fact rejects most invented Uzbek tax numbers on sight. THE CHECK DIGIT IS REAL AND ITS RULE IS NOT PUBLISHED, and the decree says so in as many words: the ninth digit "is formed by a certain algorithm in the course of assigning the identification number to the taxpayer by computer at the State Tax Committee". The Committee has never published that algorithm, and no worked example appears in the act or in any accessible tax-service document. Recovering it from sample numbers is not possible honestly — a nine-digit space and a handful of published company numbers would be fitted by roughly a tenth of all candidate weight vectors by chance — so this file DRAWS all nine digits and computes nothing. Output will not pass a genuine STIR validator. Contrast uzbekistan.docs.pinfl in this same pack, whose check digit is computed and verified against two numbers printed in the decree that defines it: the difference between the two files is exactly the difference between a rule that is published and a rule that is not. Two more things about the STIR that a foreign schema gets wrong. It is not a person number: since 1 July 2021 individuals are carried in the tax register by their PINFL, and the STIR now belongs to businesses and to individual entrepreneurs. And it is not an EU-style VAT number: it has no country-letter prefix, so "UZ" welded on the front of it is not a thing Uzbekistan issues.
