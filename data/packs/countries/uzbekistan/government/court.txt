description: The courts of Uzbekistan at the level a case is actually filed and cited. The structure is FOUR PARALLEL LADDERS rather than one, and that is the thing a foreign schema gets wrong: at the level of Qoraqalpogʻiston, each viloyat and Toshkent city there is a general-jurisdiction court, an ECONOMIC court (iqtisodiy sud, commercial disputes between businesses) and an ADMINISTRATIVE court (maʼmuriy sud, disputes with the state), each with its own chair and its own bench, all three feeding the one Oliy sud above them. So "the regional court" is three different courts in Uzbekistan and a case number tells you which. The count moves with the reforms and the names date a record: the Oliy xoʻjalik sudi, a separate supreme commercial court, was abolished in 2017 and folded into the Oliy sud; the region-level courts were then split into separate civil (fuqarolik ishlari boʻyicha) and criminal (jinoyat ishlari boʻyicha) courts, and those two were merged back into the single general-jurisdiction courts listed here — so a filing that names a "viloyat fuqarolik ishlari boʻyicha sudi" is from the window between those two reforms and names a court that no longer exists. Below this level sit the tuman (shahar) courts and the inter-district administrative courts, which are per-district and are not enumerated here; above and beside it, the Konstitutsiyaviy sud reviews legislation and hears no ordinary case at all. Harbiy sud is the military ladder, outside the territorial one. 45 entries. Source: the Law on Courts (OʻRQ-703, 28 July 2021) and the 2017 and 2023 judicial reform decrees.
