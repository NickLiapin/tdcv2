description: The 97 cities and towns of Uzbekistan, weighted by the estimate of 1 January 2013 — ONE date for all 97 rows, which is the reason this file uses a figure that is no longer current. Newer estimates (1 January 2025) are published for the 31 largest only; splicing them in beside a 2013 figure for the other 66 would over-weight the big cities by the twelve years of growth that the small towns are not credited with, and the arithmetic would look right in every row. Tashkent alone went from 2 340 900 to 3 112 748 over that span, a third again, so the distortion would not have been small. Uzbekistan is the most populous country in Central Asia and yet only about half its people live in a town at all, which is why the list is short for a country of 39 million: 120 places carry the status shahar and the rest of the map is qishloqs and 1 067 shaharchas (urban-type settlements), a Soviet category between village and town that has no English equivalent and is not counted here. Names in Uzbek Latin, which is the trap for a foreign dataset: the Russian forms that Western sources inherited are different words, not different spellings — Qoʻqon is Kokand, Margʻilon is Margilan, Xiva is Khiva, Buxoro is Bukhara, Nukus is unchanged, and Nurafshon was Toʻytepa until 2017. Source: citypopulation.de, estimate of 2013-01-01, from the Uzbek statistics agency.
