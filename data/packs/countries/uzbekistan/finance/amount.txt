address: uzbekistan.finance.amount
description: A sum of money written the way Uzbekistan writes it: groups of three separated by a NO-BREAK SPACE and the unit after the figure, 1 234 567 soʻm. That is the uz-Latn currency pattern in CLDR, and it disagrees with the English 1,234,567 habit on the separator and with the German habit on the decimal — an Uzbek comma is a DECIMAL point, so reading 1 234,5 as an English number gives 12345. THE SEPARATORS HERE ARE U+00A0, NOT ORDINARY SPACES, invisible in an editor and the reason a naive split on " " gives the wrong field count; the apostrophe in soʻm is U+02BB, not a quote. NO DECIMALS ARE PRINTED, and that is deliberate rather than a simplification: ISO 4217 gives UZS two minor units and CLDR will format two, but the tiyin has not circulated for decades and no Uzbek price, invoice or receipt carries one — a trailing ",00" is a tell that the amount came out of a library rather than out of Uzbekistan. The magnitude is drawn across five decades rather than uniformly, from a hundred soʻm to about ten million, because a uniform draw over a seven-digit range puts almost every value in the millions while a real Uzbek price list runs from a bus fare to a monthly wage. Values come out ROUND, which is not a shortcut: the smallest coin in circulation is 100 soʻm and Uzbek prices are quoted in round hundreds and thousands, so a figure like 24 997 soʻm is the one that would look wrong. This is the PRINTED form and not a number the engine can compute on; for arithmetic, generate a plain number and format it at the point of output.
generator: tdc
locale: uz-latn
