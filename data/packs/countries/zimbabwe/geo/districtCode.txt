address: zimbabwe.geo.districtCode
description: The two-digit Registrar-General office codes — the codes that open AND close a Zimbabwean national ID number, and the reason zimbabwe.docs.nationalId can name two places at once. Index-aligned with zimbabwe.geo.district and zimbabwe.geo.districtProvince, and sorted ascending by code so the three read as one table. These are REGISTRY OFFICE codes, not a statistical geography: the numbering is historical, it has gaps (there is no 01, 09, 16, 17, 20, 30, 31, 33, 36, 40, 51, 52, 55, 57, 60, 62, 64, 65, 69, 72, 74, 76, 78, 81, 82), and the gaps are not blanks waiting to be filled but codes that were never issued or were retired. The list is not published by the Registrar-General; it is reconstructed from the 2018 analysis of the biometric voters' roll, which recovered it by counting which codes actually occur across several million real numbers. Two entries are less certain than the rest and are marked in zimbabwe.geo.district: 10 and 46 appear in the roll but were not matched to a district by name with confidence, and the readings used here (Mhondoro-Ngezi and Mbire) are the ones two independent implementations agree on. 00 is deliberately ABSENT from this file: it is a valid district of ORIGIN, meaning no Zimbabwean district — a foreign-born or naturalised holder — but it is not a registry office and can never open a number, so zimbabwe.docs.nationalId adds it to the trailing position only. Zimbabwe has 64 districts today and this table has fewer, because districts created or split after the codes were assigned share their parent's code; Bulilima and Mangwe, split in 2001, still register under the single old Bulilimamangwe code.
