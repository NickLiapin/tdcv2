address: zimbabwe.docs.nationalId
description: Zimbabwean national registration number, the number on the chitupa — NN-NNNNNNA NN, and THE ONE NUMBER IN THIS PACK THAT CARRIES TWO DIFFERENT PLACES. The leading two digits are the Registrar-General office where the person REGISTERED; the trailing two digits are the district that person came FROM, taken from the father's side and inherited rather than chosen, so it does not change when someone moves and it is the same for a whole family line. Both fields use the same code table, which is why the two halves look alike and are constantly confused: 63-1234567 D 42 is someone who registered in Harare (63) and whose people are from Makoni (42), and a data set that reads either pair as "where they live" is wrong about most of the country. 00 in the TRAILING position is the deliberate marker for someone with no Zimbabwean district of origin — a foreign-born or naturalised holder — and 00 never appears in the LEADING position, because there is no registry office numbered 00; the two positions therefore do not draw from quite the same set, and this file models that asymmetry. THE CHECK LETTER IS REAL AND IS COMPUTED HERE. The rule is mod 23: read the registration code and the national number as one integer (the leading zero of an 08 office is simply part of the value), take it modulo 23, and index the table ZABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTVWXY — 23 letters, the alphabet less I, O and U, with Z at position 0. So remainder 4 is D, remainder 15 is Q and remainder 1 is A. THE REGISTRAR-GENERAL HAS NEVER DOCUMENTED THIS RULE. It is knowable only because the 2018 independent analysis of Zimbabwe's biometric voters' roll recovered it from several million real numbers and set it out in an appendix, and it is that analysis the open-source validators of Zimbabwean IDs cite as their source. It was therefore not taken on trust: before this file was written the rule was checked against eight published numbers with seven different check letters between them, all eight were accepted, and all eight were then re-tested with a single digit altered and all eight were rejected. Note what the check letter does NOT cover: the trailing district-of-origin digits are outside the sum entirely, so changing 63-1234567 D 42 to 63-1234567 D 43 produces a number this algorithm still accepts, and a corrupted district of origin is undetectable. The national number itself is a per-office serial, so two different offices can and do issue the same national number to different people; only the whole string is unique. Six-digit and seven-digit national numbers are both in circulation, older ones being shorter, and both are generated here in an even split, which is a structural choice and not a claim about the real mix — the seven-digit form is the commoner one on cards issued this century. Leading zeros inside the national number are real and are kept: 08-0123456 M 21 is well-formed and its national number is not 123456. The written form varies in the wild — 63-1234567D42, 63-1234567 D 42 and 63-1234567-D-42 all occur on real paperwork — and this file emits the spaced form printed on the card.
