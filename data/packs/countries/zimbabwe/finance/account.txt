description: Zimbabwean bank account number — STRUCTURAL ONLY, and deliberately so for two reasons that are worth stating separately. First, ZIMBABWE HAS NO IBAN. It is not in the IBAN registry, has never adopted the scheme, and there is no ZW.. account string with a MOD-97 check to compute. This pack therefore ships NO zimbabwe.finance.iban, and anyone reaching for one is reaching for something that does not exist; a plausible-looking ZW IBAN is not a near-miss, it is a fabrication, and it would pass a generic IBAN validator while naming nothing. A domestic transfer here is addressed by bank plus branch plus account number and settles through ZETSS, the Reserve Bank's RTGS system, or through ZIPIT for low-value retail transfers between banks; an international one uses SWIFT/BIC plus the account number. Second, there is no national account-number standard: each licensed bank sets its own length — commonly 10 to 13 digits, some longer — and its own internal check rule, and none of them publish it, so nothing generated here can be checked against anything and no checksum is computed. Sort codes are equally unpublished as a national table and are not modelled. THE CURRENCY OF THE BALANCE IS NOT IN THE NUMBER: a Zimbabwean customer typically holds a local-currency (ZiG) account and a separate nostro FCA in United States dollars at the same bank, and the account numbers look identical, so an account column joined to an amount column without a currency column produces figures that are wrong by a factor of tens — see zimbabwe.finance.currency.
