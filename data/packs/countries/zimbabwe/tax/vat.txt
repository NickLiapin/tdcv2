address: zimbabwe.tax.vat
description: Zimbabwean VAT registration number. Zimbabwe DOES issue one — unlike Kenya, Turkey, Israel or Japan, where the honest answer was that no separate VAT identifier exists — but it is not an EU-shaped number and it is not a checksummed one. The registration number is issued by ZIMRA on the VAT certificate when a trader crosses the threshold, currently US$25,000 of taxable turnover in twelve months or the ZiG equivalent, and it must appear on every fiscal tax invoice alongside the TIN. It is TIN-BASED under the current system: ZIMRA's Public Notice 10 of 2024 is titled for exactly that, declares every VAT number issued by the old SAP system dead from 1 January 2024, and tells traders to read their new number off the VAT certificate in the TaRMS portal — so the pre-2024 and post-2024 numbers are different strings for the same business, and input tax claimed against an old one is refused. THREE REFUSALS, all deliberate. There is NO CHECK DIGIT — none is published, none is recoverable, and none is computed here. The leading digits are NOT asserted: the public notice does name the old prefix and the new one, but the two figures sit in the notice's PDF outside its text layer and could not be read out of it, and every secondary source that repeats them disagrees with the next, so this file declines to bake a prefix into the pattern rather than hard-code a rumour. And the number is NOT delegated to zimbabwe.tax.tin as an alias, because "TIN-based" is ZIMRA's phrase for a family, not a statement that the two strings are equal — a Zimbabwean invoice prints both fields, which it would not do if they were the same value. What is modelled is what is certain: a ten-digit ZIMRA-issued registration number, distinct from the TIN, structural only. Rate for context, though no generator here emits one: 15% standard, raised to 15.5% for 2026.
