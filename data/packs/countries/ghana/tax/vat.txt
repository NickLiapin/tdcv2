description: Ghana's VAT registration identifier. GHANA ISSUES NO SEPARATE VAT NUMBER, and saying so is the answer here rather than inventing one. Ghana levies VAT and takes it seriously, but registration is administered on the taxpayer's existing TIN, so a Ghanaian VAT invoice carries the same number as every other tax filing — and for a business that is the GRA-issued entity TIN, which is why this address resolves to ghana.tax.tinCorporate. THERE IS NO GH PREFIX: nothing in Ghana is ever written GH0012345678, and a validator expecting an EU-shaped country-prefixed VAT ID will reject every real Ghanaian invoice. There is no VIES-equivalent public registry either; a number is checked at the GRA's own portal. WHAT THE RATE ACTUALLY IS, because it is the question this file gets asked and the answer changed on 1 January 2026: under the Value Added Tax Act, 2025 (Act 1151) the headline VAT rate is 15%, and the NHIL and GETFund levies of 2.5% each ride on top, giving an EFFECTIVE 20%. The 1% COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy was repealed, taking the effective rate down from 21.9%; NHIL and GETFund became deductible as input tax for the first time, which is a larger change for business than the headline; the 3% VAT Flat Rate Scheme for retailers was abolished outright; and the registration threshold rose from GHS 200,000 to GHS 750,000 of annual turnover, which takes a great many small traders out of the system entirely. A Ghanaian VAT figure computed at 21.9%, or a flat 3%, is pre-2026 arithmetic. One more Ghanaian wrinkle: an e-VAT invoice also carries a GRA-issued Sales Data Controller code, which is a device identifier and not a taxpayer number, and is not generated here.
