description: A Ghanaian post office box, written the way Ghanaians write it — "P. O. Box GP 1234". THE LETTERS ARE THE POINT and they are not the boxholder's initials: they name the POST OFFICE the box stands in, and until 2017 they were the closest thing Ghana had to a postal code. Two are firmly attested — GP, the General Post Office in Accra, which is the form the Universal Postal Union prints on its own Ghana address sheet, and AN, Accra-North. The rest below are the prefixes that recur constantly on Ghanaian letterheads, business cards and company filings — CT Cantonments, LG Legon, OS Osu, KA and KS in Kumasi, TN Tema, TD Takoradi, CC Cape Coast, TL Tamale — and Ghana Post publishes no single list of them, so they are attested by use rather than by a register, and this file does not claim to be the complete national set. WHY THIS MATTERS FOR DATA: the box, not the house, is the delivery address for most Ghanaian organisations and a great many individuals, because Ghana Post does not deliver to the door outside a few planned neighbourhoods. So a Ghanaian address record with an empty street line and a populated box line is not broken data — it is the normal case, and a schema that requires a street will reject the country. GhanaPostGPS in ghana.geo.digitalAddress did not abolish this: it gave Ghana a way to say where a place IS, which is a different job from saying where its post goes, and both are in use side by side — the UPU's own examples pair a P.O. Box with a postcode on the next line. A fourth variant not emitted here is the Private Mail Bag ("PMB, KIA, Accra"), which institutions with enough volume get instead of a numbered box, and which carries no number at all.
