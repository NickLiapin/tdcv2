description: Kenyan national ID number (the number on the kitambulisho) — 8 digits and NOTHING ELSE. It is a plain sequential serial issued by the Registrar of Persons in the order applications are processed, and it carries NO CHECK DIGIT of any kind — no Luhn, no mod-11, no weighted sum. Nothing in the eight digits verifies the other seven, so an ID can only be validated by looking it up against the register; a typo in one digit produces another perfectly well-formed ID number. This is a real and important difference from the neighbours a reader might expect it to resemble — South Africa's 13-digit ID ends in a Luhn digit, Poland's PESEL in a weighted mod-10 — and it is why this file computes nothing. The range drawn here (10000000-45000000) is the band of serials issued to people who are adults today; older cards carry six- or seven-digit numbers, which are still valid. Two related numbers are NOT modelled here. Huduma Namba (NIIMS, 2019) was declared unlawful by the High Court in 2021 for being rolled out in breach of the Data Protection Act and was then abandoned; no Huduma number was ever printed on a card in general circulation. Maisha Namba, launched 1 November 2023, is its successor — a Unique Personal Identifier assigned at birth that becomes the ID number on the third-generation Maisha Card, so for new registrants the ID number and the UPI are the same string. The transition is partial and contested (a petition against it was filed in December 2023), and the vast majority of ID numbers in circulation are still the 8-digit serial modelled here. The NTSA smart driving licence is keyed to this same national ID number rather than carrying a licence number of its own.
