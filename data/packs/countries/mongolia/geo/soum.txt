description: The soums (сум) of Mongolia, the country's second-level administrative unit and the one a rural address is actually filed against, written bare without the word сум exactly as Poland's list carries dolnośląskie without województwo. All 330 of them, taken from the Communications Regulatory Commission's postal register, which names every soum centre it issues a code for. THE LIST REPEATS AND THE REPEATS ARE THE POINT: 330 soums carry only 272 distinct names, because a soum name is unique inside its aimag and nowhere else. Булган is a soum in six aimags at once — Архангай, Баян-Өлгий, Булган itself, Дорнод, Ховд and Өмнөговь — and is also the name of an aimag; Жаргалант is a soum in six; Өлзийт in four; Алтай, Бугат, Баян-Овоо, Баян-Өндөр, Орхон and Эрдэнэ in three each. A join from a soum name alone to an aimag is therefore many-to-many and will silently multiply rows: the key is the pair (aimag, soum), never the soum by itself, and this file keeps every duplicate rather than deduplicating so that the shape of that problem stays visible. A soum divides further into баг, the smallest unit, which is usually numbered rather than named. The capital has no soums at all: Ulaanbaatar divides into дүүрэг and those into хороо, so a Mongolian address model needs both branches, and a row carrying a soum and a khoroo at once is not a Mongolian address. Several soum centres are villages of a few hundred people — a soum is a unit of land far more than it is a settlement, and with 330 of them across 1.56 million km² the average one covers about 4700 km², nearly twice Luxembourg.
