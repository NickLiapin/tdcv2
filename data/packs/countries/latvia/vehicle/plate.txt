address: latvia.vehicle.plate
description: An ordinary Latvian vehicle plate — two letters from latvia.vehicle.plateLetters, a hyphen, and four digits. The letters encode NOTHING: unlike Poland, Czechia or Germany, where the prefix names the issuing region, Latvia's pairs run in plain national sequence and a plate says nothing about where the car lives. Only Q is permanently excluded; O, I, W, X and Y were withheld from 1994 to 2004 and later released, but only as ONE of the two letters, which is why plateLetters holds 600 pairs and not 625. The regulation allows one to four digits forming a number from 1 to 9999, so AB-7, AB-42 and AB-513 are all legal plates still on the road; four digits are drawn here because the short combinations were exhausted years ago and a plate issued today has four. Two changes are worth knowing. Electric cars had their own EX and EY series in light blue, so a rule reading "EX" as electric is right about those and blind to every electric car on an ordinary plate. And in December 2024 the Saeima legislated that from 2026 the hyphen between letters and digits would be replaced by the small state coat of arms on all plates except trailers — but as of May 2026 CSDD does not expect to implement it until it signs a new manufacturing contract, possibly as late as 2028, so the hyphen is still what is printed and still what is kept here; the coat of arms has no text form in any case. Not generated: TX and TQ taxis on yellow, TA-TJ and TO motorcycles, L and LA armed forces, C/CC/CD diplomatic on red, CH honorary consuls, VS historic vehicles, IZM trial plates, and the personalised plates of two to eight free symbols.
