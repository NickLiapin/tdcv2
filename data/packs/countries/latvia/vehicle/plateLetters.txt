---
address: latvia.vehicle.plateLetters
description: The 600 two-letter combinations an ordinary Latvian A/B/C-type registration plate can carry. Latvia drops exactly ONE letter of the Latin alphabet from its plates — Q — which leaves 25, and that is the whole of the exclusion rule today; the Latvian alphabet's own diacritic letters (ā, č, ē, ģ, ī, ķ, ļ, ņ, š, ū, ž) never appear on a plate at all, so a plate is the one official Latvian string that is pure ASCII. There is a second, subtler rule that costs 25 of the 625 pairs. From 1994 to 2004 the letters O, I, W, X and Y were also withheld, and when short combinations ran out CSDD released them again — but only as ONE of the two letters, never both, so OI, WX and YY are not issuable while AO, WA and XB are. That is why this file holds 600 pairs and not 625. Nothing in the pair encodes geography: unlike Poland, Czechia or Germany, Latvian letter pairs run in plain national sequence and a plate says nothing about where the car lives or was registered. Separate series that are NOT in this list: EX and EY for electric cars (light blue characters), TX and TQ for taxis (yellow ground), TA-TJ and TO for motorcycles, L and LA for the armed forces, C, CC and CD for the diplomatic corps on red, CH for honorary consuls, VS for historic vehicles and IZM for trial plates. Source: Latvijas transportlīdzekļu numura zīmes — "divi latīņu alfabēta burti (izņemot 'Q')" and "apgrozībā tika laistas numura zīmes, kurām VIENS no burtiem var būt arī 'O', 'I', 'W', 'X' un 'Y'".
