description: Latvian passport number (pases numurs) — a two-character series followed by seven digits, nine characters with no space and no hyphen. The series is the part that catches validators out: it may be two LETTERS or two DIGITS, PMLP issues both forms, and a regex of `[A-Z]{2}[0-9]{7}` rejects a real passport while `[0-9]{9}` rejects a different real passport. Letters are what this generator draws because that is the commoner form; the all-digit series is recorded here rather than generated. There is NO check digit — the number carries no arithmetic at all, so a transposed digit is undetectable from the number alone and the only check available is a lookup against the register. That is the practical difference between the passport number and the personas kods, and it is why the personas kods, not the passport number, is the identifier Latvian systems key on: a Latvian keeps one personas kods for life and passes through many passport numbers, since the passport itself is valid ten years for an adult and expires on schedule. A Latvian passport carries the holder's personas kods on the data page, so a record holding both is holding one durable key and one perishable one. The non-citizen's passport (nepilsoņa pase), issued to permanent residents who did not become citizens after 1991 and still held by a substantial number of people, uses the same number shape. ONE MORE THING ABOUT THIS DOCUMENT, and it belongs here because this is where a personal name meets a Latvian record: Latvian is an inflected language and it DECLINES personal names, including foreign ones. A name entered in a Latvian document is respelled to Latvian orthography and given a case ending — a masculine surname of non-Latvian origin takes an -s, so the string on a Latvian passport is not the string on the passport of origin, and the same person is one name in Rīga and another in Berlin. On request, and against documents proving it, the original or historic spelling may also be recorded — but on a later page, never the data page. So a Latvian identity record can legitimately hold two spellings of one surname, an exact-match join across borders will miss, and any name generated for a Latvian record must be a Latvian-declined form rather than a foreign string dropped in. Person names themselves live in the lv LANGUAGE pack, not here; this note is about what the document does to them. Format per the Microsoft Purview entity definition for the Latvia passport number: "two letters or digits followed by seven digits with no spaces or delimiters", checksum "No".
