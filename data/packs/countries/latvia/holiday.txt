description: The svētku dienas of Latvia, in the order and under the names the law "Par svētku, atceres un atzīmējamām dienām" gives them in its 1. pants. Three things about this list are Latvian and not European. First, "Vispārējo latviešu Dziesmu un deju svētku noslēguma diena" — the closing day of the General Latvian Song and Dance Festival — is a statutory public holiday that occurs roughly once every FIVE years, so a Latvian holiday table that is complete in a festival year has one entry too many in the four that follow, and a fixed annual calendar can never be right about it. Second, 1 May is one day carrying two statutory names, Darba svētki and Latvijas Republikas Satversmes sapulces sasaukšanas diena, and Mātes diena on the second Sunday in May is in the law as a svētku diena, which is rarer than it sounds. Third, the same article writes ONE-OFF days into the permanent statute: 24 September 2018 for Pope Francis's pastoral visit, and 29 May 2023 for the day Latvia's ice hockey team took bronze at the world championship. Those two are dated events, not recurring holidays, and are therefore not in the list below — but they are in the law, and a scraper of the statute will find them. Note also that 24 and 31 December are full public holidays in Latvia, unlike Norway where both are ordinary working days, and that the two dates carrying the country's own history are 4 May 1990, the declaration restoring independence, and 18 November 1918, the proclamation of the Republic. Seven of these move with Easter or the calendar.
