description: The languages named in Chapter IV of the Constitution of Sri Lanka, in their English names, and the reason every value in this pack is written in English. SINHALA AND TAMIL ARE BOTH OFFICIAL LANGUAGES and both are national languages — Article 18, as amended in 1987 — which makes Sri Lanka one of very few countries with two co-equal official languages and no third tier between them. Sinhala alone was made official in 1956 by the Official Language Act, the "Sinhala Only" act; the elevation of Tamil to equal official status thirty-one years later was one of the central political facts of the twentieth century here, and a dataset that treats Sinhala as the language of Sri Lanka is repeating the position the constitution abandoned. ENGLISH IS THE LINK LANGUAGE, named as such in Article 18(3). It is not an official language, but it is the language of the superior courts, of higher education, of most central-government administration and of essentially all banking and commerce, and it is the script in which Sri Lankan identity documents, passports, bank statements, tax records, postal directories and number plates are actually printed. That is why this country pack is in English: the alternative would be to pick one of the two official languages, which would make it a pack for one community rather than for the country. Sinhala text belongs in a si language pack and Tamil text in the ta language pack; neither is this pack's business, and this pack does not create either. A last point for anyone modelling Sri Lankan people rather than Sri Lankan places: the language a person uses is not derivable from the district they live in, and the three names below are languages rather than communities — Sri Lankan Tamils, Indian Tamils and Sri Lankan Moors all speak Tamil, and the census counts them as three separate ethnic groups.
