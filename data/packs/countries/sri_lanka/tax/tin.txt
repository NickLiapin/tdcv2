description: TIN — the Taxpayer Identification Number issued by the Commissioner General of Inland Revenue, NINE digits, and the only tax identifier Sri Lanka issues. One TIN covers a person and a company alike, and it covers every tax the Department administers — income tax, PAYE/APIT, withholding, VAT — so a Sri Lankan record does not need two tax-number columns the way an EU one does. The Gazette that prescribes the standard VAT tax invoice (Extraordinary No. 2481/22) requires the supplier's TIN and, where the buyer is registered, the buyer's, and specifies nine digits in both places, which is the firmest public statement of the format there is. The TIN reaches well beyond filing a return — a company registration, a customs declaration, a motor-vehicle registration, a bank account for a business and a liquor or trade licence all demand one — so the number of TIN holders is far larger than the number of people who actually pay income tax. NO CHECK DIGIT AND NO PUBLISHED STRUCTURE. Second-hand sources circulate a breakdown of the nine digits into a region code, a taxpayer-type code and a serial, but they disagree with each other and the arithmetic in the commonest version does not even add to nine digits, so the pack does not repeat it; the Department itself says the format is internal to its systems. No checksum has ever been published either. All nine digits are therefore drawn uniformly, including a possible leading zero, because inventing a mod-11 would reject every genuine Sri Lankan TIN a test set was checked against while looking rigorous. A leading zero is also the reason this is a string and not an integer: a TIN that has been through a spreadsheet as a number is a TIN that may have lost its first character.
