description: The number that fills the "VAT registration number" field on a Sri Lankan tax invoice. Sri Lanka levies VAT — since 1 August 2002 under the Value Added Tax Act No. 14 of 2002, replacing the older GST and NSL — but IT ISSUES NO SEPARATE VAT IDENTIFIER the way an EU member state does. Registration for VAT is registration of an existing TAXPAYER for one more tax type, so what a Sri Lankan VAT invoice carries is the TIN, and this address resolves to sri_lanka.tax.tin and is the same nine digits rather than a second number that does not exist. Consequences a validator gets wrong. There is no country prefix: nothing here is ever written LK123456789, and a checker built for the EU shape of "two letters plus digits" rejects every genuine Sri Lankan VAT registration. There is no VIES-style lookup either — Sri Lanka is not in the EU system, and the Inland Revenue Department's own portal is the only place a registration can be checked. WHAT THIS FILE DELIBERATELY DOES NOT EMIT: Sri Lankan invoices and RAMIS screens are widely seen printing the registration as the TIN followed by a hyphen and a four-digit tax-type suffix, the VAT type being commonly reported as 7000, e.g. 134005236-7000. That convention is real enough to be worth naming, but it appears in no source this pack could verify — not the Gazette, not the Department's published guidance, both of which specify a plain nine-digit TIN — so the suffix is described here and not generated. A config that needs the printed form should append it itself and will then know it did. Two further facts before filling this column for every generated company: the registration threshold is high and has moved twice recently, so a large share of Sri Lankan businesses are not registered at all and correctly have no VAT number; and registration is per PERSON rather than per branch, so unlike Bangladesh one company holds exactly one, never several.
