description: Sri Lankan postal code — FIVE digits, introduced by Sri Lanka Post in 1997, written on its own with no letters and no separator. LEADING ZEROS ARE REAL AND LOAD-BEARING, which is the trap this file exists to keep. Colombo city occupies the 00100-01500 block — Colombo 01 (Fort) is 00100, Colombo 03 (Kollupitiya) is 00300, Colombo 07 (Cinnamon Gardens) is 00700, and Colombo 15 is 01500 — so a code that arrives as the integer 300 has silently lost two characters and no longer addresses anything. Sri Lanka Post's own guidance is explicit that the zeros are entered. That is the opposite of neighbouring Bangladesh, whose four-digit codes never begin with 0, and it is why this file emits a string of exactly five digits rather than a number. The first two digits broadly identify the postal district and the last three the delivery office within it; the verified anchors are 001-015 Colombo city, 11-19 the rest of Western Province, 20 Kandy and Central, 30 Batticaloa, 31 Trincomalee, 32 Ampara, 40 Jaffna, 50 Anuradhapura, 60 Kurunegala and North Western, 70 Ratnapura and Sabaragamuwa, 80-89 Galle and Southern, 90 Badulla and Uva. The pack draws 00100-99999 and deliberately does NOT model which of those codes Sri Lanka Post has actually allotted: the directory is published as a list of about 1,800 post offices rather than as a rule, and generating from a partial copy of it would make whole districts unreachable while making the file look authoritative. One further fact worth knowing before trusting a postal-code column in a Sri Lankan dataset — the code is very widely omitted in practice. Ordinary Sri Lankan addresses are written town-and-district and delivered on that alone, so a real extract will have a high null rate in this field, and a config that fills it for every row is describing a more codified postal system than the one that exists.
