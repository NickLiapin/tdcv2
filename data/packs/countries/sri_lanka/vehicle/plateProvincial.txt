address: sri_lanka.vehicle.plateProvincial
description: The Sri Lankan plate format used from 2000 until the 2013 change — a two-letter PROVINCE code, a two-letter class series and four digits, e.g. WP KA-1234. It is generated as its own address rather than folded into sri_lanka.vehicle.plate because it is a different shape, not a variation: eight characters against seven, a province inside the number instead of alongside it, and a two-letter class code instead of a one-letter one. It is emphatically not history — Sri Lankan vehicles are long-lived and plates are not reissued on the 2013 pattern, so a large part of the fleet on the road today still carries this form, and a parser that only knows CAB-1234 will fail on a great many real records. BOTH HALVES OF THE PREFIX ARE MEANINGFUL. The province (sri_lanka.vehicle.plateProvince) is where the vehicle was registered. The two-letter series is the CLASS, from the Motor Traffic Commission's published allocation: KA-KZ motor cars, LA-LU lorries, MA-MZ motorcycles, NA-NZ motor coaches, QA-QX motor tricycles, QY-QZ three-wheelers, and AA-FZ the block set aside for older vehicles converting from the pre-2000 numeric plates, which the Commission said would cover about 200,000 of them. Before this scheme every vehicle regardless of type was numbered from GA upward, which is why a plate from the late 1990s says nothing about what it is attached to. The FIRST letter here is drawn from those real class blocks and weighted towards the classes that dominate the Sri Lankan fleet — motorcycles by a wide margin, then three-wheelers and tricycles, then cars — so the class of every generated plate is truthful. The SECOND letter is drawn A-Z, which is exact for the K, M, N and A-F blocks and slightly wide for two: the lorry block stops at LU and the tricycle/three-wheeler split falls inside Q, so a small share of values land in the unallotted tail LV-LZ. That is stated rather than hidden, because narrowing it would mean claiming the sub-ranges are still current, and the Commission has not republished them since. The four digits run 0001-9999; when a series fills, the next opens, so in the real fleet the digits cluster by registration date rather than spreading evenly as they do here.
