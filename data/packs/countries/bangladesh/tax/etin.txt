description: e-TIN — the Taxpayer's Identification Number issued by Bangladesh's National Board of Revenue, TWELVE digits, and the number quoted on an income-tax return, a salary record and a company's own filings. The "e" is not decoration — the 12-digit number replaced an older shorter TIN when NBR moved registration online, and only the electronic one is now issued, so a Bangladeshi tax record with a shorter TIN is an old record. One e-TIN covers both people and companies, which is unlike most countries and means a Bangladeshi dataset does not need two tax-number columns; what a company additionally needs is a BIN for VAT (bangladesh.tax.bin), and NBR requires the TIN before it will issue the BIN, so a business record carries both and the TIN is the older of the two. The e-TIN is required for a great deal more than filing — a trade licence, a car registration, a credit card, land registration and company directorship all demand one — which is why the number of registered e-TIN holders is several times the number of people who actually file a return. NO CHECK DIGIT AND NO PUBLISHED STRUCTURE. NBR has not published a checksum, and it has not published what the twelve digits encode either, so this file draws all twelve uniformly rather than inventing an arithmetic that would look verified and would not be. That refusal is the accurate answer here — a fabricated mod-11 would reject every real e-TIN a test set was checked against.
