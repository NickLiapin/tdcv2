description: NID — জাতীয় পরিচয়পত্র, the National Identity Card number issued by the Bangladesh Election Commission, and the number that gates almost everything else in the country — a SIM card, a bank account, a passport, a trade licence, a vote. THIS FILE GENERATES THE 17-DIGIT FORM, which is the one on cards and forms today. What changed — the original number was 13 digits (2 district + 1 RMO + 2 upazila or thana + 2 union or ward + 6 serial), and the Commission later prefixed the holder's FOUR-DIGIT BIRTH YEAR to it, giving 17. Nothing else moved, so the last 13 digits of a 17-digit NID are exactly the old number, and the two forms convert in one direction only — 13 to 17 needs the birth year from somewhere else, 17 to 13 is a substring. That is why Bangladeshi systems still hold both, and why a dataset mixing 13- and 17-digit values is not corrupt. The layout generated here, in order — birth year (4), district code (2, in 01..64), RMO code (1), upazila or thana code (2), union or ward code (2), serial (6). The RMO digit is the one part of the number that says how the holder lives and it comes from a published set — 1 rural, 2 municipality (পৌরসভা), 3 urban outside a municipality, 4 other, 5 cantonment, 9 city corporation — weighted here towards rural, because Bangladesh still is. NO CHECK DIGIT. The Election Commission has never published a checksum for the 13- or 17-digit NID and none is computed here — verifying an NID means calling the Commission's own service, there is no arithmetic that does it offline, and inventing a plausible mod-11 would make every number in a test set fail against the real thing while looking rigorous. Not generated on purpose — the 10-DIGIT smart NID adopted for the smart card, whose nine leading digits are random and whose tenth IS a checksum. That algorithm has not been published, so this pack will not guess it — a 10-digit number with a made-up check digit is worth less than no 10-digit number at all. The 17-digit form remains valid and is what the great majority of records carry. Also not modelled — which district, upazila and union codes are actually allotted. Two incompatible district-code lists circulate publicly, one alphabetical over the old district spellings in which Dhaka is 26, and one running 01..64 north to south by division in which Dhaka is 40, and the pack refuses to pick between them, so the district digits here are well-formed and in range rather than a claim about geography. A verified district-code list does exist in this pack, but it is the BANK's rather than the Commission's — see bangladesh.finance.districtCode.
