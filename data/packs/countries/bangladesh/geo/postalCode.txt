description: Bangladeshi postal code — FOUR digits, introduced by the Bangladesh Post Office on 22 December 1986 and written on its own, with no letters and no separator. The scheme is hierarchical and blocked by district: a district is given a contiguous 100-code block and its sub-post-offices are numbered inside it, which is why Dhaka district is 1000-1399 (the GPO itself is 1000), Narayanganj 1400-1499, Munshiganj 1500-1599, Narsingdi 1600-1699, Gazipur 1700-1799, Manikganj 1800-1899, Tangail 1900-1999, Jamalpur 2000-2099, Sherpur 2100-2199, Mymensingh 2200-2299, Kishoreganj 2300-2399, Netrokona 2400-2499, Sunamganj 3000-3099, Sylhet 3100-3199, Chattogram 4000-4399, Panchagarh 5000-5099, Rajshahi 6000-6299, Barishal 8200-8299 and Khulna 9000-9299. Five General Post Offices sit at the top of the scheme — Dhaka 1000, Chattogram 4000, Rajshahi 6000, Khulna 9000 and Sylhet 3100 — and the leading digit is therefore real geography rather than filler. No block begins with 0, so a code never has a leading zero and a four-digit numeric column will not silently lose one, which is the trap the neighbouring Indian PIN and the Sri Lankan five-digit code both set. The pack draws 1000-9999 uniformly and deliberately does not model which of the roughly nine thousand possible codes the Post Office has actually allotted: the full directory is published only as a list of post offices, not as a rule, and generating from a partial copy of it would make some districts unreachable while making the file look authoritative.
