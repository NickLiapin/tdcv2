description: An ordinary Estonian vehicle registration plate (registreerimismärk), type A1 — three digits, a space and three letters: 123 ABC. Digits FIRST, which is the reverse of the Finnish ABC-123 across the gulf and of the Latvian AB-1234 to the south, and the quickest way to tell the three apart in a photograph. Nothing in the plate encodes a region. The first letter used to: until 2013 it named the county the car was registered in, and plenty of lookup tables built before then are still in circulation, so a rule that reads geography out of an Estonian plate is reading a map that was withdrawn. The letters are Latin capitals; the Estonian letters õ, ä, ö and ü never appear, because a plate has to be legible abroad. The characters are drawn freely here from A-Z and 000-999, not from the issued series, and no letter exclusions are applied: the regulation restricts some combinations on some plate types, but the published text is not specific enough to encode without guessing, and inventing an exclusion list would make the plates look more authentic while being less true. Special series have their own shapes and are not generated: diplomatic plates are white on blue, temporary plates yellow, moped plates three digits and one letter on green, trade plates read PROOV and four digits, and Defence Forces plates are white on black beginning with K or EK.
