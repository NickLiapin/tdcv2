description: Estonia's higher education institutions in Estonian — the six public universities first, then the private ones and the rakenduskõrgkoolid (universities of applied sciences). The whole country has fewer than twenty, which is the scale to hold in mind: Tartu Ülikool alone teaches roughly a third of all Estonian students, and it and Tallinna Tehnikaülikool between them more than half, so an unweighted draw over this list badly over-represents institutions with a few hundred students. Tartu Ülikool was founded in 1632 by Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden and is the oldest, older than the Estonian state by nearly three centuries; the six public universities are established by their own acts of parliament and are public-law legal persons, which is why their registry codes begin with 7 (Tartu Ülikool 74001073, Tallinna Tehnikaülikool 74000323) rather than the 1 of a company. The two-tier split matters for anything modelling qualifications: an ülikool awards bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees, a rakenduskõrgkool awards a professional higher education diploma that is a first-cycle qualification and not a bachelor's degree, and treating the two as interchangeable misstates what a graduate holds. Sisekaitseakadeemia and Kaitseväe Akadeemia train for the police, rescue, prison and defence services and sit under ministries rather than under education.
