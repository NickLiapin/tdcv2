description: The public holidays of Estonia as set by the Pühade ja tähtpäevade seadus — eleven riigipühad plus the one rahvuspüha, iseseisvuspäev on 24 February, which is a separate legal category and the only day the law calls a national holiday rather than a state one. TWO of these are independence days and the distinction is not decorative: 24 February marks the 1918 declaration, 20 August the restoration in 1991, and both are days off, which is why an Estonian calendar has an independence day in winter and another in late summer. THREE are moveable and follow Easter: suur reede is the Friday before, ülestõusmispühade 1. püha is Easter Sunday, nelipühade 1. püha is seven weeks after it — so a fixed-date holiday table is wrong for a quarter of the list in every year. Two fall on consecutive days in midsummer, võidupüha on 23 June and jaanipäev on 24 June, and together they are the longest break in the Estonian working year outside Christmas; jaanipäev is the one people actually leave the city for. Jõululaupäev, Christmas Eve, is a full public holiday here, which it is not in most of Europe. The working day before uusaasta, iseseisvuspäev, võidupüha and jõululaupäev is shortened by three hours by the same act — a fact that belongs to a working-hours model, not a holiday list, and is recorded here because nothing else in the pack carries it. Names in lower case, which is how Estonian writes them.
