description: An Estonian postal code (sihtnumber) — five digits, no letters, no separator and no space in the middle. Codes are allocated from 10000 upward, so a leading zero never occurs and a five-digit field can hold one without padding, which is the opposite of the Finnish, Norwegian and German cases where the leading zero is the thing that gets lost. The FIRST digit names a postal region and the map is not the county map: 1 is Tallinn, 2 is Narva and Narva-Jõesuu, 3 is Kohtla-Järve, 4 is the rest of Ida-Virumaa with Lääne-Virumaa and Jõgevamaa, 5 is the city of Tartu, 6 is Tartumaa outside the city with Põlva, Võru, Valga and part of Viljandi, 7 is the rest of Viljandimaa with Järvamaa, Harjumaa outside Tallinn and Raplamaa, 8 is Pärnumaa, 9 is Läänemaa, Hiiumaa and Saaremaa. Three of the nine regions are single towns in the industrial north-east, which is a 1990s allocation still in force. In towns a code identifies a delivery district; in the countryside it usually covers a whole municipality, so a rural code and a municipality are close to one-to-one and an urban one is nothing like it. This generator draws the format, not the issued set — roughly 4 500 codes exist out of the 90 000 the format allows, so a drawn code is well-formed and need not be deliverable.
