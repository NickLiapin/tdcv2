address: taiwan.finance.accountNumber
description: A Taiwanese bank account number as it is written on a transfer form: the three-digit 金融機構代號 followed by the account digits. THERE IS NO NATIONAL ACCOUNT-NUMBER FORMAT AND NO NATIONAL CHECK DIGIT — this is the honest shape of the thing and the reason nothing is validated here. Each bank numbers its own accounts, lengths run from 10 to 16 digits depending on the bank, the internal structure differs (some banks embed the four-digit branch code in the leading digits, others do not), and the only check digit that exists is a per-bank scheme that no bank publishes. So a Taiwanese account number cannot be validated offline at all: the transfer system validates it by asking the receiving bank, which is why every Taiwanese banking app makes you confirm the payee name after entering the number rather than before. Fourteen digits is generated here because it is the commonest length across the large retail banks, and the three-digit prefix is a real code from taiwan.finance.bankCode so at least the bank exists. Two things a schema needs. The number is NOT the payment address on its own — a domestic transfer needs the bank code beside it, and the two are often stored as one 17-digit string with no separator, which is why a Taiwanese "account number" column is sometimes 14 characters and sometimes 17. And 虛擬帳號 (virtual account numbers) are everywhere in Taiwanese billing: a merchant is issued a block and hands out a different account number to every customer for every invoice, so the same company receives money on thousands of distinct account numbers and a payment record keyed on account number does not identify a counterparty.
