address: taiwan.finance.bankCode
description: 金融機構代號 — the three-digit code that identifies a bank in every Taiwanese domestic transfer, ATM screen and payroll file, index-aligned line for line with taiwan.finance.bank. This is Taiwan's routing number and it is the reason the country needs no IBAN: a transfer is addressed as bank code + branch code + account number, and the full 七碼 form used on transfer slips is the three-digit bank code followed by the four-digit branch code, so 0040181 is 臺灣銀行 branch 0181 rather than an account. The blocks mean something. 004 to 054 are the older commercial and specialised banks in roughly the order they were licensed, with 004 臺灣銀行 first because it was the de facto central bank until 1961 and still issues the banknotes on the central bank's behalf. 101 to 147 are the former 信用合作社 (credit cooperatives) that converted to banks in the 1990s — 瑞興 was 臺北市第九信用合作社, 陽信 was 臺北市第三信用合作社 — and they are the codes with the strongest local character. 700 is 中華郵政 and is not a bank. 803 to 826 are the banks licensed in the 1991-1992 liberalisation and the internet banks of 2021, which is why 中國信託 (822), 玉山 (808), 台新 (812) and 永豐 (807) — four of the largest retail names in the country — all sit in the high block despite their size: the number records when a bank was licensed, not how big it is. Codes are NEVER REUSED after a merger, so a historical Taiwanese payments file contains codes with no bank behind them: 801 萬通, 802 大安, 804 中華, 810 寶華 and 815 日盛 have all been absorbed, and 810 was later reassigned to 星展（台灣）as the successor of 寶華 rather than to an unrelated bank. Leading zeros are significant and are part of the code: 004 is not 4, and a column typed as an integer has destroyed the identifier for every bank below 100 — which is most of the large ones.
