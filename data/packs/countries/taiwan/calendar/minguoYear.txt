address: taiwan.calendar.minguoYear
description: A year in the 民國 era (Minguo, the Republic of China era), WHICH IS THE OFFICIAL CALENDAR YEAR OF TAIWAN. This is the most important fact in this pack for anyone reading Taiwanese records. 民國 is not a ceremonial alternative to the Gregorian year — it is the year the government dates its laws, gazettes, budgets, court judgments, tax returns, land deeds, household registration records, driving licences, ID cards, invoices and company registrations in, and it is what a Taiwanese form means when it asks for 年. THE OFFSET IS EXACTLY 1911: 民國 1 is 1912, the year the Republic was founded, so Minguo year = CE year - 1911 and 2026 is 民國115年. Unlike the Nepali Bikram Sambat the offset is a flat constant, because the Minguo year begins on 1 January exactly as the Gregorian one does and the months and days are the ordinary Gregorian ones; only the year number differs. That makes conversion trivial and makes the failure mode subtle instead of loud. THE FAILURE MODE IS THAT A MINGUO YEAR LOOKS LIKE A DAY OR A MONTH. It is a two- or three-digit number, so 115 in a date field is 2026 and 76 is 1987, and a parser that meets 1150618 will read it as a seven-digit integer or as a malformed ISO date rather than as 民國115年6月18日. Government APIs return exactly that: the 經濟部 company registry gives TSMC's incorporation date as "0760221", zero-padded to three digits, meaning 民國76年2月21日 = 1987-02-21. A three-digit year with a leading zero is the normal machine form and no delimiter separates the fields. Printed forms write 民國115年6月18日 or 115.06.18 or 115/06/18. THIS ADDRESS EMITS THE YEAR ONLY, 民國 60 to 130, that is 1971 to 2041 CE. NOTHING IN THIS PACK CONVERTS BETWEEN THE TWO ERAS: the engine's date generator produces Common Era dates and no file here shifts them, so a config that needs a Minguo date beside its Gregorian twin must subtract 1911 itself. Two traps if you do. Dates before 1912 are counted BACKWARD as 民國前N年 (民國前1年 is 1911), so the era has no year zero and negative arithmetic is off by one. And the same three digits are used by the PRC-era Chinese calendar for nothing at all, so a 115 in a Chinese-language dataset is Taiwanese by construction — the mainland has used plain Gregorian years since 1949, and the presence of a Minguo year is itself a reliable signal that a record is from Taiwan.
