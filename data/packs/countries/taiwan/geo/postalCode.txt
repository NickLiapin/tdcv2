description: A Taiwanese postal code in the 3+2 form — a real issued 3-digit 鄉鎮市區 code from taiwan.geo.postalCode3 followed by a 2-digit delivery segment, written as five digits with no separator (10058 is the 行政院). THE 3 AND THE 2 ARE DIFFERENT KINDS OF NUMBER and only the first is a place. The three digits identify a 鄉鎮市區 and are in the register; the two digits identify a delivery walk INSIDE it — a run of house numbers on a stretch of road, redrawn whenever the post office reorganises a route — and are published only as a 3+2 lookup keyed on the full street address, not as a list of valid segments per district. They are generated here as 01 to 99, which is the printed range, and a given pair is therefore not guaranteed to be an issued segment for its district. If the code has to be real for the address, look it up; if it only has to be well-formed and to point at the right 鄉鎮市區, this is correct. TAIWAN NOW HAS SIX DIGITS AND STILL USES FIVE. On 2020-03-03 中華郵政 replaced 3+2 with 3+3, keeping the same three-digit district part and widening the segment from two digits to three; the 行政院 is 10058 in the old form and 100009 in the new one, which shows the segment digits are RENUMBERED and not merely zero-padded — 58 does not become 058. The old five-digit codes remain valid, are still accepted on mail, and are still what most people write and most Taiwanese systems store, so a Taiwanese address column in the wild holds five digits, six digits, three digits, or nothing, and the five- and six-digit forms of the same address do not match on any prefix beyond the first three. Taiwan writes the code FIRST, in front of the city, which is the reverse of Japan and of most of Europe. Before 1970 the code was three digits only; the two-digit segment was added that year and Taiwan is one of the few places to have gone 3 → 3+2 → 3+3 rather than adding digits at the end.
