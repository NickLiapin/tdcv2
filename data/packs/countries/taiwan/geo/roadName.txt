description: 臺灣實際存在的路名。TAIWANESE ROAD NAMES ARE POLITICAL, NOT LOCAL, and that is the whole character of this list: after 1945 the incoming government renamed the Japanese-era 町 grid wholesale, and it renamed it with the same two or three dozen slogans everywhere — 中正路 (for 蔣中正), 中山路 (for 孫中山), 中華路, 復興路, 光復路, 建國路, 民族路/民權路/民生路 (the 三民主義), 忠孝/仁愛/信義/和平, 成功路 (for 鄭成功). The consequence is that almost every one of the 368 鄉鎮市區 in the country has a 中正路 and a 中山路, so a road name in Taiwan is nearly useless as a location key: "中正路100號" without a city and district identifies several hundred distinct buildings. Compare a British or a Japanese address, where the street name usually does most of the work. THE ADDRESS IS FOUR LEVELS DEEP BELOW THE ROAD and this is what a schema built for a Western address cannot hold: 段 (section, splitting a long road into numbered runs), 巷 (lane, a side street numbered by the house number it leaves the road at), 弄 (alley, a side street off a lane) and finally 號 (the building number), giving 臺北市中正區忠孝東路一段1號 or 新北市板橋區文化路二段242巷15弄3號. A 巷 number is not a name — 242巷 is "the lane starting at number 242" — so lanes renumber when the road renumbers, and two different 242巷 on two different roads are unrelated. Taipei's east-west roads also take a 東/西/南/北 direction word as part of the name (忠孝東路 and 忠孝西路 are one continuous road split at 中山南路), which is part of the string and not a modifier. 臺灣大道 in 臺中市 is the exception that proves the rule: it was 中港路 until 2010 and was renamed to something local, which is rare enough to have been news. 高雄市 numbers its central roads 一心, 二聖, 三多, 四維, 五福, 六合, 七賢, 八德, 九如, 十全 — one to ten in order from south to north — the only large numbered sequence of road names in the country.
