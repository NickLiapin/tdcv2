THE MOTORCYCLE IS THE MAJORITY VEHICLE IN TAIWAN AND THAT IS THE REASON THIS FILE EXISTS SEPARATELY. At the end of March 2025 the country had 8.689 million cars and 14.664 million motorcycles — 1.7 motorcycles for every car, 62.7 motorcycles per hundred people against 37.2 cars — so a synthetic Taiwanese vehicle table drawn only from taiwan.vehicle.plate is modelling the smaller half of the fleet. A Taiwanese household commonly owns no car and two scooters. THE FORMAT COLLIDES WITH THE CAR FORMAT AND THAT IS A REAL BUG THE MINISTRY HAD TO FIX: when the 8代1版 series came in on 2012-12-17 both cars and motorcycles were issued AAA-0001 from overlapping letter ranges, the registration system could not tell them apart, and in April 2013 twenty-six of the country's thirty-seven 監理站 stopped issuing motorcycle plates in the AAA-ADE band while the databases were split into separate car, motorcycle and trailer series. So a plate string ALONE does not identify a vehicle type in Taiwan and a vehicle table needs a type column beside it. The current motorcycle series ranges are MAY-PZZ, JAA-JZZ, XAA-ZZZ, SFA-SZZ, QNA-QZZ, LNA-LZZ, HEA-HZZ, KUB-KUL, KUN-KZZ, KQE-KQZ and WFA-WZZ; the first M plate was issued at 屏東監理站 on 2014-04-14, the first N in 2019 and the first P in 2023, which is close enough to a date stamp to be worth knowing. As with cars, I and O are never used. A 普通輕型機車 (under 50cc) carries a GREEN plate with the same layout, a 大型重型機車 over 550cc carries a white car-style plate and two of them, and an electric scooter had its own series from 2018 — none of those is generated here.
