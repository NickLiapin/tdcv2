address: myanmar.geo.postalCode
description: A Myanmar postcode — five digits, no letters and no separator, written bare (11181, 05011, 15011). The FIRST TWO DIGITS ARE THE STATE OR REGION and are drawn from the real fifteen in myanmar.geo.postalPrefix, so the division half of every value exists; the last three identify the delivery office inside that division and are drawn freely from 000 to 999, so a value is a well-formed code in a real division but usually not a code any office actually serves. That is the same compromise this pack makes for phone subscriber digits and for the NRC tail, and it is stated rather than hidden. There is no check digit. THE REAL POINT ABOUT MYANMAR POSTCODES IS THAT ALMOST NOBODY USES THEM. Myanmar Post assigns them, but a domestic Myanmar address is written as building, street or ward, TOWNSHIP, then town — မြို့နယ် is the unit that routes the letter, not the number — and postal delivery to a residential address is not something the country generally does: mail goes to a post office box or is carried by a private courier, and the address given to a courier is a description ending in a landmark. So a Myanmar address record with an empty postcode field is normal data, not missing data, and a validation rule that requires one will reject most of the country. The leading zero matters: ten of the fifteen prefixes begin with 0, so this column must be text — 01234 read as a number becomes 1234 and stops being a postcode. Not modelled: the office-level assignment, and the box numbers that most business post actually uses.
