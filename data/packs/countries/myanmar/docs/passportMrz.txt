address: myanmar.docs.passportMrz
description: The second line of the machine-readable zone of a Myanmar passport — 44 characters of TD3, WITH ALL FIVE ICAO CHECK DIGITS COMPUTED FOR REAL. This is the one place in this pack where a Myanmar identity document carries arithmetic that can be verified, and it is worth being exact about why: the check digits do not belong to the passport number, they belong to the printed zone. Myanmar itself publishes no checksum for the passport number, none for the NRC and none for the TIN, so nothing else in this pack computes one. What IS published is ICAO Doc 9303, and it applies to every Myanmar passport issued since the biometric book: take the field, give its characters the values 0-9 for digits, 10 to 35 for A to Z and 0 for the filler '<', multiply by the repeating weights 7, 3, 1, sum, and take the remainder modulo 10. The algorithm here was verified against the specimen printed in Doc 9303 itself — document number L898902C3 check 6, date of birth 740812 check 2, expiry 120415 check 9, optional data check 1, composite check 0 — and against that same specimen with a digit altered, which the check rejects. THE LAYOUT, position by position: 1-9 the document number left-aligned and padded with '<' (a Myanmar number is eight characters, so there is exactly one filler); 10 its check digit; 11-13 the nationality, MMR, which is Myanmar's ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code and is NOT MYA or BUR — MYA is the IOC code used at the Olympics and BUR is the pre-1989 code, and all three are in circulation in different datasets; 14-19 date of birth as YYMMDD; 20 its check; 21 sex, M, F or '<'; 22-27 expiry as YYMMDD; 28 its check; 29-42 optional data, left blank on Myanmar books; 43 its check; 44 the composite check over positions 1-10, 14-20 and 22-43 together. NOTE THE TWO-DIGIT YEAR, which is a live ambiguity rather than a historical one: an MRZ carries no century, so a date of birth of 30 is 1930 on an old holder's book and 2030 on a child's, and the reader is expected to disambiguate from context. Not modelled here: line 1, which carries the surname and given names transliterated into Latin — and Burmese names transliterate badly and inconsistently, do not divide into surname and given name at all (a Myanmar person has one name, and အောင် ကျော် is not a first name plus a family name), and are truncated to fit 39 characters, so line 1 of a Myanmar MRZ frequently does not round-trip back to the holder's actual name.
generator: tdc
