description: A Myanmar vehicle registration plate in the form introduced in 2011 and re-issued from July 2021, e.g. "YGN-36 3G-7224". IT IS A TWO-LINE PLATE and the string here is the two lines joined by a space. The top line is the region code and the registering township's numeral, drawn as a real pair from myanmar.vehicle.regionCode; the bottom line is a six-character serial written as two characters, a hyphen and four digits. THE SERIAL HAS NO PUBLISHED PATTERN — the reference that describes the plate says so explicitly — so the shape here is taken from the published example plates (3G-7224 on a private car, 1A-1234 on a hire vehicle, AB-1234 on a taxi), which show the first character as either a digit or a letter and the second always a letter. Nothing on the plate is a check character and nothing verifies anything else; a Myanmar plate is an allocation, and confirming one means asking the ကုန်းလမ်းပို့ဆောင်ရေးညွှန်ကြားမှုဦးစီးဌာန. WHAT THE PLATE ALSO CARRIES, and it is why a Myanmar plate photograph holds more than its number: the make and model of the vehicle are printed on the plate itself, below the serial, which no European plate does. THE COLOUR IS THE VEHICLE'S CLASS, not its region — black with white text is a private car, yellow with black a religious vehicle, red with white a taxi or hire car, blue a tour vehicle, green a volunteer service vehicle — so a Myanmar plate stripped to its characters has lost the field that says what the vehicle is for, and no character in the string encodes it. Not modelled: that colour class, the older Burmese-script plates in the ၁က/၁၂၃၄ form still on the road, the separate motorcycle series, and the fact that Myanmar drives on the RIGHT with mostly right-hand-drive imported cars, which is a road-safety fact rather than a plate one but explains why the fleet is Japanese second-hand and the model names on the plates are Japanese.
