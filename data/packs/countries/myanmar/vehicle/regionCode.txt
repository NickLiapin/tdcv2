address: myanmar.vehicle.regionCode
description: The top line of a Myanmar number plate — a three-letter region code and the numeral of the township that registered the vehicle, kept together as one value because THE NUMERAL RANGE IS DIFFERENT IN EVERY REGION and drawing the two apart produces townships that do not exist. Yangon runs YGN-1 to YGN-45, Shan runs SHN-1 to SHN-55, Chin only CHN-1 to CHN-9, and Nay Pyi Taw NPW-1 to NPW-8; the ranges follow the number of townships each region has. All fifteen codes: AYY ဧရာဝတီ, BGO ပဲခူး, CHN ချင်း, KCN ကချင်, KYH ကယား, KYN ကရင်, MDY မန္တလေး, MGY မကွေး, MON မွန်, NPW နေပြည်တော်, RKE ရခိုင်, SGG စစ်ကိုင်း, SHN ရှမ်း, TNI တနင်္သာရီ, YGN ရန်ကုန်. THESE ARE LATIN LETTERS ON A BURMESE PLATE and that is the recent and deliberate part: Myanmar plates were written in Burmese script with Burmese numerals until September 2013, when the Road Transport Administration Department switched to Latin so that plates could be read by cameras and by drivers from the neighbouring countries. Older Burmese-script plates in the ၁က/၁၂၃၄ form are still on the road. This is A THIRD NUMBERING OF MYANMAR'S REGIONS, unrelated to the NRC state numbers and unrelated to the postcode prefixes — the plate scheme identifies a region by letters and then counts townships inside it, so the only thing the three schemes share is the set of places they cover. Not modelled: the numeral is drawn independently of any township named elsewhere in a record.
