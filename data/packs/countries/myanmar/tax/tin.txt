NINE DIGITS, numeric throughout, no country prefix and no letters — the same nine-digit shape for an individual and for a company, unlike the two-tier schemes of most of the region. THERE IS NO PUBLISHED CHECK DIGIT AND THIS PACK COMPUTES NONE. The Tax Administration Law makes the IRD responsible for determining and issuing each TIN and says nothing about its composition; no notification, no IRD form and no published guide states a checksum rule, so the nine digits are drawn and no arithmetic is claimed over them. That is a deliberate refusal: a mod-11 invented to look like Poland's NIP would validate here and nowhere in Myanmar, which is worse than no check at all because it looks verified. Validating a Myanmar TIN means asking the IRD, and the IRD offers no public lookup. THE TIN IS RECENT AND THIS CHANGES WHAT MYANMAR TAX DATA LOOKS LIKE: it became compulsory on 1 April 2022, replacing a mess in which businesses were identified by their export/import registration number, their DICA company number or their old taxpayer file reference depending on which office was asking. So Myanmar records dated before 2022 frequently carry no TIN at all, and a schema that treats the field as required will reject the country's own recent history. Note also that Myanmar's fiscal year moved: it ran April to March until 2018, then October to September for a transitional period, then back to April to March from 2022 — three different year boundaries in five years, which is a much bigger hazard in Myanmar tax data than any identifier format.
