description: The agencies, services, funds and public legal entities of the Azerbaijani state — the bodies BELOW the ministries, which is what makes this list complementary to the ministry list already in the az language pack (az/government/agency.txt) rather than a duplicate of it. Nothing here repeats a name from there: the ministries, the Milli Məclis, the Cabinet and the Presidential Administration live in the language pack because they are what the Azerbaijani words mean, and these are the operating bodies a record actually names as a counterparty. Two Azerbaijani inventions dominate the citizen's side of it. ASAN XİDMƏT — formally the State Agency for Public Service and Social Innovations — put every document-issuing function of the state into one counter in 2012, so an Azerbaijani renewing a passport, registering a company and paying a fine does all three in one building and one queue, and the model has since been exported as a consultancy product; DOST does the same for social benefits and pensions. Both are agencies under the President rather than under a ministry, which is why they have no ministerial parent in an org chart. The financial bodies are worth knowing before reading any Azerbaijani financial record: DÖVLƏT NEFT FONDU (SOFAZ) holds the oil revenue outside the budget and is larger than the banking system; MƏRKƏZİ BANK runs the manat's dollar peg and, since the 2020 abolition of the separate markets supervisor, also supervises the banks and insurers; DÖVLƏT XƏZİNƏDARLIQ AGENTLİYİ settles every payment the state makes; and SOCAR, the state oil company, is legally a company rather than an agency and is therefore not in this list even though it is the largest employer in the country. The names move: the Ministry of Taxes became the Dövlət Vergi Xidməti under the Ministry of Economy in 2019, the Ministry of Transport, Communications and High Technologies was folded into the Rəqəmsal İnkişaf və Nəqliyyat Nazirliyi in 2021, and the financial markets supervisor Maliyyə Bazarlarına Nəzarət Palatası was abolished in November 2020 — all three appear in records dated before those changes and none is current.
