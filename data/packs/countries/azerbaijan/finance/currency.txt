description: The two Azerbaijani money units. The currency is the manat (ISO 4217 AZN), divided into 100 qəpik. Three facts about it are worth carrying. First, THE SYMBOL IS ₼, U+20BC MANAT SIGN, adopted in 2006 and added to Unicode only in version 7.0 in 2014 — so any font, terminal or PDF pipeline older than that renders it as a box, which is why Azerbaijani invoices from the 2010s fall back to "AZN" or to the letters "man." and why a pack that assumes the symbol always displays is optimistic. Second, THE CURRENT MANAT IS THE SECOND ONE: on 1 January 2006 Azerbaijan redenominated at 5 000 old manat to 1 new, so a price of 10 000 manat is either a 2005 loaf of bread or a 2026 car depending on which currency it is quoted in, and historical Azerbaijani financial data spanning that date is unusable without knowing which side of it a figure sits on. The old currency's ISO code was AZM and the new one AZN; a dataset that carries the amount and not the code has lost the distinction. Third, the manat is not floated: it has been pegged to the US dollar at 1.70 AZN to the dollar since 2017, so an Azerbaijani price series looks flat against the dollar and moves against everything else, and the two devaluations of 2015 — from 0.78 to 1.05 in February and to 1.55 in December — are the sharpest breaks in any modern Azerbaijani time series. The plural of manat is manat, with no ending, and the same for qəpik; "2 manat" is correct Azerbaijani and "2 manatlar" is not. See azerbaijan.finance.amount for how a figure is written.
