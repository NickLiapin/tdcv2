description: The 64 districts (rayonlar) of Azerbaijan as the State Statistical Committee's İnzibati Ərazi Bölgüsü Təsnifatı, 2024 lists them — approved by collegium decision 2/2 of 16 February 2024 and amended on 24 April 2024 and 8 July 2026. THE COUNT IS THE TRAP. Almost every secondary source still says 66, and 66 was right until 2023, so a pack that copies it is wrong in two different directions at once. Two things moved. (1) AĞDƏRƏ WAS CREATED on 5 December 2023 by law 1043-VIQ, out of territory that had been administered as parts of Ağdam, Kəlbəcər and Tərtər since the district was abolished in 1992 — it is the first new Azerbaijani district in a generation and it is missing from every list written before 2024. (2) The classification does NOT list Lənkəran, Şəki and Yevlax as rayons: each of those three is a city of republic subordination whose administration covers the surrounding rural territory, so the classification carries LƏNKƏRAN ŞƏHƏRİ, ŞƏKİ ŞƏHƏRİ and YEVLAX ŞƏHƏRİ with all their villages under them, and no district of the same name. The SSC's own demographic tables, confusingly, DO print "Lənkəran rayonu" as a unit name for the same territory, which is where the extra three in "66" come from. So 64 is the administrative-classification count, 67 is what you get if you add those three back to the post-Ağdərə list, and 66 is the pre-Ağdərə version of that. Seven of the 64 are in the Naxçıvan Autonomous Republic — Babək, Culfa, Kəngərli, Ordubad, Sədərək, Şahbuz, Şərur — which is an EXCLAVE, separated from the rest of the country by Armenian territory and reachable only through Iran or by air, so a route or logistics model that treats Azerbaijan as one connected polygon is wrong about a seventh of its districts. Five more — Cəbrayıl, Kəlbəcər, Qubadlı, Laçın, Zəngilan, and with them Ağdam, Füzuli, Xocalı, Xocavənd, Şuşa — were under occupation until 2020–2023 and are being resettled, which is why their populations in azerbaijan.geo.city are small and rising fast rather than stable. Sorted in Azerbaijani alphabetical order, which is NOT the English one: Ç follows C, Ə follows E, Ğ follows G, X follows H, İ follows I, Ö follows O, Ş follows S and Ü follows U. THE ALPHABET ITSELF IS THE OTHER HAZARD IN THIS PACK. Azerbaijani Latin carries Ə (U+018F capital, U+0259 small), the schwa, which almost no other Latin orthography uses and which no Latin-1 or Windows-1252 pipeline can represent — it appears 750 times across this pack and in 21 of its 26 files, so a byte-level round trip through a legacy encoding destroys most of the data here rather than a corner of it. And Azerbaijani, like Turkish, has FOUR letters where English has two: the dotless ı (U+0131) with its capital I, and the dotted i with its capital İ (U+0130), and they are DIFFERENT LETTERS, not case variants of each other. A case-insensitive comparison must therefore fold with the Azerbaijani (or Turkish) rules and not the default ones: the default lowercase of İ is the two code points U+0069 U+0307, an i with a loose combining dot that no user will ever type, so "İmişli" naively lower-cased will not match a search for "imişli"; and the default uppercase of the plain i is I rather than İ, so an upper-then-lower normalisation turns Bakı into "baki" and Xırdalan into "xirdalan", changing the name of the capital into a different word. 45 of this pack's values change under a default lowercase and are stable under an Azerbaijani one. Values are stored in NFC with the schwa as a single code point, never as a base letter plus a combining mark. Written bare here; in an address the word rayonu follows the name — "Quba rayonu, Alpan kəndi".
