description: An Azerbaijani postcode — the letters AZ and four digits, written without a space and placed BEFORE the settlement name on the same line, "AZ1010 Bakı şəh." That position is the thing a foreign address template gets wrong: the Universal Postal Union's Azerbaijani specification puts the postcode to the LEFT of the locality, the whole address block right-aligned in the bottom right corner of the envelope, which is the opposite of the British and American habit and different again from the German one. The AZ is part of the code, not a country prefix bolted on for export: Azərpoçt prints it on domestic mail too, and stripping it leaves four bare digits that no Azerbaijani sorting office recognises as a postcode. The first two digits are the district (see azerbaijan.geo.postalPrefix) and the last two the post office within it, with 00 the district centre — AZ0100 is Xırdalan, the centre of Abşeron, and AZ6715 is Nehrəm village in Babək district in the Nakhchivan exclave, both published examples. Baku takes the whole of AZ10nn and AZ11nn. HOW THIS FILE DIFFERS FROM ITS NORWEGIAN AND POLISH EQUIVALENTS, and the difference matters: Azerbaijan does not publish an open postcode register, so where those packs ship the real list of issued codes, this one draws a real district prefix and then two digits. A value from here is therefore well-formed and lands in a district that exists, but the last two digits are not guaranteed to be an issued post office — it is a correct-format code, not a deliverable one. Before independence the whole country used the Soviet six-digit form 37NNNN; nothing in that shape survives. Sources: UPU addressing specification for Azerbaijan (07/2019); Azərpoçt district index table.
