---
address: azerbaijan.geo.postalPrefix
description: The 65 two-digit district codes that open an Azerbaijani postcode — the NN in AZNNnn. They are a real register, not a range: within 01-80 the values 14, 26, 28, 29, 32, 39, 41, 45, 58, 64 and 75-79 are not issued, and nothing above 80 is used at all, so a validator that accepts AZ + any four digits accepts about a quarter more codes than exist. Baku holds TWO of them, 10 and 11, which is why the codes either side of Baku's block are shifted by one against the vehicle-plate register. That shift is the thing to know, because the postal codes and the number-plate region codes (azerbaijan.vehicle.regionCode) are LARGELY THE SAME NATIONAL REGISTER AND NOT QUITE: 01 Abşeron, 05 Ağstafa, 15 Cəlilabad, 17 Şabran, 18 Şirvan, 20 Gəncə, 27 Xaçmaz, 50 Sumqayıt, 55 Şəki, 67 Babək, 72 Culfa, 73 Sədərək and 74 Kəngərli agree in both, but Beyləqan is 11 on a plate and 12 in a postcode, Biləsuvar is 12 and 13, and Xızı is 29 on a plate and 80 in a postcode. So a lookup that converts one to the other is right about most of the country and quietly wrong about the rest — the two registers were drawn up separately and only mostly reconciled. Sources: Azərpoçt's district index table; the plate register is compared in azerbaijan.vehicle.regionCode.
