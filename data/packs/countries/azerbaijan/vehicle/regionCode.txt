address: azerbaijan.vehicle.regionCode
description: The two-digit region codes that open an Azerbaijani number plate — the 10 in 10-AA-123. Unlike Norway, which abolished its county letters in 1971, and unlike most of Europe, Azerbaijan's plate DOES still say where the vehicle was registered, and the code is the first thing an Azerbaijani reads off a plate. The register runs 01 to 74 with 13 deliberately unissued, plus Baku's extra blocks and one national code. BAKU HOLDS FIVE: 10, 77, 88, 90 and 99, because one two-letter-plus-three-digit series is 676 000 plates and the capital exhausted 10 and then 90; 99 is often described as a nationwide code and is issued in Baku. The Nakhchivan exclave has its own block, 67 Babək, 68 Şərur, 69 Ordubad, 70 and 75 Naxçıvan city, 71 Şahbuz, 72 Culfa, 73 Sədərək, 74 Kəngərli, and 85 for the autonomous republic — which is the only administrative division in the country with two parallel code ranges, and a consequence of the exclave running its own vehicle registration. Codes 14 Cəbrayıl, 26 Xankəndi, 28 Xocavənd, 32 Kəlbəcər, 39 Qubadlı, 41 Laçın, 58 Şuşa and 64 Zəngilan belong to districts that were under occupation from the early 1990s until 2020-2023 and are only now issuing plates again. THIS REGISTER IS ALMOST BUT NOT QUITE THE POSTAL ONE: 01 Abşeron, 05 Ağstafa, 15 Cəlilabad, 17 Şabran, 18 Şirvan, 20 Gəncə, 27 Xaçmaz, 50 Sumqayıt, 55 Şəki, 67 Babək, 72 Culfa, 73 Sədərək and 74 Kəngərli carry the same number in both, but Beyləqan is 11 here and 12 in a postcode, Biləsuvar 12 and 13, and Xızı 29 here and 80 in a postcode. Anyone tempted to derive one from the other should read azerbaijan.geo.postalPrefix first. Two codes name places that are not districts in the current administrative classification — 26 Xankəndi and 18 Şirvan are cities of republic subordination — and Ağdərə, created in December 2023, has no plate code yet. Source: the vehicle registration code table published by the State Traffic Police and mirrored in the Azerbaijani-language reference lists.
