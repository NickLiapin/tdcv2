description: An ordinary Azerbaijani vehicle registration plate — a two-digit region code, a hyphen, two letters, a hyphen and three digits: 10-AA-123. The layout is Turkish in origin by way of the old French system, black on white, with the Azerbaijani flag and the letters AZ in a blue band on the left and, since September 2011, an RFID chip embedded below the flag. Three things about it are Azerbaijani rather than generic. THE HYPHENS ARE PART OF THE PLATE, not a display convention: the plate is stamped 10-AA-123 and a database that stores 10AA123 has to put them back, unlike Norway where the space is typographic. THE REGION CODE IS REAL AND CURRENT — Azerbaijan never abolished it, so unlike most of Europe the plate does say where the vehicle was registered, and azerbaijan.vehicle.regionCode carries the whole table with its gaps. And the DIGITS RUN FROM 001, not from 100: the published series runs 90-AA-001 to 90-ZZ-999, so a plate can and does end in a number with leading zeros, and a column typed as an integer turns 10-AA-007 into 10-AA-7. The letters are the full Latin A-Z in both positions — Azerbaijani plates do not use the Azerbaijani-only letters Ə, Ç, Ğ, İ, Ö, Ş or Ü, because a plate has to be readable at a border, which is the one place in this pack where the national alphabet is deliberately absent. Several two-letter pairs are reserved when combined with Baku's code 10 and are not ordinary plates even though they match this pattern: AA is senior officials, AM the Ministry of Defence, AP the police, AZ, AN and AE the Ministry of Internal Affairs, AO the Ministry of Justice, AS the State Security Service, PA the Presidential Administration, PM the presidential protection service and YP the traffic police. Diplomatic plates (001 D 001, red), foreign-resident plates (H 023 100, yellow), public transport (blue) and motorcycles (10 A 100) all have their own shapes and are not generated here.
