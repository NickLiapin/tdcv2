description: Azerbaijan's VAT number, and the honest answer is that THERE ISN'T A SEPARATE ONE. Azerbaijan has had value-added tax — ƏDV, əlavə dəyər vergisi — since 1992 and charges it at 18 per cent, but the State Tax Service issues no second identifier for it: a business registered for ƏDV is identified by its ordinary ten-digit VÖEN, and that is the number printed in the VAT box on an Azerbaijani invoice. There is no country-letter prefix, no suffix, and nothing in the number that says whether its holder is VAT-registered at all — registration is a separate fact held in the tax register, compulsory once taxable turnover passes 200 000 manat in twelve months and voluntary below that, so a company can hold a perfectly valid VÖEN and charge no VAT. Two traps follow. First, an EU VAT routine cannot be reused: Azerbaijan is not in the EU and not in VIES, an AZ-prefixed VAT number does not exist, and the check-digit rule the EU formats carry has no counterpart here — see azerbaijan.tax.voen for why the VÖEN's own ninth digit is not computable from published material. Second, VAT payment in Azerbaijan runs through a dedicated mechanism that has no equivalent in most countries: the ƏDV depozit hesabı, a single state VAT deposit account at the Treasury through which the tax portion of every invoice is settled separately from the goods portion, so an Azerbaijani B2B payment is often TWO transfers rather than one, and a payments model that assumes one transfer per invoice will not reconcile. This address therefore emits a VÖEN, because that is what an Azerbaijani VAT field contains.
