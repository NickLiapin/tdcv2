description: Philippine vehicle registration plate issued by the Land Transportation Office. The current private-car series, running since 2014, is three letters, a space, four digits — "ABC 1234", black on white. Motorcycles are a different shape entirely and are generated here too, in the three layouts the LTO has used since 2020: three digits then three letters, a letter then three digits then two letters, or two letters then three digits then a letter. THE COLOUR IS THE CLASSIFICATION, NOT THE CHARACTERS. A plate's background says what the vehicle is licensed to do: white for private, YELLOW FOR PUBLIC UTILITY — every jeepney, taxi, tricycle and bus for hire — red on white for government, green on white for electric and hybrid, blue on white for diplomatic. That yellow-plate distinction matters more in the Philippines than in most countries, because public utility vehicles are the backbone of transport and a for-hire vehicle on a white plate is committing an offence, so "plate number" alone loses information a Philippine record usually needs. Diplomatic plates are seven digits with the first three a country code, not letters at all, and are not generated here. NO CHECK DIGIT — the series is sequential stock allocation and encodes nothing verifiable. The first letter reflects the region of original registration (A Ilocos, D Calabarzon, N Metro Manila and so on), but a vehicle keeps its plate when it moves, so the letter is where it was first registered rather than where it is.
