description: Filipino surnames, roughly by frequency. THIS SITS IN A COUNTRY PACK ON PURPOSE, and it is the one file here where that placement deserves an argument. Apply the two tests: would it still be true if the country changed its language? Yes — a Philippines that spoke only English tomorrow would still be full of Bautistas and Reyeses. Would it still be true in the country next door speaking the same language? No — Spain's surname distribution is not this one, and neither is any neighbour's. The stock is a property of the PLACE, and of one administrative act. THE 1849 CLAVERIA DECREE IS WHY. Governor-General Narciso Claveria y Zaldua, unable to run a census or collect tax on a population that mostly used no fixed surname, issued the Catálogo alfabético de apellidos on 21 November 1849: 60,662 surnames, Spanish and indigenous, bound into 141 pages, distributed to every province. Civil servants then handed them out ALPHABETICALLY BY TOWN — a town was allotted a stretch of the catalogue and worked through it — so to this day whole municipalities share an initial, and a Filipino's surname often says which town an ancestor was registered in rather than who the family descended from. This is why a Spanish surname in the Philippines is usually NOT evidence of Spanish ancestry, and reading it that way is the standard mistake. That town-by-initial clustering is real and is deliberately NOT modelled here: no published table maps catalogue stretches to towns, so pairing this file with philippines.geo.city gives an independent draw rather than the correlation the decree created. Also present are the surnames the decree did not touch — pre-Hispanic Tagalog and Visayan names families were allowed to keep (Macapagal, Panganiban, Katigbak, Dimaculangan), the single-syllable Hokkien names of the Chinese-Filipino community (Tan, Lim, Sy, Ong, Uy, Go, Chua), and the Arabic-derived names of Muslim Mindanao (Abdul, Alonto, Mastura, Hataman), none of which came from the catalogue. GIVEN NAMES ARE NOT SHIPPED: Filipino first names are English and Spanish and the en and es packs already carry them, so there is nothing country-specific to add and adding it would be the language mistake this file is arguing its way out of.
