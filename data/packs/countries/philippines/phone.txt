description: Philippine mobile number in E.164 form — +63 followed by the ten-digit national number. Written nationally it is eleven digits with a leading zero, 09XX-XXX-XXXX; dropping that zero and prefixing +63 gives the form here, so 09171234567 and +639171234567 are the same number and both turn up in Philippine data. The two digits after the country code are the real carrier block: Globe (with TM and GOMO) holds 90, 91, 93, 95, 96, 97 and 99; Smart (with TNT and the former Sun) holds 81, 90, 91, 92, 93, 94, 96, 97, 98 and 99; DITO, licensed in 2019 as the third network, holds 89, 92, 94 and 99. The blocks overlap because they are subdivided at the third digit — 0917 is Globe and 0918 is Smart inside the same 91 block — and only the two-digit blocks are drawn here, because the full three-digit allocation table is not published anywhere that could be verified; the third digit is free, so a generated prefix is in a real block without claiming to be a real allocation. THE 08 BLOCK IS THE ODD ONE AND IT IS CORRECT: almost every Philippine mobile number starts 09, but 081 (Smart) and 089 (DITO) are genuinely assigned, so a validator built on "09 plus nine digits" will reject real numbers. Prefixes are still read as carrier badges in the Philippines, but number portability has been live since 2021, so a prefix no longer proves the carrier. THE COUNTRY IS MOBILE-ONLY IN PRACTICE: landline penetration is in low single digits while mobile subscriptions exceed the population, so a Philippine contact record with no mobile number is far stranger than one with no landline, and the mobile number doubles as the payment address for GCash and Maya. SIM registration has been compulsory since the SIM Registration Act of 2022, which tied every number to an identity document. Landlines are a different shape and not generated here: area code 2 with an eight-digit subscriber number in Metro Manila since the 2019 renumbering, two-digit area codes with seven digits elsewhere.
