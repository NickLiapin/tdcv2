description: Universities of the Philippines that exist, in English — which is not a translation choice but the fact of the matter: Philippine higher education is conducted in English, degrees, transcripts and diplomas are issued in English, and these are the institutions' own registered names. THE UNIVERSITY OF SANTO TOMAS, FOUNDED IN 1611, IS THE OLDEST UNIVERSITY IN ASIA — older than Harvard, and still operating on its own campus in Manila. The list mixes three systems that behave differently and are worth keeping apart when generating records: the University of the Philippines, the national university, whose constituent campuses (Diliman, Los Baños, Manila, Visayas, Mindanao) are separate institutions with separate admissions and are named that way here; the Catholic universities founded by religious orders — Santo Tomas by the Dominicans, Ateneo and Xavier by the Jesuits, De La Salle by the Christian Brothers, San Carlos by the Society of the Divine Word — which are among the most selective in the country; and the state universities and colleges, one or more per province, which teach most Philippine students. Names carry Spanish word order and Spanish articles because the orders that founded them were Spanish ("Ateneo de Manila", "Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila" being the Tagalog exception), so alphabetising these by first word puts a dozen institutions under A and D.
