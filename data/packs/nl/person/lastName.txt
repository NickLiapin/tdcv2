description: Dutch and Flemish surnames, roughly frequency-ordered. A Dutch full name carries a single surname, so draw once. Many of these begin with a tussenvoegsel - a particle such as de, van, van der, van den, ten or ter - and two rules follow from it that no config can infer. ALPHABETISATION IGNORES THE PARTICLE: de Vries files under V, van den Berg under B, so sorting these values as plain strings gives the wrong order for a Dutch reader. CAPITALISATION DEPENDS ON WHAT PRECEDES: the particle is lowercase when a given name or initial comes first (Jan de Vries, J. de Vries) and capitalised when it does not (meneer De Vries, De Vries kwam binnen). The values here are stored in the lowercase form, which is correct for '<firstName> <lastName>'; capitalise the particle yourself when the surname stands alone. Note that this is the Netherlands rule - in Flanders the capitalisation is fixed per family and forms part of the official name, which is why the Flemish entries below appear as Vandenberghe, De Smet or Vanhoutte and should not be recased.
