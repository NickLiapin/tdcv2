description: Language name (Telugu). The list is led by తెలుగు itself and then by the 22 languages of the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, which are the ones an Indian form actually offers, followed by the major world languages. About the first entry: Telugu is spoken by roughly 95 million people, is the official language of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana and one of India's six classical languages, and is agglutinative — grammar is built by stacking suffixes onto a stem rather than by separate words, so ఇంటికి is "to the house" in one word and ఇళ్ళల్లోనుంచి is "from inside the houses" in another. It is also vowel-final to a degree almost no other Indian language matches: nearly every Telugu word ends in a vowel, which gives the language the open, syllable-timed sound that earned it the name "the Italian of the East". The script is Telugu (U+0C00–U+0C7F), an abugida written left to right — it is NOT Devanagari, and it is not Kannada either, though the Telugu and Kannada scripts descend from a shared Kadamba ancestor and still resemble each other closely enough that readers of one can half-read the other.
