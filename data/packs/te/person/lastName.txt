description: Telugu family name — the ఇంటిపేరు, literally "house name". THE ORDER IS THE OPPOSITE OF HINDI AND OF ENGLISH: in Telugu the family name comes FIRST and the given name follows, so the name is written ఘట్టమనేని మహేష్ బాబు, నందమూరి తారక రామారావు, కందుకూరి వీరేశలింగం — never the reverse. Any config joining a first and a last name for this locale must join lastName + " " + firstName; the two fullName generators in person/male and person/female do exactly that. The reason for the order is what the word means: an ఇంటిపేరు is the name of the family HOUSE or of the ancestral VILLAGE (కందుకూరు, గురజాడ, తెనాలి, పాములపర్తి are all places), so a Telugu name reads as "of house X, the person Y" — the place is stated first, exactly as a postal address is. In everyday writing the house name is nearly always abbreviated to its initial and only the given name is spelled out: పి. వి. నరసింహారావు is పాములపర్తి వేంకట నరసింహారావు, ఎన్. టి. రామారావు is నందమూరి తారక రామారావు. A dataset that prints the house name in full is therefore MORE explicit than real Telugu paperwork usually is, not less. Deliberately EXCLUDED are the caste-derived title words — రెడ్డి, నాయుడు, రాజు, చౌదరి, శర్మ, శాస్త్రి, వర్మ, గౌడ్, ఆచారి — because those follow the GIVEN name rather than preceding it (వై. ఎస్. రాజశేఖర రెడ్డి has house name యెడుగూరి సందింటి and title రెడ్డి), so putting them in this list would place them in the wrong slot on every row; a config that wants one should append it after the given name. The weights use a deliberately flat curve, which is the honest shape here: Telugu house names number in the thousands and none of them is dominant — this is the opposite of the Vietnamese or Korean case, and a sharply concentrated curve would misdescribe the language badly.
locale: te
weighted: true
