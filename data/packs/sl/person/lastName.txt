description: Slovene surname, ONE list for both sexes. Slovene surnames do NOT inflect for sex in official use: Ana Novak and her brother Marko Novak carry the identical registered form, and so do Kovačič, Zupančič and the rest — unlike Czech Novák/Nováková or Slovak Novák/Nováková, and like Croatian. That is why this pack has no person/female/lastName.txt: its absence is the design, not an omission. What DOES exist is the -ova possessive — Novakova, Zupančičeva, "the Novak woman" — and in Slovene it is not merely colloquial: newspapers and sports commentary use it as the normal way to refer to a woman by surname alone (Garnbretova, Štuhčeva), while the registry, the ID card and any legal document use the bare form. So the -ova forms are deliberately not here: a data pack models the register, not the sports page. What differs between the sexes is declension, not form — a man's surname declines through the six cases (videl sem Marka Novaka) while a woman's stays in the nominative (videl sem Ano Novak). The order is the real one: Novak leads the Slovene register at roughly 11.000 bearers in a country of 2,1 million, about 0,5 percent, followed by Horvat, Kovačič, Krajnc and Zupančič. Weights are the pack default rank-decay curve rather than measured counts. Over this list the curve gives its top entry 0,7 percent against Novak's real 0,5 percent, so it is slightly steep at the head and much too flat at the tail — the 230th name here draws about half as often as the first, where the real 230th Slovene surname has a few hundred bearers against Novak's eleven thousand. Slovene surnames really are spread, so the curve does not misrepresent the country badly, but a config measuring collision rates should know the tail is modelled, not counted. NOTE ON WHAT IS ABSENT: surnames of Bosnian, Serbian and Croatian origin ending in -ić and -ović are genuinely common in Slovenia — the largest single group after the Slovene ones, from the Yugoslav-era migration — but they are deliberately not in this list, because ć and đ are letters of the Croatian and Serbian alphabets and not of the Slovene one, and admitting them would make this pack indistinguishable from those two on the one test that separates them. A config that needs them should draw from the hr or sr pack alongside this one.
