description: Language name (Slovene, lowercase — Slovene orthography does not capitalise language names, unlike English). Slovene names a language with a noun in -ŠČINA built on the people's name, so the ending is a reliable signature of the list. Slovene itself is a South Slavic language written in the LATIN alphabet with č, š and ž and nothing else added: it has NO q, w, x or y, and — the point that separates it from its southern neighbours — no ć and no đ, which belong to Croatian and Serbian. It is the only living Indo-European language outside Sorbian to have kept the DUAL, a full third grammatical number for exactly two that sits beside the singular and the plural and changes nouns, adjectives, pronouns and verbs alike; and it has six cases, of which the nominative is the citation form used throughout this pack. Its other extreme is dialect: Slovene is normally counted as SEVEN dialect groups holding some fifty dialects, across barely two million speakers, which is the densest dialect spread of any European language — speakers from Prekmurje and from the Soča valley may not understand one another, and everyone uses the standard to bridge it.
