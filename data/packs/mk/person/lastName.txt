description: Macedonian surnames in the MASCULINE form, commonest first. THEY INFLECT FOR GENDER, so person/female/lastName.txt is a real file, and it is DERIVED from this one line for line by a rule rather than maintained by hand — the two must stay aligned row for row or a generated household gets a husband and a wife from different families. THE RULE IN FULL, four cases that cover every value here: -ски becomes -ска (Стојановски -> Стојановска); -ов becomes -ова (Иванов -> Иванова); -ев becomes -ева (Георгиев -> Георгиева); and -иќ DOES NOT CHANGE AT ALL (Поповиќ stays Поповиќ), because -иќ is the Serbian patronymic type and Serbian surnames have no separate feminine form. THE -СКИ TYPE IS THE MACEDONIAN ONE and it dominates: it is an ADJECTIVE, not a patronymic — Стојановски is "of the Stojan family" — and it agrees with its bearer the way any adjective agrees, which is precisely why a woman cannot carry the masculine form. Note the two spellings, -овски and -оски: Стојановски and Стојаноски, Николовски and Николоски, Ефремовски and Ефремоски are the same name, and the shorter one with the в dropped is the WESTERN MACEDONIAN variant, standard on documents around Ohrid, Struga, Kicevo and Prilep. Neither is a misspelling of the other and both must be expected in one dataset. The -ов / -ев type is commoner in the east and is the shape Bulgarian and Russian have. The -иќ type belongs to the Serbian community and is a small minority here, the reverse of the proportion in Serbia. Macedonian-only letters appear in ordinary surnames and not as decoration: Ѓорѓиевски, Ѓуровски, Ѓошев, Џамбазовски, Џоновски, Бојаџиев, Велјановски, Иљоски, Огњановски. Хаџи- at the front is a fossilised pilgrimage title that became part of the family name. THE WEIGHTS ARE A FITTED rank-decay curve (rank^-0.25), NOT measured bearer counts — North Macedonia publishes no surname register — and the exponent puts the leading name near its estimated real share of about 1%.
