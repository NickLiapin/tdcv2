description: Common adjective (Swahili) in TWO shapes, and the difference matters. The entries with a LEADING HYPHEN are bare stems: they cannot stand alone, because a Swahili adjective takes a prefix agreeing with the noun class of whatever it describes — mtu MKUBWA, kitu KIKUBWA, vitu VIKUBWA, magari MAKUBWA, nyumba KUBWA, all from -kubwa. The hyphen is the dictionary citation form and is there so that a config which concatenates blindly produces something visibly wrong rather than quietly wrong. The entries WITHOUT a hyphen are invariable, mostly Arabic loans, and can be joined to any noun as they stand. Swahili adjectives follow the noun.
