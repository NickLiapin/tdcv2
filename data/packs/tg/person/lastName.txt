description: Tajik surname. THREE SYSTEMS ARE CURRENT AT ONCE AND ALL THREE ARE ON THE STREET TODAY. (1) The Soviet pattern, still the largest: a father's or grandfather's given name plus the Russian genitive -ов / -ев — Раҳмонов, Каримов, Ҷалилов. It was imposed at registration in the 1920s-30s and most Tajikistanis alive still carry it. (2) The RESTORED PERSIAN forms: -зода (born of), -иён (of the line of), -пур (son of, the rarest and much commoner in Iran than here), -фар, and the bare relative adjective in -ӣ — Раҳмонзода, Муродиён, Зарифӣ, Хуҷандӣ. (3) The bare ancestral name with nothing attached, usually a deliberate de-Russification: Эмомалӣ Раҳмонов became Эмомалӣ Раҳмон in 2007 and the country followed. THE 2016 LAW is why the second and third groups keep growing: amendments to the law on civil-status registration barred ethnic Tajiks from registering a newborn with Russian -ов / -ова / -ович / -овна endings, so anyone registered since carries a Tajik suffix while their parents and grandparents mostly do not. A DATASET SPANNING GENERATIONS MUST MIX THE TWO — an all--ов pack is a Soviet-era snapshot and an all--зода pack is a child's classroom. A FOURTH PATTERN uses the IZOFAT instead of a suffix: given name + -и + father's name, as in Рустами Эмомалӣ, 'Rustam of Emomali'. It cannot be built by gluing an ending on and is not in this list, because it is a two-name construction, not a surname. THE FEMININE FORM IS SHIPPED in person/female/lastName.txt, DERIVED FROM THIS FILE BY SCRIPT line for line: -ов takes -а and -ев takes -а, while -зода, -иён, -ӣ, -пур, -фар and the bare names DO NOT CHANGE AT ALL. Weights are rank-derived Zipf: Tajikistan publishes no surname frequency table and no single surname dominates.
