description: Writing system, named in Tamil. தமிழ் comes first, and the fact worth recording is that the Tamil script is NOT Devanagari and is not derived from it — it descends from Tamil-Brahmi through the round வட்டெழுத்து hand, and the two systems look and behave differently. Tamil is an abugida occupying U+0B80–U+0BFF, and it is unusually economical: 12 vowels, 18 consonants, the aytam ஃ, and 216 vowel-consonant combinations, 247 letters in all. It has NO separate letters for voiced or aspirated stops — one letter க carries k, g, h and kh, and which one is meant follows from position in the word — where Devanagari spells all five of ka, kha, ga, gha and nga apart with its own letter for each. The Grantha letters ஜ ஷ ஸ ஹ க்ஷ ஸ்ரீ were added to write Sanskrit and foreign words and are still felt as borrowings. வட்டெழுத்து and தமிழி (Tamil-Brahmi, attested in cave inscriptions from the third century BCE) are the historic Tamil scripts.
