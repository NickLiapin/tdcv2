description: The word that occupies the SURNAME FIELD of a Tamil record — which is not a surname. Tamil has no hereditary family name in the north-Indian sense. The name of record in Tamil Nadu, in Sri Lanka, in Singapore and in Malaysia is initial(s) + given name — "ரா. குமார்" / "R. Kumar" — and the initial expands to the FATHER's given name (ராமசாமி), or, in an older and still very live pattern, to the ancestral village or town (எம். ஜி. ராமச்சந்திரன், where M is Marudur). So this list holds two things. The bulk of it is the male-given-name stock used patronymically, because that is what the field actually contains. The rest is the inherited community titles that genuinely do pass down — பிள்ளை, முதலியார், செட்டியார், கவுண்டர், நாடார், தேவர், நாயுடு, ஐயர், ஐயங்கார் — a shrinking share, because the Dravidian movement made dropping a caste title a deliberate political act and a great many Tamils since the 1950s carry none at all. WHAT A CONFIG JOINING FIRST + LAST ACTUALLY PRODUCES: "ஆனந்த் ராமசாமி" is a correct Tamil name of record, but it means "Anand, son of Ramasamy", NOT "Anand of the Ramasamy family". Three consequences. Two rows sharing ராமசாமி are siblings, not cousins. The value must NOT be inherited by a second generation — the son of ஆனந்த் ராமசாமி is <given name> ஆனந்த், so a config building a family tree has to re-derive the field from the father's given name at every generation instead of copying it down. And a married woman in Tamil Nadu conventionally replaces her father's initial with her HUSBAND's given name, so the field changes on marriage while the given name does not. Two further notes. The Singapore NRIC and the Malaysian MyKad write the relation out instead of leaving it to an initial — "Kumar s/o Ramasamy", "Kumar a/l Ramasamy", with d/o and a/p for daughters — so a config targeting those two countries should join with that particle rather than with a bare space; the particles themselves are a country convention and live in the country packs, not here. Finally, there is deliberately no person/female/lastName.txt: none of these words inflects for gender, and daughters take the father's name in exactly the form sons do. The weights are a tiered long tail: Tamil has no dominant name, and the head of this list is common because a handful of male given names were overwhelmingly popular in the generation that is now the fathers of record, not because a lineage is large.
