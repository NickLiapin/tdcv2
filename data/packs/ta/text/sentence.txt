Handwritten: Project Gutenberg holds no Tamil book at all — the language search returns no records — so these were written by hand rather than extracted from a public-domain corpus by data/scripts/build-text-corpus.mjs the way the en, pl and ru filler is. A reader should be able to tell which locales have a real corpus behind them and which do not, and this one does not. These are in WRITTEN Tamil (செந்தமிழ்), the literary standard, and that choice matters more in Tamil than in most languages: Tamil diglossia is extreme, and written and spoken Tamil differ enough in verb endings, pronouns and vocabulary to work as two nearly separate registers. Nobody speaks the sentences in this file, and nobody writes the way they speak — a Tamil novel narrates in written Tamil and puts spoken Tamil (பேச்சுத்தமிழ்) inside quotation marks. So this filler reads correctly as prose, as a document, as an article; a config generating chat messages, subtitles or dialogue with it will produce something no Tamil speaker would ever say. They end in an ordinary Latin full stop, which is correct: unlike Devanagari and Bengali, Tamil never adopted the danda and uses the same period, comma and question mark as English.
